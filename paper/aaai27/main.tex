\begin{abstract}
Offline model-based optimization ships a surrogate and an optimizer together and reports one number, leaving open which of the two it credits, as the subfield's own 2026 survey concedes \citep{kim2025mbosurvey}. We cross both axes under one shared protocol, three surrogate classes against three search routines, and decompose the outcome variance. The crossed design estimates a term a one-factor-at-a-time study cannot, our strongest result: the surrogate$\times$optimizer interaction ($\eta^2_{\text{inter}}\approx0.15$ under min--max normalization) excludes zero in all four engine corners and exceeds the optimizer main effect in eleven of twelve corner$\times$normalizer combinations, inverting only under rank normalization; a design that varies only the surrogate \citep{li2024bnnsurrogates} cannot see it. It also dissolves a confusion: a small optimizer \mdemph{marginal} ($\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.038$) licenses ``optimizer choice explains little variance,'' never ``optimizer choice is arbitrary,'' since its \mdemph{conditional} effect is four to thirty-three times larger. Any $\eta^2$ headline is a joint artifact of five operating-point coordinates---$K$, $\beta$, search budget, engine corner, and the per-task normalizer---so a headline stating fewer is not reproducible. The instrument exposing them is a taxonomy of five confounds specific to offline-MBO surrogate comparison, each traced to a line of the scoring path with a signed effect and a pre-registered kill criterion, of which the $\beta$--$\sigma$ criterion fired. We find no prior offline-MBO work reporting a crossed surrogate$\times$optimizer decomposition; Policy-Guided Gradient Search, whose Table 1 supplies our reference point, fixes the surrogate and never crosses it \citep{chemingui2024pggs}. Correcting the two \mdemph{protocol} confounds moves the surrogate effect \mdemph{up}, not down---from our decomposition of PGS's Table 1 ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}\approx0.37$) to $0.405$ $[0.290,0.556]$, a direction we found no precedent for---though the corners do not resolve at $n{=}7$ tasks, so we claim the direction, not a level; $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}>\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ in all twelve. What the advantage \mdemph{is} stays open, narrowed by seven controls (Section~\ref{sec:mech}). On Design-Bench we detect no surrogate difference at this power, which at $n{=}7$ tasks is not equivalence, and matching query budgets \mdemph{raises} the optimizer effect $1.5$ to $1.9\times$. This is no field reversal: that the statistical model often outranks the acquisition heuristic is a decade-old doctrine \citep{shahriari2016humanoutoftheloop}; we falsify only the local premise of one recent paper \citep{chemingui2024pggs}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Offline model-based optimization (MBO) seeks a design $x$ maximizing an expensive black-box $f$ from a fixed dataset $\D$, with no further queries to $f$ during search \citep{trabucco2022designbench,kim2025mbosurvey}. The dominant recipe fits a surrogate $\hat f$ and optimizes it; because optimization pushes designs where $\hat f$ is unconstrained, the surrogate is made conservative, by a learned regularizer \citep{trabucco2021coms} or a pessimistic acquisition such as the lower confidence bound (LCB) $\mu(x)-\beta\sigma(x)$ \citep{srinivas2010ucb}. A common intuition holds that Gaussian-process LCB (GP-LCB) beats neural-ensemble LCB at low dimension because the GP posterior is better calibrated. But the two pipelines differ in \mdemph{two} coupled factors: the surrogate class (GP posterior vs.\ ensemble disagreement) and the acquisition optimizer, since GPs are usually optimized by perturbation or quasi-Newton restarts and neural surrogates by gradient ascent on $x$. Every method in this lineage ships both at once and reports one number---leaving open which the number credits.

\paragraph{The attribution question is open, by the field's own account.} The subfield's 2026 survey states that existing benchmarks ``often emphasize overall optimization performance without clarifying whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance'' \citep{kim2025mbosurvey}. Resolving that takes a design nobody has run: both axes varied against each other under one shared protocol, the outcome variance decomposed between them. Existing systematic studies fix one axis. The most thorough is \mdemph{broader} than ours on the surrogate axis precisely because it fixes the other: a seven-class Bayesian neural-network comparison that holds Monte-Carlo expected improvement fixed, runs \mdemph{online}, and decomposes nothing \citep{li2024bnnsurrogates}. A neighbouring line varies the \mdemph{training objective} at fixed architecture---a ranking loss \citep{tan2025ltr} or a conservative regularizer \citep{trabucco2021coms}---a different factor from model class, since a GP posterior cannot be instantiated without its marginal-likelihood fit. Offline RL does the same: its closest re-evaluation varies uncertainty-penalty heuristics while holding the policy optimizer fixed \citep{lu2022revisiting}. A design that fixes a factor cannot estimate it, so a seven-class comparison at one fixed acquisition rule is not a smaller version of this experiment but a transposed one. We run the crossed surrogate$\times$optimizer factorial (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}) and report the two-way decomposition; no prior offline-MBO work reports one, and the nearest near-miss is close enough to name.\footnote{Table 3 of \citet{tan2025ltr} is the nearest near-miss: a genuine $9\times2$ crossed grid in offline MBO with a clean $4\times2$ optimizer-on-trained-model sub-grid, but it crosses training \mdemph{loss} against method, not model class, and its method axis bundles model family with search paradigm, so it is not commensurable with our optimizer axis. One further title names both axes \citep{elsayed2014robust} but its primary text was unobtainable, and robust-parameter-design methodology is characteristically sequential, which would fail the crossing requirement.} Our reference point is PGS, which reports per-task results and claims surrogate class dominates but computes no decomposition \citep{chemingui2024pggs}; we reproduce its Table 1, compute the decomposition ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}\approx0.37$ on the uncorrected engine), then audit it.

\paragraph{The contribution.} The instrument, and the confound set that running it exposed. The one-way analogue is fANOVA at a fixed search run \citep{hutter2014fanova}; the closest two-axis design names fANOVA and \mdemph{declines} it \citep{moosbauer2022benchmarkdriven}. An instrument inherits every confound in the grid it measures, and building the crossed grid surfaced five specific to offline-MBO surrogate comparison, each traced to a line of the scoring path. The contribution is that composition: the five confounds, the protocol that removes them, and what the decomposition says once they are gone.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Five named confounds and a removal protocol} (Sections~\ref{sec:confounds}--\ref{sec:deconf}), each with a code-level trace, a signed effect on $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$, and a pre-registered kill criterion; the $\beta$--$\sigma$ criterion fired. This is the paper's novel object. Correcting the two \mdemph{protocol} confounds moves $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ \mdemph{up}, from our decomposition of PGS's Table 1 ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}\approx0.37$) \citep{chemingui2024pggs} to $0.405$ $[0.290,0.556]$---no precedent in the ML audit literature---though the corner intervals do not resolve at $n{=}7$; we claim the direction, not a level.
\item \textbf{A surrogate$\times$optimizer interaction, the term the design exists to estimate} (Section~\ref{sec:deconf}): $\eta^2_{\text{inter}}$ of $0.146$--$0.165$, a bootstrap interval excluding zero in all four corners, four to thirty-three times the optimizer main effect. Its conditional and marginal ($\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.038$) effects are different quantities---which is why ``optimizer choice explains little \mdemph{marginal} variance'' and ``optimizer choice is arbitrary'' are not the same claim.
\item \textbf{An operating-point thesis} (Section~\ref{sec:deconf}): any $\eta^2$ headline is a joint artifact of five coordinates---$K$, $\beta$, search budget, engine corner, and the per-task normalizer---so a headline stating fewer is not reproducible.
\item \textbf{A mechanism narrowed by elimination} (Section~\ref{sec:mech}): seven controls---$\sigma$, width, budget, held-out accuracy, mean roughness, premise coverage, and distance-to-support with inflation---and none survives.
\item \textbf{A Design-Bench null with its power stated} (Section~\ref{sec:db}): no detectable difference at $n{=}7$ tasks, never equivalence \citep{agarwal2021precipice}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} No field reversal. That the statistical model often matters more than the acquisition heuristic is the organizing doctrine of the standard BO review \citep{shahriari2016humanoutoftheloop}, a decade old; our optimizer-axis result falsifies the \mdemph{local} premise of one recent paper \citep{chemingui2024pggs} and nothing wider. We claim no mechanism discovery, no equivalence on Design-Bench, and no result about ensemble size.

\section{Background and Related Work}

\paragraph{LCB, pessimism, and coverage.} GP-LCB $\mu-\beta\sigma$ has regret guarantees under GP assumptions \citep{srinivas2010ucb}; deep ensembles \citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles} give a cheap uncertainty proxy with no calibration guarantee. The pessimism principle behind conservative offline methods is that a valid lower bound prevents the optimizer exploiting model error \citep{jin2021pessimism}; we make that premise measurable. Conformal methods give distribution-free coverage, including inside the BO loop \citep{angelopoulos2023conformal,stanton2023conformalbo}; we use coverage of the LCB premise only as a \mdemph{measurement}, where the optimizer operates, and Section~\ref{sec:mech} shows it separable from outcome.

\paragraph{Offline MBO and its evaluation.} The conservative-surrogate lineage runs from Conservative Objective Models \citep{trabucco2021coms} and its CQL-style regularizer \citep{kumar2020cql} through generative surrogates \citep{brookes2019cbas,krishnamoorthy2023ddom}, all holding the search rule fixed while varying the model. A neighbouring line varies the training \mdemph{objective} at fixed architecture---a ranking loss \citep{tan2025ltr} beside a conservative regularizer \citep{trabucco2021coms}. The diagnosis that oracle-based design queries its surrogate where its training data does not reach, so outputs and uncertainty alike become unreliable, is due to \citet{fannjiang2020autofocused}. Optimizer-as-contribution work \citep{chemingui2024pggs} proposes a method rather than decomposing; its motivating premise---that offline black-box optimization has focused on improving surrogate models while using fixed search strategies---is the one \mdemph{local} claim our design falsifies. Design-Bench \citep{trabucco2022designbench} standardizes tasks and documents the confounded status quo. The second half of our method---naming unreported choices, giving the protocol that removes them, showing the ranking move---is the established reality-check genre, whose shape we claim no credit for \citep{henderson2018matters,agarwal2021precipice,balduzzi2018reevaluating,ferraridacrema2019worrying,musgrave2020realitycheck,lucic2018gansequal}; in offline optimization SOO-Bench \citep{qian2025soobench} stress-tests optimizer \mdemph{stability}, not attribution. The online-BO controlled study \citep{li2024bnnsurrogates} is broader on the surrogate axis but silent on search and attribution, and none of the two-axis precedents \citep{hutter2014fanova,liang2021benchmarking,moosbauer2022benchmarkdriven} decomposes.

\section{Experimental Grid and Confounds}
\label{sec:confounds}

\paragraph{The grid.} The crossed grid (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}) runs 7 synthetic tasks (Branin-2D through Griewank-30D, drawn once at seed 0) at 30 seeds and 7 Design-Bench tasks at 16 seeds. \textbf{Surrogates:} a $K{=}5$ ensemble of two-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs (width 96), with $\mu,\sigma$ the member mean and standard deviation; an \mdemph{exact GP} (BoTorch \texttt{SingleTaskGP}, marginal-likelihood fit on a score-biased subsample); and a \mdemph{sparse variational GP} (SVGP). \textbf{Optimizers:} 100 Adam steps on $x$; 5 rounds of Gaussian hill-climbing; CMA-ES. All three surrogates are differentiable, so all nine cells are realizable (full configuration in the supplement). Every cell maximizes the LCB $\mu(x)-\beta\sigma(x)$ at $\beta{=}2$, scored by the oracle on its 128 returned designs at the 100th percentile. Per task we min--max normalize the nine cell means and take a two-way ANOVA; $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ and $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ are the variance fractions of the two main effects, with task-and-seed hierarchical bootstrap intervals ($B{=}10{,}000$).

Two details of that description are engine state: the exact GP's default covariance is ARD RBF under the BoTorch pinned for the synthetic grid but ARD Mat\'ern-$5/2$ under the older Design-Bench version, and the GPs alone spend per-run marginal-likelihood budget that a matched-tuning arm freezes to zero (the surrogate effect retains $75\%$). Neither is recoverable from a method description that does not stamp its engine.

Against that grid we name five confounds. Each is a specific line of the scoring path, not a judgment call, and each carries a signed effect (Table~\ref{tab:confounds}; four-corner decomposition, Supp.\ Fig.\ S1).

\paragraph{Confound 1: target scaling.} The ensemble regresses on raw targets spanning $-1560$ to $+34$ while both GP surrogates $z$-score theirs (\texttt{mbo.py:34,157--162,181--187} vs \texttt{mbo.py:293,379--381}). Correcting this alone moves $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ from $0.367$ $[0.254,0.559]$ to $0.283$ $[0.186,0.444]$. This is a measurement confound, not a bug: the prior analysis states a min--max normalization that exists only in the analysis path, never in training. A matched-tuning control (freezing per-run marginal-likelihood budget) reaches nearly the same value from a different axis; whether the two are independent or one entanglement our data cannot settle.

\paragraph{Confound 2: candidate/oracle protocol.} Two of three optimizers propose 256 designs and report the oracle-selected best 128, while the third proposes 128 (\texttt{mbo.py:35--40,249--255,269--271}). The reported percentile is therefore two different estimands in one column. Correcting this alone moves $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ from $0.367$ to $0.450$ $[0.312,0.649]$. This is the strongest of the five: the protocol the prior analysis declares it holds identical is the identifiability license for the whole comparison, and the license is false.

\paragraph{Confound 3: ensemble size $K$.} $K$ is a confound to control, never a mechanism, with two halves read together. It is \mdemph{sensitive}: $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ is $0.326$, $0.366$, $0.408$, $0.389$ at $K = 2,3,5,10$, peaking at exactly the $K{=}5$ the field fixes by convention \citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles} (Supp.\ Fig.\ S1, right). It does \mdemph{not} reverse: at $K{=}2$ the ensemble marginal is $0.283$ against the exact GP's $0.767$ and SVGP's $0.739$, so our pre-registered kill criterion---``if the ensemble marginal at $K{=}2$ exceeds the GP's, the surrogate effect is a $K$ artifact and the headline reverses''---did not fire. The prior supplement's $0.95$ at $K{=}2$ \mdemph{does not reproduce} on the audited engine, which gives $0.283$ there. (Confound 1's $0.283$ is a variance-explained statistic; this one a cell marginal; neither corroborates the other.) Our sweep shares three of its four points with the $K\in\{2,5,10\}$ over which ensemble surrogates were found robust in online BO \citep{li2024bnnsurrogates}, so we claim no priority on the range---but that work asks whether ensemble \mdemph{performance} is robust to $K$ (it is), we whether the variance \mdemph{attributed to the surrogate axis} is (it is not), and only the second bears on a decomposition.

\paragraph{Confound 4: $\beta$--$\sigma$ mismatch.} We registered that a shared $\beta{=}2$ delivers different effective pessimism across surrogate classes, killing the confound ``if the ratio is $\sim$1.'' \mdemph{That kill fired}: the median $\sigma_{\text{GP}}/\sigma_{\text{ens}}$ is $1.19$ at matched $K{=}5$ ($1.21$ pooled over $K$), so the fifth unmatched hyperparameter does not exist grid-wide, and we withdraw the aggregate claim. What survives is per-task: the ratio spans $0.07$ (Branin-2D) to $1.44$ (Styblinski-5D, Rastrigin-15D), a 20-fold spread a single multiplier cannot track. The general principle that a fixed multiplier on an uncalibrated interval is not comparable across model classes is owned by the interval-validity literature \citep{dewolf2022intervals}; a sharper within-class result sits beside it in offline RL, where sharing pessimistic targets across ensemble members can \mdemph{invalidate} nominal pessimism outright, flipping the bound's sign \citep{ghasemipour2022pessimistic}. That is within-class, not cross-model-class; we claim only the offline-MBO acquisition-comparison application, in its narrowed per-task form.

\paragraph{Confound 5: search-intensity budget.} Surrogate-query budgets are unmatched by $11.8\times$ across the optimizer axis, \mdemph{measured} not derived: gradient $51{,}456$, perturbation $4{,}352$, CMA-ES median $6{,}528$. Three corrections: the previously estimated ``$6\times$--$59\times$'' spread was never measured, and CMA's nominal $3000$ budget is a cap that rarely binds; the proposal-count half is already removed by Confound 2, so the surviving inequality is in queries alone; and the audited trust region is inactive in the main grid, so equalizing queries isolates budget. Matching every optimizer at $Q{=}51{,}456$ (none of 630 cells over $5\%$ off) moves the optimizer main effect from our unmatched $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ of $0.005$ to $0.038$ $[0.003,0.123]$, an eightfold understatement we disclose. The same matching on Design-Bench \mdemph{raises} the optimizer effect (Section~\ref{sec:db}), so this confound is measured out of both suites.

\section{De-Confounding Protocol and Results}
\label{sec:deconf}

\paragraph{The protocol.} Standardize the ensemble's regression targets. Equalize the candidate proposal count across optimizers and impose one selection rule, so the oracle never chooses the reported set. Hold $K$, $\beta$ and the query budget fixed and \mdemph{report them}. Stamp the engine (library versions, flags, commit) with every number. Switching the first two on and off gives four corners (Table~\ref{tab:corners}).

\paragraph{The correction strengthens the surrogate effect.} The named audits in this genre each report a claimed advantage shrinking: an LSTM baseline overtaking newer architectures once hyperparameters are controlled \citep{melis2018sota}, the GAN comparison \citep{lucic2018gansequal}, the metric-learning \citep{musgrave2020realitycheck} and neural-recommender \citep{ferraridacrema2019worrying} audits. This one strengthens the effect. Correcting the two protocol confounds moves the surrogate advantage \mdemph{up}, from our decomposition of PGS's Table 1 ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}\approx0.37$) \citep{chemingui2024pggs} to $0.405$ $[0.290,0.556]$. The two corrections are not additive: each alone moves the headline about $0.08$ (down for target scaling, up for candidate/oracle), yet together they land $0.038$ above the uncorrected corner (Table~\ref{tab:corners}), so $0.405$ is a measured joint level, not a sum; the ensemble-size confound moves it the other way, so the strengthening is the net of the two protocol confounds, not a property of de-confounding in general. We found no ML de-confounding audit reporting a corrected variance-explained statistic above its own published value. Two partials: an ImageNet replication reports a relative slope above one ($1.69$/$1.11$) but not a variance-explained scalar, its headline an $8$--$11$ point drop \citep{recht2019imagenet}; and deep-RL reanalysis moves effects both ways \citep{agarwal2021precipice}. The direction is not surprising---a removed confound can suppress as easily as manufacture \citep{bressan2019confounds}, and at scale corrections run up 19 against down 14 \citep{maassen2020reproducibility}---so the claim is unprecedented as an instance, not anomalous.

\paragraph{Corner resolution at $n{=}7$ tasks.} We pre-registered that it would not resolve, and it did not. The four corner intervals have widths $0.26$--$0.34$ against a corner range of $0.167$, sharing a common region $[0.312,0.443]$ every one contains (Supp.\ Fig.\ S1). So $0.405$ never appears without its interval, and no corner is asserted to differ from another. The bootstrap is validated against the reproduction target's precision: our uncorrected corner's width $0.305$ matches the $0.32$ recorded for it \citep{chemingui2024pggs}.

\paragraph{The $\beta$-dependence is the firm result.} $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ rises monotonically with pessimism ($0.184$ $[0.085,0.354]$, $0.214$, $0.282$, $0.406$ $[0.285,0.564]$, $0.408$ at $\beta = 0,0.5,1,2,5$), and the $\beta{=}0$ and $\beta{=}2$ intervals barely overlap where the corner intervals do not separate at all (Figure~\ref{fig:beta}). This is the axis the seven tasks come closest to resolving, and we lead with it. The query budget goes further: $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ more than doubles from $0.243$ at $Q{=}4{,}352$ to $0.526$ at $Q{=}51{,}456$, those intervals \mdemph{disjoint} ($[0.189,0.355]$ vs $[0.421,0.719]$)---the only outright separation in this decomposition---with a ranking flip, gradient leading low and perturbation high. That axis is not ours to claim: budget-dependent benchmarking is a decade-old category \citep{hansen2021coco}, and that bad models can outperform good ones given enough budget dates to 2018 \citep{lucic2018gansequal}. The \mdemph{magnitude} of any headline $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ is therefore a joint artifact of five operating-point coordinates---$K$, $\beta$, query budget, engine corner, and the per-task normalizer---so a headline stating fewer is not reproducible. We add the normalizer because every $\eta^2$ here is on per-task min--max normalized cell means whose range is set by two of the nine arms, an endogenous form known to be outlier-exploitable \citep{jordan2024position}, ranking-flipping at its origin \citep{bellemare2013ale}, and pool-dependent as rank statistics are \citep{balduzzi2018reevaluating}. This strengthens the decomposition: \mdemph{within this synthetic grid} the \mdemph{direction} is invariant to all four---across every $\beta$, $K$ and corner, the GP marginals sit at $0.66$--$0.85$ and the ensemble's at $0.17$--$0.35$, never crossing, smallest gap $0.32$. Only the size moves, and that invariance does not travel: on Design-Bench the same direction is not detectable at this power (Section~\ref{sec:db}).

\paragraph{The interaction is the term the design exists to estimate.} Table~\ref{tab:corners}'s fourth column is what a one-factor-at-a-time study cannot produce. $\eta^2_{\text{inter}}$ is $0.165$, $0.146$, $0.152$, $0.160$ across corners, intervals $[0.107,0.260]$, $[0.088,0.249]$, $[0.094,0.230]$, $[0.087,0.236]$: excluding zero in all four, at four to thirty-three times the optimizer main effect, surviving bias correction ($0.134$--$0.156$). It is the most stable quantity in the paper---its range across corners is $0.018$ against the headline's $0.167$.

Two things follow. It is what the crossed design buys, since one-factor-at-a-time experimentation cannot estimate interactions \citep{nist2012handbook,box1978statistics}---why the closest two-axis precedent declines the analysis \citep{moosbauer2022benchmarkdriven}---and it is why a small $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ licenses ``optimizer choice explains little \mdemph{marginal} variance'' but never ``arbitrary'': optimizer choice has a small marginal and a substantial conditional effect, textbook for a factor that is a minor main effect yet dominates the interactions \citep{nist2012handbook}. We concede the precedent: interaction reporting is routine in functional ANOVA \citep{hutter2014fanova}; what we find no instance of is one between \mdemph{surrogate model class} and \mdemph{search routine} as independently manipulated pipeline components.

The bound: this is an existence-and-ordering claim under a disclosed normalizer, not the stable quantity ``$0.15$.'' Across four corners and three normalizers (supplement), $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}>\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ and $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ largest in \mdemph{all twelve}; the interaction exceeds the optimizer main effect in \mdemph{eleven}, inverting only in the on/off corner under rank normalization, which carries the largest optimizer effect. Rank roughly halves the interaction throughout. Second, suite-specific: on Design-Bench $\eta^2_{\text{inter}}$ is only $0.007$--$0.036$ and subordinate to $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$, which we cannot explain. Frozen cells are a candidate, but Design-Bench's own $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ of $0.05$--$0.17$ shows the axes are not all flattened, so we state the scoping as an unexplained limit, not a resolved one.

\section{Isolating the Source of the Gap}
\label{sec:mech}

Seven controls could each explain the GP--ensemble gap; none does (Table~\ref{tab:elim}). Two were built to confirm accounts of our own and refuted them. Two scopes travel with the count: capacity is eliminated at fixed depth, $\sigma$ at this ensemble construction. Eliminations~1 ($\sigma$/pessimism) and~7 (the oracle-value localization) carry the weight and are developed below; the other five are Table~\ref{tab:elim}.

\paragraph{On our tasks, $\sigma$ tracks distance more than error.} The ensemble's $\sigma$ correlates with pointwise absolute error at only $\rho\approx0.07$ but with $k$-NN distance to the training data at $\rho\approx0.26$, positive on all seven tasks; the prior analysis called $\sigma$ uninformative by measuring it against the wrong target. Read against the distance-aware literature it is a \mdemph{contrast}: deep ensembles are exactly the class characterized as \mdemph{not} distance aware \citep{liu2020sngp,vanamersfoort2020duq}, whereas ours shows a modest positive distance correlation (those results are classification-only). One alternative for $\rho\approx0.07$ we do not exclude: our $\sigma$ is the standard deviation across $K$ squared-error members, reported to underestimate uncertainty \citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, so Elimination~1 removes $\sigma$ \mdemph{as constructed here}; a likelihood-trained variant is an open arm.

\paragraph{Elimination 1: it is not $\sigma$.} With pessimism removed ($\beta{=}0$, pure posterior-mean maximization), $61\%$ of the GP's advantage survives and the interval excludes zero: the marginal gap is $0.319$ $[0.196,0.460]$ at $\beta{=}0$ against $0.525$ $[0.406,0.614]$ at $\beta{=}2$, both on one $\beta$-invariant normalizer (a per-task min--max fit once over pooled cell means of both, refit inside each resample). The pessimism increment is itself significant ($0.203$ $[0.007,0.396]$, $p{=}0.020$): base-plus-amplification, pessimism \mdemph{amplifying} a mean-quality base rather than creating it, refuting any claim that the advantage is independent of pessimism. It passes narrowly---the surviving base of $0.319$ clears its pre-registered collapse threshold of $0.263$ by a margin inside the interval. The cross-check is our strongest single evidence: on a per-task $z$-score the same conditions give $0.904$ against $1.473$ standard deviations, a ratio of $0.614$ against $0.608$ on min--max---within $0.006$ on a different scale, so this is a measurement, not a normalization artifact.

\paragraph{Elimination 7: it is not distance, nor surrogate inflation.} The next hypothesis: the ensemble's optima sit further off-support, where its mean invents maxima, and over-predict more. We instrumented every returned optimum with its $10$-NN distance and over-prediction ($168$ cells, $30$ seeds). Both are null: the ensemble's optima are $+0.116$ $[-0.004,0.333]$ further out and $+0.043$ $[-0.652,1.085]$ more inflated, both covering zero (the distance is one task: Branin-2D $+0.743$, the other six $-0.022$ to $+0.060$). The loss is not null: at those optima the ensemble is worse in \mdemph{true oracle value} by $-1.406$ $[-2.803,-0.375]$ in units of $\mathrm{sd}(y_\D)$, excluding zero, worse in four of five distance bins. The landscape law is real (across $5{,}040$ optima, distance predicts over-prediction $\rho{=}{+}0.758$ and true loss $\rho{=}{-}0.818$) but does not discriminate (Figure~\ref{fig:landscape}): the classes sit at the same median distance ($0.87$ vs $0.86$) and differ in outcome anyway. Off-support maxima are inflated and worthless---a property of the problem, not the ensemble.

That law is not ours. \citet{fannjiang2020autofocused} articulated it: oracle-based design queries the oracle where its training data does not reach, so outputs, \mdemph{including uncertainty}, become unreliable off-support. Our measurement confirms it and shows it \mdemph{insufficient} for our question: off-support degradation is shared by every surrogate class, so it cannot explain a difference \mdemph{between} classes---at matched median distance the ensemble is still worse by $-1.406$ $[-2.803,-0.375]$. The story is right about where surrogates fail, silent about which maximizers each class admits once there. That degradation has a quantitative form elsewhere, along curves whose coefficients depend on the proxy's class \citep{gao2023reward}; Elimination~7 moves it onto architecture class rather than model scale.

\paragraph{The gap survives seven controls.} None survives (Table~\ref{tab:elim}); more search budget even corroborates the mean-geometry reading, the ensemble getting relatively worse as both GPs convert budget into score. The seventh adds a positive localization: the loss is real and sits at the returned optimum's oracle quality, while every landscape property is matched between the classes. What differs is \mdemph{which} off-distribution maximizers a surrogate's mean admits---not how rough the means are or how far the optima lie. That is a diagnosis, not a mechanism with a causal test. A skeptic could read this and the interaction as one phenomenon counted twice, but these geometry measurements do not read off the optimizer axis---the $\sigma$-distance correlation is a property of the surrogate alone, and the matched-distance gap is pooled across optimizers---whereas the interaction is a surrogate$\times$optimizer decomposition. The mechanism is one phenomenon triangulated by two independent instruments. (A second positive arm, prior-mean reversion, also returned an elimination; supplement.)

\paragraph{Is the advantage conditional on the optimizer?} In raw oracle units---no normalization, no bootstrap---the exact-GP--ensemble gap is smaller under perturbation than under \mdemph{both} gradient and CMA on all seven tasks, closing entirely on Ackley-20D (supplement). We report this as a question, not a result: a floor effect (perturbation underperforming, compressing all three cells) would produce the same shape, and our one discriminating case points to it---on Styblinski-5D, where perturbation is the grid's \mdemph{best} optimizer (GP with perturbation at $35.9$), the attenuation is \mdemph{weakest}, $40\%$ of the aggressive gap against at most $8.8\%$ elsewhere, what a floor effect predicts. The descriptive fact is solid; the mechanism is not.

\paragraph{The acquisition ranks its hallucinations above designs it holds.} The candidate/oracle correction puts the starting designs \mdemph{inside} the returned pool: every cell is seeded with the top-128 offline designs plus perturbed copies, and gradient and perturbation pool every iterate with that seed set before returning the top 128 by surrogate LCB. A cell returning something worse than it was handed has ranked an invented design above a real one it was holding. We call that an inversion (Figure~\ref{fig:x0}). It is close to ensemble-specific: the ensemble inverts on at least one optimizer on all seven tasks, against three for the SVGP and two for the exact GP. The sharpest cell is Branin-2D under ensemble gradient ascent (seven cells in all at unit inversion rate and unit mean fraction worse), where all 30 seeds invert and \mdemph{every one} of the 128 returned designs scores below the best design the cell started with. This is the elimination-7 localization made concrete, descriptive at $n{=}7$, not a mechanism with a causal test. Offline RL names it \mdemph{safe policy improvement}---a policy should not be worse than its baseline \citep{thomas2015hcpi,laroche2019spibb,kumar2020cql}; offline MBO optimizers ship no such guarantee, and the inversion is that absence measured.

\paragraph{Three corrections to the prior analysis.} Our released tuning sweep concluded the ensemble's gradient collapse is an artifact a trust region repairs; we report it in full, including where it fires against us. By its $5\%$ rule the tasks whose collapse survives tuning number $2/7$ (neither correction), $0/7$ (target scaling), $5/7$ (protocol), $4/7$ (audited)---genuine surrogate geometry on most tasks, driven by the candidate/oracle correction, on one corner not genuine at all. The collapse is also not gradient-specific: gradient and CMA proposals differ in out-of-distribution coverage by $0.010$ under the matched protocol against $0.117$ under the unmatched, so the claim narrows to any aggressive selector. And the premise-coverage $0.97$ in the prior analysis belongs to a scikit-learn exact GP, not the differentiable GP the grid scores, whose coverage averages $0.831$ and falls to $0.38$ on Styblinski-5D; both are reported with their model, and coverage of $\{\mu-f\le\beta\sigma\}$ is exactly the lower bound's validity.

\section{Design-Bench Results}
\label{sec:db}

\paragraph{The surrogate null.} Across all four engine corners the Design-Bench surrogate main effect is essentially zero ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ of $0.001$--$0.032$ on five tasks, every estimate at the floor of its interval) and the Friedman omnibus fails to reject ($p = 0.19$ to $0.76$). We report no detectable difference at this power, never equivalence: at $n{=}7$ a paired test needs $|d_z|\geq1.27$ for $80\%$ power, larger than anything we observe \citep{agarwal2021precipice}. The null is not fragile to the task set. Dropping GFP leaves it ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ $0.001$--$0.021$); adding the two MuJoCo tasks makes the omnibus reject in three of four corners but does \mdemph{not} lift $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ off the floor---it rises only to $0.044$--$0.091$, still four to nine times below the synthetic $0.405$, while the optimizer effect runs $1.6$ to $4.5\times$ larger and the one non-rejecting corner carries the \mdemph{larger} surrogate effect ($0.053$ vs a rejecting corner's $0.044$). Surrogate size does not track rejection; the optimizer axis does, which is what the added tasks recruit. The null also survives the exact-oracle subset ($p = 0.34$ to $0.51$) and a machine-precision noise floor, so it is not manufactured by approximate oracles or noise, though with two exact-oracle tasks that subset test is low-powered (supplement). A prior pooling defect (eleven cells, two undefined) is unified to the nine defined cells before any rank claim \citep{benavoli2016meanranks}.

\paragraph{Matching the query budget strengthens the optimizer axis.} Everything above is at native budgets, where gradient outspends perturbation by $6.25\times$ (X3-off) to $11.8\times$ (X3-on), so the optimizer axis could be search intensity in a costume. Equalizing surrogate queries across the seven-task grid (at $Q{=}25{,}600$ and $51{,}456$; none of 252 cells over $5\%$ off) runs \mdemph{backwards} to that objection: matching raises the optimizer effect $1.46$ to $1.88\times$ in every corner ($\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ $0.096\!\to\!0.180$, $0.145\!\to\!0.255$, $0.193\!\to\!0.282$, $0.181\!\to\!0.281$) while $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ \mdemph{falls} in all four, flipping off\_off into rejection, so \mdemph{all four} corners reject ($p = 0.0179$ to $0.00057$) where three did. The native imbalance was \mdemph{masking} the effect, not manufacturing it. Two limits: this is \mdemph{established at high budget}---at the matched low level $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ reaches $0.126$ above our $0.10$ floor and one corner ties ($0.085$ vs $0.081$), so the pre-registered primary level decides---and the within-corner intervals overlap, so what is licensed is point-estimate localization across corners. The count is the seven-task set; the five-task and GFP-dropped sets reject three of four under matching. A native-budget control reproduces the published corners to within $0.004$ on every $\eta^2$, so the contrast is budget, not call order.

\paragraph{Frozen cells are a benchmark-construction finding.} Part of the null is structural, and reusable: several Design-Bench cells are degenerate for surrogate attribution, freezing at the dataset reference so they carry neither a main effect nor an interaction. On TF-Bind-8 the three exact-GP cells return exactly $1.0$ on all 16 seeds with zero variance, ensemble-with-perturbation joining them in two corners---up to four frozen cells of nine---where $1.0$ is not a score but the min--max-normalized dataset maximum, already attained by tied rows. On Ant, two of three GP cells return the bit-identical constant $1.5287419557571411$ with standard deviation exactly $0.0$ across all sixteen seeds (a third, SVGP-with-perturbation, on ten): converging on one value across two surrogate classes is retrieval, not coincidence. An attribution study here must screen for frozen cells first, or read a surrogate axis that cannot move as evidence about surrogates. The mechanism is \mdemph{LCB paralysis}, not decode snap-back: snap-back predicts a freeze needs a decode step, but Ant is continuous with no argmax decode, so our pre-registered kill fires and the GP's lower confidence bound is simply locally maximal at the data. The nearest prior formalization is an inconsistency result for expected-improvement BO \citep{yarotsky2013inconsistency}, differing on three axes; the trust-region literature uses the same locality \mdemph{deliberately} \citep{eriksson2019turbo}, here an unintended terminal state. This does not rescue our positive signal, which stays synthetic-only.

\paragraph{The inversion and the synthetic null are one finding.} The section's most awkward number is that the optimizer axis \mdemph{inverts} here---perturbation leads all four corners ($0.76$--$0.81$ against gradient's $0.40$--$0.68$), $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ exceeding $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ in point estimate everywhere (intervals overlap, so this is point-estimate localization, never a within-corner separation)---while the synthetic optimizer effect is small ($\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.038$ $[0.003,0.123]$, a magnitude for that grid, not a general verdict). Read through the frozen cells the suites stop disagreeing: if the GP cannot be moved, every optimizer scores it identically, the surviving between-optimizer variance comes from the unfrozen ensemble cells, and the axis inverts as an arithmetic consequence of the freeze---the payoff of a decomposition over a leaderboard. Four limits: it is two of three Ant GP cells (the gradient cell returns $1.3242 \pm 0.1975$, unfrozen); fact and label are separable; frozen and near-tied cells are never pooled; and the freeze explains why the axis inverts, not how large the optimizer effect is, which the matched-budget arm establishes.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Pre-registered predictions and their outcomes.} Four registered predictions came back against us and two mechanism arms failed to deliver; we report all six, since undisclosed they would read as selective. Our registered headline was that the acquisition optimizer explains most of the gap: it does not ($\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.038$ even after correcting the budget imbalance), and the pre-committed contingency---pivot to the mechanism---is what this paper does. The ensemble-size reversal criterion did not fire, but its accompanying prediction confirmed: the headline is reported at the $K$ that maximizes it (Confound~3). The $\beta$--$\sigma$ criterion fired, narrowing Confound~4 to its per-task form, and the gradient-specific coverage framing was retracted on our own test. The two mechanism arms are the most expensive: smoothing the mean was built to confirm our published account and refuted it, and the phantom-maxima probe was built for a mechanism and returned a seventh elimination.

\paragraph{Interpreting the optimizer effect.} Under a matched budget of $Q{=}51{,}456$, $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}=0.038$ $[0.003,0.123]$, so optimizer choice explains little variance in this grid. It replaces our own unmatched $0.005$, an eightfold understatement we disclose. The confirmation is not comfortable: the upper bound $0.123$ lies inside our pre-registered inconclusive band, so an effect up to $\sim$0.12 is not excluded at $n{=}7$, and the secondary level does not corroborate cleanly ($0.066$ $[0.014,0.340]$ at $Q{=}4{,}352$)---so the null is established at high budget, underpowered at low. The \mdemph{best} optimizer even changes with budget: gradient leads low ($0.731$), perturbation high ($0.732$). But the small \mdemph{marginal} is not a verdict that optimizer choice is arbitrary---its \mdemph{conditional} effect through the interaction is four to thirty-three times larger, so which optimizer to use depends on the surrogate.

\paragraph{Scope.} We make no field reversal: that the statistical model often outranks the acquisition heuristic is a decade-old doctrine \citep{shahriari2016humanoutoftheloop}; we falsify only the \mdemph{local} premise of one recent paper---that offline black-box optimization ``has focused on improving surrogate models while using fixed search strategies'' \citep{chemingui2024pggs}---and claim ensemble size as no discovered mechanism \citep{li2024bnnsurrogates,abe2022ensembles}, no equivalence on Design-Bench \citep{agarwal2021precipice,lakens2017equivalence}, no priority on the $K$ range, and distance-aware uncertainty as not ours \citep{liu2020sngp,vanamersfoort2020duq}. The Design-Bench null is a failure to detect, not equivalence: a two-one-sided-tests bound rejects equivalence at both a $0.5$ and a $0.3$ margin (supplement) \citep{lakens2017equivalence}. The two protocol arms make the same shape of finding on two axes---correcting them raised the synthetic surrogate effect and matching budgets raised the Design-Bench optimizer effect, both against the reality-check genre's shrink direction \citep{melis2018sota}---but it is two instances, not a law, since the ensemble-size confound moves the surrogate effect the other way.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Seven synthetic tasks is a small $n$, binding the corner decomposition, the optimizer interval, and the omnibus alike; the datasets are drawn once at seed 0, so reported variance is training and optimization, not data-draw. GFP is quarantined from coverage claims for its degenerate decode; excluding it flips one coverage claim (the exact GP's in-distribution coverage from below to above nominal) while the effect-size null is unchanged. The decomposition's direction survives $z$-score, rank, and leave-one-task-out (the headline ranges $0.373$--$0.485$; Griewank-30D, widest in raw range, is the \mdemph{smallest} lever), and the strengthening survives $\epsilon^2$ bias correction ($0.351\to0.395$; supplement). No landscape covariate we tested---dimension, multimodality, separability---predicts the gap at this power (supplement). Two of the seven controls are narrower than the count suggests: capacity is eliminated at fixed depth 2 (the depth form open), and $\sigma$ at one ensemble construction (a likelihood-trained variant untested). The surviving mechanism is a diagnosis with seven eliminations behind it and no positive causal test in front---both positive accounts we pre-registered returned eliminations---so what remains open is finite: those two arms, plus whether the surrogate-geometry account and the optimizer interaction are one mechanism or two. The positive signal stays synthetic-only.

\section{Conclusion}

The reported advantage of GP-LCB in offline MBO is measured through five confounds specific to this comparison. Removing the two protocol confounds moves the surrogate main effect from our decomposition of PGS's Table 1 ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}\approx0.37$) \citep{chemingui2024pggs} to $0.405$ $[0.290,0.556]$: against the direction every named audit in this genre ran, and the net of two corrections against a third. That level is not resolvable at seven tasks. Two firmer results remain: any $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ here is a joint artifact of five operating-point coordinates papers do not state, and the surrogate$\times$optimizer interaction---which a one-factor-at-a-time study cannot estimate---exceeds the optimizer main effect in every corner, its interval excluding zero in all four. What the advantage \mdemph{is} stays open, narrowed by seven controls but not closed. What is measured is the loss---the ensemble's optima worse in true oracle value at matched distance and inflation---and its shape, an acquisition ranking invented designs above real ones it was holding. On the optimizer axis the claim stays narrow: under matched budgets $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.038$ $[0.003,0.123]$ \mdemph{in this grid}, falsifying the local premise of one recent paper \citep{chemingui2024pggs} and nothing the standard review settled a decade ago \citep{shahriari2016humanoutoftheloop}. What we offer is the taxonomy, the protocol, and the engine stamp that makes either reproducible.
