\title{Decomposing the GP Advantage in Offline Model-Based Optimization}

\author{Anonymous Submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Offline model-based optimization ships a surrogate and an optimizer as a single pipeline and reports a single number, so when GP-LCB beats a neural ensemble, nothing in the record says which component earned the win---a gap the subfield's 2026 survey concedes. We run the design the question requires: three surrogate classes crossed with three search routines under one shared protocol, decomposed by two-way ANOVA. Building the grid surfaced five confounds specific to offline-MBO surrogate comparison (target scaling, an unequal candidate/oracle rule, and an $11.8\times$ query-budget imbalance), each traced to a scoring-path line, signed, and pre-registered with a kill criterion. Removing the two protocol confounds moves the surrogate effect up, not down ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ from $0.367$ uncorrected to $0.405$ $[0.290, 0.556]$), against every audit we could find; seven tasks cannot resolve the level, so we claim the direction, not a point value. Three findings anchor the decomposition. First, on the synthetic grid, the surrogate$\times$optimizer interaction---which a one-factor-at-a-time study cannot estimate---excludes zero in all four engine corners and exceeds the optimizer main effect in eleven of twelve corner$\times$normalizer combinations: which optimizer to use depends on the surrogate. Second, the surrogate that loses is the more accurate one---the ensemble's held-out RMSE beats the GP's at every width, while its acquisition function ranks designs it invented above real designs it was handed (on Branin, in all thirty seeds). Third, on Design-Bench the surrogate effect is undetectable at this statistical power---never reaching equivalence---partly because several cells freeze at the dataset reference, a benchmark-construction defect any attribution study must screen for first. Any $\eta^2$ headline is a joint artifact of five operating-point coordinates; we supply the taxonomy, the protocol, and the engine stamp that make one reproducible.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Offline model-based optimization (MBO) seeks a design $x$ maximizing an expensive black-box $f$ from a fixed dataset $\D$, with no further queries to $f$ during search \citep{trabucco2022designbench,kim2025mbosurvey}. The dominant recipe fits a surrogate $\hat f$ and optimizes it; because optimization pushes designs where $\hat f$ is unconstrained, the surrogate is made conservative, by a learned regularizer \citep{trabucco2021coms} or a pessimistic acquisition such as the lower confidence bound (LCB) $\mu(x)-\beta\sigma(x)$ \citep{srinivas2010ucb}. A common intuition holds that Gaussian-process LCB (GP-LCB) beats neural-ensemble LCB at low dimension because the GP posterior is better calibrated. But the two pipelines differ in \mdemph{two} coupled factors: surrogate class (GP posterior vs.\ ensemble disagreement) and acquisition optimizer, since GPs are usually optimized by perturbation or quasi-Newton restarts and neural surrogates by gradient ascent on $x$. Every method in this lineage ships both and reports one number, leaving open which the number credits.

\paragraph{The attribution question is open, by the field's own account.} The subfield's 2026 survey states that existing benchmarks ``often emphasize overall optimization performance without clarifying whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance'' \citep{kim2025mbosurvey}. That takes a design nobody has run: both axes varied against each other under one shared protocol, the variance decomposed between them. Existing systematic studies fix one axis. The most thorough is \mdemph{broader} than ours on the surrogate axis precisely because it fixes the other: a seven-class Bayesian neural-network comparison holding Monte-Carlo expected improvement fixed, run \mdemph{online}, decomposing nothing \citep{li2024bnnsurrogates}. A neighbouring line varies the \mdemph{training objective} at fixed architecture, a ranking loss \citep{tan2025ltr} or a conservative regularizer \citep{trabucco2021coms}: a different factor from model class, since a GP posterior cannot be instantiated without its marginal-likelihood fit. Offline RL's closest re-evaluation likewise varies uncertainty-penalty heuristics at a fixed policy optimizer \citep{lu2022revisiting}. A design that fixes a factor cannot estimate it, so a seven-class comparison at one fixed acquisition rule is not a smaller version of this experiment but a transposed one. We run the crossed surrogate$\times$optimizer factorial (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}) and report the two-way decomposition; no prior offline-MBO work reports one, and the nearest near-miss---Table 3 of \citet{tan2025ltr}, a genuine crossed grid that varies training \mdemph{loss} against method rather than model class---is close enough to name and not commensurable with ours (supplement). Our reference point is PGS, which reports a per-task table and claims surrogate class dominates but publishes no decomposition \citep{chemingui2024pggs}; we rebuild the grid under its released protocol, compute on that reproduction the decomposition PGS does not report ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}{=}0.367$ on the uncorrected engine), then audit our own number.

\paragraph{The contribution.} An instrument inherits every confound in the grid it measures, and building the crossed grid surfaced five specific to offline-MBO surrogate comparison, each traced to a line of the scoring path. The contribution is that composition.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Five named confounds and a removal protocol}, each traced, signed, and pre-registered (Sections~\ref{sec:confounds}--\ref{sec:deconf}).
\item \textbf{A surrogate$\times$optimizer interaction}, the term the design exists to estimate (Section~\ref{sec:deconf}).
\item \textbf{An operating-point thesis}: five coordinates, or the headline is not reproducible (Section~\ref{sec:deconf}).
\item \textbf{A mechanism narrowed by elimination}: seven controls, none survives (Section~\ref{sec:mech}).
\item \textbf{A Design-Bench null with its power stated}, never equivalence (Section~\ref{sec:db}) \citep{agarwal2021precipice}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} No field reversal: that the statistical model often matters more than the acquisition heuristic is the standard BO review's decade-old organizing doctrine \citep{shahriari2016humanoutoftheloop}, and our optimizer-axis result falsifies the \mdemph{local} premise of one recent paper \citep{chemingui2024pggs} and nothing wider. The remaining scope limits---no mechanism discovery, no equivalence, no result about ensemble size---are stated in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\section{Background and Related Work}

\paragraph{LCB, pessimism, and coverage.} GP-LCB $\mu-\beta\sigma$ has regret guarantees under GP assumptions \citep{srinivas2010ucb}; deep ensembles \citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles} give a cheap uncertainty proxy with none. The pessimism principle behind conservative offline methods is that a valid lower bound prevents the optimizer exploiting model error \citep{jin2021pessimism}; we make that premise measurable. Conformal methods give distribution-free coverage, including inside the BO loop \citep{angelopoulos2023conformal,stanton2023conformalbo}; we use coverage of the LCB premise only as a \mdemph{measurement}, where the optimizer operates, and Section~\ref{sec:mech} shows it separable from outcome.

\paragraph{Offline MBO and its evaluation.} The conservative-surrogate lineage runs from Conservative Objective Models \citep{trabucco2021coms} and its CQL-style regularizer \citep{kumar2020cql} through generative surrogates \citep{brookes2019cbas,krishnamoorthy2023ddom}, all holding the search rule fixed while varying the model. Optimizer-as-contribution work \citep{chemingui2024pggs} proposes a method rather than decomposing; its motivating premise is the one \mdemph{local} claim our design falsifies. Design-Bench \citep{trabucco2022designbench} standardizes tasks and documents the confounded status quo. Naming unreported choices, giving the protocol that removes them and showing the ranking move is the established reality-check genre, whose shape we claim no credit for \citep{henderson2018matters,agarwal2021precipice,balduzzi2018reevaluating,ferraridacrema2019worrying,musgrave2020realitycheck,lucic2018gansequal}; in offline optimization SOO-Bench \citep{qian2025soobench} stress-tests optimizer \mdemph{stability}, not attribution. The one-way analogue is fANOVA at a fixed search run \citep{hutter2014fanova}, and of the two-axis precedents none decomposes, the closest naming fANOVA and \mdemph{declining} it \citep{liang2021benchmarking,moosbauer2022benchmarkdriven}.

\section{Experimental Grid and Confounds}
\label{sec:confounds}

\paragraph{The grid.} The crossed grid (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}) runs 7 synthetic tasks (Branin-2D through Griewank-30D, drawn once at seed 0) at 30 seeds and 7 Design-Bench tasks at 16 seeds. \textbf{Surrogates:} a $K{=}5$ ensemble of two-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs (width 96), with $\mu,\sigma$ the member mean and standard deviation; an \mdemph{exact GP} (BoTorch \texttt{SingleTaskGP}, marginal-likelihood fit on a score-biased subsample); and a \mdemph{sparse variational GP} (SVGP). \textbf{Optimizers:} 100 Adam steps on $x$; 5 rounds of Gaussian hill-climbing; CMA-ES. All three surrogates are differentiable, so all nine cells are realizable (configuration in the supplement). Every cell maximizes the LCB $\mu(x)-\beta\sigma(x)$ at $\beta{=}2$, scored by the oracle on its 128 returned designs at the 100th percentile. Per task we min--max normalize the nine cell means and take a two-way ANOVA; $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ and $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ are the variance fractions of the two main effects, with task-and-seed hierarchical bootstrap intervals ($B{=}10{,}000$).

Two details there are engine state: the exact GP's default covariance is ARD RBF under the BoTorch pinned for the synthetic grid but ARD Mat\'ern-$5/2$ under the older Design-Bench version, and the GPs alone spend per-run marginal-likelihood budget a matched-tuning arm freezes to zero (the surrogate effect retains $75\%$). Neither is recoverable from a method description that does not stamp its engine. Against that grid we name five confounds, each a specific line of the scoring path rather than a judgment call and each carrying a signed effect (summarized in Supp.\ Table~S4; four-corner decomposition, Supp.\ Fig.\ S1).

\paragraph{Confounds 1--2: target scaling and the candidate/oracle protocol.} The ensemble regresses on raw targets spanning $-1560$ to $+34$ while both GPs $z$-score theirs (\texttt{mbo.py:34,157--162,181--187} vs \texttt{mbo.py:293,379--381}); correcting this alone moves $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ from $0.367$ $[0.254,0.559]$ to $0.283$ $[0.186,0.444]$. It is a measurement confound, not a bug: the prior analysis states a min--max normalization that exists only in the analysis path, never in training. (The matched-tuning control reaches nearly the same value from a different axis; whether the two are independent our data cannot settle.) Separately, two of three optimizers propose 256 designs and report the oracle-selected best 128 while the third proposes 128 (\texttt{mbo.py:35--40,249--255,269--271}), so the reported percentile is two estimands in one column; correcting that alone moves $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ to $0.450$ $[0.312,0.649]$. It is the strongest of the five: the protocol the prior analysis declares it holds identical is the identifiability license for the whole comparison, and the license is false.

\paragraph{Confound 3: ensemble size $K$.} $K$ is a confound to control, never a mechanism, and its two halves are read together. \mdemph{Sensitive}: $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ is $0.326$, $0.366$, $0.408$, $0.389$ at $K = 2,3,5,10$, peaking at exactly the $K{=}5$ the field fixes by convention \citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles} (Supp.\ Fig.\ S1, right). \mdemph{Non-reversing}: at $K{=}2$ the ensemble marginal is $0.283$ against the exact GP's $0.767$ and SVGP's $0.739$, so our pre-registered kill criterion---``if the ensemble marginal at $K{=}2$ exceeds the GP's, the headline reverses''---did not fire. The prior supplement's $0.95$ at $K{=}2$ does not reproduce on the audited engine. (Confound 1's $0.283$ is a variance-explained statistic, this one a cell marginal; neither corroborates the other.) Three of our four $K$ values lie in the $K\in\{2,5,10\}$ over which ensemble surrogates were found robust in online BO \citep{li2024bnnsurrogates}, so we claim no priority on the range; but that work asks whether ensemble \mdemph{performance} is robust to $K$ (it is), we whether the variance \mdemph{attributed to the surrogate axis} is (it is not), and only the second bears on a decomposition.

\paragraph{Confounds 4--5: $\beta$--$\sigma$ mismatch and search-intensity budget.} We registered that a shared $\beta{=}2$ delivers different effective pessimism across surrogate classes, to be killed ``if the ratio is $\sim$1.'' \mdemph{That kill fired}: the median $\sigma_{\text{GP}}/\sigma_{\text{ens}}$ is $1.19$ at matched $K{=}5$ ($1.21$ pooled over $K$), so the fifth unmatched hyperparameter does not exist grid-wide and we withdraw the aggregate claim. What survives is per-task: the ratio spans $0.07$ (Branin-2D) to $1.44$ (Styblinski-5D, Rastrigin-15D), a 20-fold spread a single multiplier cannot track. That a fixed multiplier on an uncalibrated interval is not comparable across model classes is owned by the interval-validity literature \citep{dewolf2022intervals}, with a sharper within-class result beside it in offline RL, where sharing pessimistic targets across ensemble members can \mdemph{invalidate} nominal pessimism outright and flip the bound's sign \citep{ghasemipour2022pessimistic}; being within-class, it leaves us only the offline-MBO acquisition-comparison application, in its narrowed per-task form. Budgets, meanwhile, are unmatched by $11.8\times$ across the optimizer axis, \mdemph{measured} not derived: gradient $51{,}456$, perturbation $4{,}352$, CMA-ES median $6{,}528$. Three corrections to that measurement travel with it, including that the surviving inequality is in queries alone and that equalizing them therefore isolates budget (supplement). Matching every optimizer at $Q{=}51{,}456$ (none of 630 cells over $5\%$ off) moves the optimizer main effect from our unmatched $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ of $0.005$ to $0.038$ $[0.003,0.123]$, an eightfold understatement we disclose; the same matching \mdemph{raises} the optimizer effect on Design-Bench too (Section~\ref{sec:db}), so this confound is measured out of both suites.

\section{De-Confounding Protocol and Results}
\label{sec:deconf}

\paragraph{The protocol.} Standardize the ensemble's regression targets. Equalize the candidate proposal count across optimizers and impose one selection rule, so the oracle never chooses the reported set. Hold $K$, $\beta$ and the query budget fixed and \mdemph{report them}. Stamp the engine (library versions, flags, commit) with every number. Switching the first two on and off gives four corners (Supp.\ Table~S3).

\paragraph{One primary operating point.} A paper arguing that an $\eta^2$ without its coordinates is not reproducible must name its own. Ours is the audited corner (X1 and X3 on), $K{=}5$, $\beta{=}2$, \mdemph{native} query budgets, per-task min--max normalizer, 7 synthetic tasks at 30 seeds, and its decomposition is reported as a triple: $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}{=}0.405$ $[0.290,0.556]$, $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.005$, $\eta^2_{\text{inter}}{=}0.160$ $[0.087,0.236]$. Every number below sits there unless it names another point, and one carried in from another---a different $\beta$, $K$, corner or budget---is quoted with that coordinate attached, since all three terms move when one coordinate does.

\paragraph{The correction strengthens the surrogate effect.} The named audits in this genre each report a claimed advantage shrinking: an LSTM baseline overtaking newer architectures once hyperparameters are controlled \citep{melis2018sota}, the GAN \citep{lucic2018gansequal}, metric-learning \citep{musgrave2020realitycheck} and neural-recommender \citep{ferraridacrema2019worrying} audits. Correcting the two protocol confounds moves the surrogate advantage \mdemph{up}, from $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}{=}0.367$ on our reproduction grid under PGS's released protocol \citep{chemingui2024pggs} to $0.405$ $[0.290,0.556]$---both ends computed here, on one grid, since PGS publishes no decomposition to quote. The corrections are not additive: each alone moves the headline about $0.08$ (down for target scaling, up for candidate/oracle), yet together they land $0.038$ above the uncorrected corner, so $0.405$ is a measured joint level, not a sum; and the ensemble-size confound moves it the other way, so the strengthening is the net of the two protocol confounds, not a property of de-confounding in general. We found no ML de-confounding audit reporting a corrected variance-explained statistic above its own published value. Two partials: an ImageNet replication reports a relative slope above one ($1.69$/$1.11$) but no variance-explained scalar, its headline an $8$--$11$ point drop \citep{recht2019imagenet}, and deep-RL reanalysis moves effects both ways \citep{agarwal2021precipice}. The direction is not surprising, since a removed confound can suppress as easily as manufacture \citep{bressan2019confounds} and at scale corrections run up 19 against down 14 \citep{maassen2020reproducibility}; the claim is unprecedented as an instance, not anomalous.

\paragraph{Corner resolution at $n{=}7$ tasks.} We pre-registered that it would not resolve, and it did not. The four corner intervals have widths $0.26$--$0.34$ against a corner range of $0.167$, sharing a common region $[0.312,0.443]$ every one contains (Supp.\ Fig.\ S1). So $0.405$ never appears without its interval, and no corner is asserted to differ from another, though the omnibus rejects in all four ($p\le1.7\times10^{-3}$). What seven tasks license is the \mdemph{direction}, not a point value. The bootstrap is validated against the precision the prior analysis reported on this same uncorrected grid: our corner's width $0.305$ matches its $0.32$.

\paragraph{The $\beta$-dependence is the firm result.} $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ rises monotonically with pessimism ($0.184$ $[0.085,0.354]$, $0.214$, $0.282$, $0.406$ $[0.285,0.564]$, $0.408$ at $\beta = 0,0.5,1,2,5$), and the $\beta{=}0$ and $\beta{=}2$ intervals barely overlap---by only $0.069$---where the corner intervals do not separate at all (Supp.\ Fig.\ S6). This is the axis that comes closest to separating what the engine corners could not. The query budget goes further: $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ more than doubles from $0.243$ at $Q{=}4{,}352$ to $0.526$ at $Q{=}51{,}456$, those intervals \mdemph{disjoint} ($[0.189,0.355]$ vs $[0.421,0.719]$), the only outright separation, with a ranking flip. That axis is not ours: budget-dependent benchmarking is a decade-old category \citep{hansen2021coco}, and budget-driven rank flips date to 2018 \citep{lucic2018gansequal}. The \mdemph{magnitude} of any headline $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ is therefore a joint artifact of the five operating-point coordinates---$K$, $\beta$, query budget, engine corner, and the per-task normalizer. We add the normalizer because every $\eta^2$ here is on per-task min--max normalized cell means whose range is set by two of the nine arms, an endogenous form known to be outlier-exploitable \citep{jordan2024position}, ranking-flipping at its origin \citep{bellemare2013ale}, and pool-dependent as rank statistics are \citep{balduzzi2018reevaluating}. \mdemph{Within this synthetic grid} the \mdemph{direction} is nonetheless invariant to all four, the GP marginals sitting at $0.66$--$0.85$ against the ensemble's $0.17$--$0.35$ across every $\beta$, $K$ and corner, never crossing, smallest gap $0.32$. Only the size moves, and that invariance does not travel: on Design-Bench the same direction is not detectable at this power (Section~\ref{sec:db}).

\paragraph{The interaction is the term the design exists to estimate.} $\eta^2_{\text{inter}}$ is $0.165$, $0.146$, $0.152$, $0.160$ across corners, intervals $[0.107,0.260]$, $[0.088,0.249]$, $[0.094,0.230]$, $[0.087,0.236]$: excluding zero in all four, at four to thirty-three times the optimizer main effect ($\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ $0.013$, $0.036$, $0.006$, $0.005$), surviving bias correction ($0.134$--$0.156$). It is the most stable quantity in the paper, its range across corners $0.018$ against the headline's $0.167$.

Two things follow. It is what the crossed design buys, since one-factor-at-a-time experimentation cannot estimate interactions \citep{nist2012handbook,box1978statistics}, which is why the closest two-axis precedent declines the analysis \citep{moosbauer2022benchmarkdriven}. And it is why a small $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ licenses ``optimizer choice explains little \mdemph{marginal} variance'' but never ``arbitrary'', textbook for a minor main effect that dominates the interactions \citep{nist2012handbook}. We concede the precedent: interaction reporting is routine in functional ANOVA \citep{hutter2014fanova}; what we find no instance of is one between \mdemph{surrogate model class} and \mdemph{search routine} as independently manipulated pipeline components.

The bound: this is an existence-and-ordering claim under a disclosed normalizer, not the stable quantity ``$0.15$.'' Across four corners and three normalizers (supplement), $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}>\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ and $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ largest in \mdemph{all twelve}; the interaction exceeds the optimizer main effect in \mdemph{eleven}, inverting only in the on/off corner under rank normalization, which carries the largest optimizer effect. Rank roughly halves the interaction throughout. Second, suite-specific: on Design-Bench $\eta^2_{\text{inter}}$ is only $0.007$--$0.036$ and subordinate to $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$, which we cannot explain. Frozen cells are a candidate, but Design-Bench's own $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ of $0.05$--$0.17$ shows the axes are not all flattened, so the scoping is an unexplained limit, not a resolved one.

\section{Isolating the Source of the Gap}
\label{sec:mech}

Seven controls could each explain the GP--ensemble gap; none does (Table~\ref{tab:elim}). Two were built to confirm accounts of our own and refuted them. Two scopes travel with the count: capacity is eliminated at fixed depth, $\sigma$ at this ensemble construction. Elimination~3 reverses the intuition: the ensemble's held-out normalized RMSE lies \mdemph{below} the exact GP's $0.479$ at every width---$0.445$, $0.405$, $0.393$, $0.388$ at $w{=}96,256,512,1024$, on a $20\%$ split the grid never saw---and it still loses by ${\sim}0.48$. The surrogate that loses is the more accurate one, so accuracy is not what the gap measures (registered reading: the two RMSE-tied cells, gap $0.375$; the $7/7$-task result strengthens it post hoc). Eliminations~1 ($\sigma$/pessimism) and~7 (the oracle-value localization) carry the weight and are developed below.

\paragraph{Elimination 1: it is not $\sigma$.} With pessimism removed ($\beta{=}0$, pure posterior-mean maximization), $61\%$ of the GP's advantage survives and the interval excludes zero: the marginal gap is $0.319$ $[0.196,0.460]$ at $\beta{=}0$ against $0.525$ $[0.406,0.614]$ at $\beta{=}2$, both on one $\beta$-invariant normalizer (per-task min--max fit once over pooled cell means of both, refit inside each resample). The increment is itself significant ($0.203$ $[0.007,0.396]$, $p{=}0.020$): pessimism \mdemph{amplifies} a mean-quality base rather than creating it, refuting any independence from pessimism. It passes narrowly, $0.319$ clearing its pre-registered collapse threshold of $0.263$ by a margin inside the interval. A per-task $z$-score cross-check gives $0.904$ against $1.473$ standard deviations, a ratio of $0.614$ against $0.608$ on min--max, so this is a measurement, not a normalization artifact. On our tasks $\sigma$ also tracks distance more than error, correlating with absolute error at only $\rho\approx0.07$ but with $k$-NN distance at $\rho\approx0.26$, positive on all seven tasks; the prior analysis called $\sigma$ uninformative by measuring it against the wrong target. That \mdemph{contrasts} with the distance-aware literature, where deep ensembles are exactly the class characterized as \mdemph{not} distance aware \citep{liu2020sngp,vanamersfoort2020duq} (classification-only results). One alternative we do not exclude: our $\sigma$ is the standard deviation across $K$ squared-error members, reported to underestimate uncertainty \citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, so this removes $\sigma$ \mdemph{as constructed here} and a likelihood-trained variant stays open.

\paragraph{Elimination 7: it is not distance, nor surrogate inflation.} Next hypothesis: the ensemble's optima sit further off-support, where its mean invents maxima, and over-predict more. Instrumenting every returned optimum with its $10$-NN distance and over-prediction ($168$ cells, $30$ seeds), neither term excludes zero: $+0.116$ $[-0.004,0.333]$ further out, $+0.043$ $[-0.652,1.085]$ more inflated. By the standard we hold Design-Bench to, that is a failure to detect and not an absence---the distance term missing only marginally, its bound $0.004$ below zero, on an interval one task carries (Branin-2D $+0.743$, the other six $-0.022$ to $+0.060$). The elimination rests instead on the outcome gap at \mdemph{matched} distance, where the ensemble is worse in \mdemph{true oracle value} by $-1.406$ $[-2.803,-0.375]$ in units of $\mathrm{sd}(y_\D)$: that one \mdemph{does} exclude zero, in four of five distance bins. The landscape law is real (across $5{,}040$ optima, distance predicts over-prediction $\rho{=}{+}0.758$ and true loss $\rho{=}{-}0.818$) but does not discriminate (Supp.\ Fig.\ S3): the classes differ in outcome at matched median distance ($0.87$/$0.86$/$0.84$). That law is not ours; \citet{fannjiang2020autofocused} articulated it, oracle-based design querying the oracle where its training data does not reach, so outputs, \mdemph{including uncertainty}, become unreliable off-support. Our measurement confirms it and shows it \mdemph{insufficient} here: off-support degradation is shared by every class, so it cannot explain a difference \mdemph{between} classes. That degradation has a quantitative form elsewhere, along curves whose coefficients depend on the proxy's class \citep{gao2023reward}; Elimination~7 moves it onto architecture class, not model scale.

\paragraph{The gap survives seven controls.} Budget even corroborates the mean-geometry reading, the ensemble getting relatively worse as both GPs convert budget into score. The seventh adds a positive localization: the loss sits at the returned optimum's oracle quality with every landscape property matched between the classes. What differs is \mdemph{which} off-distribution maximizers a surrogate's mean admits, not how rough the means are or how far the optima lie. A skeptic could read this and the interaction as one phenomenon counted twice, but the geometry measurements do not read off the optimizer axis---the $\sigma$-distance correlation is a property of the surrogate alone, the matched-distance gap pooled across optimizers---whereas the interaction is a surrogate$\times$optimizer decomposition: two independent instruments, one phenomenon. The gap is also smaller under perturbation than under gradient or CMA on all seven tasks, but our one discriminating case---Styblinski-5D, where perturbation is the grid's \mdemph{best} optimizer (GP with perturbation at $35.9$) and the attenuation is \mdemph{weakest}---points to a floor effect rather than a conditional advantage. (That arm and a second positive one, prior-mean reversion, are in the supplement; both returned eliminations.)

\paragraph{The acquisition ranks its hallucinations above designs it holds.} The candidate/oracle correction puts the starting designs \mdemph{inside} the returned pool: every cell is seeded with the top-128 offline designs plus perturbed copies, and gradient and perturbation pool every iterate with that seed set before returning the top 128 by surrogate LCB. A cell returning something worse than it was handed has ranked an invented design above a real one it was holding: an inversion (Figure~\ref{fig:x0}). It is close to ensemble-specific, the ensemble inverting on at least one optimizer on all seven tasks against three for the SVGP and two for the exact GP. The sharpest cell is Branin-2D under ensemble gradient ascent (seven cells in all at unit inversion rate and unit mean fraction worse), where all 30 seeds invert and \mdemph{every one} of the 128 returned designs scores below the best design the cell started with. This is the elimination-7 localization made concrete, descriptive at $n{=}7$, not a mechanism with a causal test. Offline RL names the missing guarantee \mdemph{safe policy improvement}, that a policy should not be worse than its baseline \citep{thomas2015hcpi,laroche2019spibb,kumar2020cql}; offline MBO optimizers ship none, and the inversion is that absence measured.

\paragraph{Three corrections to the prior analysis.} We report three in full, including where they fire against us: the released tuning sweep's verdict that the ensemble's gradient collapse is a trust-region-repairable artifact holds on the pre-audit engine but not the audited one, where the collapse is genuine surrogate geometry on most tasks; the collapse is not gradient-specific, so that claim narrows to any aggressive selector; and the premise-coverage $0.97$ belongs to a scikit-learn exact GP, not the differentiable GP the grid scores, whose coverage averages $0.831$ and falls to $0.38$ on Styblinski-5D, both reported with their model (counts, coverages and the $\{\mu-f\le\beta\sigma\}$ identity in the supplement).

\section{Design-Bench Results}
\label{sec:db}

\paragraph{The surrogate null.} In all four corners the Design-Bench surrogate main effect is essentially zero ($\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ $0.001$--$0.032$ on five tasks, every estimate at its interval's floor) and the Friedman omnibus fails to reject ($p = 0.19$ to $0.76$): no detectable difference at this power, never equivalence, since at $n{=}7$ a paired test needs $|d_z|\geq1.27$ for $80\%$ power, larger than anything we observe \citep{agarwal2021precipice}. Nor is it fragile to the task set. Dropping GFP leaves it ($0.001$--$0.021$); adding the two MuJoCo tasks makes three of four corners reject but does \mdemph{not} lift $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ off the floor, only to $0.044$--$0.091$, four to nine times below the synthetic $0.405$, while the optimizer effect runs $1.6$ to $4.5\times$ larger and the one non-rejecting corner carries the \mdemph{larger} surrogate effect ($0.053$ against $0.044$). Surrogate size does not track rejection; the optimizer axis does, which the added tasks recruit. The null survives the exact-oracle subset ($p = 0.34$ to $0.51$) and a machine-precision noise floor, so neither approximate oracles nor noise manufacture it, though at two such tasks that test is low-powered (supplement). A prior pooling defect (eleven cells, two undefined) is unified to nine before any rank claim \citep{benavoli2016meanranks}.

\paragraph{Matching the query budget strengthens the optimizer axis.} All of that is at native budgets, where gradient outspends perturbation by $6.25\times$ (X3-off) to $11.8\times$ (X3-on), so the optimizer axis could be search intensity in a costume. Equalizing queries across the seven-task grid ($Q{=}25{,}600$ and $51{,}456$; none of 252 cells over $5\%$ off) runs \mdemph{backwards} to that objection: matching raises the optimizer effect $1.46$ to $1.88\times$ in every corner ($\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ $0.096\!\to\!0.180$, $0.145\!\to\!0.255$, $0.193\!\to\!0.282$, $0.181\!\to\!0.281$) while $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ \mdemph{falls} in all four, flipping off\_off into rejection so \mdemph{all four} reject ($p = 0.0179$ to $0.00057$) where three did. The imbalance was \mdemph{masking} the effect, not manufacturing it. Two limits: it is \mdemph{established at high budget}, since at the matched low level $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ reaches $0.126$ above our $0.10$ floor and one corner ties ($0.085$ against $0.081$), so the pre-registered primary level decides; and within-corner intervals overlap, licensing point-estimate localization across corners only. The count is the seven-task set, the five-task and GFP-dropped sets rejecting three of four. A native-budget control reproduces the published corners to within $0.004$ on every $\eta^2$, so the contrast is budget, not call order.

\paragraph{Frozen cells are a benchmark-construction finding.} Part of the null is structural, and reusable: several Design-Bench cells are degenerate for surrogate attribution, freezing at the dataset reference so they carry neither a main effect nor an interaction. On TF-Bind-8 the three exact-GP cells return exactly $1.0$ on all 16 seeds with zero variance, ensemble-with-perturbation joining them in two corners (four frozen cells of nine), where $1.0$ is not a score but the min--max-normalized dataset maximum, already attained by tied rows. On Ant, two of three GP cells return the bit-identical constant $1.5287419557571411$ with standard deviation exactly $0.0$ across all sixteen seeds, a third (SVGP-with-perturbation) on ten: one value across two surrogate classes is retrieval, not coincidence. An attribution study here must screen for frozen cells first, or read a surrogate axis that cannot move as evidence about surrogates. The mechanism is \mdemph{LCB paralysis}, not decode snap-back: snap-back needs a decode step, but Ant is continuous with no argmax decode, so our pre-registered kill fires and the GP's lower confidence bound is simply locally maximal at the data. The nearest formalization is an inconsistency result for expected-improvement BO \citep{yarotsky2013inconsistency}, differing on three axes, and the trust-region literature uses the same locality \mdemph{deliberately} \citep{eriksson2019turbo}, here an unintended terminal state. This does not rescue our positive signal, which stays synthetic-only.

\paragraph{The inversion and the synthetic null are one finding.} The section's most awkward number is that the optimizer axis \mdemph{inverts} here: perturbation leads all four corners ($0.76$--$0.81$ against gradient's $0.40$--$0.68$) and $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}$ exceeds $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ in point estimate everywhere (intervals overlap, so this is point-estimate localization, never a within-corner separation), while the synthetic optimizer effect is small ($\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.038$ $[0.003,0.123]$, a magnitude for that grid, not a general verdict). Read through the frozen cells the suites stop disagreeing: if the GP cannot be moved, every optimizer scores it identically, the between-optimizer variance that survives comes from the unfrozen ensemble cells, and the axis inverts as an arithmetic consequence---the payoff of a decomposition over a leaderboard. Four limits: it is two of three Ant GP cells (the gradient cell returns $1.3242 \pm 0.1975$, unfrozen); fact and label are separable; frozen and near-tied cells are never pooled; and the freeze explains why the axis inverts, not how large the optimizer effect is, which the matched-budget arm establishes.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Pre-registered predictions and their outcomes.} Four registered predictions came back against us and two mechanism arms failed; we report all six, since undisclosed they would read as selective. Our registered headline was that the acquisition optimizer explains most of the gap: it does not ($\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.038$ even after correcting the budget imbalance), and the pre-committed contingency, a pivot to the mechanism, is this paper. The ensemble-size reversal criterion did not fire, but its accompanying prediction confirmed: the headline is reported at the $K$ that maximizes it (Confound~3). The $\beta$--$\sigma$ criterion fired, narrowing Confound~4 to its per-task form, and the gradient-specific coverage framing was retracted on our own test. The two mechanism arms cost most: smoothing the mean was built to confirm our published account and refuted it; the phantom-maxima probe was built for a mechanism and returned a seventh elimination.

\paragraph{Interpreting the optimizer effect.} Under a matched budget of $Q{=}51{,}456$, $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}=0.038$ $[0.003,0.123]$: optimizer choice explains little variance in this grid. It replaces our unmatched $0.005$, an eightfold understatement we disclose. The confirmation is not comfortable: the upper bound $0.123$ lies inside our pre-registered inconclusive band, so an effect up to $\sim$0.12 is not excluded at $n{=}7$, and the secondary level does not corroborate cleanly ($0.066$ $[0.014,0.340]$ at $Q{=}4{,}352$). The null is established at high budget, underpowered at low, and the \mdemph{best} optimizer changes with budget: gradient leads low ($0.731$), perturbation high ($0.732$). Two things travel under \mdemph{budget}. Confound~5 is the $11.8\times$ \mdemph{imbalance} across the optimizer axis, which matching removes; the budget \mdemph{level} $Q$ is an operating-point coordinate, which matching only fixes. So the matched grid is a \mdemph{different} point, with its own $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}{=}0.526$ and $\eta^2_{\text{inter}}{=}0.110$ against the primary point's $0.405$ and $0.160$: the $0.038$ and the $0.405$ are never set side by side as one decomposition. But the small \mdemph{marginal} is not a verdict that optimizer choice is arbitrary: on the synthetic grid its \mdemph{conditional} effect through the interaction is four to thirty-three times larger, so which optimizer to use depends on the surrogate.

\paragraph{Scope.} We make no field reversal: that the statistical model often outranks the acquisition heuristic is a decade-old doctrine \citep{shahriari2016humanoutoftheloop}; we falsify only the \mdemph{local} premise of one recent paper---that offline black-box optimization ``has focused on improving surrogate models while using fixed search strategies'' \citep{chemingui2024pggs}---and claim ensemble size as no discovered mechanism \citep{li2024bnnsurrogates,abe2022ensembles}, no equivalence on Design-Bench \citep{agarwal2021precipice,lakens2017equivalence}, no priority on the $K$ range, and distance-aware uncertainty as not ours \citep{liu2020sngp,vanamersfoort2020duq}. The Design-Bench null is a failure to detect, not equivalence: a two-one-sided-tests bound rejects equivalence at both a $0.5$ and a $0.3$ margin (supplement) \citep{lakens2017equivalence}. The two protocol arms make the same shape of finding on two axes, correcting them raising the synthetic surrogate effect and matching budgets raising the Design-Bench optimizer effect, both against the reality-check genre's shrink direction \citep{melis2018sota}; but that is two instances, not a law, since the ensemble-size confound moves the surrogate effect the other way.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Seven synthetic tasks is a small $n$, binding the corner decomposition, the optimizer interval and the omnibus alike; datasets are drawn once at seed 0, so reported variance is training and optimization, not data-draw. GFP is quarantined from coverage claims for its degenerate decode; excluding it flips one coverage claim (the exact GP's in-distribution coverage from below to above nominal) while the effect-size null is unchanged. The decomposition's direction survives $z$-score, rank and leave-one-task-out (the headline ranges $0.373$--$0.485$; Griewank-30D, widest in raw range, is the \mdemph{smallest} lever), and the strengthening survives $\epsilon^2$ bias correction ($0.351\to0.395$; supplement). No landscape covariate we tested---dimension, multimodality, separability---predicts the gap at this power (supplement). Two of the seven controls are narrower than the count suggests: capacity is eliminated at fixed depth 2 (the depth form open), and $\sigma$ at one ensemble construction (a likelihood-trained variant untested). The surviving mechanism is a diagnosis with seven eliminations behind it and no positive causal test in front, both pre-registered positive accounts having returned eliminations, so what stays open is finite: those two arms, plus whether the surrogate-geometry account and the optimizer interaction are one mechanism or two. The positive signal stays synthetic-only.

\section{Conclusion}

At the one operating point we name (Section~\ref{sec:deconf}) the decomposition is one triple: $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}{=}0.405$ $[0.290,0.556]$, $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.005$, $\eta^2_{\text{inter}}{=}0.160$ $[0.087,0.236]$. Removing the two protocol confounds put it there, up from $0.367$ on our reproduction grid under PGS's released protocol \citep{chemingui2024pggs}---against the genre's shrink direction, the net of two corrections against a third, and not resolvable at seven tasks, so we claim the direction, not a point value. Two firmer results: any $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}$ here is a joint artifact of five operating-point coordinates papers do not state, and on the \mdemph{synthetic} grid the surrogate$\times$optimizer interaction exceeds the optimizer main effect in every engine corner, its interval excluding zero in all four---a scope the suite forces: Design-Bench reverses that ordering. What the advantage \mdemph{is} stays open after seven controls; what is measured is the loss---the ensemble's optima worse in true oracle value at matched distance---and its shape, an acquisition ranking invented designs above real ones. On the optimizer axis the claim stays narrow, with its coordinate: at matched $Q{=}51{,}456$, $\eta^2_{\text{opt}}{=}0.038$ $[0.003,0.123]$ \mdemph{in this grid}, alongside $\eta^2_{\text{surr}}{=}0.526$ and $\eta^2_{\text{inter}}{=}0.110$ there---a different point, not numbers to read against the primary $0.405$---falsifying the local premise of one recent paper \citep{chemingui2024pggs} and nothing the standard review settled a decade ago \citep{shahriari2016humanoutoftheloop}. We offer the taxonomy, the protocol, and the engine stamp that makes either reproducible.
