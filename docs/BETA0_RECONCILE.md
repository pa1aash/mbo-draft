# 0A.1 — Reconciliation of the two beta=0 GP–ensemble gap computations

**Status: RECONCILED.** The two numbers were never in numerical conflict. They are the same
estimator applied to **two different engines**. GATE_KBETA's 0.378 is computed on the audited
**on_on** engine; the novelty audit's 0.504 -> 0.511 is computed on **off_off**, the pre-audit
artifact that X1 was introduced to supersede. On_on is the correct engine, so **0.378 is the
number that stands** — but it is expressed in beta-dependent units, and once put on a
beta-invariant ruler the reconciled figure is **0.319**.

**Artifacts:** `code/beta0_reconcile.py` -> `results/beta0_reconcile.json` (engine-stamped:
X1=on, X3=on, K=5, TOP=128, OPT_STEPS=100, 30 seeds `0..29`, 7 synthetic tasks, git_sha
`812bcb9`, torch 2.11.0+cpu / numpy 2.4.4 / botorch 0.18.1 / gpytorch 1.15.2 / cma 4.4.4,
envs/pod-synth).

## 2. Which difference actually causes the disagreement

Four differences separate the paths: engine, surrogate set (GP+SVGP vs botorchgp), normalizer
scope (9 cells vs 16), and data file. They were isolated by applying **Path A's own estimator**
(kbeta normalization, both GPs) to the **off_off** camera grids:

| estimator | engine | gap @ beta=2 | gap @ beta=0 | direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Path A (`_gp_ens_gap`) | on_on | 0.556 | **0.378** | shrinks |
| Path A (`_gp_ens_gap`) | off_off | 0.496 | **0.516** | flat/up |
| Path B (`task_norm`, botorchgp only) | off_off | 0.504 | **0.511** | flat/up |

Holding the estimator fixed and switching only the engine flips the direction. Holding the
engine fixed and switching the estimator moves the number by <0.02. **The disagreement is
entirely the engine — normalizer scope and surrogate pooling are not the cause.**

The mechanism is X1. Off_off, the ensemble regresses on **raw** y while both GPs z-score
(`code/mbo.py:144-170`), across a suite spanning Griewank ~ −2600 to Branin ~ −10 at a fixed
lr/epoch budget. That handicaps the ensemble most where the pure-mean (beta=0) fit is all that
matters. X1 removes the handicap, the ensemble's beta=0 score rises, and the beta=0 gap falls
from 0.516 to 0.378. **The audit's flat-to-up finding is an artifact of the target-scaling bug
the audit itself motivated fixing.**

**Verdict on which is correct: on_on.** X1 is a documented correctness fix, not a tuning choice;
`results_camera.json` is explicitly flagged as superseded. Path B is a correct computation on
the wrong artifact.

## 3. The residual problem with 0.378, and the reconciled number

0.378 is still not the right quantity for "is the GP's mean better, independent of pessimism",
because **Path A refits its min–max normalizer separately at each beta**
(`code/kbeta_analyze.py:50-51`). gap(beta=0) and gap(beta=2) are therefore expressed in
different units and their ratio is not interpretable — which is exactly what KB2's kill
criterion (`gap0 < 0.5*gap2`) compares.

The correct estimand fits **one normalizer per task, over the pooled cell means of both beta
conditions**, then reads the gap off at each beta — beta-invariant units, same ruler.
Implemented at `code/beta0_reconcile.py:80-96` (`path_C_normalizers` / `path_C`).

**Reconciled result** (task+seed hierarchical bootstrap, 10,000 resamples; the normalizer is
refit inside each resample so its sampling variability is propagated, not conditioned away —
`code/beta0_reconcile.py:112-146`):

| quantity | estimate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| **GP–ens gap @ beta=0 (beta-invariant units)** | **0.319** | **[0.196, 0.460]** |
| GP–ens gap @ beta=2 (beta-invariant units) | 0.525 | [0.406, 0.614] |
| pessimism increment (beta=2 − beta=0) | 0.203 | [0.007, 0.396] |

`p(increment <= 0) = 0.020`. Normalization-free cross-check (per-task z-score by the task's own
pooled cell-mean sd, `code/beta0_reconcile.py:99-109`): 0.904 sd @ beta=0, 1.473 sd @ beta=2 —
same ratio (0.61), so the conclusion is not an artifact of min–max scaling.

### Per-task gaps (beta-invariant units)

| task | beta=0 | beta=2 |
|---|---|---|
| Branin-2D | 0.664 | 0.468 |
| Styblinski-5D | 0.286 | 0.229 |
| Levy-8D | 0.199 | 0.659 |
| Rosenbrock-10D | 0.226 | 0.559 |
| Rastrigin-15D | 0.485 | 0.509 |
| Ackley-20D | 0.275 | 0.587 |
| Griewank-30D | 0.098 | 0.665 |

Heterogeneous. On Branin and Styblinski the beta=0 gap **exceeds** the beta=2 gap (pure mean
quality is the whole story); on Griewank-30D the beta=0 gap is nearly absent (0.098) and almost
all of the GP's advantage is pessimism-mediated. n=7, so this spread is descriptive only.

## 4. What this does to C2

**C2's mean-quality claim SURVIVES; the paper's stated "no beta-dependence" result DOES NOT.**

Survives: at beta=0 the LCB is pure mean maximization with no sigma term at all, and the GP
family still leads the ensemble by **0.319 [0.196, 0.460]** in beta-invariant normalized units.
The CI excludes zero by a wide margin. The GP's mean is genuinely better, and this is not a
calibration effect — there is no calibration in the beta=0 arm. The NOVELTY_V3 concern that the
gap might be flat-to-up (0.504 -> 0.511) is retired: that was off_off.

Does not survive: the phrase **"independent of pessimism."** Pessimism adds a further
**0.203 [0.007, 0.396]** — 39% of the beta=2 gap — and the increment's CI excludes zero
(p = 0.020). "Independent" claims no dependence; the data show a significant, if imprecisely
estimated, dependence. The honest claim is a **base-plus-amplification** one.

**Required rewrite of C2's central sentence.** Replace "the GP's mean is genuinely better
independent of pessimism" with, in substance:

> With the pessimism term removed entirely (beta=0), the GP family retains 61% of its advantage
> over the deep ensemble (0.319 [0.196, 0.460] vs 0.525 [0.406, 0.614] at beta=2, task+seed
> bootstrap, 10k). The surrogate-class effect therefore rests on a substantial mean-quality
> base that pessimism amplifies rather than creates — though the amplification is itself
> significant (increment 0.203 [0.007, 0.396]), so the mechanism is not purely a mean-geometry
> one.

Two further constraints on how hard C2 may lean:

1. **Do not cite 0.378 or 0.556 as a ratio.** They are in beta-refit units. Cite 0.319 / 0.525,
   or cite 0.378 only as a within-beta descriptive figure.
2. **The paper's current beta=0 passage is refuted and must be rewritten.**
   `paper/aaai27/main.tex:198` states the gap is "essentially unchanged — 0.51 at beta=2 versus
   0.47 at beta=0 ... the paired difference has 95% CI [-0.02, 0.10], indistinguishable from
   zero" and concludes "The advantage is a property of the GP's smooth mean, not of sigma."
   `paper/aaai27/supplement.tex:106` repeats it ("pessimism is not the source of the GP
   advantage"). Under the corrected on_on computation the paired increment is
   **0.203 [0.007, 0.396]**, which **excludes zero** — the opposite of the paper's stated CI.
   The claim of no beta-dependence does not hold on the audited engine. Additionally, the
   specific pair (0.51, 0.47) is **not reproduced by any traced computation**: the 0.51
   magnitude matches the off_off botorchgp 3-optimizer figure (0.504), but the direction is
   wrong there too (off_off goes 0.504 -> 0.511, up, not down to 0.47). The 0.47 is
   unattributed. Both the number and the "indistinguishable from zero" CI must be struck and
   replaced with the base-plus-amplification wording above.

KB2's stated verdict in `docs/GATE_KBETA.md` ("smooth-mean claim survives") is upheld under the
corrected computation — the kill threshold does not fire on the beta-invariant numbers either
(0.319 > 0.5 x 0.525 = 0.263) — but it fires much closer than the beta-refit numbers implied
(0.378 vs 0.278), and the margin is inside the bootstrap CI. **KB2 passes, but not comfortably.**
