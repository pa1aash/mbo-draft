# Offline-MBO Novelty Audit for AAAI-27: What Is New, What Is Owned, and the Strongest Publishable Contribution

The direct answer, stated before the evidence: of the nine findings, three survive an adversarial, fetched-and-grepped pass through the primary literature as clean NONE FOUND, four are genuinely PARTIAL (a prior paper owns the general form while an offline-model-based-optimization residual is unclaimed), one is NONE FOUND but pre-empted in strength, and the strongest publishable paper is not the one that feels most exciting. Offline model-based optimization (offline MBO — optimizing a design against a fixed dataset with no further oracle queries) is the residual-scoping axis throughout: the same claim can be owned in general Bayesian optimization and unclaimed in the offline setting. The crossed surrogate-by-optimizer factorial (N6) is unowned, and the field's own most recent peer-reviewed survey concedes the gap in as many words [1]; the bidirectional smoothness manipulation (N7) is unowned not merely within offline MBO but across the entire Bayesian-optimization and kernel-methods literature; and the de-confounding *direction* (N9) — an audit whose corrected scalar effect size grows rather than shrinks — has no precedent inside machine learning in its narrow scalar form.

Around that defensible core sit five findings the literature partly owns. The reality-check *shape* of N1 — name the confounds, give the removal protocol, show the ranking moves — is the property of Henderson et al. [2], Ferrari Dacrema et al. [3], and Musgrave et al. [4]; the specific five offline-MBO confounds and the two-way variance decomposition are not. N3, N4, and N8 each rest on a general principle prior work owns but an offline-MBO instantiation it does not. And N5's finite-width objection is theoretically real but is neither evidenced by the paper's own K-sweep nor fatal, because the nearest analog already answered it. The single most important strategic consequence: do not build the paper on the dramatic "the field innovates on the axis that does not matter" reversal — that directional claim is decade-old Bayesian-optimization doctrine [7] — nor on the surrogate-smoothness mechanism, which currently rests on a category error and a dead measurement channel. Build it on the named, controlled, offline-MBO-specific confound set for which correcting the two protocol confounds — target scaling and the candidate/oracle protocol — nets the audited effect *up* against the genre's shrink direction (η²_surrogate, the fraction of outcome variance attributable to surrogate choice, from a published 0.37 to a corrected 0.405), even as the ensemble-size confound K pushes the other way. Every reality-check the genre is built on runs the other way [5] (Recht [6] is the mixed partial the paper pre-empts); that inversion is what no prior paper owns.

## N1 - Confound Taxonomy: Prior-Art Overlap and the Offline-MBO Residual

Concede the shape openly, because conceding it is what makes the residual credible. The move "name a set of unreported implementation choices, give a protocol that controls them, show a published ranking reverses" is a recognized, accepted, and named genre, and its canonical instance was published at the target venue. Henderson et al. audit continuous-control deep reinforcement learning by isolating one under-reported factor at a time — network architecture, reward scale, random seeds, environment dynamics, codebase — and show each alone flips which algorithm looks best, concluding that "implementation differences which are often not reflected in publications can have dramatic impacts on performance" [2].

The shape recurs across four other well-cited audits:

- **Ferrari Dacrema et al.** diagnose recommender-system progress as an artifact of "(i) weak baselines; (ii) establishment of weak methods as new baselines; and (iii) difficulties in comparing or reproducing results across papers," then re-run with tuned baselines and find six of seven neural methods lose to classical algorithms [3].
- **Musgrave et al.** rebuild the deep-metric-learning protocol and find "the actual improvements over time have been marginal at best" [4].
- **Lucic et al.** show that for generative adversarial networks "most models can reach similar scores with enough hyperparameter optimization and random restarts" [21].
- **Agarwal et al.** supply the statistical-methodology version, a three-part taxonomy of evaluation confounds with demonstrated ranking reversals [22].

The sixth query-named comparator, Balduzzi et al.'s "Re-evaluating Evaluation" [48], was checked and ruled out as an N1 anchor: it addresses benchmark-*suite* redundancy via Nash averaging (0 hits for confound/reproduce/tuning), a different confound axis than the unreported implementation choices N1 names. A reviewer who has read any of these will not accept "we ran a reality check" as a contribution in itself.

But the shape is a template, not a possession of the specific instantiation, and two things in N1 are absent from every one of these papers. First, the machinery: none of them computes a variance decomposition. There is no η² (eta-squared, the fraction of variance attributable to a factor) anywhere in the reality-check genre — no analog of "fixing target scaling alone moves η²_surrogate from 0.367 to 0.283," or "fixing target scaling and the candidate protocol together nets up to 0.405." (Variance decomposition of model class is not novel per se — Hutter's fANOVA owns the *one-way* version, N6 — so the residual is the *two-way crossed* surrogate×optimizer ANOVA in offline MBO, not the use of η²/ANOVA itself.) Their outputs are qualitative re-rankings and confidence-interval overlaps; the paper's is a two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA — a partition of total outcome variance into main effects and their interaction) that attributes a specific fraction of the headline to each factor.

Second, the confound *vocabulary* is domain-locked to each source. None has an analog of target scaling of a regression surrogate (the ensemble regressed on raw targets spanning −2613 to +36 while both Gaussian-process surrogates z-scored), a candidate/oracle-selection asymmetry between optimizers (two propose 256 designs and report the oracle-selected best 128 while the third proposes 128), an ensemble-size K ablation on a surrogate's uncertainty quality, or a shared-β acquisition term whose effective conservatism differs by surrogate σ-scale.

The interpretive beat the sources do not state: a reality-check genre matures by migrating from field to field, and each migration is publishable precisely when the confounds are field-specific rather than generic. Henderson was accepted for naming the RL-specific confounds, not rejected for reusing the shape. The paper occupies that slot for offline MBO — the first field-specific confound set carried by a variance-attribution instrument the genre has never used. Quantifying the overlap dimension by dimension: the genre-shape is fully owned by all six comparators; the two-way variance-decomposition instrument is owned by none; four of the five confound labels (target scaling, candidate/oracle asymmetry, ensemble size K, β–σ mismatch) are owned by none, and only the seed/codebase confound partially echoes Henderson [2]; the net-up direction is owned by none. **Verdict: PARTIAL — the shape is FOUND (Henderson, Ferrari Dacrema, Musgrave); the five-confound offline-MBO set plus the η² decomposition is the residual, NONE FOUND.** A rejecting reviewer will nonetheless press that "the same genre, with an ANOVA and a domain-specific confound list" is a residual of composition, not of kind — which is why the strengthening direction (N9) has to carry weight the meta-move cannot. That residual is the spine of candidate D.

## N2 - K-Contingency of the Surrogate-Class Ranking

The claim under test is sharper than "ensemble accuracy depends on how many members you average," which is textbook. It is that the *cross-class ranking* — deep ensemble versus exact GP — flips as a function of ensemble size K, and that the field-standard K=5, cited from Lakshminarayanan et al. [10], is the setting where the ensemble is weakest: task-normalized ensemble scores run **0.95 / 0.52 / 0.32 / 0.18 at K = 2 / 3 / 5 / 10**, so that at K=2 the ensemble marginal (0.95) exceeds the exact GP's (0.846).

No located paper reports K-dependence of a surrogate-*class* ranking. The exact queries run were "ensemble size dependence surrogate model comparison Bayesian optimization ranking"; "number of ensemble members Bayesian optimization ranking changes"; "ensemble vs Gaussian process surrogate size Bayesian optimization comparison"; "deep ensemble ablation number of networks Bayesian optimization surrogate outperforms Gaussian process"; and "offline model-based optimization surrogate ensemble size ablation Design-Bench." Abe et al. — the query's own "nearest known" — compare an M-network ensemble against a single larger network and find a capacity story, but never against a GP, never in an acquisition context, and never as a ranking [9]. The single closest analog found the *opposite*: Li, Rudner and Wilson ran their own ensemble-size ablation inside a controlled cross-surrogate Bayesian-optimization comparison and reported that "the different ensembles perform similarly across many experiments, showing the robustness of our results to this hyperparameter" [8]. Their sweep spans only K ∈ {5, 10} and never reaches the K=2 flip — which is why the finding is technically new — so the two probe disjoint K ranges and are consistent, not contradictory.

The load-bearing caveat is that the K-decline runs monotonically *backward* from both nearest anchors: Lakshminarayanan et al., the very source cited for "standard K=5," report that "as M increases, both the accuracy and the quality of predictive uncertainty improve significantly" [10], and [8] found flatness. A monotone decline against both makes the most parsimonious reading of the K=2 spike a small-sample σ-estimation artifact — the standard deviation across two members is an extremely noisy estimator, and a lucky K=2 draw feeding a lower-confidence-bound acquisition (LCB, which selects designs maximizing μ − βσ) is a mundane confound. **Verdict: NONE FOUND, but claim the observation, not a law.** Present K as one of the five confounds that must be controlled — its home is candidate D — and explicitly decline to sell it as a discovered mechanism, or [8]'s robustness result will be turned against you.

## N3 - Unmatched Effective Pessimism

The claim: because a deep ensemble's σ and a GP's σ live on structurally different scales, a single nominal β applied to both delivers different *effective* conservatism, so the LCB acquisition was never actually matched across surrogate classes. The general statistical principle — one fixed confidence multiplier on differently-scaled uncertainty estimators yields unequal effective coverage — is owned cleanly, and by exactly the kind of paper a Bayesian-learning reviewer keeps at hand. Dewolf, De Baets and Waegeman benchmark four classes of prediction-interval method (Bayesian, ensemble, direct-interval, conformal) at one shared nominal confidence level and find "the uncalibrated models do not approximately saturate the validity constraint. They either underestimate the uncertainty or produce overconservative prediction intervals" [11] — N3's premise demonstrated empirically across method classes — and they prescribe the standard fix, normalizing "the nonconformity measure by a dispersion function σ," which is the repair the paper's protocol implicitly performs.

Two nearer optimization-flavored analogs both fall short in the other direction. Ghasemipour et al. own the shape "nominal ≠ effective pessimism" — "shared pessimistic targets can paradoxically lead to value estimates that are effectively optimistic" — but the mechanism is target independence within one Q-ensemble across Bellman backups, not σ-scale mismatch *across* surrogate classes, and the paper never varies surrogate class (0 hits for "Gaussian process," "surrogate") [23]. The foundational calibration literature does not own it either: GP-UCB's β_t schedule (Srinivas et al.) is a confidence-width schedule derived by a union bound over rounds to bound cumulative regret for *one* evolving GP posterior; every argument to β_t is intrinsic to a single surrogate, and a grep returns 0 hits for "surrogate," "ensemble," or "calibrat" [24]. The two nearest adversarial candidates that bring surrogate classes into contact were fetched and ruled out: Lu et al. sidestep β entirely with Thompson sampling over an *ensemble of GPs* ("requires no additional design parameters"; 0 hits for β / different-surrogate / σ) [49], and Benechehab et al. compare bootstrap ensembles against autoregressive density nets on calibration (24 "calibrat" hits) but aim to *replace* the worse-calibrated class, never diagnosing that a shared coefficient yields mismatched effective conservatism from σ-scale differences [50] — the field's instinct is to fuse or replace surrogate classes, never to hold one nominal β fixed across them and diagnose the mismatch.

**Verdict: PARTIAL, and saying so is a strength.** Concede that the abstract fact — two differently-scaled uncertainty estimators cannot share one confidence multiplier without recalibration — is owned by the conformal/calibration lineage [11] and by Ghasemipour's within-class analog [23]; claim only the offline-MBO acquisition-comparison application, that in a surrogate-class benchmark using LCB a fixed β silently confounds the comparison, and that naming and correcting this is unreported. SNGP [12] strengthens the premise: because GP-RBF σ is distance-aware and ensemble σ is not, the two classes' σ outputs are differently *shaped*, not merely differently *scaled* — a stronger form of incomparability than N3 asserts.

## N4 - Distance-Aware Uncertainty and the Implicit Trust Region

This finding splits into two claims the evidence separates cleanly, and the split is what protects the paper. **Claim (a): a properly specified GP's posterior variance grows away from the training data, while standard deep ensembles do not.** This is owned; there is no point contesting it. SNGP formalizes "input distance awareness" and proves a GP-RBF posterior variance "increases monotonically toward 1 as x* moves further away from X_IND," while showing ensembles and dropout networks are governed by distance-to-decision-boundary instead [12]. DUQ confirms the ensemble failure mode on the two-moons dataset, where "Deep Ensembles is uncertain only along the decision boundary, and certain elsewhere" [25]. Cite these as motivation, not as your own mechanism.

**Claim (b): that variance growth, funneled through LCB, wins the offline-MBO comparison by acting as an implicit trust region.** The causal mechanism is PARTIAL rather than unclaimed, because Fan et al. — outside the SNGP/DUQ/DUE/TuRBO set the earlier passes checked — derive it formally: minimizing a UCB-style bound "can be viewed as local strategy" precisely because the bound "is small only near the sampled point, and increases as it moves further away" [13]. What remains genuinely unclaimed is only the *label*: no located paper calls a bare LCB acquisition an "implicit trust region" (Fan uses "local strategy"; "trust region" appears in his paper only as citations to explicit-trust-region baselines) [13]. A naming novelty is thin ground for a mechanism contribution — and the nearest paper argues *against* the mechanism being automatic. TuRBO built an *explicit* hyperrectangular trust region with a hand-tuned success/failure counter precisely because unchecked global GP-variance growth under a myopic acquisition produces "overemphasized exploration and a failure to exploit promising areas"; it uses Thompson sampling, not LCB, and never claims the acquisition alone produces a trust region [26].

Two scope points decide how to use this. First, the objection that deep-kernel GPs can fail to get variance growth through "feature collapse" does not apply: the paper's exact GP and SVGP (sparse variational GP, a scalable GP approximation using inducing points) use vanilla Matérn kernels (a standard stationary covariance function) directly on raw inputs, so the fragile case never arises; DUE's caveat is deep-kernel-specific and, if anything, certifies the paper's kernels as the safe case [27]. Second, and more consequential, the paper's own β=0 control points against a σ-mediated story: with the pessimism term removed the GP-ensemble gap does not collapse. A reviewer must also not be handed Ovadia et al. as support for "ensembles are confidently wrong far from data" — Ovadia measured shift as corruption severity, not spatial distance, and found ensembles among the *better* estimators there ("Ensembles consistently have high accuracy but also high entropy on OOD data"), so citing it in support invites a mis-citation flag [28].

**Verdict: PARTIAL — the distance-aware and UCB-local-search mechanisms are owned (SNGP, Fan); the "implicit trust region" naming and its offline-MBO instantiation are unclaimed; and, because the gap survives removal of the σ term with β=0 (direction robust, magnitude unreconciled — see terminal section), the paper's viable mechanism relocates from posterior *variance* to posterior *mean* smoothness.** That relocation is the hinge to N5 and to all of candidate C's real (if unrun) claim. Do not assert the implicit-trust-region mechanism as your explanation; your own control undercuts it, and a reviewer who knows TuRBO will say so.

## N5 - NTK / Spectral Bias and the Finite-Width Objection

This is the strongest theoretical objection to a mechanism paper, and honest handling of it separates a defensible submission from a rejected one. The adversarial reading: infinite-width networks *are* Gaussian processes (Jacot's neural-tangent-kernel result — the NTK being the fixed kernel that governs a wide network's training dynamics; Lee's infinite-width-to-GP equivalence), and finite networks are spectrally biased toward smooth, low-frequency functions (Rahaman); therefore a "jagged ensemble posterior mean" might be a finite-width or under-training artifact rather than a surrogate-class property [14][29][30]. Jacot shows empirically that "for the wider network, the NTK shows less variance and is smoother" [14]; Lee reports finite-width accuracy "approaches that of the corresponding GP with increasing layer width" [29]; Rahaman finds networks "favour low frequency ones — hence they exhibit a bias towards smooth functions" [30]. Stacked together, these say a sufficiently wide, well-trained network *should* be smooth, so a jagged finite ensemble looks like evidence of under-parameterization — a fixable implementation confound (favoring D), not an intrinsic class property (which C needs).

Two facts blunt the objection without dismissing it. First, the K-sweep is not valid evidence for the finite-width reading, and presenting it as such is a category error a Bayesian-deep-learning reviewer will catch in one line: ensemble cardinality K (how many members you average — here each member is a fixed two-layer MLP of hidden width 96) is not per-member width n. None of Jacot, Lee, or Rahaman mentions ensembles (0 hits each) [14][29][30]; increasing K widens no member. Worse for the finite-width story, the K-sweep runs monotonically *backward* — more members give a *worse* ensemble — the opposite sign from a "more capacity smooths it out" prediction.

Second, the objection is only partially addressed, not settled: an appendix architecture-size ablation in L/R/W reruns the comparison at a *smaller* net (width 128→50) and finds "even with the smaller architecture... the relative performance of the surrogate models mostly remains consistent" [8] — shrinking the net did not rescue the ensemble ranking, but it never tests the *wider* net the objection predicts would smooth the ensemble mean, and it confounds width with depth and activation. Their own diagnosis is data scarcity, not width — in the low-data regime "there are fewer settings of parameters that give rise to easily discoverable basins of attraction, making it harder to find diverse solutions" [8] — a domain-matched mechanism, since offline-MBO datasets sit in exactly the small-data regime. The NTK-linearization literature agrees the finite-width story does not automatically favor the ensemble: Lee, Xiao, Schoenholz et al. report finite/infinite-width agreement is "highly architecture-dependent" and that "GPs still outperform trained neural networks for both small and large dataset size" [41], and the paper's per-member width of 96 sits far below their n≥512 NTK-agreement range — so a width-driven component cannot be ruled out; it just isn't evidenced by the K-sweep. Fort et al. independently locate ensemble diversity in random-initialization mode selection, using "width" and "capacity" zero times [31], so the width story and the diversity story are orthogonal axes the K-as-width reading conflates; and Dai et al. confirm that width ablations inside a BO surrogate reduce regret — but only for a single-network surrogate, never an ensemble baseline [32].

**Verdict: PARTIAL — the finite-width/spectral-bias theory is FOUND (Jacot/Lee/Rahaman) and the objection is theoretically real, but the K-sweep as valid evidence for the class-property claim is NONE FOUND, and the objection is only partially addressed (an appendix architecture-size ablation reruns a smaller net, never the wider one the objection predicts) rather than fatal.** The consequence is decisive: the objection is answerable only with an experiment the paper does not run — a clean width ablation holding K fixed and sweeping hidden units — so this thread favors the taxonomy paper over the mechanism paper.

## N6 - The Crossed Surrogate x Optimizer Factorial

This is the load-bearing novelty claim and the cleanest NONE FOUND in the audit. The requirement is specific: a controlled grid crossing a shared set of surrogate *classes* against a shared set of *optimizers*, both manipulated, under one evaluation protocol, with a two-way variance decomposition, in offline MBO. One counterexample would be fatal; none exists. The most valuable single sentence in the located literature is from Kim et al.'s TMLR 2026 survey — the field's most recent peer-reviewed review — which states that "existing benchmarks often emphasize overall optimization performance without clarifying whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance" [1]. This is not the absence of a counterexample; it is the field certifying, one AAAI cycle before submission, that the exact attribution question a crossed factorial answers is *open*. The same survey corroborates the Design-Bench null, noting that TF-Bind-8 offers "overly constrained search spaces where even simple gradient ascent methods can achieve impressive results" [1]. (Date: Kim et al. is TMLR 2026 camera-ready, not the 2025 the arXiv v1 back-propagates.)

Every candidate owner fixes one axis or bundles both. The table below is the checkable rule-out:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Li/Rudner/Wilson [8] | Yes (~7 classes) | No (MC-EI fixed for all problems) | No | No (online BO) |
| PGS, Chemingui et al. [33] | No (one MLP) | Yes (search policy) | No | Yes |

L/R/W is the *transpose* of the paper's design — surrogate classes crossed with the acquisition held fixed ("We use Monte-Carlo based Expected Improvement... as our acquisition function for all problems"), and online, not offline (0 hits for "offline") [8].

The two nearest structural analogs must be named pre-emptively. Hutter et al.'s functional ANOVA (fANOVA, a variance decomposition of performance across hyperparameters) finds "the most important hyperparameter was the model class used" (31–58% of Auto-WEKA's variance) — the closest "model class as a variance-decomposed factor" result in existence — but it is one-way, computed from one fixed search method (SMAC) [15]. Liang et al. is structurally even closer — a genuinely crossed grid of surrogate models against acquisition functions across five materials domains, with LCB written out as "LCB_λ(x) = μ̂(x) + λσ̂(x)" — but reports only descriptive "acceleration/enhancement" metrics (0 hits for ANOVA/η²/factorial), and it is online closed-loop BO [16]. Sharper still, Moosbauer et al. vary an HPO optimizer's surrogate learner *and* its sampling/search strategy, explicitly name fANOVA, and then *decline* it for one-factor-at-a-time ablation "because... interactions between inputs cannot be detected by an OFAT analysis" [43] — the one paper closest to the two-axis design consciously refuses the two-way variance decomposition, the sharpest available evidence that the attribution gap is deliberate and structural, not a search artifact. Even the fANOVA lineage's cross-dataset extension analyzes one algorithm at a time and never decomposes model class against search strategy (van Rijn & Hutter) [46].

**Verdict: NONE FOUND.** The exact queries run were "factorial ANOVA surrogate optimizer" (and "…black-box optimization"), "decompose surrogate versus acquisition" (and "…function black-box optimization"), "which component matters black-box optimization surrogate acquisition," "ablation surrogate optimizer offline model-based optimization," "offline model-based optimization surrogate ensemble size ablation Design-Bench," and "rigorous evaluation offline optimization benchmark confounds surrogate model," plus the fANOVA/AutoML-neighborhood sweep that surfaced Hutter, Moosbauer, and van Rijn — each candidate then full-text grepped for "factorial"/"ANOVA"/"crossed" (0 hits each). State the residual precisely as "a crossed surrogate-class × optimizer factorial with a two-way ANOVA/η² decomposition, in offline MBO," and cite Hutter and Liang defensively as the one-way and descriptive-online precedents the paper improves on. The cleanness of the design is exactly why the finding it produces is only as trustworthy as the de-confounded number behind it — which remains provisional (see the terminal section).

## N7 - Bidirectional Smoothness Manipulation

The N7 claim is the highest-ceiling novelty: manipulate surrogate smoothness in *both* directions — smooth the network and roughen the GP — to identify smoothness itself, rather than surrogate-class identity, as the causal axis of the performance gap. The verdict is NONE FOUND at the broadest scope searched, and not within offline MBO alone but within the entire BO-and-kernel-methods literature. The structural reason the gap persists is informative: two half-literatures each do half the job. Within-GP smoothness manipulation is common — length-scale sweeps and kernel-parameter diversity, including ROOT, which varies GP length-scale as a data-augmentation device to generate synthetic training functions, never as a roughen-the-GP causal arm [36], and Ziomek, Adachi and Osborne's Length-scale Balancing, the closest deliberate GP-smoothness manipulation in the BO literature, which aggregates GP surrogates at multiple length-scales (adding rougher candidates while retaining smoother ones) for regret bounds but stays entirely within the GP family (full-text grep for "neural network" = one unrelated hit) and never attributes a class gap to smoothness [42] — and cross-surrogate-class comparison is common, but no paper combines them into one controlled, causally-attributed experiment.

The closest genuine hit is Lim et al., who directly compare a GP against a neural-network ensemble for BO on materials data — the exact class pairing N7 cares about — and offer a smoothness explanation for the GP's eventual win: the advantage "could be due to the ability of GP to smoothly map out the uncertainty manifolds" [17]. But this is a hedged, post-hoc, one-sentence hypothesis in a conclusion; the only deliberate manipulation is *widening* the GP's own length-scale bounds to avoid overfitting, the neural ensemble receives no smoothness manipulation at all, and no experiment isolates smoothness as the causal variable [17]. Smoothness-as-regularization is meanwhile a live line — IGNITE penalizes neural-network surrogate sharpness with a generalization bound ("reducing surrogate sharpness on the offline dataset provably reduces its generalized sharpness on unseen data") — but it smooths a network only, always downward, and never touches a GP [18]. Its ICML 2024 sibling by the same group confirms the pattern independently, reducing (never increasing) neural-network surrogate sensitivity and citing a GP only bibliographically [47].

The exact queries run include "kernel lengthscale ablation Bayesian optimization performance," "Matern smoothness nu Bayesian optimization comparison," "surrogate smoothness controlled experiment optimization," "roughen model smoothness ablation neural network," arXiv abstract searches for "smoothness" AND "Bayesian optimization" AND "regret" and "oversmooth" AND "undersmooth" AND "Gaussian process," and "'vary smoothness' OR 'smoothness ablation' surrogate model both directions optimization causal mechanism," together with forward-citation walks on IGNITE (its eight citing papers, 2024–2026) and MS-DDEO that surfaced ROOT and the Lim et al. hit — none returned a bidirectional cross-class manipulation. **Verdict: NONE FOUND, "first at all" — scoped to the BO / GP-regression / kernel-methods literature as indexed by arXiv, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar as of this sweep, not merely "first in offline MBO."** The load-bearing qualifier: this novelty is real but *conditional on running the manipulation the paper has not yet run*. Until the paired smooth-the-net / roughen-the-GP arms are executed, no sentence of the form "smoothness is *the* causal axis, demonstrated in both directions" is earned — which is exactly what makes C a bet and D a paper.

## N8 - Platform and Library-Version Dependence of Benchmark Results

The claim: an ensemble cell moves roughly 0.5 normalized units between macOS and Linux on TF-Bind-8 while the exact-GP cell matches to 1.000 and every synthetic cell reproduces to twelve decimal places cross-platform — so platform variance is of the same order as the method differences under study, on a Design-Bench grid whose whole span is roughly 1.0–2.5 and which the paper argues is null.

The general phenomenon — that execution-environment nondeterminism can produce variance comparable to the signals of interest — is owned in deep RL. Nagarajan et al. is the tightest analog: a controlled single-factor ablation holding seed, initialization, exploration, and codebase identical, in which GPU-operation nondeterminism alone produces a learning-curve variance that "looks similar to the curves for exploration and weight initialization," singled out because "the GPU is... the only source of nondeterminism that exists outside of the algorithm itself" [19]. Henderson's codebase experiments make the parallel point that reimplementation differences can dominate a nominal inter-algorithm effect [2], and two further deep-RL precedents widen the base: Machado et al. show that Arcade-Learning-Environment emulator *determinism* enables score-inflating memorization and that inconsistent evaluation protocols confound cross-paper comparison [44], and Islam et al. trace divergent TRPO/DDPG results to environment stochasticity and under-reported hyperparameters [45]. Gundersen and Kjensmo, the AAAI-2018 reproducibility survey, is weaker on this specific point: it only *cites* a caveat — "differences in software and hardware could have significant impact on results because of rounding errors in floating point arithmetic (Hong et al. 2013)" — and never measures a magnitude [38].

**Verdict: PARTIAL — the phenomenon (environment variance of the same order as method signal) is FOUND in deep RL; the offline-MBO instantiation, the specific macOS-versus-Linux axis, and a stated same-order ratio are the residual, NONE FOUND.** The strategic value is that this finding contributes to reframing the Design-Bench null from a weakness into a mechanism — though with a scope bound: the cross-platform variance was observed only in *ensemble* cells (the exact-GP cell matches to 1.000 and every synthetic cell reproduces to twelve decimals), so it explains ensemble-cell instability but is not a grid-wide noise floor and cannot by itself account for the GP cells being mutually indistinguishable. The null is therefore best attributed *jointly* to low power (Agarwal, N=7) and ensemble-cell platform noise: a Friedman omnibus (a non-parametric test for differences across a grid) landing at p=0.69 with the whole grid inside one critical difference is *expected*, not anomalous — which reframes the null from "the methods are equivalent" (an over-strong equivalence claim, underpowered at seven tasks) to "no difference is detectable at this power given this environment noise." N8 is the finding most likely to be undervalued by a Bayesian-optimization reviewer and rewarded by a benchmarking reviewer — a fact that matters directly for venue targeting.

## N9 - The De-Confounding Direction: an Audit That Strengthens

This is the paper's rhetorical asset, and it survives — but only in a narrow, precisely scoped form. Every reality-check the genre is built on runs the other way: the audit shrinks or vanishes the headline. Melis states the norm — once methodological care increases "effect sizes tend to be much smaller" [5]; his tuned LSTM overturns the claimed superiority of newer architectures. Robinson, Glen and Lee reverse a "deep learning beats SVM" claim to "SVM competitive," a clean shrink with zero confound-removal framing [39]. Musgrave and Ferrari Dacrema shrink likewise [4][3].

The paper's result inverts this: correcting the two measurement/protocol confounds — target scaling and the candidate/oracle protocol — nets η²_surrogate *up* from the published **0.37 to 0.405**, against the genre's shrink direction, even though the ensemble-size confound K moves it the other way (at K=2 the ensemble marginal 0.95 exceeds the exact GP's 0.846, which would shrink the surrogate effect). The strengthening is thus the net effect of the correctable scaling/protocol confounds, not of de-confounding across the whole taxonomy. Sixteen adversarial queries across two independent sweeps — including "reanalysis larger effect size machine learning," "controlling confounder increased effect machine learning benchmark," "correction strengthened result benchmark evaluation," and "regression attenuation measurement error correction larger coefficient machine learning" — returned no ML/CS reality-check or reproducibility audit reporting a corrected scalar effect size exceeding its own published value. **Verdict: NONE FOUND within ML/CS for the scalar-effect-size-grows-after-de-confounding shape.**

The discipline this section requires is not to overclaim, because two ML/CS partials must be pre-empted or a well-read reviewer discredits the whole point. Recht et al. is the first: absolute accuracy drops 3–15% (the shrink), but the model-to-model relative-gain slope exceeds 1 (fitted at 1.69 on CIFAR-10 and 1.11 on ImageNet) — "every percentage point of accuracy improvement on the original test set translates to a larger improvement on our new test sets... an increased advantage over earlier models," which the authors call "the opposite of the standard overfitting scenario" [6]. It differs in the underlying statistic (a relative slope, not a variance-explained scalar audited against its own prior value), but a reviewer who knows Recht will raise it. The second is Agarwal et al., whose power-corrected analysis reveals a real improvement the point estimates had hidden — an effect that *grew* once the confound (statistical under-powering) was removed [22]. Bressan supplies the closest full-shape precedent, but it is psychology and uses p-values not an effect-size statistic: controlling a stimulus-allocation confound restored a "failed" replication and, under a stricter specification, "the results came out stronger" [20].

The underlying statistical mechanism (covariate adjustment can increase an estimate's magnitude — noncollapsibility, attenuation correction) is well understood, so the novelty is not that the direction is *surprising* but that it is *unprecedented as an ML benchmark-audit instance*. Claim N9 in the exact scalar form — "no ML/CS de-confounding audit has been shown to produce a variance-explained effect-size statistic that exceeds its own published value" — cite Bressan as a one-line cross-disciplinary analog, pre-empt Recht and Agarwal explicitly, and never make the broad claim ("audits always shrink, we are the exception"), which is false.

## Candidate Contributions: The One Sentence Each Owns (A / C / D)

The test: for each candidate paper, the one sentence a reviewer could not obtain from any prior paper — or a statement that no such sentence exists.

**Candidate A — Repaired Measurement.** The one sentence A owns: *"In offline model-based optimization, under a single shared evaluation protocol, a crossed surrogate-class × optimizer factorial attributes performance variance overwhelmingly to surrogate class (η²_surrogate ≈ 0.37–0.405) and negligibly to optimizer class (η²_optimizer ≈ 0.01) — the first ANOVA-quantified surrogate-versus-optimizer attribution in the offline setting."* This is genuinely unobtainable elsewhere: PGS fixes the surrogate and cannot produce a number for its effect [33]; L/R/W fixes the optimizer as a nuisance variable, and a fixed-nuisance design is logically incapable of estimating the fixed factor's effect size [8]; Design-Bench and RaM bundle both axes [35][34]. A is therefore *under*-credited by any "L/R/W already did the surrogate comparison" objection.

Two caveats constrain it. The directional gloss "the field innovates on the axis that does not matter" overclaims, because surrogate-over-acquisition primacy is decade-old doctrine — Shahriari et al. state "the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic" [7]. But that doctrine governs *online* sequential BO and the acquisition-*function*-family choice (EI/PI/UCB), not the numerical optimizer/search axis (gradient ascent, hill-climbing, CMA-ES) in the *offline* setting. A therefore retains a genuine offline/optimizer-axis residual and can honestly claim to be the first offline-MBO-specific, ANOVA-quantified *falsification of PGS's own motivating premise* — the verbatim claim that "offline BBO has focused on improving surrogate models while using fixed search strategies" [33], which PGS advances to justify varying only the search policy while holding the surrogate fixed at a single MLP — rather than a refutation of field belief. And A must keep this design claim separate from the still-unresolved η²_optimizer = 0.01 magnitude, which is confounded by the unequal oracle budgets.

**Candidate C — Mechanism.** The sentence C would own, conditional on an experiment not yet run: *"Surrogate posterior-mean smoothness — not surrogate-class identity, uncertainty calibration, or architecture — is the causal axis of the GP-versus-ensemble performance gap, demonstrated by manipulating it in both directions."* Per N7 this is NONE FOUND across the entire BO/kernel-methods literature, so its ceiling is the highest of the three — "first at all." But two constraints make C a bet, not a deliverable. The bidirectional manipulation that earns the sentence is planned but unrun, so the sentence is owned only contingently. And C's easiest evidence channels are foreclosed: the β=0 control shows the gap survives σ removal (direction robust, magnitude unreconciled — see terminal section), which argues against a σ-mediated mechanism (N4), and the finite-width version is a category error the K-sweep cannot support (N5). C's viable mechanism — relocated by the N4/N5 convergence from σ to the posterior *mean* — is novel but conditional on running the manipulation, and it must still survive the NTK/spectral-bias objection that a jagged mean is an under-parameterization artifact. **As submitted, C's one sentence does not exist.**

**Candidate D — Confound Taxonomy.** The one sentence D owns: *"We identify five previously unreported, individually decisive confounds specific to offline-MBO surrogate comparison — target scaling, candidate/oracle-budget asymmetry, optimizer trust-region tuning, ensemble size K, and cross-class β–σ pessimism mismatch — give the protocol that removes them, and show that correcting the two measurement/protocol confounds (target scaling and the candidate/oracle protocol) does not shrink but *strengthens* the rewarded surrogate advantage (η²_surrogate 0.37 → 0.405), against the reality-check genre's shrink direction, even though the ensemble-size confound moves it the other way."* No prior paper owns this composition. The genre-shape is conceded to Henderson, Ferrari Dacrema, and Musgrave [2][3][4]; the individual principles behind N3 and N4 to Dewolf, Ghasemipour, SNGP, and Fan [11][23][12][13]; but the offline-MBO-specific five-confound set, the η² decomposition the genre has never used, and the strengthening direction no ML/CS reality-check has ever reported [5][6] are new in combination. D depends on no unrun experiment (the 0.405 strengthening has landed) and on no over-strong number.

The three candidates order cleanly across the dimensions a reviewer weighs:

| Candidate | Novelty ceiling | Evidence status now | Depends on unrun experiment? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** Repaired measurement | Moderate (first offline-MBO ANOVA attribution) | Design claim solid; η²_opt magnitude provisional | No for the design; yes for the magnitude | Under-credited but narrow — fold into D |
| **C** Mechanism | Highest ("first at all," N7) | σ-channel dead, K-evidence a category error | Yes (bidirectional manipulation) | A bet — reserve as a second paper |
| **D** Confound taxonomy | Compositional but genuine | Fully executed; 0.405 landed | No | **Recommended** |

**The strategic recommendation: lead with D, present A as D's quantitative core (with the optimizer null flagged as provisional), and hold C back until the manipulation is run.** The forward-looking stakes make this urgent rather than optional. The offline-MBO literature is accelerating toward exactly this territory — the IGNITE lineage already regularizes surrogate smoothness in one direction [18], ROOT extends the same group's smoothness-as-translation program [36], and the field's own survey is actively soliciting attribution studies [1] — so a paper that ships the crossed-factorial-plus-taxonomy contribution (D+A) now claims a real gap, whereas a paper that waits to ship the mechanism (C) risks being scooped on the taxonomy by a competitor who runs the bidirectional experiment first. Splitting them is not timidity; it is the only framing in which every sentence the paper asserts is one a reviewer cannot already find in the literature.

## The Three Strongest Rejecting Citations

Ranked by how much of the paper each can sink, and stated as the reviewer would state them.

**1. Li, Rudner and Wilson — ICLR 2024, arXiv:2305.20028 [8].** The single most dangerous citation, because it is the closest methodological analog and pre-empts three findings at once. A hostile reviewer would say: "The controlled cross-surrogate comparison for Bayesian optimization already exists, and it is more thorough than this — it compares seven surrogate classes, diagnoses the ensemble's underperformance via a basin-of-attraction/data-scarcity mechanism, finds ensemble performance *robust* to model count, and shows the ranking mostly holds under a smaller architecture." That threatens N2 (they found K-robustness), N5 (they partially address the finite-width objection with a smaller-net ablation — though never the wider net the objection predicts — via an experiment the paper did not run), and shaves the perceived size of the N6 gap. The defense is exact scoping — L/R/W is *online* BO with the acquisition fixed, a disjoint K range, no offline setting, no variance decomposition — but the scoping must be explicit and up front, because a reviewer who is one of these authors (a live possibility in the small BO-surrogate community) reads N2 and N5 as already answered. (Date trap: ICLR 2024, not the 2023 that back-propagates from the arXiv v1.)

**2. Shahriari, Swersky, Wang, Adams and de Freitas — Proc. IEEE 2016 [7].** The reversal-killer. If any part of the paper frames "surrogate matters more than optimizer" as overturning a field belief, this ~5,900-citation survey rejects it in one sentence: "the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic," restated in its conclusion as the acquisition's role being "secondary" [7]. The reviewer would say: "Your headline directional result is the organizing thesis of the most-cited BO survey ever written; it is a decade old." The defense is never to make the field-reversal claim, and to scope Shahriari precisely: his doctrine concerns *online* BO and the acquisition-*function*-family choice, not the numerical optimizer/search axis in offline MBO — so A's offline/optimizer-axis residual survives it. Narrow the claim to falsifying PGS's local premise (candidate A).

**3. Agarwal, Schwarzer, Castro, Courville and Bellemare — NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2108.13264 [22], with Demšar's rule of thumb.** The statistical-rigor rejecter, aimed at the Design-Bench null. Agarwal's thesis — "the lack of statistically significant results does not demonstrate the absence of effect," with Demšar's guideline of more than ten datasets for the very Friedman test the paper uses — means a seven-task, p=0.69 omnibus is under-powered and cannot license a positive equivalence claim. The reviewer would say: "With N=7 you are below the threshold for the test you ran; you have shown no *detectable* difference at this power, not equivalence." The defense converts the objection into a strength: present the result as "no detectable difference at this power," attach an explicit power/N specification, and carry N8's platform-variance finding as the *reason* the omnibus is null. This same pool holds the genre-establishing citation (Henderson [2], at the target venue) and the NTK cluster [14][29][30] that would sink candidate C, so a submission must pre-empt those too.

The tail worth naming so the paper forecloses it: **Henderson [2]** (the reality-check genre, at AAAI — concede the shape up front, as D does), the **Jacot/Lee/Rahaman cluster [14][29][30]** (kills any C framing leaning on the K-sweep), **Dewolf [11]** (reduces N3 to an application claim), and **Fan [13]** (reduces N4 to a naming claim). Citing all of these up front and scoping accordingly makes the paper far harder to reject.

## AAAI-27 Venue Fit and Reviewer Pool

The AAAI-27 areas-and-topics page lists, among the relevant subareas, `ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis`, `ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification`, `SO: Algorithm Configuration & Sampling-based Search`, and `RU: Stochastic Optimization`, under the standing rule that a submission is filed under the subarea of its *main* contribution so that an expert in that subarea evaluates it most effectively [40]. (The call sets abstracts due 21 July 2026, full papers 28 July 2026, and the conference 16–23 February 2027 in Montréal.) The reviewer pool a paper summons is therefore a direct function of which candidate it submits, and the mapping is a trap: whichever subarea each candidate selects summons the reviewers most primed to know its owning prior work.

- **Candidates D and A → primary `ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis`.** This summons the empirical-rigor and reproducibility pool — the readers of Henderson [2], Musgrave [4], Ferrari Dacrema [3], Lucic [21], and Agarwal [22] — reviewers whose priors *reward* a named confound set with a removal protocol and a variance decomposition, and to whom the strengthening direction reads as a notable result rather than an anomaly. This is the friendliest pool for the paper's actual strength and the recommended mapping. It is also the pool most likely to hold the Design-Bench null to Agarwal's standard, so a loosely-worded A does not survive it while a genre-aware D does. Cross-list secondary: `SO: Algorithm Configuration & Sampling-based Search`, which summons the AutoML/fANOVA pool (readers of Hutter [15]) who will immediately place the variance-decomposition move and expect the paper to cite it.

- **Candidate C → primary `ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification`.** This is the worst possible pool for the paper as currently constituted — the readership of SNGP [12], Fan [13], the NTK and spectral-bias theory [14][29][30], and above all L/R/W [8]. These are exactly the reviewers who catch the K-versus-width category error in one line, know that L/R/W's smaller-net ablation partially addresses the finite-width objection against the paper, and recognize the distance-aware and UCB-local-search mechanisms as owned. Submitting C here is submitting the mechanism claim to the precise audience holding every citation that undercuts it. The forward-looking implication is decision-relevant: because the Bayesian-learning subarea is where the surrogate-comparison literature actually lives and is growing, C's exposure will only increase over the next cycle as more of that pool internalizes [8]. If the paper insists on a mechanism framing, route it through the evaluation/benchmarking subarea and frame smoothness as an empirical confound to control (D's language), not as a causal claim to defend (C's) — the benchmarking pool is more forgiving of an unrun manipulation than the Bayesian-learning pool is of a category error.

## What I Could Not Verify and Why

Several load-bearing quantities rest on the paper's own internal artifacts rather than on independently verifiable primary literature; honesty about them is part of the audit's integrity.

- **The η²_surrogate 0.37 → 0.405 strengthening (N9, candidate D) is confirmed, not provisional.** It reflects an on-machine 30-seed run and is the paper's one solid, landed asset; the recommendation to build D on it is safe. (This was verified against repository commit metadata rather than by re-executing the run, so it is repo-asserted at the reproduction level — but the direction and magnitude are confirmed as landed.)

- **The exact β=0 control numbers (N4) are NOT VERIFIABLE.** The query cites the GP-ensemble gap moving 0.51 → 0.47 with the pessimism term removed; an independent recomputation from the results file via the paper's own normalization helpers gave 0.504 → 0.511 (flat-to-slightly-up), not the cited ~0.04 shrink. Both agree on the decision-relevant direction — the gap does not collapse when σ is removed, which undercuts a σ-mediated mechanism — but the magnitude is unreconciled. This limits only how hard N4's σ-rejection and candidate C's redirection toward mean-smoothness can lean; it does not change the N4 verdict.

- **The η²_optimizer = 0.01 magnitude (candidate A, the "reversal") is provisional.** It is confounded by the unequal oracle budgets (256 versus 128 proposals) and by a trust-region constraint on gradient ascent that suppresses the one failure mode (gradient collapse) that would make optimizer choice matter, and those confounds are not yet fully removed. Treat "no one has made this attribution before" (verified, and independent of the number) as separate from "and the number is 0.01" (not yet trustworthy). A owns the attribution *shape*, not yet the *magnitude* of the optimizer null.

- **MS-DDEO at the sentence level (N7) is NOT VERIFIABLE.** The paper is fully closed-access (403 on the publisher page; null abstract on Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex; not open per Unpaywall), so its rule-out rests on abstract-level paraphrase, not a verbatim grep [37]. The N7 verdict does not depend on it — MS-DDEO is a within-RBFN selection method that never touches a GP even on the paraphrase — but a reviewer with institutional access could surface a detail the paraphrase misses.

- **N9's scope boundary.** The NONE-FOUND verdict holds for ML/CS reality-check and reproducibility papers indexed by the academic APIs and web search as of this sweep. It does *not* extend to adjacent quantitative fields: the noncollapsibility and regression-attenuation literature in biostatistics establishes that covariate adjustment can mathematically increase an effect magnitude, so the phenomenon is not statistically surprising — it simply has not been instantiated on an ML/CS benchmark audit. Bressan [20] is psychology, not ML. If the paper generalizes N9 to "any empirical field," the search does not support it; the verified claim is narrowly "within ML/CS."

- **Forward-citation completeness (N7, N3).** Semantic Scholar was rate-limited (HTTP 429) throughout the sweeps, so forward-citation walks relied on OpenAlex and web search. Coverage of arXiv-indexed BO/kernel work is high, but non-arXiv workshop papers, theses, and non-English venues (relevant given MS-DDEO's swarm-and-evolutionary-computation home, where non-English DDEA/RBFN work may sit) were not exhaustively searched; a bidirectional smoothness manipulation buried in such a venue cannot be ruled out with arXiv-level confidence. Likewise, the conformal/calibration literature behind N3 was flagged but not exhaustively fetched — an abstract statement of the shared-multiplier principle divorced from any acquisition framing may exist there, which would leave N3 exactly as PARTIAL rather than changing the verdict.

- **The internal reproducibility of the K=2 spike (N2).** Whether the K=2 ensemble result is stable across seeds is an internal question about the audited experiment, not a literature question, and could not be adjudicated from the primary sources. If K=2 does not replicate cleanly, the "small-sample σ-noise" reading in N2 is correct and the observation should be reported cautiously; if it does replicate, it deserves a mechanistic explanation the current evidence cannot supply.
