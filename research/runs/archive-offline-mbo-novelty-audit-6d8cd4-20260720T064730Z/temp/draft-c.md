# Offline-MBO Novelty Audit — The Reconciled Verdict (Draft C)

The honest answer to "which of N1–N9 is genuinely new" is that **not one of the nine claims is fully unowned, and not one is fully owned** — every verdict is a boundary condition, and the paper's publishability depends entirely on scoping each claim to the exact residual the primary literature leaves open. The strongest defensible contribution is **not** the dramatic "the field innovates on the axis that doesn't matter" reversal, and **not** the mechanism story about surrogate smoothness. It is the mundane-sounding but genuinely unclaimed composite of Candidate **D**: a named set of five offline-MBO-specific confounds, a protocol that removes them, a two-way variance attribution nobody in offline MBO has ever computed, and a de-confounding *direction* (the corrected effect grows, η²_surr 0.37 → 0.405) that has no scalar-effect-size precedent inside machine learning. The reality-check *genre* is owned by Henderson [1]; the surrogate-over-optimizer *finding* is decades-old Bayesian-optimization doctrine per Shahriari [4]; and the field's own most recent survey [2] already names the surrogate-vs-optimizer attribution question as open — which is precisely why the specific, quantified, offline-MBO instantiation is still available to claim. The single most dangerous paper in the literature is Li/Rudner/Wilson [3], which independently pre-empts the *strength* of three separate claims (N2, N5, and the N6-adjacent design) without fully owning any of them.

Below: the verdict table first, then per-claim boundary conditions, the one sentence each candidate uniquely owns, the three citations most likely to reject the paper, the AAAI-27 venue mapping, and a terminal accounting of what I could not verify.

## N1 - Confound Taxonomy: Prior-Art Overlap and the Offline-MBO Residual

The *shape* of N1 — name unreported implementation choices, give a protocol that removes them, show the reward ranking changes — is a mature, accepted genre, and Henderson et al. own it most tightly [1]. Their thesis sentence is nearly a verbatim template for N1: *"We find that implementation differences which are often not reflected in publications can have dramatic impacts on performance"* [1], and they proceed exactly as the paper does — isolating five factors (network architecture/activation, reward scale, random seeds, environment dynamics, codebase) one at a time under otherwise-fixed settings and showing that each alone flips which method is reported as best. Agarwal et al. own a second instance of the same shape [5]: their Table 1 is an explicit three-row taxonomy (desideratum / current-protocol flaw / recommendation), and reapplying it reverses published rankings — *"our analysis suggests that DER may in fact be better than OTR, unlike what the reported point estimates suggest"* [5]. Ferrari Dacrema [6], Musgrave [7], and Lucic [8] round out the genre in recommendation, metric learning, and GANs respectively; Musgrave's *"the actual improvements over time have been marginal at best"* [7] and Lucic's *"most models can reach similar scores with enough hyperparameter optimization and random restarts"* [8] are the same audit move in different subfields.

The residual is therefore not the *idea* of a confound audit — conceding that genre to Henderson up front is what makes the rest credible. The residual is the *content*: (a) a two-way η² variance decomposition, which no reality-check paper in this lineage computes (Henderson reports training curves and t-tests, never an effect-size attribution of the form "fixing target scaling alone moves η²_surr 0.367 → 0.283"; Agarwal reports CI overlaps, never a fraction-of-variance-explained); and (b) four confounds that are specific to offline-MBO surrogate comparison and have no analogue in the RL/recommender/GAN audits — the ensemble regressing on raw targets spanning −2613 to +36 while both GPs z-scored; the candidate/oracle-budget asymmetry (two optimizers propose 256 designs and report the oracle-selected best 128, the third proposes 128); the ensemble-size K sweep; and the β-pessimism σ-scale mismatch. My committed reading: **N1 is PARTIAL — genre FOUND, offline-MBO confound set + η² accounting + net-up direction NONE FOUND.** The interpretive payoff the sources do not state is that conceding the shape is not a weakness but the *rhetorical engine* of the contribution: because the genre is so well established, a reviewer's prior is "audits shrink effects and expose weak baselines," and the offline-MBO residual is credible precisely because it violates that prior (see N9). The novelty lives in the composition, not in any single confound and not in the audit form.

## N2 - K-Contingency of the Surrogate-Class Ranking

N2 claims that the ensemble-versus-GP ranking is contingent on ensemble size K, and that the field-standard K=5 is where the ensemble is weakest. **Verdict: NONE FOUND — genuinely unreported — but pre-empted in strength by the single closest analog, and resting on a mechanism the paper cannot yet defend.** The decisive comparison is Li/Rudner/Wilson [3], who ran their own ensemble-size ablation inside a controlled cross-surrogate BO comparison and reached the *opposite* qualitative conclusion: *"We compare the behavior of ensembles with different numbers of models, and we find that the different ensembles perform similarly across many experiments, showing the robustness of our results to this hyperparameter"* [3]. Crucially, their sweep spans K∈{5,10}; the paper's flip is driven by K=2 (task-normalized ensemble score 0.95, exceeding the exact GP's 0.846), which [3] never tested. So the two findings are *consistent, not contradictory* — [3] shows flatness over {5,10}; the paper reports a jump at K=2 that lies below their tested range. The paper must present N2 as a genuine unreported observation, not as a refutation of [3], or a reviewer who knows [3] will read overclaiming.

The load-bearing caveat is that the paper's own K-sweep runs *backward* from the two nearest field baselines. Lakshminarayanan et al. — the very source the paper cites for "standard K=5" — report that as M increases, "both the accuracy and the quality of predictive uncertainty improve significantly" [10], i.e., ensemble quality is monotone *increasing* in K. The paper's numbers (0.95 / 0.52 / 0.32 / 0.18 at K=2/3/5/10) are monotone *decreasing*. A monotone decline against the direction predicted by both [10] and [3]'s robustness is most parsimoniously read not as a discovered surrogate-class law but as small-sample noise: the standard deviation across only two ensemble members is a very high-variance σ estimate, and a lucky K=2 draw feeding an LCB (μ − βσ) acquisition on a modest 7+7-task suite is a mundane statistical artifact. Abe et al. — the query's own "nearest known" — does not rescue N2 either: it shows a single larger model reproduces ensemble accuracy gains [9], but never against a GP and never as a ranking claim. My committed reading: **N2 is a real NONE FOUND, but the paper should claim only the observation, explicitly disclaim the mechanism as unresolved, and concede [3]'s robustness result as the reason a strong K-contingency claim would be discounted.**

## N3 - Unmatched Effective Pessimism

N3 says a shared β across surrogate classes with different σ magnitudes delivers different *effective* conservatism, so the LCB acquisition was never matched across the surrogates being compared. **Verdict: PARTIAL — the general statistical principle is owned; the offline-MBO acquisition-comparison instantiation is NONE FOUND.** Dewolf et al. own the general principle cleanly [11]: benchmarking four uncertainty-method classes (Bayesian, ensemble, direct-interval, conformal) at one shared nominal 90% coverage, they show *"the uncalibrated models do not approximately saturate the validity constraint. They either underestimate the uncertainty or produce overconservative prediction intervals"* [11], and they identify the standard fix as normalizing "the nonconformity measure by a dispersion function σ." That is N3's underlying mechanism — one fixed multiplier across differently-scaled uncertainty estimators does not yield matched effective conservatism — stated in general UQ form and demonstrated across method classes. But grep of the full body returns zero hits for "acquisition," "Bayesian optimization," "pessimis," or "lower confidence bound" [11]: it never crosses into a sequential-decision or acquisition-function setting, and its framing is the *opposite* of N3's (it shows how to *repair* the shared-threshold problem per-method, not how an un-repaired shared β miscalibrates a cross-class ranking during optimization).

The two nearest optimization-flavored analogs both fall short in the other direction. Ghasemipour et al. own the *shape* "nominal ≠ effective pessimism" — *"shared pessimistic targets can paradoxically lead to value estimates that are effectively optimistic"* [12] — but the mechanism is target-independence within one Q-ensemble across Bellman backups, not σ-scale mismatch across surrogate classes (0 hits for "Gaussian process" or "surrogate" [12]). Srinivas et al.'s canonical GP-UCB β_t schedule is intrinsically single-surrogate: every argument to β_t is a property of one evolving GP posterior, and the paper's own comparisons hold one GP fixed while varying the acquisition rule — the transpose of N3's axis [13]. My committed reading: **N3 is PARTIAL, and the honest claim is narrow but real — the general "shared multiplier miscalibrates differently-scaled estimators" fact belongs to the conformal/UQ literature (Dewolf [11]), while its extension to comparing surrogate *classes* under one shared β inside an LCB acquisition in offline MBO is unclaimed.** SNGP [14] actually strengthens the premise: because GP-RBF σ is distance-aware and ensemble σ is not, the two classes' σ outputs are differently *shaped*, not merely differently *scaled* — a stronger form of incomparability than N3 asserts.

## N4 - Distance-Aware Uncertainty and the Implicit Trust Region

N4 splits into two claims that the evidence separates cleanly. **Claim (a) — "GP posterior variance grows away from data while standard ensembles do not" — is PRIOR WORK FOUND and cleanly owned.** SNGP formalizes "input distance awareness" and proves the GP-RBF posterior variance *"increases monotonically toward 1 as x* moves further away from X_IND"* while showing ensembles are governed by distance-to-decision-boundary instead [14]; DUQ confirms it empirically on two-moons — *"Deep Ensembles is uncertain only along the decision boundary, and certain elsewhere"* [15]. **Claim (b) — "that variance growth, through an LCB, is why the GP wins in offline MBO, making the acquisition an implicit trust region" — is where the novelty and the danger both live.** The causal mechanism itself is now PARTIAL rather than NONE FOUND, because Fan et al. — outside the SNGP/DUQ/DUE/TuRBO set the earlier passes checked — derive it formally for a confidence-bound acquisition: *"minimizing UCB can be viewed as local strategy"* [16], grounded in the bound being small near sampled points and growing away from them. What remains genuinely unclaimed is only the *label*: Fan calls it "local strategy," never "trust region" (0 hits for "implicit"), so "implicit trust region" as a name for a bare LCB in offline MBO is a novel framing move, not a novel mechanism.

Two scope facts matter. First, the DUE caveat — that a GP output layer does not guarantee variance growth if an unconstrained neural feature extractor causes "feature collapse" [17] — does *not* apply to this paper, whose exact GP and SVGP use vanilla Matérn kernels directly on raw inputs with no feature extractor; DUE's caveat is deep-kernel-specific and, if anything, implies the paper's kernels are the *safe* case SNGP already proves has the property. This scope catch holds in the paper's favor. Second, and cutting the other way, TuRBO is direct evidence *against* claim (b) being automatic: the one paper actually about trust-region BO built an *explicit* hyperrectangle with a success/failure counter precisely because unchecked global variance growth under a myopic acquisition causes "overemphasized exploration" — a problem to counter, not a free mechanism [18]; it uses Thompson sampling, not LCB (0 hits for "LCB" [18]). And the paper's own β=0 control undercuts the σ-mediated story from the inside: with the σ term removed entirely the GP-ensemble gap barely moves, which is the opposite of what a σ-mediated mechanism predicts. A reviewer must also not be handed Ovadia [19] as support for "ensembles are confidently wrong far from data" — Ovadia reports ensembles are among the *best* estimators under shift and operationalizes "far" as corruption severity, not spatial distance, so citing it here invites a mis-citation flag. My committed reading: **N4 is PARTIAL — cite SNGP/DUQ/Fan for real, established distance-awareness as background; claim only the "implicit trust region" naming and the offline-MBO instantiation as novel; and, because the β=0 control kills the σ channel, relocate the paper's actual mechanism from posterior *variance* to posterior *mean smoothness*.** That relocation is the hinge between N4 and N5: both the β=0 result and the K-sweep point at the mean, not σ — which is candidate C's only surviving mechanism, and the one it has not yet demonstrated.

## N5 - NTK / Spectral Bias and the Finite-Width Objection

N5 is the strongest theoretical objection to a mechanism paper, and it is important to state precisely what the theory does and does not license. Infinite-width networks converge to Gaussian processes (Jacot's NTK result [20]; Lee's NNGP equivalence, with finite-width accuracy that "approaches that of the corresponding GP with increasing layer width" [21]) and finite networks are spectrally biased toward smooth, low-frequency functions (Rahaman [22]). Jacot even shows empirically that wider networks yield a smoother, lower-variance tangent kernel [20]. So the adversarial reading — "the ensemble's jagged posterior mean is a finite-width / under-training artifact, not a surrogate-class property" — is a *real* threat that a mechanism (C) paper must engage. **Verdict: the finite-width objection is PRIOR WORK (owned by [20][21][22]); the paper cannot claim novelty here, and the specific evidence it currently offers for its mechanism is invalid.**

The critical correction is that the paper's K-sweep does not evidence the finite-width story, because K (ensemble cardinality) and n (per-member width) are different variables. NTK and spectral-bias theory are width statements; none of [20], [21], or [22] so much as mentions ensembles (0 hits each). Increasing K does not widen any member — it averages more equally-narrow functions — so "K=2 ≫ K=5 proves a width artifact" is an unsupported bridging inference that a Bayesian-deep-learning reviewer will catch instantly. Worse for the K-as-width reading, the sweep's *direction* contradicts both Lakshminarayanan's monotone-improvement-with-M [10] and [3]'s K-robustness. And the objection is *partially answered* by a K-fixed experiment already in the literature: Li/Rudner/Wilson's Appendix D.1.2 re-runs the full surrogate comparison (including the K=5 ensemble) at a much smaller architecture (width 128→50, depth 3→2, tanh→ReLU) and reports *"even with the smaller architecture, we find that the relative performance of the surrogate models mostly remains consistent. We still find that HMC often outperforms other approximate inference methods such as deep ensembles"* [3]. That is evidence *against* "ensemble weakness is a fixable finite-width artifact," at least across the one alternate size tested. My committed reading: **N5 is PARTIAL and cuts against candidate C — the theory is owned, the K-sweep is a K≠width category error, and [3]'s D.1.2 already runs a confounded version of the decisive experiment.** The paper's correct move is to (i) never present the K-sweep as finite-width evidence, (ii) name the missing experiment as a clean single-factor width ablation holding K fixed, and (iii) note that the leading domain-matched alternative to a width story is data-scarcity-driven diversity collapse (Fort's mode-diversity mechanism [23]; [3]'s own basin-of-attraction diagnosis for small-data BO), which predicts landscape *smoothness*, not jaggedness — the opposite of N5's surface framing.

## N6 - The Crossed Surrogate x Optimizer Factorial

This is the load-bearing novelty claim, and it survives the hardest adversarial pass. **Verdict: NONE FOUND — the strongest clean negative in the audit.** The field's own most recent, peer-reviewed survey certifies the gap as open one AAAI cycle before the paper: existing benchmarks emphasize overall performance *"without clarifying whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance"* [2]. That is an active certification, not mere silence. Every candidate counterexample fixes one axis or bundles both: Li/Rudner/Wilson cross surrogate classes with acquisition *held fixed* — *"We use Monte-Carlo based Expected Improvement… as our acquisition function for all problems"* — and are online BO, not offline MBO (0 hits for "offline," "factorial," "crossed," "optimizer") [3]; PGS varies only the search policy while holding the surrogate at one MLP [28]; Tan varies five surrogates with gradient ascent fixed [27]; Design-Bench compares pre-coupled surrogate+optimizer bundles (BO-qEI is the only GP baseline and is never re-paired with CMA-ES/REINFORCE) [29].

The two nearest structural analogs must be named pre-emptively, because a reviewer will reach for them. Hutter's fANOVA is the closest "model class as a variance factor" result — *"the most important hyperparameter was the model class used"* — but it is a one-way decomposition over the Auto-WEKA configuration space computed from data gathered by *one fixed search method* (SMAC), never crossing model class against a second, independently varied optimizer axis [25]. Liang et al. is the closest *structural* match — a genuinely crossed grid of surrogate models × acquisition functions (RF, five GP kernels, GP-ARD × EI/PI/LCB_λ) across five materials domains — but it reports only descriptive "acceleration/enhancement" metrics and qualitative ranking, with *zero* ANOVA/η²/variance-decomposition statistics anywhere in the paper, and it is online, closed-loop experimental BO, not offline MBO [26]. My committed reading: **N6 is NONE FOUND, and the precise, defensible residual is "a crossed surrogate-class × optimizer-class factorial with a two-way ANOVA / η² decomposition, in offline MBO."** One counterexample would be fatal; none exists across the five named candidates plus the fANOVA/AutoML literature. The interpretive point the sources do not make: N6's cleanness is exactly why the paper's *number* (η²_opt) becomes the vulnerability — the design is unowned, but the finding it produces is only as trustworthy as the de-confounded number, which is still provisional (see the terminal section).

## N7 - Bidirectional Smoothness Manipulation

N7 is the highest-ceiling claim: has anyone manipulated surrogate smoothness in *both* directions — smooth the network and roughen the GP — to identify smoothness as the causal axis of a surrogate-class performance gap? **Verdict: NONE FOUND at the broadest scope searched (offline MBO, Bayesian optimization, kernel methods, GP regression) — "first at all," not merely "first in offline MBO."** Three half-literatures each do part of the job and none combines them. Within-GP smoothness manipulation is common (length-scale sweeps), and cross-surrogate-class comparison (GP vs. NN ensemble) is common, but no paper does both plus causal attribution. IGNITE regularizes only neural-network sharpness, always downward, never touching a GP — *"reducing surrogate sharpness on the offline dataset provably reduces its generalized sharpness on unseen data"* [30]. The closest genuine cross-class hit, Lim et al., compares GP against a neural-network ensemble and offers a smoothness-flavored explanation for the GP's eventual win — the win *"could be due to the ability of GP to smoothly map out the uncertainty manifolds"* [31] — but its only deliberate manipulation is *widening* the GP's own length-scale bounds for hyperparameter fit (never a deliberately roughened arm), the NN ensemble receives no smoothness manipulation at all, and the attribution is an explicitly hedged post-hoc hypothesis ("could be due to," "probably enables"), not a controlled ablation. Ziomek's Length-scale Balancing is the closest lexical match to "vary smoothness" but pools only GPs (one incidental "neural network" hit, unrelated) [33], and MS-DDEO selects among preset RBFN smoothness tiers but is closed-access, so its precise design is NOT VERIFIABLE at the sentence level [32].

My committed reading: **N7 is a genuine NONE FOUND at "first at all" scope — the strongest novelty ceiling in the audit — but it is entirely contingent on an experiment the paper has not run.** The absence is structurally informative rather than a search artifact: the two half-literatures each exist in volume, yet in roughly fifteen years of BO/GP work nobody stitched them into a paired rough-the-GP / smooth-the-net causal design. The paper should cite Lim [31] as the nearest prior approach (naming its shortfall: correlational, one-directional, NN un-manipulated) and the IGNITE/Dao lineage [30] as the nearest prior *causal-attribution* framing (naming its shortfall: NN-only). But the novelty is conditional: until the bidirectional manipulation is executed, no sentence of the form "smoothness is *the* causal axis, demonstrated in both directions" is earned. This is the finding that makes C a bet rather than a contribution.

## N8 - Platform and Library-Version Dependence of Benchmark Results

N8 observes an ensemble cell moving ~0.5 normalized units between macOS and Linux on TF-Bind-8 while the exact-GP cell matches to 1.000 and every synthetic cell reproduces to twelve decimals — i.e., platform variance of the same order as the method differences under study. **Verdict: PARTIAL — the phenomenon "execution-environment variance ≈ method-difference variance" is owned in deep RL; the offline-MBO setting, the specific macOS-vs-Linux axis, and a stated same-order ratio are the residual.** Henderson demonstrates it for codebases: swapping only the implementation of an identical published algorithm produces return spreads (DDPG on HalfCheetah, roughly 0–5000) on the same scale as inter-algorithm effects the paper elsewhere finds non-significant — *"implementation differences which are often not reflected in publications can have dramatic impacts on performance"* [1]. Nagarajan isolates the cleaner analog: with *all* seeds, initialization, and hyperparameters held identical, enabling nondeterministic GPU operations alone produces variance whose *"learning curve looks similar to the curves for exploration and weight initialization"* [34], concluding *"even with a fixed implementation run under identical experimental conditions… a single source of nondeterminism can substantially impact results"* [34]. Gundersen & Kjensmo supply only the conceptual scaffold — their R1/R2/R3 taxonomy separates hardware/implementation noise from method signal, and they warn that *"differences in software and hardware could have significant impact on results because of rounding errors in floating point arithmetic"* — but they quantify nothing and borrow even that caveat from a climate-model paper [35].

My committed reading: **N8 is PARTIAL, and the residual is genuine but modest.** The phenomenon is not new to ML reproducibility; what is unclaimed is its instantiation in offline MBO, on a *cross-operating-system* axis (Henderson and Nagarajan study codebase and GPU-op nondeterminism on one machine, never macOS-vs-Linux), and framed as a same-order ratio against the specific method differences the benchmark is trying to resolve. The interpretive beat the sources leave implicit: N8 is what makes the Design-Bench null (Friedman p=0.69) *coherent* rather than merely weak — if platform noise on the ensemble cell is the same order as the between-cell differences, then the grid being within one critical difference is exactly what N8 predicts. That reframes the null from "the methods are equivalent" (an over-strong equivalence claim, and underpowered at N=7 tasks) to "no difference is detectable at this power given this much environment noise," which is the honest and defensible statement.

## N9 - The De-Confounding Direction: an Audit That Strengthens

N9 is a rhetorical asset if — and only if — it is scoped to the narrow scalar-effect-size shape. **Verdict: NONE FOUND within ML/CS for the specific claim "a confound-controlled audit whose corrected scalar effect-size statistic exceeds its own published value," across sixteen adversarial queries in two independent sweeps; but the broad claim "reality-checks always shrink, we are the exception" is false and must pre-empt two ML/CS partials.** Every canonical ML/CS reality-check runs the other way: Melis states outright that *"effect sizes tend to be much smaller"* once tuning is controlled [36]; Robinson/Glen/Lee reverse a deep-learning-beats-SVM claim toward "SVM competitive," a clean shrink with zero confound-removal framing [37]; Musgrave [7], Lucic [8], and Ferrari Dacrema [6] all shrink or nullify. No ML/CS paper reports a corrected variance-explained statistic exceeding its published value, which is exactly the shape of η²_surr 0.37 → 0.405.

But two ML/CS partials are close enough to sink an unscoped claim. Recht et al. is a two-track result: absolute accuracy drops 3–15%, yet the model-to-model slope exceeds 1 ("increased advantage over earlier models," "the opposite of the standard overfitting scenario") — a *relative* growth statistic, not a de-confounded scalar, but rhetorically adjacent and, given Recht's prominence, one a reviewer will know [38]. Agarwal supplies a second near-miss: more runs reveal that *"the improvement is significant"* where point estimates had shown none [5] — power-driven, not confound-driven, but still "audit reveals an understated effect." The closest full-shape precedent is outside ML entirely: Bressan's *"the results came out stronger"* under a stricter specification [39], but it is psychology, reports p-values not an effect size, and frames the finding as restoration of significance. My committed reading: **N9 is a real, narrowly-scoped NONE FOUND that the paper should name explicitly — no ML/CS confound-removal audit has produced a scalar effect-size statistic larger than its own published value — while conceding Recht's relative-slope growth [38] and Agarwal's power-revealed effect [5] as the nearest partials and citing Bressan [39] as a one-line cross-disciplinary analog.** The underlying statistical mechanism (covariate adjustment can increase an estimate's magnitude — noncollapsibility, attenuation correction) is well understood, so the novelty is not that the direction is *surprising* but that it is *unprecedented as an ML benchmark-audit instance* — a distinction the paper must draw itself, before a reviewer draws it against them.

## Candidate Contributions: The One Sentence Each Owns (A / C / D)

The reconciliation across all nine claims resolves the three candidates into a clean ordering: **D is the safe, fully-executed core; A is under-credited but narrow and number-dependent; C has the highest ceiling and the weakest current evidence.**

**Candidate A — Repaired Measurement.** A's uniquely owned sentence exists, and it is narrower than the paper's instinct: *"In offline model-based optimization, a controlled crossed surrogate-class × optimizer-class factorial with a two-way ANOVA attributes performance variance overwhelmingly to surrogate class rather than to optimizer/search-strategy choice — the first such quantified attribution in the offline-MBO setting."* No offline-MBO paper crosses both axes (N6); L/R/W fixes the optimizer as a nuisance variable [3], PGS fixes the surrogate [28], and a fixed-nuisance design is logically incapable of producing an effect-size claim about the fixed variable — so A is genuinely *under-credited* by any "L/R/W already showed this" reading. What A does **not** own is the dramatic framing: Shahriari's survey already holds, as decade-old online-BO doctrine, that *"the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic"* [4], so A confirms field doctrine and can at most falsify PGS's *local* motivating premise, not "the field's belief." A's owned sentence is also contingent on the η²_opt number surviving de-confounding, which it has not yet done.

**Candidate C — Mechanism.** C's would-be sentence is the most exciting and the only one not currently earned: *"Surrogate posterior-mean smoothness — not surrogate-class identity, uncertainty calibration, or architecture — is the causal axis of the GP-versus-ensemble performance gap, demonstrated by manipulating it bidirectionally (smoothing the network and roughening the GP)."* At the scope of the entire BO/kernel-methods literature this sentence is unclaimed (N7, "first at all" [30][31]) — the highest novelty ceiling available. But three findings converge to make it a bet rather than a contribution: the σ-channel is dead (the β=0 control leaves the gap intact, N4), the K-sweep evidence is a category error (K≠width, N5), and the manipulation that would earn N7's novelty is planned but unrun. **No such sentence is currently owned, because the demonstrating experiment does not yet exist.** C's mechanism, correctly relocated from σ to mean-smoothness by the N4/N5 convergence, is viable and novel — but only conditional on running the bidirectional manipulation, and it must survive the NTK/spectral-bias objection (N5) that a jagged mean is an under-parameterization artifact.

**Candidate D — Confound Taxonomy.** D owns the sentence that is both unclaimed and fully executed: *"Five specific, individually decisive, previously-unreported confounds in offline-MBO surrogate comparison — target scaling, candidate/oracle-budget asymmetry, optimizer tuning, ensemble size K, and β-pessimism σ-scale mismatch — a shared protocol that removes them, and the demonstration that removing them moves the rewarded surrogate ranking and nets the effect UP (η²_surr 0.37 → 0.405) rather than down."* The genre-shape is conceded to Henderson [1] up front; the residual — the offline-MBO confound *set* (N1, N3), the two-way η² attribution (N6), and the strengthening *direction* (N9, unclaimed in ML/CS) — is a composition no single paper owns. D depends on no unrun experiment (the 0.405 strengthening has landed) and on no over-strong number (it does not require η²_opt to be trustworthy, only that the confound-removal moves the ranking). My committed recommendation: **the strongest publishable contribution is D**, with A as its defensible narrower sibling (fold A's ANOVA attribution into D as one of the five confounds' payoffs), and C reserved as a high-risk follow-up contingent on the bidirectional manipulation.

## The Three Strongest Rejecting Citations

**1. Li/Rudner/Wilson, ICLR 2024 [3] — the single most dangerous paper.** It is the closest methodological analog in existence (a controlled cross-surrogate BO comparison) and it independently pre-empts three of the paper's claims at once. Against N2, it found ensemble-size *robustness*, not contingency [3]. Against N5/C, its Appendix D.1.2 shows the ensemble-vs-GP ranking survives a large architecture reduction, undercutting "ensemble weakness is a fixable finite-width artifact" [3]. Against N6, it is the nearest crossed-surrogate design (transpose: acquisition fixed). A reviewer who knows this paper can argue "the ensemble underperformance, its robustness to ensemble size, and its robustness to architecture are already established — what is new?" The defense is scope (online BO, acquisition fixed, no offline MBO, no two-way ANOVA), but the paper must engage [3] explicitly and repeatedly or it will look under-read.

**2. Shahriari et al., Proc. IEEE 2016 [4] — kills the reversal rhetoric.** Its thesis — *"the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic"* [4] — means the paper's headline (surrogate ≫ optimizer) confirms decade-old BO doctrine rather than overturning a field belief. Any framing of A as "the field innovates on the axis that doesn't matter" invites this citation as a rebuttal. The defense is to narrow the claim to falsifying PGS's local premise in the specific offline-MBO setting, never "the field's belief."

**3. Jacot [20] / Lee [21] / Rahaman [22] — the NTK/spectral-bias cluster kills C's mechanism.** Infinite-width nets are GPs and finite nets are spectrally biased toward smooth functions, so a jagged ensemble mean is plausibly an under-parameterization/under-training artifact, not a surrogate-class property — and the paper's K-sweep does not evidence otherwise, because K is not width. A Bayesian-deep-learning reviewer will raise this against any mechanism (C) paper and will not accept the K-sweep as a rebuttal. The only adequate defense is the clean width ablation the paper has not run.

An honorable-mention fourth, relevant if the paper leans on the Design-Bench null: **Agarwal, NeurIPS 2021 [5]** — "the lack of statistically significant results does not demonstrate the absence of effect," and Demšar's own rule of thumb is N>10 datasets. At N=7 tasks the Friedman p=0.69 is underpowered, so it cannot support a positive equivalence claim; it must be stated as "no difference detectable at this power."

The candidate framing dictates the primary keyword, and therefore the reviewer pool — which is the highest-leverage submission decision.

- **Candidates D and A → primary `ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis`.** The main contribution of D/A is a reproducibility/benchmark-audit finding (named confounds, a removal protocol, a variance attribution, a cross-platform reproduction), which per AAAI's rule summons empirical-rigor and benchmarking specialists — reviewers whose priors *reward* reality-check contributions and are least likely to reject on "no new method." This is the friendliest pool for the paper's actual strength. Cross-list secondary: `SO: Algorithm Configuration & Sampling-based Search` (to reach the black-box-optimization community that owns the surrogate/optimizer vocabulary) and optionally `ML: AutoML & Hyperparameter Tuning` (for the platform/tuning-confound angle, N8).

- **Candidate C → primary `ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification`.** If the paper's core claim is the smoothness mechanism, it must face the BO/UQ theorists who own SNGP [14], Fan [16], and the distance-awareness literature — and who will demand the bidirectional manipulation (N7) and know the NTK/spectral-bias objection (N5) cold. This is a higher bar and a higher reward. Cross-list: `SO: Algorithm Configuration & Sampling-based Search`, and `ML: Deep Learning Theory & Learning Theory` if the smoothness argument is made via spectral bias (to summon a reviewer who can adjudicate the K≠width point in the paper's favor rather than against it). `RU: Stochastic Optimization` is a plausible alternative primary only if the framing emphasizes the stochastic-optimization formulation over the learning-surrogate framing.

The reviewer-pool implication is the decisive strategic point: **routing the paper as D/A to the benchmarking pool aligns the contribution with reviewers predisposed to value it, whereas routing it as C to the UQ/BO-theory pool exposes it to exactly the experts most equipped to demand the unrun experiment and press the finite-width objection.** Given that D is fully executed and C is a bet, the venue mapping reinforces the recommendation: submit D/A to `ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis`, cross-listed to `SO: Algorithm Configuration & Sampling-based Search`.

## What I Could Not Verify and Why

Several load-bearing items rest on internal repository state I did not independently re-run, and several verdicts are bounded by search coverage rather than by proof:

- **The exact β=0 control numbers (N4).** The query cites 0.51 → 0.47; an independent quick reproduction from the repository's own results gave 0.504 → 0.511 (flat-to-slightly-up). Both point the same qualitative direction (the gap survives σ removal), but the magnitude discrepancy is unresolved, and I cannot certify either figure. The decision-relevant conclusion (σ is not the mediator; relocate C's mechanism to the posterior mean) holds under both.

- **The de-confounded η²_opt reversal magnitude (A).** The near-zero optimizer effect (η²_opt ≈ 0.01) is itself confounded by the unequal oracle/candidate budgets (256 vs. 128 proposals) and by a trust-region constraint on gradient ascent that suppresses the one failure mode (gradient collapse) that would make optimizer choice matter. Until those are equalized, the "optimizer barely matters" number is provisional. A's owned sentence is safe as a *design* claim (first crossed factorial with ANOVA in offline MBO) but not yet as a *magnitude* claim. Note: the η²_surr 0.37 → 0.405 strengthening (D, N9) is on a different footing — it has reportedly landed via an on-machine 30-seed run — but I verified this only against repository commit metadata, not by re-executing the run, so I flag it as repo-asserted rather than independently reproduced.

- **N7 at "any ML field" scope.** The NONE-FOUND verdict is exhaustive within BO/kernel-methods/GP-regression (academic APIs + web sweeps, structurally explained absence), but I did not exhaustively search NTK/spectral-bias-adjacent deep-learning theory, robotics/control BO variants, or non-English venues (notably the Chinese/Japanese evolutionary-computation journals where MS-DDEO sits). A bidirectional design in one of those adjacent literatures cannot be ruled out with the same confidence.

- **MS-DDEO at the sentence level (N7).** The paper is closed-access; its precise smoothness-selection design is characterized from the abstract only, so any claim that it does *not* perform bidirectional cross-class manipulation is NOT VERIFIABLE at the primary-source level.

- **N3 outside the BO/bandit calibration literature.** The NONE-FOUND verdict is high-confidence for the classic GP-UCB β_t lineage and for papers that explicitly compare uncertainty-estimator classes, but the conformal/calibration literature was flagged and not exhaustively fetched; an abstract statistical statement of "two differently-scaled confidence sets cannot share one multiplier without recalibration," divorced from any acquisition framing, may exist there and would leave N3 exactly as PARTIAL (general principle owned, acquisition application unclaimed) rather than changing the verdict.

- **N9 forward-citation coverage.** I did not run a forward-citation sweep of Recht [38] to check whether any third party already characterizes its slope>1 result as an "audit strengthens" instance; if such a framing is conventional, the distance between N9 and Recht narrows. Semantic Scholar was rate-limited (HTTP 429) throughout, so citation-graph routes were partially unavailable; the negative verdicts rest on OpenAlex + arXiv + web coverage.

- **Ferrari Dacrema's arXiv identifier** was not confirmed in the sources I read; I cite it by venue and year only to avoid asserting an identifier I did not verify.

The through-line of these limits is consistent with the reconciled verdict: the claims that are *fully verifiable and unowned right now* (D's confound set, the N6 factorial design, the N9 direction) are the ones the recommendation privileges; the claims that are *unowned but unverifiable or unrun* (C's mechanism via N7, A's magnitude via η²_opt) are exactly the ones flagged as bets.
