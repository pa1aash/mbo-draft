# Interim report: k-and-finite-width-artifact-vs-class-property

**Locus question:** Is the ensemble's underperformance a K/finite-width/capacity artifact (favouring candidate D, taxonomy) or an intrinsic surrogate-class property (favouring candidate C, mechanism)? Reconcile our K-flip with Li/Rudner/Wilson's K-robustness and with NTK/spectral-bias theory.

**Flavor:** dialectical

## What the corpus already said

The width sweep already did substantial work here. Li/Rudner/Wilson (L/R/W, ICLR 2024) — the single closest methodological analog, a controlled cross-surrogate BO comparison — ran their own ensemble-size ablation and found the **opposite qualitative conclusion** from the audited paper: "We compare the behavior of ensembles with different numbers of models, and we find that the different ensembles perform similarly across many experiments, showing the robustness of our results to this hyperparameter" (Fig. A.7 caption, [[a-study-of-bayesian-neural-network-surrogates-for-bayesian-optimization-iclr-202]]). Abe et al. (NeurIPS 2022), the query's own "nearest known" for N2, never touches Gaussian processes as a competing surrogate class and never sweeps ensemble size against a cross-class ranking — 0 hits for "rank," "Bayesian optimization," "acquisition function" ([[deep-ensembles-work-but-are-they-necessary-neurips-2022-arxiv220206985]]). The three canonical NTK/spectral-bias theory papers are all already in the vault and pre-read: Jacot et al. prove infinite-width nets are GPs and show empirically that "for the wider network, the NTK shows less variance and is smoother" ([[180607572-neural-tangent-kernel-convergence-and-generalization-in-neural-network]]); Lee et al. (2018) show finite-width trained-network accuracy "approaches that of the corresponding GP with increasing layer width" ([[deep-neural-networks-as-gaussian-processes-iclr-2018-arxiv171100165]]); Rahaman et al. show width helps a network fit high frequencies but the effect is "considerably weaker" than depth ([[on-the-spectral-bias-of-neural-networks-icml-2019-arxiv180608734]]). The vault's own miss-catcher search log independently re-ran the N2 sweep and reached the same **NONE FOUND** verdict, explicitly flagging that "Li/Rudner/Wilson's own controlled ensemble-size ablation did NOT find a large K-dependent swing in performance, which weakens (or at least complicates) using 'K=2 scores 3x K=5' as clean evidence for the width/under-training-artifact story" ([[miss-catcher-search-log-n2-n3-n6-n9-n1-residual]]).

## What the new sources say

**Fort, Hu & Lakshminarayanan, "Deep Ensembles: A Loss Landscape Perspective" (arXiv:1912.02757)** — fetched and full-text-grepped this pass ([[deep-ensembles-a-loss-landscape-perspective-arxiv191202757]]). This is the DeepMind group's own follow-up explaining *why* ensembles work, and it locates the mechanism entirely in **loss-landscape mode diversity from random initialization** — a mechanism that has zero textual connection to width or capacity (0 hits for "width," 0 hits for "capacity" in 875 lines of full text). Their central claim: "random initializations explore entirely different modes, while functions along an optimization trajectory or sampled from the subspace thereof cluster within a single mode predictions-wise" (Abstract). This is a genuinely distinct axis from the NTK/spectral-bias width story — it explains member-to-member *disagreement*, not the smoothness of any one member's function.

**Lee, Xiao, Schoenholz et al., "Wide Neural Networks of Any Depth Evolve as Linear Models Under Gradient Descent" (NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1902.06720)** — fetched and full-text-grepped this pass ([[wide-neural-networks-of-any-depth-evolve-as-linear-models-under-gradient-descent]]). This is the direct empirical stress-test of the Jacot/Lee-2018 theory on practically-sized networks (widths tested from n=32 up to n=8192). Two findings are load-bearing and cut against a clean width-fixes-it story. First, on generality: "Some layers of modern neural networks may be operating far from the linearized regime... it is shown that the comparison of performance between finite- and infinite-width networks is **highly architecture-dependent**... It is still an **open research question** to determine the main factors that determine these performance gaps" (Discussion). Second, and more directly on point for offline MBO's small-data setting: "GPs **still outperform trained neural networks for both small and large dataset size**" even as width grows toward the infinite limit (Discussion, citing Lee et al.'s own earlier NNGP work). Offline MBO / BO datasets are exactly the small-data regime L/R/W independently characterize as "rarely exceed[ing] about 600 data points."

**Li/Rudner/Wilson's own Appendix D.3 mechanism (re-read in full this pass, beyond what the width sweep quoted)** — the exact wording is important and was not fully surfaced before: "With minimal training data, the loss landscape is relatively smooth, and separately-trained models are less diverse... in the low data regime there are fewer settings of parameters that give rise to easily discoverable basins of attraction, making it harder to find diverse solutions simply by re-initializing optimization runs" (Appendix D.3, Fig. A.6 caption and surrounding text). They also cite a second, independent line of evidence for the same conclusion: "These findings are also supported by results shown in Dai et al. (2022), where deep ensembles do not perform well in Bayesian optimization because they are unable to explore the space effectively." And crucially, their own hyperparameter-sensitivity study across architecture, training-data amount, and ensemble count concludes "these hyperparameters generally have minimal effects on the performance" (Sec. 4.1) — directly undercutting an ensemble-*size* explanation of any kind, width-mediated or not.

## Evidence synthesis

There is a **conflation buried in the N5 argument as stated in the query** that the corpus evidence surfaces clearly: NTK and spectral-bias theory govern per-member network **width** (n, the number of hidden units), not ensemble **cardinality** (K, the number of members averaged). Jacot, Lee (2018), and Rahaman are unanimous and explicit that their width-vs-smoothness results are about a single network's parameter count — none of the three so much as mentions "ensemble" (0 hits each, confirmed by grep in the width-sweep notes). Increasing K does not make any individual ensemble member wider; it only changes how many independently-trained, equally-narrow functions are averaged. So "our K=2≫K=5 is evidence FOR [the width-artifact] reading" is not a direct entailment from NTK/spectral-bias theory — it requires an unstated bridging assumption (that more members somehow approximates more width, or that fewer members happen to land closer to the smooth GP limit) that no source in this locus supplies or tests.

Once that conflation is separated out, the K-sweep's own **direction** becomes the more interesting evidence, and it point the wrong way for a clean artifact story. If jaggedness in individual undertrained members were the problem and averaging washed it out, more members (higher K) should, if anything, move the ensemble mean *toward* smoother, more GP-like behavior — consistent with Lakshminarayanan's own finding that "as M increases, both the accuracy and the quality of predictive uncertainty improve significantly" ([[simple-and-scalable-predictive-uncertainty-estimation-using-deep-ensembles-neuri]]), and consistent with L/R/W's flat K=5-vs-10 result. The audited paper's own numbers run backward relative to both of the two closest empirical analogs in the field: 0.95 / 0.52 / 0.32 / 0.18 at K = 2 / 3 / 5 / 10 is a **monotonic decline** with more members, the opposite sign from Lakshminarayanan's monotonic *increase* and the opposite of L/R/W's flat/robust result. A monotonic decline with K is much more readily explained by ordinary small-sample statistics — the sample standard deviation across only 2 ensemble members is an extremely high-variance estimator, and if that noisy sigma feeds into an LCB acquisition (mu − beta·sigma), a lucky K=2 draw on a modest benchmark suite (7 synthetic + 7 Design-Bench tasks) is a mundane confound, not a class-defining discovery — than by a NTK/spectral-bias mechanism that was never built to make K-dependent predictions in the first place.

### Position A: the NTK/spectral-bias objection is real and cuts against candidate C

Steelmanned: infinite-width-equals-GP is one of the most robustly reproduced results in deep learning theory (Jacot's Proposition 1 and Theorem 1, extended empirically by Lee et al. 2019 across FC/conv/ResNet architectures and multiple optimizers). Jacot's own numerical experiment directly measures narrower networks having a noisier, more "inflating" NTK during training than wider ones — a literal instance of finite width producing training-induced roughness that shrinks as width grows. Lee (2018) shows this converts into an empirical performance gap that narrows monotonically with width on real benchmarks. If the audited paper's ensemble members are modest-width MLPs (plausible for Design-Bench-scale tasks), it is entirely reasonable that each individual member's function is undertrained/rough relative to what a much wider network (or the GP limit) would produce, and that this per-member roughness is part of why the ensemble as a class underperforms the GP as a class — a genuine width/capacity confound that a "surrogate smoothness is the causal axis" mechanism paper (candidate C) would want to name and, ideally, demonstrate by directly widening the members.

### Position B: the K-sweep does not evidence this, and the field's better-matched mechanism is data-scarcity, not width

Steelmanned: nothing in Jacot, Lee (2018), Rahaman, or Lee et al. (2019) ties their results to ensemble cardinality; all four are silent on ensembles entirely (0 hits for "ensemble" in each, confirmed by grep). The paper that actually studies *why ensembles are diverse* (Fort et al.) locates the mechanism in loss-landscape mode selection from random initialization, with zero use of "width" or "capacity" in its argument — a mechanism that predicts ensemble behavior is governed by how many *distinct basins* independent training runs land in, which L/R/W show is a function of **dataset size**, not width: "With minimal training data, the loss landscape is relatively smooth, and separately-trained models are less diverse." That is a domain-matched (BO, small-data), directly-tested mechanism that requires no width manipulation and, notably, predicts the *opposite* surface phenomenon from a naive width story — the failure mode is landscape *smoothness* (too little diversity) in the low-data regime, not landscape *jaggedness*. Lee et al. (2019)'s own qualifiers — the finite/infinite-width performance gap is "highly architecture-dependent," an "open research question," and GPs "still outperform trained neural networks for both small and large dataset size" regardless of width — mean that even a demonstrated width fix would not be guaranteed to close the gap in exactly the small-data regime offline MBO occupies. Combined with the K-sweep's anomalous direction relative to Lakshminarayanan and L/R/W, the more parsimonious reading is that K is a confound worth naming and controlling (candidate D's job) rather than evidence for a general, causally-demonstrated smoothness mechanism (candidate C's job).

Position B is better evidenced within this locus's scope: it is corroborated by two independent, domain-matched empirical mechanisms (L/R/W's basin-of-attraction data-scarcity story, backed by a second citation to Dai et al. 2022; Fort et al.'s width-orthogonal mode-diversity story) plus a direction-reversal against the two nearest K/M-sweeps in the literature, versus Position A's purely theoretical/analogical extrapolation from single-network, non-ensemble results that never once mention ensembles or K. The one place Position A gets real empirical traction is *outside* this locus's small-data BO scope: Abe et al.'s large-image-data finding that ensemble UQ/robustness gains correlate strongly (Pearson r=0.81/0.76) with single-larger-model capacity gains is genuine width/capacity-mechanism evidence for candidate C — but it is drawn from CIFAR/ImageNet-scale data (tens of thousands of points), not the ~600-point BO regime, and Abe never compares against a GP at all. That scope mismatch is itself informative: the capacity-mechanism story that IS well-evidenced belongs to a different data regime than the one the audited paper's K-sweep occupies.

## Committed position

The evidence in this locus favors **candidate D (taxonomy) over candidate C (mechanism)** on this specific axis, but not for the reason the query's own N5 framing assumes. The K-sweep is not, by itself, evidence for a finite-width/undertraining-artifact reading — K (ensemble cardinality) and n (per-member width) are different hyperparameters, and none of the three canonical NTK/spectral-bias papers (Jacot, Lee 2018, Rahaman) makes any claim about ensemble cardinality; treating "K=2≫K=5" as direct NTK/spectral-bias evidence is an unsupported bridging inference. What DOES undermine confidence in a clean surrogate-class mechanism story is (1) the K-sweep's own direction runs opposite to the two closest empirical analogs in the field (Lakshminarayanan's monotonic-improvement-with-M, L/R/W's flat K=5-vs-10), which is more parsimoniously read as small-sample noise in a 2-member sigma estimate than a discovered law, and (2) the best-evidenced, domain-matched mechanism for ensemble weakness in exactly this small-data BO/offline-MBO regime is L/R/W's basin-of-attraction/data-scarcity story (independently structurally corroborated by Fort et al.'s width-orthogonal mode-diversity mechanism), which predicts landscape *smoothness* causes underperformance, not landscape jaggedness — the opposite surface framing from the query's N5 hypothesis. Given this, the paper's safest and best-supported move is to report K as one of five confounds requiring control (candidate D), name data-scarcity-driven diversity collapse as the leading literature-grounded alternative mechanism to any width story, and NOT assert that surrogate smoothness/width is "the causal axis" (candidate C) without an actual width ablation (holding K fixed) that this locus's corpus shows nobody — including the audited paper itself, as described — has run.

- **Position:** N5's K-sweep evidence for a width/undertraining-artifact reading does not hold up under scrutiny (K ≠ width, and the sweep's direction contradicts the two nearest empirical analogs), so this locus's evidence favors candidate D (name K/data-scarcity as confounds) over candidate C (assert surrogate smoothness as a demonstrated causal mechanism); N2 remains a genuine NONE FOUND, but with L/R/W's robustness finding pre-empting the strength (not the existence) of the novelty claim.
- **Confidence:** medium (roughly 65%). High confidence on the narrower logical point (K and width are distinct variables, and no NTK/spectral-bias source studies ensembles) — that part is close to definitional and well-grounded in exhaustive grep evidence (0 hits for "ensemble" across all three canonical theory papers). Medium, not high, confidence on the broader C-vs-D call, because this locus's corpus cannot verify the audited paper's actual per-member network width, so a genuine width-driven component to the ensemble's weakness cannot be ruled out — it just isn't evidenced by the K-sweep specifically.
- **Boundary conditions:** this reading applies to the offline-MBO / small-data BO setting specifically (datasets on the order of 10^2–10^3 points, matching L/R/W's "rarely exceed about 600" characterization and Design-Bench's typical dataset sizes). Outside that regime — e.g., Abe et al.'s CIFAR/ImageNet-scale (10^4–10^5 points) comparisons — the capacity/width mechanism (candidate C's general spirit) is well-evidenced (Pearson r=0.81/0.76 correlation between ensembling gains and single-larger-model gains); it just isn't the regime this paper's K-sweep or Design-Bench grid occupies.
- **What would change this position:** (1) evidence that the audited paper's ensemble members use unusually narrow architectures (e.g., <100 hidden units) relative to the GP-comparable widths Lee et al. (2019) test (n≥512–2048 for good NTK-regime agreement) — that would make a width story directly plausible for the FIRST time in this locus, independent of the K-sweep; (2) a controlled ablation (not located in this sweep) that fixes K and varies per-member width in a BO/offline-MBO ensemble, showing the GP-ensemble gap closes as width grows — that would be direct FOUND-type evidence for candidate C in this exact regime and would flip this position; (3) if the K=2-vs-K=5 result replicates cleanly across independent random seeds and a larger/different benchmark suite (ruling out the small-sample-sigma-noise explanation), the "mundane statistical artifact" reading would need to be dropped and the anomaly would deserve a more serious mechanistic explanation (though still not necessarily a width one).
- **Evidence weight:** 3 theoretical/empirical papers (Jacot, Lee 2018, Rahaman) support width→smoothness/GP-convergence in general but none studies ensembles or K (0/3 mention "ensemble"); 1 empirical paper (Abe et al.) supports a capacity/width mechanism for ensemble behavior but in a large-data, non-BO, non-GP-comparison regime outside this locus's scope; against a direct K-as-width-evidence reading: 2 domain-matched mechanism papers (L/R/W's basin-of-attraction, Fort et al.'s mode-diversity) plus 2 direction-reversal data points (Lakshminarayanan's monotonic-improvement-with-M, L/R/W's own flat K=5-vs-10 sweep) plus 1 explicit architecture-dependence/small-data caveat (Lee et al. 2019).

- Has anyone run a controlled width ablation (holding K fixed) inside a BO or offline-MBO ensemble specifically? Not located in this sweep or the prior width sweep; would need a dedicated forward/backward citation chase on Fort et al. and on Dai et al. (2022, cited by L/R/W for the "unable to explore the space effectively" mechanism) — Dai et al. itself was not fetched this pass and could be a further useful primary source if budget allows.
- Is the audited paper's K=2 result stable across random seeds? Not answerable from the literature — this is an internal reproducibility question for the audited paper itself, not a novelty-audit question, but it directly bears on whether the "statistical artifact" reading in this note's Position B is correct.
