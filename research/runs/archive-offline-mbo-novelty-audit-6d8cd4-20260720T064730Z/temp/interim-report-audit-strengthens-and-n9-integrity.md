**Re-confirmed by direct full-text re-read this pass**: Recht et al. ([[do-imagenet-classifiers-generalize-to-imagenet-icml-2019-arxiv190210811]]) is a two-track result — absolute accuracy drops 3-15%/11-14% (shrink), but the fitted slope of new-test accuracy vs. original-test accuracy exceeds 1 on both CIFAR-10 (1.69) and ImageNet (1.11), which the authors themselves call "the opposite of the standard overfitting scenario" and "an increased advantage over earlier models." This is a *relative*, model-to-model growth statistic, not an audited scalar effect size (no eta^2 analog) exceeding its own originally published value. Bressan ([[confounds-in-failed-replications-bressan-frontiers-in-psychology-2019-doi103389f]]) is a psychology reanalysis (RP:P replication data), not ML/CS: controlling for a stimulus-allocation confound restores a "failed" replication (p=n.s.) to significance matching the original (p=0.001 original vs. p=0.002 restored), and a separate robustness check states explicitly "the results came out stronger using the stricter window" — the closest full-strength "audit strengthens" language located anywhere in this sweep, but it reports p-values, never a standardized effect-size number comparable to eta^2=0.37-vs-0.405. Melis et al. ([[on-the-state-of-the-art-of-evaluation-in-neural-language-models-iclr-2018-arxiv1]]) is a textbook shrink-to-reversal case (properly-tuned LSTM overturns claimed RHN/NAS SOTA) and states in its own conclusion that "effect sizes tend to be much smaller" once methodological care increases — a direct textual assertion of the shrink norm N9 is trying to contradict.

Four new adversarial OpenAlex searches were run this pass beyond the miss-catcher log's original eight: "reanalysis larger effect size machine learning," "controlling confounder increased effect machine learning benchmark," "correction strengthened result benchmark evaluation," "Simpson's paradox confound control effect amplify machine learning," "adjusting for confounder amplifies treatment effect estimate," "label noise correction increases true accuracy gap deep learning," "regression attenuation measurement error correction larger coefficient machine learning," and "noncollapsibility effect size adjustment increase magnitude." None returned an ML/CS reality-check or reproducibility-audit paper reporting a corrected/de-confounded effect size exceeding its own originally published value. The searches did surface adjacent statistical literature worth naming: "Noncollapsibility and its role in quantifying confounding bias in logistic regression" (2021) and the general noncollapsibility/regression-attenuation literature establish that covariate adjustment can mathematically increase an estimated effect magnitude (this is a well-known statistical mechanism — attenuation-corrected coefficients are never smaller than their naive, error-ridden counterparts) — but these are pure statistics/biostatistics papers, never applied to an ML/CS benchmark audit, and were not fetched (search-summary level only, correctly NOT elevated to a citation in this note per the anti-fabrication rule).

## Evidence synthesis

The evidence divides cleanly into three tiers. Tier 1 (clean ML/CS shrink confirmations): Melis, Ferrari Dacrema ([[are-we-really-making-much-progress-a-worrying-analysis-of-recent-neural-recommen]]), Lucic ([[are-gans-created-equal-a-large-scale-study-neurips-2018-arxiv171110337]]), Musgrave, and now Robinson/Glen/Lee — five separate ML/CS reality-check papers, all audits that shrink, nullify, or reverse an originally claimed effect, none using confound-removal to produce a larger effect. Tier 2 (partial ML/CS analogs, correctly not counted as full precedents): Recht's relative-slope-growth finding and Agarwal's statistical-power-reveals-a-real-effect finding — both genuine "growth" phenomena inside ML/CS papers, but neither is the same claim shape as N9 (confound-removal producing a scalar effect-size number larger than its own originally published value). Tier 3 (full-shape precedent, wrong field): Bressan, whose "the results came out stronger" language is the closest linguistic and structural analog located anywhere in two independent sweeps (8 queries + this pass's additional 8 queries, 16 total), but it is psychology, uses p-values not a variance-explained statistic, and its primary framing is restoration-of-significance rather than exceeding-an-original-magnitude.

### Position A: N9 is a clean, unclaimed rhetorical differentiator — claim it without qualification
The strongest case for this reading is negative-space breadth: sixteen adversarial queries across two independent sweeps, hitting OpenAlex, web search, and targeted candidate-checks (including this pass's specific chase of the noncollapsibility/regression-attenuation literature, which is the most plausible *theoretical* place such a paper would live), turned up zero ML/CS papers that name or demonstrate a confound-controlled audit whose corrected effect-size exceeds its own originally-published value. Every canonical reality-check paper this project catalogued — Recht, Melis, Musgrave, Ferrari Dacrema, Lucic, Agarwal, and now Robinson/Glen/Lee — either shrinks, reverses, or nullifies. That is a remarkably consistent pattern across a genuinely broad, adversarially-searched sample, and absence-of-counterexample after this much searching is meaningful evidence, not just an artifact of insufficient effort. On this reading, N9 should be claimed straightforwardly as a genuine field-level pattern-break.

### Position B: N9 is not cleanly unclaimed — the partials are close enough to create reviewer risk if overclaimed
The strongest case against an unqualified claim is that Recht's finding is not merely tangential — it is a *headline* result of one of the most-cited reality-check papers in ML (do ImageNet classifiers generalize to ImageNet?, thousands of citations), and its "increased advantage," "opposite of the standard overfitting scenario," "no diminishing returns" language is rhetorically almost identical in spirit to what N9 wants to claim, even though the underlying statistic (relative slope vs. absolute eta^2) differs. A hostile AAAI reviewer who has read Recht (very likely, given its prominence in exactly this literature) could reasonably say "this pattern already exists, you're just measuring a different statistic" — and Agarwal's "improvement from SPR over DER... is in fact real" (power revealing a hidden true effect) adds a second, independent near-miss. The paper's claim of novelty is therefore only safe if it is scoped precisely: not "no ML/CS audit ever grows an effect" (false, per Recht/Agarwal) but "no ML/CS confound-removal audit has been shown to produce a scalar variance-explained effect-size statistic that exceeds its own originally-published value" (true, per this sweep). The narrower claim survives; the broader one does not.

## Committed position

The evidence adds up to a real but narrowly-scoped novelty claim, not a clean field-wide first. Sixteen adversarial queries across two independent sweeps found no ML/CS paper demonstrating a confound-controlled audit whose corrected scalar effect-size statistic exceeds its own originally-published value — that specific claim shape (eta^2_surr: 0.37 → 0.405, same statistic, same paper, larger after de-confounding) is unclaimed and should be named explicitly in the paper. But the paper cannot frame this as "reality-check audits always shrink, ours is the first exception" without engaging Recht (relative model-ranking slope growth, a headline finding of a highly-cited paper in the exact genre being invoked) and, more briefly, Agarwal (statistical power revealing a real-but-previously-hidden improvement) as PARTIAL, must-cite counter-considerations — omitting them is the single likeliest way a well-read reviewer discredits the novelty claim. Bressan should be cited as a one-line cross-disciplinary analog ("the strengthens-after-deconfounding shape has a precedent in psychology reproducibility work, but not in ML/CS, and not with a comparable effect-size statistic") rather than treated as a full precedent, since it never computes a standardized effect size and frames its finding as restoration of significance rather than growth beyond an original magnitude.

- **Position:** N9's specific claim shape ("a confound-controlled audit whose corrected effect-size STATISTIC exceeds its own originally-published value") is unclaimed within ML/CS and can be asserted as a genuine novelty; the broader, unscoped claim ("reality-check audits always shrink effects, this is the first that doesn't") is false and must not be made, because Recht (relative slope growth) and Agarwal (power-revealed real effect) are both partial ML/CS counter-instances that a reviewer is likely to know.
- **Confidence:** medium-high for the narrow claim (roughly 75-80%; two independent, adversarially-designed sweeps totaling 16 queries plus direct full-text grep of every named candidate found zero counter-examples), medium for the recommendation that Recht/Agarwal must be pre-empted in the text (this is a judgment call about reviewer behavior, not a factual verdict).
- **Boundary conditions:** this verdict holds for ML/CS venues and the specific literature searched (reality-check / reproducibility-audit / reanalysis papers indexed by OpenAlex and general web search as of this sweep). It does not extend to adjacent quantitative fields — psychology (Bressan) and biostatistics (noncollapsibility/regression-attenuation literature) both contain the phenomenon in some form, meaning the underlying statistical mechanism is well-understood and unsurprising even though no ML/CS paper has yet instantiated it on a benchmark-audit-style effect. If N9 is generalized to "in any empirical field" rather than "within ML/CS," this locus's search does not support NONE FOUND.
- **What would change this position:** discovery of any ML/CS reality-check/reproducibility paper reporting a named confound (or set of confounds), a removal protocol, and a post-correction scalar effect-size number (eta^2, Cohen's d, R^2, or equivalent) that is LARGER than the originally published value for the same quantity. A single such paper, fetched and grepped, would downgrade N9 from "unclaimed" to "prior work found" and this position would reverse. Absent that, the position would also soften if a reviewer-level reading established that Recht's slope-growth result is conventionally treated in the literature as equivalent in kind to a de-confounded-effect-grows result — this sweep found no evidence of that equivalence being drawn by any third party, but it was not exhaustively checked via citation-chain-forward analysis of Recht's own citing papers.
- **Evidence weight:** 5 ML/CS papers support the shrink-norm (Melis, Ferrari Dacrema, Lucic, Musgrave, Robinson/Glen/Lee — all now fetched+grepped, none abstract-only); 2 ML/CS papers are partial complications requiring explicit engagement (Recht's relative-slope growth, Agarwal's power-revealed effect); 1 cross-disciplinary full-shape precedent exists outside ML/CS with no comparable statistic (Bressan); 0 direct ML/CS counter-examples found despite 16 adversarial queries across two sweeps; adjacent statistical theory (noncollapsibility, regression attenuation) confirms the phenomenon is mechanistically unsurprising but was never found instantiated in an ML/CS benchmark-audit paper.
