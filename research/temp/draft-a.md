# Decomposing the GP Advantage in Offline MBO — Adversarial Audit (Draft A)

**The answer, first.** The paper's load-bearing novelty claim survives, but narrowly, contingently, and for a reason weaker than the one the paper gives. No prior work runs a crossed surrogate-model-class x optimizer factorial with a two-way variance decomposition in the offline setting: N6 is **CONFIRMED NONE-FOUND**, after a 2026 recency sweep, a forward-citation walk over roughly 347 citing papers, and a first-time sweep of three regions where the analysis was *more* likely to exist than in offline MBO [1][2][3]. But the claim now rests on scoping rather than on absence, one of its three defensive grounds was falsified on contact with a competitor's own appendix, and one paywalled lead whose title names three surrogate classes *and* two optimizer families remains unread.

**And the paper's citations are in far worse condition than its numbers.** Twelve mandatory fixes follow. Four are severe. `demsar2006statistical` is cited for a numeric threshold that does not exist anywhere in the source, and the citation inverts the source's actual reasoning [4]. `fan2024minucb` is cited three separate ways and all three fail, including one where a **convergence theorem is cited as the authority for a failure mode** [5]. `liu2020sngp` is cited to bound a claim its own Figure 1 asserts the opposite of [6]. And the claim that the ensemble-size sweep "extends below" the prior range is simply **false** — Li, Rudner & Wilson tested K=2 and reported robustness there [7].

**The asymmetry is the finding.** Every quantitative claim this audit could check against the repository's own artifacts reproduced exactly — four corner point estimates and their intervals, the corner range, interval widths, seven figures in Elimination 1 including a paired bootstrap delta, three inversion counts, both frozen-cell claims to the individual seed, and two code-level line traces. Exactly one integer does not: "five other cells tie it" should be six. So of the mandatory items, eleven concern what a *source* says and one concerns an integer recount. **None concerns a number the paper computed.** A paper that verifies `1.5287419557571411` across sixteen seeds should not be attributing a numeric threshold to a JMLR paper that does not contain it. That gap — meticulous with its own evidence, careless with everyone else's — is the single most actionable thing an audit can tell these authors, because it says the remaining pre-deadline effort belongs entirely in the related-work and scoping prose and not in re-running anything.

## N6 Verdict: The Crossed Surrogate x Optimizer Factorial

**Verdict: CONFIRMED NONE-FOUND, with one paywalled lead unresolved.** No paper found satisfies all four conjuncts simultaneously: at least two genuinely distinct surrogate **model classes**, at least two optimizer/search routines, the two **crossed**, and a **two-way variance decomposition**, in an offline or fixed-dataset setting. The first contribution stands. What follows is why it stands more narrowly than the paper currently claims, and where a reviewer will press.

### A. The queries, run verbatim

The 2026 recency frontier was swept first: eight offline-MBO and offline-BBO papers from January through June 2026, full PDFs extracted and grepped for twelve decomposition terms (`factorial`, `crossed`, `ANOVA`, `analysis of variance`, `eta squared`, `main effect`, `variance decomposition`, `two-way`, `interaction effect`, `attribution`, `OFAT`, `simple effects`). **Zero hits, all eight papers, all twelve terms.** The verdicts: SPADE ablates only its surrogate backbone against a fixed evolutionary-plus-LCB optimizer, one axis at a time [8]; the diffusion-LLM paper's ablation is leave-one-component-out rather than a crossed 2x2 [9]; the ranking-learnability paper varies training loss across five Design-Bench tasks with a **single fixed gradient-ascent optimizer throughout every experiment** [10]; and SOO-Bench's contribution is a stability indicator tracking degradation relative to the offline dataset, not variance attribution [11]. The subfield's own 2026 survey returns zero hits for the same terms across 25,965 words — it reviews the literature, it does not run the experiment [12].

The forward-citation walk covered roughly 347 citing papers across Hutter 2014 (62 citers, restricted to 2023-2026), Liang 2021 (166), Moosbauer 2022 (4, all years), and van Rijn & Hutter 2018 (115), with six candidates deep-verified and two fetched in full [1]. EXPObench compares six surrogate-assisted algorithms on four real problems with zero ANOVA or factorial hits, and its "offline" refers to offline surrogate *training*, not offline MBO. Bischl et al.'s 2023 HPO survey — at 836 citations the highest-cited Moosbauer citer — returns zero hits and cites Moosbauer only as related work. PED-ANOVA, the quantum-neural-network fANOVA lineage, CART-ANOVA transfer learning, and Instance Space Analysis all extend functional ANOVA *within a single algorithm's hyperparameter space*, never across surrogate class and optimizer.

Then the three previously-unsearched regions, chosen precisely because a crossed factorial with ANOVA was *more* likely there than in offline MBO: surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation, AutoML/CASH/kernel selection, and simulation-optimization. The most on-target hit in the region with the strongest design-of-experiments culture — Kudela & Dobrovsky's comparison of eleven surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms on CFD problems — returns **zero hits on all nine decomposition terms across 47,342 characters**, because it compares eleven *bundled* method identities via Friedman and Wilcoxon rather than crossing a surrogate factor against an optimizer factor [2]. This is the single most informative negative available: the community best positioned to have already run this analysis has not run it.

### C. The prosecution: why the survival is contingent, not clean

Three things should make the authors uncomfortable, and a reviewer from the offline-design community will find at least two of them.

**First, one of the three defensive grounds was falsified on contact.** The defence of the residual against RaM has three parts: loss type is not model class; the nine Table 3 methods are bundled rather than a clean optimizer factor; and the reporting is descriptive. The second is **false as stated**. RaM's own Appendix E.5 describes four of the nine — BO-qEI, CMA-ES, REINFORCE, Gradient Ascent — as *"baselines that optimize a trained model"* [15]. That is precisely the clean optimizer sub-grid the ground claimed did not exist. **A 4x2 crossed optimizer x training-loss sub-grid is present inside RaM Table 3, in offline MBO, published before this submission.** The ground survives only for the other five: CbAS and MINs are genuinely aliased in the strict technical sense — model family and search paradigm co-occur on every run and cannot be separated by the design's contrast structure [17] — and Tri-Mentoring, PGS and Match-OPT each bundle extra machinery.

**Second, and worse, the audit proved in the act of defending N6 that the decomposition is derivable from a competitor's already-published table.** RaM reports mean and standard deviation per cell over a uniform eight seeds, so both moments and N are available for all ninety cells. Running a per-task two-way ANOVA on those published numbers takes about fifteen lines of code and yields a loss-axis eta-squared of 0.027 against a method-axis eta-squared of 0.577 [15]. This cuts both ways, and the paper must choose the surviving half deliberately. It **strengthens** "no prior work *reports* a crossed surrogate x optimizer decomposition" — still true. It **destroys** "no prior work *could have*" — demonstrably false, and this audit demonstrated it in under a day. N6 must therefore be stated as a claim about what the literature *reports*, never about what the design space permits. The contribution is running the decomposition as the **primary analysis on a purpose-built grid**, not the arithmetic itself. That is a narrower claim than the paper currently implies and it is the one that survives contact with Tan et al.

**Third, the grounds are nowhere on the page.** The paper's introduction currently files `tan2025ltr` under "surrogate-class comparisons hold the optimizer constant," in the same breath as `li2024bnnsurrogates` [7]. That description is accurate about RaM's *main* experiment — the five-surrogate Spearman comparison genuinely does hold gradient ascent fixed — so this is **incomplete rather than false**, and the distinction matters. But it is incomplete in exactly the direction that makes RaM look safely outside the residual before a reader checks Table 3. A hostile reviewer who reads only the current prose will reasonably conclude the authors either did not read RaM's appendix or hoped nobody else would.

The distinction the defence actually needs is statable and defensible, and the paper should state it: **a change is a model-class change if it cannot be instantiated without also changing the estimator family** — a GP posterior requires marginal-likelihood fitting, an ensemble requires per-member MAP training — **and a training-objective change if it can be applied while holding the family fixed**, as RaM does by swapping MSE for ListNet inside one unchanged MLP [15]. This criterion has domain precedent: the field's canonical uncertainty-quantification survey organizes method family as the top-level taxonomy and catalogues loss function as a separate cross-cutting axis in its own tables [18]. It is also self-consistent, because it correctly classes the paper's own comparator COMs — a regularizer on a fixed architecture — as a training-objective study, exactly as the introduction already does. The empirical asymmetry supports it too: if loss and model class were the same kind of factor, one would not expect swapping one to move 2.7% of variance while swapping the other moves the dominant share.

### D. The required fix, exactly

1. **Relocate `tan2025ltr`** out of the "surrogate-class comparisons" bucket into a **training-objective comparisons** bucket alongside `trabucco2021coms`, which is what RaM actually is.
2. **Add a footnote naming RaM Table 3** as the nearest near-miss, with the precise four-versus-five breakdown and the observation that its loss axis carries eta-squared ~0.027 against the method axis's ~0.577.
3. **Restate N6 as a claim about what the literature reports**, and say so in the sentence itself.
4. **Name `lu2022revisiting`** — currently sitting uncited in the paper's own bibliography. Full-text grep returns zero for all eight decomposition terms across 37 pages; it varies uncertainty-penalty heuristics while holding the policy optimizer fixed, and flags MOReL's differing optimizer only as an unresolved implementation confound in an appendix. Naming it *strengthens* the no-prior-instance claim by showing the adjacent field's closest attempt also held an axis fixed. A reviewer who sees it in the bibliography and not in the text will ask why.

**This is a stronger position than the paper currently occupies**, because it concedes the real thing and defends the actual residual — the model-class axis, which RaM never varies.

### E. The lead that is still open

**Elsayed & Lacor (2014), "Robust parameter design optimization using Kriging, RBF and RBFNN with gradient-based and evolutionary optimization techniques,"** *Applied Mathematics and Computation* 236:325-344, DOI 10.1016/j.amc.2014.03.082. The title names **three surrogate classes and two optimizer families** — the only title found anywhere in this audit that names both axes explicitly. Primary text could not be obtained: ScienceDirect, Unpaywall (no open-access location), Crossref, Semantic Scholar (abstract elided by the publisher), Academia.edu, ResearchGate and the institutional repository were all exhausted, and no verdict was rendered from secondary snippets.

The open question is precise. If the crossing is **sequential** — surrogates compared first, then one winner optimized two ways — it is not a kill. If it is a **genuine crossing with a variance decomposition**, it is. This belongs in the N6 verdict itself rather than buried in a limitations paragraph, because the author can resolve it through institutional access in minutes and the reviewer who finds it first will not be so charitable.

## (i) Claims the Literature Contradicts or That Are Miscited

Ordered by severity descending. For each: the claim, the paper's framing, what the source actually says, and the fix.

### 1. `demsar2006statistical` — a numeric threshold that does not exist, attached to inverted reasoning

**The claim** (`main.tex:220`, and echoed in the contributions list): *"we are below the threshold for the test we ran: this omnibus is recommended for more than ten datasets."*

**What the source contains.** Nothing of the kind. The full thirty-page JMLR paper was extracted and grepped directly [4][19]: `more than ten` = **0**, `at least ten` = **0**, `ten or more` = **0**. The only two occurrences of "ten data sets" describe **Demsar's own power-simulation sampling procedure** — "samples of ten data sets were randomly selected," "1000 random selections of ten data sets" — the setup of his simulation study, not a usage threshold for the Friedman test.

**Why this is the audit's most serious citation defect.** It is not an over-read of something stated loosely. It is a **specific numeric recommendation attributed to a real source that does not make it**, in a paper whose entire rhetorical position is that unstated protocol details are the field's central methodological failure. A reviewer who opens Demsar and searches for "ten" finds a simulation-methodology sentence.

**And it inverts his reasoning, which is the part that actually matters.** What Demsar recommends, verbatim, is *"a set of simple, yet safe and robust non-parametric tests... the Friedman test with the corresponding post-hoc tests for comparison of more classifiers over multiple data sets"* [19] — with no dataset-count floor attached anywhere. And his stated design context is explicitly the small-n regime: *"the number of data sets is usually much less than 30."* **Demsar recommends the non-parametric route precisely because n is small.** The paper cites him as establishing that the omnibus needs n > 10 — the opposite of his argument. Independently verified: his own Figure 5 shows Friedman as *more* powerful and more replicable than ANOVA at small samples [4].

**Fix.** Delete "recommended for more than ten datasets" and the Demsar citation from that clause. The conclusion is unaffected and better supported without it: the power limitation is independently established by the rest of the same sentence — the paired-test calculation requiring |d_z| >= 1.27 for 80% power at alpha = 0.05 with n = 7 — and by Agarwal's never-claim-equivalence point, which is verified verbatim and correctly used [20]. **The fix removes a false warrant and an unnecessary self-deprecation simultaneously.** The paper was conceding a methodological weakness it does not have.

### 2. `fan2024minucb` — three uses, three failures, including a convergence result cited as authority for a freeze

Fan, Wang, Ng & Hu, "Minimizing UCB: a Better Local Search Strategy in Local Bayesian Optimization," NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2405.15285. Full text extracted and grepped across 1,550 lines [5].

**2a. "The reading of a UCB-style acquisition as local search" is not established prior work.** The paper writes: *"as is the reading of a UCB-style acquisition as local search"* — presenting it as a settled result it merely applies. Fan et al.'s own words: *"we **propose** our first algorithm... MinUCB, which **replaces** gradient descent step with a step that minimizes the UCB in GIBO"*, and *"This discovery is also meaningful as it **opens up possibilities for new designs**"* [5]. It is a method they propose in 2024, not a reading the field had established. The underlying two-stage local-BO premise traces to GIBO, which Fan et al. explicitly modify — and **GIBO's exploitation step is plain gradient descent, with no confidence bound anywhere** [21]. GIBO's own "local search" language refers to using the function's local derivative, a different sense entirely.

**2b. The LCB-paralysis citation inverts the source's central result. This is the most serious of the three.** The paper writes: *"The surviving mechanism is LCB paralysis: the GP's lower confidence bound is locally maximal at the data, so the optimizer never leaves... This is the offline instance of a known reading of UCB-style acquisitions as local search, applied rather than discovered."*

Full-text grep of Fan et al.: **zero hits** for `offline`, `LCB`, `lower confidence bound`, `stuck`, `frozen`, `freeze`, `paralysis` [5]. They define UCB(x) = mu(x) + beta*sigma(x) and **minimize** it because their objective is minimization; they never treat a maximization setting and never perform the mirror transform. Decisively: **their Theorem 1 proves MinUCB converges at a polynomial rate to a genuine local optimum** — contingent on an *increasing* beta schedule and *continual active resampling every iteration*, both structurally impossible on a static offline dataset with no further oracle queries. The paper cites this convergence result as the authority for an "optimizer never leaves" **failure** mode. That is not a stretch of a source; it is a source pointed in the opposite direction.

**Fix.** Drop `fan2024minucb` from the LCB-paralysis sentence entirely. **This costs the paper nothing and removes a reviewer's cleanest kill**, because the paper's own evidence is stronger than the borrowed authority: bit-identical constants across sixteen seeds, in two GP classes, on a continuous task with no argmax decode.

**A correction this audit owes itself.** An earlier pass concluded "nobody owns the trapped-at-the-data failure mode, so claim it outright." That was reached by *excluding* three candidates — TuRBO, which diagnoses the opposite pathology of over-exploration; Fan, which proves convergence; GIBO, which uses no confidence bound [21] — without sweeping the failure-mode vocabulary. With the vocabulary swept, **Yarotsky (2013), "Examples of inconsistency in optimization by expected improvement,"** *J. Global Optimization* 56(4):1773-1790, is the closest prior formalization in existence: his Theorem 3 rigorously proves that expected-improvement-driven BO started at the true optimum has a trajectory converging back to that single point and "not dense." It differs on three real axes — expected improvement rather than a confidence bound, online rather than offline, and an adversarial worst-case start rather than a seed-robust empirical observation. **The "no prior art" phrasing is withdrawn; the paper owes Yarotsky a scoping sentence.** Two adjacent findings were checked and are genuinely different: Ament et al. (NeurIPS 2023 Spotlight) diagnose a *numerical* vanishing-gradient pathology specific to expected improvement, and Kim & Choi bound the regret gap between local and global optimizers of PI/EI/GP-UCB via a collapse probability — touching GP-UCB directly but concerning within-round inner-optimization fidelity, not the outer-loop stuck-at-the-data claim [22].

**2c. The distance-aware co-citation is unsupported.** The scope paragraph reads *"Nor do we claim... distance-aware uncertainty as ours \citep{liu2020sngp,fan2024minucb}."* Fan et al. has **zero hits** for `distance-aware` and uses vanilla GP posterior variance [5]. **Fix: remove `fan2024minucb` from that co-citation.**

### 3. `liu2020sngp` — cited to bound a claim its own Figure 1 contradicts

**The claim:** *"sigma is a distance signal, not an error signal... this is the corrected measurement, **bounded by prior work on distance-aware uncertainty** \citep{liu2020sngp,vanamersfoort2020duq}."*

**SNGP argues the opposite, about the exact model class at issue.** Verbatim from its Figure 1 discussion: *"deep ensembles (Figures 1b, 1g) and MC Dropout... are based on dense output layers that are **not distance aware**. As a result, both methods quantify their predictive uncertainty based on the distance from the decision boundaries, **assigning low uncertainty to OOD examples even if they are far from the data**"* [6]. SNGP's Definition 1 formalizes input-distance-awareness as a property standard deep models **lack**, and that lack is SNGP's entire motivation for adding a GP output layer and spectral normalization. The same research group's follow-up work continues the program on the same premise, arguing that standard softmax cross-entropy "undoes the distance constraint" [23]. Independently corroborated by DUQ: *"Deep Ensembles is uncertain only along the decision boundary, and certain elsewhere."*

**A second, independent scope problem.** All SNGP experiments are **classification** — a 2D toy, Wide-ResNet on CIFAR, ImageNet, BERT. **Zero regression experiments** [6]. The paper under audit is regression throughout.

**Fix.** The claim cannot be "bounded by" SNGP. Two honest options, and the second is better: (i) drop the bounding citation and present rho ~ 0.26 as a bare measurement on this grid; or (ii) **reframe as a contrast** — "unlike the ensembles SNGP and DUQ characterize as distance-unaware, ours shows a modest positive distance correlation" — which is weaker as an appeal to authority and considerably more interesting as a result. A finding that runs *against* SNGP's characterization is more publishable than one that leans on it, provided it is labelled as such.

### 4. The sigma dichotomy itself is contradicted, in regression, peer-reviewed

**The claim:** sigma is a distance signal *not* an error signal, presented as a corrected general measurement.

**The direct counterexample.** Carrete et al. 2023, *J. Chem. Phys.* 158:204801, neural-network force fields, a regression task: *"the Spearman correlation coefficient between uncertainty and error over the validation data set is **0.90 for the committee and 0.91 for the bootstrap-aggregation ensemble**"* [24]. That is roughly **thirteen times** the paper's rho ~ 0.07, in regression. A second, independent confirmation in a different domain: a deep ensemble's uncertainty map correlates with per-pixel prediction error at Pearson 0.715 in seismic-facies segmentation [25].

**But the resolution is scoped, not a rout, and the paper should say so precisely.** Carrete's 0.90 is measured on validation data drawn from the *same* distribution as training; on their genuinely out-of-distribution test the per-atom correlation degrades while staying informative in aggregate [24]. The load-bearing variable is **where** uncertainty is measured. In-distribution, distance and error largely coincide. Far off-support, the SNGP/DUQ counterexamples bite, because a dense output layer measures distance from a decision boundary rather than from the data manifold [6].

**Fix, two parts.** (a) Scope the framing to this grid — "on our tasks, sigma tracks distance more than error" — rather than as a correction to a general belief. (b) **Drop the dichotomy.** The literature does not treat distance-awareness and error-tracking as mutually exclusive; SNGP explicitly ties distance-awareness to *both* calibration and OOD detection [6]. The paper's "not" is a false opposition, and that is a framing fix independent of the numbers.

**A live confound the paper never examines, stated carefully.** Lakshminarayanan et al. explicitly contrast their NLL-trained heteroscedastic ensemble against *"a commonly used heuristic... to train an ensemble of NNs to minimize MSE and use the empirical variance of the networks' predictions as an approximate measure of uncertainty"*, and show that this naive variant **"consistently underestimates the true uncertainty"** — an 80% nominal interval covering roughly 20% of test points [26]. **The paper's ensemble is exactly that construction**: K=5 MLPs, MSE, cross-member empirical variance. Note also that on their own 1D regression toy the NLL-trained ensemble's predictive standard deviation *does* grow with distance from data, so ensembles are not categorically distance-blind either.

**This audit over-generalized this on first pass and corrects itself.** "MSE-trained ensembles underestimate uncertainty" is too strong. Ghasemipour et al. treat standard init-seed-diversified deep ensembles — the audited paper's construction — as the **reliable reference**, citing Ovadia; what fails in their study is weight-shared "efficient" approximations, which this paper does not use [27]. Ovadia's own large-scale benchmark finds deep ensembles the **best** UQ method under dataset shift, though entirely in classification with zero occurrences of `correlat`, `distance` or `regression` in the text [28]. D'Angelo & Fortuin note standard deep ensembles "do not offer any guarantees for diversity" and can saturate toward single-model behaviour as members are added — yet their own Table 1 shows standard ensembles winning OOD detection in one setup, so this is not a uniform indictment [29]. Fort et al. establish why diversity arises at all (independent initializations reach different loss-landscape modes) but measure function-space diversity, not input-distance awareness, so it neither supports nor undercuts the rho figures [30].

**What survives, precisely:** the MSE-versus-NLL comparison inside Lakshminarayanan's own paper is a real, in-scope caveat about sigma *magnitude* calibration for exactly this construction. It is **not** a general finding that init-seed ensembles are unreliable. Ensemble construction remains a live, unexamined alternative explanation for rho = 0.07, worth one scoping paragraph — phrased as "this specific sigma construction has a known calibration caveat," never as "MSE ensembles are broken."

### 5. `li2024bnnsurrogates` — the "extends below" claim is false

**The claim (Confound 3):** *"Our sweep runs over K in {2,3,5,10} and therefore **extends below** the K in {5,10} range over which ensemble surrogates were found robust \citep{li2024bnnsurrogates}, **sharing its two upper points and adding K=2,3**."*

**What Li, Rudner & Wilson actually did.** Figure A.7 tests ensemble size at **K = {2, 5, 10}**. This was verified first-hand rather than by relay: arXiv:2305.20028v2 was fetched directly (39 pages, 111,042 characters) and the Figure A.7 legend tokens extracted from page 28 read `['2 Models', '5 Models', '10 Models']`; a second route reached the same result by rendering that page as an image. Their verbatim finding from the same caption: *"We compare the behavior of ensembles with different numbers of models, and we find that **the different ensembles perform similarly across many experiments, showing the robustness of our results to this hyperparameter.**"* A second K=2 appearance is in Figure A.5, and the main text adds that these hyperparameters "generally have minimal effects on the performance" [7].

**So the range is {2,5,10}, not {5,10}.** They already went down to K=2 and found robustness there. The "extends below" claim is false as written, and the specific priority the paper asserts — "sharing its two upper points and adding K=2,3" — collapses to "adding K=3."

**Why this is worse than a wording slip.** The K-sensitivity framing exists to establish that the headline sits at the *maximum* of a sensitive curve. Li, Rudner & Wilson is the closest prior work on exactly this question and it reports the opposite conclusion — robustness — over an overlapping range including the very endpoint the paper claims to have contributed. A reviewer from that group, a live possibility in a community this small, reads the sentence as the paper claiming credit for an experiment they ran and published.

**Fix, and it must be a real fix rather than a hedge.**
1. Correct the range to K in {2,5,10} wherever {5,10} appears.
2. Restate the residual honestly. The paper adds K=3, and — far more importantly — reports **eta-squared sensitivity** where Li, Rudner & Wilson report **performance robustness**. Those are different quantities: they measure whether ensemble BO *performance* changes with model count; the paper measures whether the *variance attributed to the surrogate axis* changes. **That distinction is the paper's actual defence and it appears nowhere in the current text.** Made explicitly, the claim survives in a narrower and more interesting form.
3. Do not delete the K-sensitivity result. It is real on this grid. Delete the priority claim.

Incidentally, the year trap here is clean: the arXiv comments field reads "ICLR 2024" against a v1 submission date of 31 May 2023, and the paper has it right [7].

### 6. `abe2022ensembles` — cited for a direction the source never measures

**The claim (Confound 3):** *"the decline with K runs against **the direction reported for ensemble quality** \citep{abe2022ensembles}."*

**What Abe et al. actually did.** Full text extracted, 15,095 words. "Deep Ensembles Work, But Are They Necessary?" (NeurIPS 2022) runs **zero ensemble-size ablation**. Its configuration is fixed — M=4 on CIFAR-10 ("combining 4 out of the 5 random seeds") and M=5 on an ImageNet subset — fixed by seed availability rather than swept. Its thesis is *ensembles versus a single larger model*, not quality as a function of K. **There is no reported direction-with-K in that paper to run against.**

**Fix.** Replace with the source that does report it: Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel & Blundell, Table 4, ImageNet M = 1 to 10, monotonic diminishing-but-never-negative improvement (Top-1 error 22.166% to 18.675%), verbatim *"as M increases, both the accuracy and the quality of predictive uncertainty improve significantly"* [26]. State the honest caveat: that is classification calibration, not offline-MBO reward, so the cross-domain transfer must be argued rather than assumed. `abe2022ensembles` can stay for what it does support, or go.

### 7. `rahaman2019spectralbias` — motivates the wrong ablation, and the architecture makes it worse

**The claim (Elimination 2):** *"The gap could be an under-parameterization artifact: wide networks approach GP behaviour \citep{jacot2018ntk,lee2018dnngp,lee2019widenets} and **finite networks show spectral bias** \citep{rahaman2019spectralbias}. ... so we ran the ablation that does. At fixed K=5, sweeping per-member **width** over a 10.7x range leaves the gap unchanged."*

**Three of the four are clean, and the audit should say so.** Jacot (NTK, training-dynamics regime), Lee 2018 (NNGP, the at-initialization correspondence) and Lee 2019 (wide nets evolving as linear models under gradient descent) collectively *do* support "wide networks approach GP behaviour," covering the at-initialization and during-training regimes respectively; all three were re-fetched fresh [31][32]. This is the citation cluster most likely to be assumed sloppy and it holds up. The only precision gap is that Lee 2019's own text notes the gradient-descent-limit GP is "noticeably different" from the NNGP Bayesian posterior — a nuance the blanket phrase elides but does not falsify.

**The fourth is miscited in effect.** Rahaman et al.'s theorem holds *"for arbitrary width and depth"* — it is not a width-limit statement at all — its defining experiment varies training *iterations* rather than width, and its own controlled ablation finds that *"increasing the depth (for fixed width) significantly improves the network's ability to fit higher frequencies... increasing the width (for fixed depth) also helps, but the effect is **considerably weaker**"* — despite width scaling exponentially (16 to 128) against depth's linear increase (3 to 6) [32]. The paper cites spectral bias to motivate a **width** ablation when the cited source says width is the weaker lever, and the sentence structure ("wide... **and** finite...") invites a width-monotonic reading the source actively argues against.

**And the architecture compounds it.** `main.tex:88` fixes the ensemble at a **two-hidden-layer MLP across the entire width sweep** [32]. **So the paper varies the axis Rahaman calls weak and holds fixed the axis Rahaman calls dominant. The width-only ablation cannot have tested the depth-driven spectral-bias mechanism it invoked Rahaman to motivate.**

**Fix — the second option is better.** (1) Minimal: drop `rahaman2019spectralbias` from that sentence; the width objection stands on the NTK/NNGP lineage alone, which is verified faithful. (2) Better: keep it and state the limit — spectral bias is reported as primarily a *depth* phenomenon, the ablation sweeps width at fixed depth 2, and the depth-driven version of the objection therefore **remains open**. This costs one clause, converts a miscitation into a correctly-scoped limitation, names a concrete follow-up, and pre-empts a reviewer who knows Rahaman.

**Note the asymmetry with Elimination 2's own framing.** The paper is scrupulous about saying the sweep "stops at 1024 while the kernel limits are asymptotic, so this answers the objection at practical widths and makes no asymptotic claim." That same scrupulousness has simply not been applied to the depth axis. This matters for the headline count: **Elimination 2 is eliminated at fixed depth 2, not eliminated**, so "seven controls, none survives" is doing rhetorical work at least one of the underlying controls does not fully support.

### 8. `melis2018sota` — one instance asked to certify a genre-wide law

**The claim, made twice and load-bearing for Contribution 2:** *"Audits in this genre **usually shrink** the effect they audit"* and *"...a direction with no precedent we could find in the ML audit literature, **where audits normally shrink**."*

**What Melis et al. actually says.** Full text verified. Their finding is *"we find that LSTMs outperform the more recent models, contra the published claims"* — a **ranking reversal**, an old baseline beating newer architectures, not a shrinking effect size and certainly not a law about the direction an entire genre runs [33]. Melis explicitly frames the work as one instance: *"this paper joins other recent papers in warning of the under-acknowledged existence of replication failure in deep learning."* Full-text search finds **no sentence anywhere** asserting a genre-wide directional pattern.

**Fix, and the better option is nearly free.** (i) Recast as an observation over named instances — Melis, Ferrari Dacrema, Musgrave and Lucic each independently show a *claimed advantage* shrinking or reversing — and cite all four for the pattern rather than one for a law [34]. The paper already cites all four in its genre paragraph, so this costs a comma. (ii) Or drop "normally" and say only that the audits surveyed moved downward. As written, **one citation is asked to certify a claim about a literature — which is the exact defect this audit was commissioned to find.**

### 9. `ghasemipour2022pessimistic` — an over-extended analogy, where the accurate version is more useful

**The claim (Confound 4):** *"The general principle that a fixed multiplier on an uncalibrated interval is not comparable across model classes is owned by the interval-validity literature \citep{dewolf2022intervals} and **its offline-RL instance** \citep{ghasemipour2022pessimistic}."*

**What Ghasemipour, Gu & Nachum actually do.** Their entire scope is **one** surrogate class, Q-ensembles. Their axis of comparison is an *internal training-procedure* choice — shared versus independent Bellman targets, and full versus weight-shared "efficient" ensembles. **They never compare across distinct model families**, so they are not an instance of a *cross-model-class* incomparability principle [27]. Their actual finding is stronger and different in kind: shared pessimistic targets can flip the LCB's **sign**, rendering an ensemble paradoxically optimistic, rather than merely rescaling an interval.

**Fix.** Either drop the "offline-RL instance" framing or restate it accurately: *a training-procedure choice within one surrogate class can invalidate, not merely miscalibrate, nominal pessimism.* The accurate version is more useful to the paper than the inaccurate one, because it is the same source that supplies the candidate positive mechanism for the inversion result (deliverable iii), and stating what it really shows sets that up.

**`dewolf2022intervals`, by contrast, is verified clean and correctly narrow.** Its central empirical finding — that uncalibrated methods across four regression-UQ families do not deliver matched effective coverage at a shared nominal level — genuinely supports the general principle the paper cedes to it, and grep confirms **zero hits** for `acquisition`, `Bayesian optimization`, `pessimis`, `lower confidence bound` across roughly 23,000 words, so the paper's claim to the offline-MBO acquisition-comparison application as its own residual is correctly scoped [35]. **No fix needed.**

### 11. The N9 "no precedent" claim is bracketed on both sides

**The claim:** *"We searched the ML reality-check and reproducibility literature for a de-confounding audit reporting a corrected variance-explained statistic above its own published value and found none."*

**The narrow scalar claim survives, and is now better evidenced than when the paper made it.** An exhaustive fresh sweep in this exact framing — fourteen verbatim queries across the arXiv API and targeted search, including the ML Reproducibility Challenge and ReScience C as the natural homes for such a result — returned **zero grow-direction hits**, and turned up a third directly-on-target shrink-direction reanalysis (Robinson, Glen & Lee, *J. Comput. Aided Mol. Des.* 2020, reanalysing Mayr et al.'s ChEMBL benchmark) alongside Musgrave and Ferrari Dacrema.

**But two flanks are now exposed.**

*(a) The ML literature's documented base-rate explanation for this exact direction is artifact.* Hamdan et al., verbatim: *"this common approach to confound removal **biases ML models, leading to misleading results**. Specifically, this common deconfounding approach **can leak information such that what are null or moderate effects become amplified to near-perfect prediction** when nonlinear ML approaches are subsequently applied"* [36]. This is not a kill on novelty — different genre, confound-removal methodology rather than a benchmark reality-check — but it is worse than a kill in one respect: **in the one ML literature where "confound removal increased an effect" has actually been studied, the default diagnosis for that direction is leakage.**

**Fix (mandatory, and a strengthening if done well).** The paper currently treats the upward direction as merely unprecedented and defends it as not statistically surprising. That defence is now insufficient. The paper must **affirmatively rule out confound-leakage**, and it can do so cheaply: its two corrections are a target rescaling and a candidate-selection rule, neither of which regresses a confound out of features — which is the mechanism Hamdan's leakage requires. But that argument has to be **made on the page**, because a reviewer who knows this paper will otherwise supply the leakage explanation for free.

*(b) The surrounding rhetoric is contradicted even though the narrow claim is not.* Maassen et al. (PLOS ONE 2020) recomputed 500 primary effect sizes across 33 psychology meta-analyses: *"We did not find any evidence for systematic bias... we estimated **19 pooled effect sizes to be larger** than originally reported and **14 to be smaller**."* Corrections go up about as often as down. This does not kill a claim explicitly scoped to "the ML reality-check literature," but it kills the *implication* that an upward correction is intrinsically surprising. The paper half-anticipates this already, so the fix is a citation at that sentence, which converts a hedge into a supported statement.

### 12. The conformal proposition is formally correct and practically inert — and the paper never says so

**This item was initially recorded as the audit's most serious finding, on a relayed report that the proposition omits a load-bearing hypothesis. Reading the supplement directly showed the report was overstated, and the earlier framing is withdrawn.** Propagating an overstatement is precisely the failure this audit exists to catch.

`supplement.tex:48` reads: *"Under a shifted proposal Pi != P **with density ratio w = dPi/dP**, validity is restored by weighting the calibration quantile by w (weighted conformal)."* It defines w as **the true density ratio**, not an estimate. So the statement is exactly Tibshirani et al.'s Theorem 2 and is **formally correct as written. The proposition is not missing a hypothesis and it is not wrong.**

**What is genuinely true, and still worth a fix.** The clause is correct and **inert in the paper's own setting**. Tibshirani et al.'s guarantee holds for **known** w; their abstract separates the estimated case (*"known — or, in practice, can be estimated accurately"*) and supports it **empirically only**, roughly 91.0% against 90% nominal on airfoil data, with **no theorem bounding the coverage gap under an estimated weight** [37][38]. Angelopoulos & Bates corroborate verbatim: *"This algorithm addresses a somewhat restricted case—that of a known covariate shift"*, and weighted procedures are *"exact when the magnitude of the distribution shift is known"* [39]. In this paper Pi is the distribution of designs an optimizer proposes after ascending a surrogate — the setting where w is least knowable and arguably not well-defined, since the proposals are the output of a deterministic optimization concentrated on a low-dimensional set. Nothing in the paper estimates w or applies weighted conformal.

**Fix (moderate, one or two sentences), plus a free win.** Note after Proposition 2 that the covariate-shift clause requires a known or reliably estimable w, that no such estimate is available for optimizer-generated proposals, and that the clause is stated for completeness rather than applied. **Then take the win:** the supplement already reports that conformal repair *"restores in-distribution coverage to its 0.90 target on every task but **leaves OOD coverage erratic**"* — 0.00 on Styblinski, Griewank, UTR, Ant and D'Kitty. That is *exactly what one should expect* when the repair's precondition is unavailable. Saying so converts an unexplained table into a correctly-scoped negative result and reinforces the paper's separate argument that premise coverage is separable from optimization outcome. **Severity: moderate, not severe. It is a scoping omission around a correct proposition, ranked below the Demsar fabrication and the Fan inversion, not above them.**

### 14. Internal arithmetic — one integer, two presentation gaps, and a statistic reported without its known bias

**14a. "Five other cells tie it" should be six.** Applying the paper's own stated criterion (`inversion_rate == 1.0` AND `mean_frac_worse == 1.0`) to `results/x0_inversion.json` at the audited engine returns **seven** cells: Branin ens:grad plus six others (Branin ens:cma; Styblinski ens:cma, ens:grad, ens:perturb, svgp:cma, svgp:grad). Severity is low and the direction is unaffected — but it is a bare integer a reviewer with the released artifact can check in one line, and this paper's entire credibility posture rests on such checks passing. **It is the only figure in the whole numeric sweep that does not reproduce.**

**14b. Two paired-bootstrap deltas read as arithmetic errors.** The beta=0 increment `0.203` sits in the same sentence as point estimates whose difference is 0.206; it is correct (the artifact's paired delta is 0.20321) but a reader who subtracts thinks they have caught an error. The width shrinkage `-0.006` against endpoints differing by 0.004 is the same class. **Fix: one clause each saying these are paired bootstrap deltas, not differences of marginals.**

**14c. eta-squared is positively biased at n=7 and the paper never says so.** This is now first-hand rather than secondhand. Okada (2013), *Behaviormetrika* 40(2):129-147, contains the exact Monte Carlo bias table at one million replications per condition: eta-squared bias is **+0.054 at n=10 per group**, falling to **+0.005 at n=100**, **always positive**, while epsilon-squared and omega-squared are always small and negative. Liu (2022), *Methodology* 18(1):44-57, CC BY, states firsthand that "eta squared is not an unbiased estimate and is known to have positive bias... depends on sample size," attributing the corrections to Kelley (1935) for epsilon-squared and Hays (1963) for omega-squared. **Okada's simulation also overturns the folk belief that omega-squared is the least-biased correction — epsilon-squared is** — so the recommendation should name epsilon-squared, or report both. The paper's own bootstrap artifacts already contain the bias estimate: the bootstrap mean exceeds the point estimate in **all four corners**, by +0.0099 to +0.0184.

**Fix, and it is a strengthening.** Report epsilon-squared or the bootstrap-bias-corrected values alongside eta-squared. **The headline gets stronger:** bias-corrected, the two protocol confounds move the surrogate effect 0.351 to 0.395, a rise of +0.0437, against the reported 0.367 to 0.405 rise of +0.0376. Direction survives; the magnitude of the *change* grows.

**14d. There is a fifth operating-point coordinate and the paper does not name it.** The paper's central methodological claim is that any headline eta-squared is a joint artifact of four coordinates — K, beta, budget, engine corner — "so a headline that does not state all four is not reproducible." **The per-task normalizer is a fifth, and arguably the most consequential**, because unlike the other four it is an *analysis* choice, it is applied to every number in the paper, and its leverage scales with how outlier-dominated a task is. It is also a documented deviation from the benchmark's own convention: Design-Bench normalizes to an *exogenous* range drawn from a larger dataset, explicitly not from the compared arms; the synthetic grid normalizes by the min and max of the nine compared cells themselves [40].

Two sources make this a citable engagement rather than a general worry. Jordan et al. name **this exact endogenous functional form** and its failure mode verbatim: *"algorithms could be near 0 or 1 if there is an outlier algorithm that does very well or poorly... We would like the evaluation procedure to be robust to the addition of poor algorithms"* [41]. And Bellemare et al. — the *origin* of the technique — document it actually flipping a ranking, in the Zaxxon case [40]. Note that "just use ranks" is not a clean escape: rank statistics are pool-dependent, which is the same class of problem, and the paper already cites Benavoli correctly for exactly that defect elsewhere [42].

**The honest empirical resolution, which corrects one of this audit's own framings.** A recomputation across five alternative normalizers over four corners, five beta levels and both budget levels — 33 combinations plus leave-one-task-out, run directly on the paper's own raw result files — finds that **every one of the 33 preserves eta-squared_surr > eta-squared_opt**. Direction is robust; magnitude is not, with eta-squared_opt moving up to 3.4x and the interaction consistently halved under rank. **And the framing that motivated the concern was wrong: Griewank-30D, the catastrophic-outlier task, is empirically the *smallest* lever on the headline, not the largest** — dropping it moves 0.405 to 0.395, while dropping Styblinski-5D moves it to 0.485 [40]. Recorded because this audit raised the alarm on Griewank specifically. One further disclosure the check surfaced: min-max-normalized residuals reject normality and homoscedasticity while rank-transformed residuals satisfy both, so the point estimate is computed on an assumption-violating scale; the nonparametric bootstrap is the right hedge, but the scale is not disclosed.

## (iii) Scope of Novelty, Ranked

### Rank 1 — Interpret the interaction term. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

**The paper argues that interaction detection is what justifies its design, runs the design, finds a significant interaction in all four corners, prints it in a table column, and never mentions it again.** The word `interaction` appears **exactly once** in `main.tex` — inside a related-work sentence citing Moosbauer's stated reason for declining fANOVA [46].

The numbers: eta-squared_inter is 0.165 / 0.146 / 0.152 / 0.160 across the four corners, with bootstrap intervals excluding zero in **all four** (lower bounds 0.087-0.107). It is the **second-largest effect in every corner**, ordered surrogate > interaction > optimizer without exception, at 4x to 33x the optimizer main effect. And it is **more stable than the headline**: the surrogate main effect ranges 0.283-0.450 across corners (range 0.167) while the interaction ranges 0.146-0.165 (range 0.018) — **9.2x more stable across the one coordinate the paper demonstrates the headline is most sensitive to**. It survives bias correction at 0.134-0.156 [46].

**Why this is a major result and not a technicality.** *(a)* It is the strongest available vindication of N6. The entire case for a crossed factorial over one-factor-at-a-time is that OFAT cannot estimate interactions — the paper *makes that argument*, citing Moosbauer's own reason for declining. A significant interaction is the empirical payoff of the design: the thing a fixed-one-axis study **could not have found**. Every near-miss above is structurally incapable of producing this number. **It is the answer to "why did anyone need to run this?"** *(b)* It reframes the optimizer null, currently the paper's weakest passage. The paper warns, correctly but without evidence, that a small eta-squared_opt "licenses 'optimizer choice explains little variance' and never 'optimizer choice is arbitrary' — the second reading is false on our own data." **The interaction is that evidence and it is not cited there.** The supported statement is that optimizer choice has a small *marginal* effect and a substantial *conditional* one. *(c)* It is a significant positive quantitative result in a paper whose mechanism section is seven negatives — which matters at a venue that folds novelty and significance into one bundled criterion with no audit-genre rubric [47], unlike the segregated track that states outright that "negative results are valuable when rigorously supported" [48].

**Three independent routes reached this finding — two analysts and the orchestrator's own artifact work, none prompted by the others. Convergence on a quantity the paper prints four times and never discusses is itself a finding.**

**Two qualifications that must travel with it.** It is **synthetic-grid-specific**: on Design-Bench eta-squared_inter is only 0.006-0.041, an order of magnitude smaller and *subordinate* to eta-squared_opt there [46]. That is consistent rather than contradictory — where surrogate cells are frozen, neither main effects nor interactions involving them can appear. And it should be promoted as an **existence-and-ordering claim** ("surrogate and optimizer are not separable; the interaction exceeds the optimizer main effect in every corner") rather than as the stable quantity "0.15", because it is an eta-squared and the magnitude fragility above applies to it directly — halved under a rank normalizer [40]. Recommending an unqualified 0.15 would import the exact fragility this audit is simultaneously warning about.

**The positive DOE authority the paper is missing.** Its bibliography contains **zero** design-of-experiments citations — no Montgomery, no Box, no Fisher, no NIST handbook — so its only warrant for the crossed design is Moosbauer's *negative* framing [46]. Two open, citable sources supply the positive mirror: the NIST/SEMATECH handbook's own statement that "OFAT experimentation leaves us in the dark about factor interactions," and George Box's "Do Interactions Matter?" (1989), written specifically to argue that interactions are practically decisive, with the SKF bearing experiment where "the life of a bearing can be increased fivefold if the two factors... are increased together" and the diagnosis that "because most engineers have, until recently, employed only one factor at a time experimentation, interaction effects have been missed." The formal statistical concept is standard and the handbook's aliasing section supplies the definition [17].

**The honest counterweight, and it cuts for the paper.** Sommet et al.'s preregistered metastudy of 159 psychology studies found median power to detect a typical interaction of just **.18**, with only 4% reporting an adequate shape-aware power analysis, and cite Gelman's rule that you need sixteen times the sample size to estimate an interaction as a main effect [49]. McClelland & Judd's much-cited finding that interaction tests can have "less than 20% of the efficiency of optimal experimental tests" is explicitly about **field/observational** studies with restricted-range moderators — **not** designed experiments with manipulated, maximally separated levels [46]. This grid is the "optimal experimental test" case: three architecturally distinct surrogate classes against three distinct search routines. **A naive citation of "interactions are hard to detect" would misapply a field-study result to a controlled factorial** — and citing this literature correctly is a small, cheap display of exactly the methodological care the paper is claiming.

**And no precedent exists for interpreting a component-by-component interaction in ML benchmarking.** Three primaries were fetched and grepped across three candidate regions. Schmidt, Schneider & Hennig's "Descending through a Crowded Valley" (ICML 2021) returns **zero hits** on all formal terms, offering only qualitative "depends on the problem" language. Choi et al. on optimizer comparisons returns **zero hits** and argues rankings are confounded by asymmetric tuning budgets, which is not an interaction term. Steiner et al.'s "How to train your ViT?" (TMLR 2022) explicitly runs a grid crossing augmentation strength x model size x data budget and calls it *"interplay"* — but **never computes a formal interaction or ANOVA statistic**. Note that the ViT check is where the audit's third homonym trap fired: `ANOVA` false-matched inside *"Toutanova."*

### Rank 2 — The raw-units conditional-advantage result, posed as a question the paper tests. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

Taking the GP-minus-ensemble gap in **raw oracle units** from the supplement's own Table `tab:sfull`, per optimizer, per task: on **7 of 7 tasks the surrogate gap under perturbation is smaller than under both gradient and CMA**, averaging 5.9% of the aggressive-optimizer gap — roughly 17x attenuation. Branin 0.38 against 8.87/13.61; Ackley 0.04 against 3.11/3.72; Griewank 125.00 against 2591.06/2612.00. This is arithmetic on published numbers, in raw units, so **neither the normalizer challenge nor the small-n eta-squared bias applies to it**.

A formal per-optimizer simple-effects analysis, run on stored per-seed data with the paper's own shared nine-cell normalizer, independently confirms it with intervals the raw-units version lacks: **eta-squared_surr collapses to 0.007 [0.001, 0.048] under perturbation against 0.735 and 0.762 under gradient and CMA** in the primary corner [46]. Textbook methodology requires exactly this decomposition once an interaction is present — for a 3x3 design, two main effects and six simple effects. **One trap must be stated if the table is added:** re-normalizing to the three cells within a single optimizer column inflates the perturbation figure to ~0.73, because min-max rescaling to any three points always restores a full [0,1] spread. A correct simple-effects computation must share the omnibus model's normalizer [46].

**But the causal reading is not established, and this audit's own test went against it.** The competing explanation is that perturbation attenuates the gap merely because it *underperforms*, compressing everything, rather than because it neutralizes surrogate differences. Testing this: **the one task where perturbation is the grid's best optimizer (Styblinski-5D, GP+Pert at 36.15) is the task with the weakest attenuation (14.3%)** — exactly the pattern a floor effect predicts. That single data point is worth more than the seven-task count, because it is the only case that discriminates between the two explanations.

**So the descriptive fact is solid and the causal reading is not. Fold it in as a question the paper poses and tests, never as a result it asserts.** The disentangling analysis is cheap and nameable: condition each optimizer's gap on how much that optimizer improves over the seed set, using `mean_x0_best` and `mean_ret_best`, which the inversion artifact already stores. That is re-analysis, not a new run.

**What it would unify if it holds.** Six results the paper currently reports as separate observations in five sections: the interaction eta-squared; the cross-proposal coverage table; the x0-inversion; Elimination 4 (more budget widens the gap); the Design-Bench frozen cells (GP+perturbation being the frozen pairing); and the Design-Bench optimizer inversion. And it would explain the paper's own biggest puzzle — why the optimizer axis is negligible on synthetic tasks but inverts on Design-Bench with perturbation leading.

### Rank 3 — Connect the two tables that already measure the same thing. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

Supplement Table `tab:cross` reports premise coverage at beta=2: the ensemble holds at 0.73 in-distribution, **0.41 on its own proposals**, and **0.97 on the GP's**; the GP holds at 0.98 / 0.97 / 0.93. The paper's own reading is correct: *"The ensemble is not miscalibrated in general---only where its own gradient optimizer drives it."*

**That sentence is a description of an interaction, and the paper never says so.** The ensemble's premise validity is not a property of the ensemble; it is a property of the ensemble-optimizer pair. **So the paper has, in two different tables, (a) a significant interaction term it never interprets and (b) a direct mechanistic measurement of what that interaction consists of — and it never connects them.** `tab:corners` says surrogate and optimizer are not separable; `tab:cross` says *why*: each surrogate is well-behaved except where its own search rule pushes it.

This upgrades the interaction from a bare eta-squared into a claim with an independent, unnormalized measurement behind it — **coverage figures are probabilities, not min-max-normalized scores**, so the 0.41-versus-0.97 contrast is untouched by the normalizer critique. One sentence in each direction. Zero computation.

### Rank 4 — The budget axis is an instance of a named line the paper does not know it belongs to. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP. **The clearest under-stated finding.**

The paper presents its budget result **defensively**, as an objection it has removed: "the obvious objection is that the optimizer axis is search intensity wearing a costume." Read against the literature it is not a defence at all — it is a contribution to an established line, carrying **the strongest evidence in the paper** (the only disjoint intervals anywhere in the decomposition: [0.189,0.355] against [0.421,0.719]) plus a ranking flip.

COCO/BBOB Section 3.5 formally names **budget-dependent benchmarking** as a methodological category with a decade-old apparatus: *"The performance of some solvers depends on the total budget of function evaluations given as an additional parameter to the solver... a single performance assessment of such solvers cannot be expected to faithfully predict their performance for budgets that are different from the one that was used"* — with fixed-budget and fixed-target views and anytime assessment as the prescribed treatment [50]. COCO is uncited.

And there is a direct precedent **already in the paper's bibliography, cited for the genre but not for this**: Lucic et al., verbatim, *"bad models can outperform good models given enough computational budget"* and *"a [model] trained on a large budget can outperform a good model trained on a small budget"* [34]. **That is the paper's own finding — gradient leading at Q=4,352 and perturbation at Q=51,456 — stated at NeurIPS 2018.** A sibling-field confirmation exists in metaheuristics, where budget-matched comparison is standard practice and raising the budget "may significantly affect the final verdict."

### Rank 5 — Report the normalizer robustness check and name the fifth coordinate. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

Covered in item 14d. Two points make it more than a defensive move. **The repository already knew**: `docs/FLAW_LEDGER.md` P1-2 flags exactly this, and `docs/scripts_eta_robustness.py` is a prior, abandoned attempt — it reproduces the paper's eta-squared to eight decimal places and recomputes it under rank, z-score, log-regret and winsorized normalizations, but every number in its raw output was computed on the **uncorrected published grid**, never on the corrected headline, the four corners, or the sweeps [40]. And the machinery exists twice over, since the per-task z-score cross-check is already used for Elimination 1. **This lands almost exactly where the paper's own framing already sits** — "the direction is invariant... only the size of the gap moves" — so the fix extends an argument the paper already makes to one more axis, and the extension is supportive.

### Rank 6 — Report the computed TOST bound. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

The supplement's entire treatment of equivalence is one clause: paired TOST tests "are computed by the released `stats.py`." **They were, and the result is informative:** gap 0.3762, 90% CI [-0.1078, 0.8602], effect bound 0.4840, **not equivalent at either a 0.5 or a 0.3 margin**. The paper is scrupulous about never claiming equivalence and correctly says a failure to reject is not a demonstration of absence — **but it has the actual equivalence test in hand and it is a positive statement about what the null does and does not license**, stronger than the disclaimer currently in its place. Lakens (2017), the standard reference covering paired-means TOST with Cohen's d_z, is absent from the bibliography, and his own caution should travel with the number: "rejecting small effects in an equivalence test requires large samples." A separate confirmed negative closes the thread cleanly — there is no ML-benchmark-specific equivalence-testing literature; OpenAlex and arXiv searches return only clinical hits. Zero new computation.

### Rank 7 — The landscape negative, reported in three sentences. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

A decisive quantified negative, which is more useful than the speculative positive one might expect. Tested against `tab:sfull` under all three optimizers: **no standard landscape covariate predicts the GP-versus-ensemble gap.** Spearman with dimension is 0.107 (p=0.82) under gradient, -0.109 under perturbation, 0.071 under CMA — indistinguishable from zero under every optimizer. Textbook multimodality **actively mis-sorts**: within the four-function "many local minima" bucket the gap spans 2.09 to 2591.06, three orders of magnitude inside one category, while the only strictly unimodal task has the smallest gap of all and Styblinski has the second-largest. Separability fares no better [51].

**But the heterogeneity is not noise**: the per-task gap ranking is **stable across optimizers at Spearman 0.84-0.96**. Composed with rank 2, this is a real constraint on any mechanism account that no single analysis produced — tasks keep their rank order under every optimizer while the overall scale collapses under the weak one, which rules out both a purely optimizer-side story and a purely task-side story. **The mechanism has to be multiplicative: a task factor scaled by an optimizer-aggressiveness factor.**

**Reporting this closes a hostile reviewer's "isn't this just landscape?" objection with a falsifiable, already-computed answer** instead of the current one-line hedge. Every number is in `tab:sfull`.

One documented curiosity cuts against the naive reading and is worth a clause: Griewank is known for "getting simpler to be optimized with increasing dimension, although the number of local minima increases," so attributing the ensemble's collapse there to landscape ruggedness is probably backwards [51].

### Rank 8 — Name the inversion, and cite the guarantee it lacks. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

The paper states its inversion result flatly: "A cell that returns something worse than what it was handed... We call that an inversion... it is a demonstration within this grid, descriptive at n=7 tasks, not a mechanism."

**It is an instance of a named, decades-old failure category.** The offline-RL literature formalizes exactly this as **Safe Policy Improvement**: `P(rho(pi,M) >= rho(pi_b,M) - zeta) >= 1 - delta`, the requirement that a policy learned offline not be worse than the baseline it was given, with high probability. Thomas, Theocharous & Ghavamzadeh (ICML 2015) is the founding formalization; Laroche, Trichelair & Tachet des Combes prove their algorithm is a zeta-approximate safe improvement, and — pointedly — measure safety via the **1%-CVaR** across roughly 100,000 randomly generated MDPs precisely because algorithms without such a guarantee return policies below the baseline in a nontrivial fraction of runs [52].

**The reframing this licenses:** the inversion is not an awkward number, it is the observation that **offline MBO optimizers ship with no safe-improvement guarantee, and the absence bites.** The sibling field considered this important enough to build algorithms around it a decade ago; offline MBO has not. Two sentences, two citations, and a descriptive count becomes a statement about a missing guarantee.

### Rank 9 — Test the one candidate positive mechanism the seven eliminations do not rule out. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP if per-member predictions were stored; FOLLOW-UP if not.

Ghasemipour, Gu & Nachum prove that **shared pessimistic targets across ensemble members can render an ensemble paradoxically optimistic**, with member independence as the load-bearing condition [27]. This is directly on point for a paper whose central puzzle is that a pessimistic, LCB-penalised ensemble ranks its own hallucinations above real data. **If insufficient member independence converts intended pessimism into effective optimism, that is a positive mechanism for the inversion — the first this audit found that the seven eliminations do not already exclude.** It is testable on data the paper has: measure pairwise prediction correlation across the K=5 members at the returned optima and check whether inversion rate tracks it.

This compounds with item 4: an MSE-trained ensemble taking cross-member empirical variance as sigma [26] is precisely the construction most vulnerable to insufficient diversity [29]. **Two independent lines now point at ensemble construction as an unexamined confound, and the paper treats its ensemble as a fixed given.**

### Rank 10 — Xu et al.'s extrapolation theorem, the mechanism the paper is missing. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

Xu et al., "How Neural Networks Extrapolate" (ICLR 2021), Theorem 1: in the NTK regime, ReLU MLPs **provably converge to linear functions along rays from the origin outside the training support**, at rate O(1/t). This yields a positive, falsifiable geometric prediction that is exactly the paper's own diagnosis made mechanical: **an ensemble mean growing linearly without bound far from support admits unbounded maximizers at the box boundary; a GP posterior mean, reverting toward its prior, does not.** "Which off-distribution maximizers a surrogate's mean admits" stops being a diagnosis and becomes a testable claim about each surrogate's far field. **Runnable now** — fit lines to existing model outputs along the already-stored optimization trajectories. No new training.

**A tension was flagged and resolved: this is compatible with Elimination 2.** They are asymptotic in different variables — Xu in far-field *distance from support*, Elimination 2 in *width*, and Elimination 2 explicitly disclaims any asymptotic-width claim. Xu's own robustness check confirms the linear far-field behaviour holds approximately across depths, widths, learning rates and batch sizes, so the mechanism does not require literally infinite width [32]. **One clarifying sentence is required if the paper adopts Xu**, because nothing currently distinguishes "NTK regime" from "the kernel limits are asymptotic" for a reader who meets both in one paper.

### Rank 11 — Cite Fannjiang as the origin of the diagnosis, then state the refinement. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

Handled as a liability in item 13; here is the upside. Cite Fannjiang & Listgarten as the founding articulation, then state the advance: **the off-support story is right in aggregate and insufficient as an explanation of the class gap**, because at matched distance the classes still differ — medians 0.87 / 0.86 / 0.84, with the ensemble worse by -1.406 in true oracle value. That is a genuine advance on a named prior position, and far stronger than presenting the diagnosis as unprecedented.

### Rank 12 — Fit Gao's overoptimization curve; cite the entry already in the bibliography. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

Gao, Schulman & Hilton give closed forms for proxy-reward overoptimization with `d := sqrt(KL(pi||pi_init))`: `R_bon(d) = d(alpha - beta*d)` and `R_RL(d) = d(alpha - beta*log(d))`. Degradation is **class-dependent** — the functional form differs by optimization method — but they vary only *scale within one architecture family*. **So Elimination 7 is a refinement of Gao's principle onto a new axis (surrogate architecture class rather than scale) in a new domain, consistent with the scaling law rather than a counterexample.** Two surrogate classes at matched distance with different true loss is exactly what a class-dependent degradation curve predicts, and the paper has 5,040 instrumented optima to fit it on. The three bib defects go with it.

### Rank 13 — Reframe the PGS falsification as a named-belief-refuted hook. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

The optimizer-axis result locally falsifies a premise asserted verbatim by a paper published **at this venue two years earlier** [43]. The current draft uses this only as a scope-limiting aside. Reframed as "a claim made at this venue in 2024 is directly measured and falsified by our factorial," it is the genre's actually-accepted shape — Henderson, Musgrave and Ferrari Dacrema all lead with a named belief refuted, not with a decomposition offered cold. **That argument form requires no genre familiarity to register as a contribution**, which matters given that AAAI's main track bundles novelty and significance into one criterion scored by generalist reviewers with a stated preference for "new territory" [47], and offers no analogue to the segregated rubric that pre-authorizes rigorously-supported negative results [48]. One further mechanical, free move: select the evaluation-and-benchmarking topic keyword rather than a Bayesian-learning one, since keyword choice determines the reviewer pool [53].

### Rank 14 — Give the beta=0 decomposition a lineage. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.

The beta=0 result — "pessimism amplifies a mean-quality base rather than creating it" — has an uncited precedent in shape. Fujimoto & Gu's TD3+BC ablation shows RL alone is "insufficient to achieve a high performance" without the behaviour-cloning term, with performance robust across a broad alpha range and degrading only at the extremes [54]. Same decomposition: a method's gain split between base-model quality and a regularization term. **State the limit precisely** for the theoretical sibling: PEVI decomposes offline-RL suboptimality and proves pessimism is minimax-optimal, but contains **no empirical base-versus-conservatism ablation**, so it supports the framing and is not a precedent for the experiment. That distinction is the whole subject of this audit.

### Rank 15 — The double-dissociation transplant. FOLLOW-UP-PAPER / EXPENSIVE (CHEAP-to-MEDIUM if confined to one benchmark).

Deep Kernel Learning plus a bi-Lipschitz-constrained deterministic-uncertainty model would give the cleanest **causal** test available: an unconstrained GP-with-neural-features should fail like the ensemble; a constrained variant should recover GP-like behaviour. Confined to one 2D benchmark it is affordable; done properly it is a different paper. Related and also expensive: a three-arm graded battery in the style of BCQ, to upgrade the binary inversion result into a dose-response curve; and a genuine BBOB/COCO-scale landscape study with standard ELA feature sets, which would face the circularity problem that landscape features computed through a surrogate are "more indicative of the surrogate model than the original landscape" [51].

### Rank 16 — The (alpha, omega)-sensitivity measure. FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP, with a venue caveat.

Dao et al.'s sensitivity definition perturbs the trained surrogate's own **parameters** and measures how much the prediction at the found optimum moves. **None of the seven eliminations touches weight-space fragility** — it is a genuinely new axis, computable on already-trained checkpoints with zero new training. **Cite as arXiv:2503.04181 (2025), venue unverified**; see the terminal section for why.

### Rank 17 — Name the ranking flip and state the seed count. NOT-WORTH-IT to expand, but free to fix.

Two presentation items with no research content: the budget-matched synthetic arm reports 630 cells, implying 10 seeds rather than the 30 the reader has been given for main results, and the seed count should be stated where the number appears. And the beta-sweep endpoint at beta=2 (0.40636, 10 seeds) and the headline on/on corner (0.40455, 30 seeds) are **two independent runs landing 0.0018 apart** — a replication the paper currently obscures by letting the reader assume they are one number at two precisions. **A paper whose central scalar is challenged as an operating-point artifact should be advertising an independent reproduction of it.**

### Explicitly NOT-WORTH-IT

- **Adding meta-commentary that cites the venue's own review criteria.** It reads as reviewer-gaming and the argument is stronger on the merits [53].
- **Hunting for a citation that owns LCB paralysis.** After sweeping the vocabulary, Yarotsky is the closest and is a scoping obligation, not an owner. The frozen-cell evidence is the paper's own and is stronger unattributed than mis-attributed.
- **Retracting the K-sensitivity or the seven eliminations.** Both are real. What needs narrowing is the priority claim attached to the first and the scope statement attached to the second.
