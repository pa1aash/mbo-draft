**Executive summary.** The strongest publishable paper you can extract from this material is the confound-taxonomy paper (candidate D), and its core is genuinely new. Three of your nine findings survive an adversarial, fetched-and-grepped search of the primary literature as clean NONE FOUND verdicts: the crossed surrogate-by-optimizer factorial in offline model-based optimization (offline MBO — black-box optimization from a fixed dataset with no further oracle queries) is unowned, and the field's own most recent peer-reviewed survey concedes the gap in as many words [1]; the bidirectional smoothness manipulation (smooth the network, roughen the Gaussian process) is unowned not merely within offline MBO but across the entire Bayesian-optimization and kernel-methods literature [12]; and the de-confounding *direction* — an audit whose corrected scalar effect size (η²_surr, the fraction of outcome variance attributable to surrogate choice) grows from 0.37 to 0.405 rather than shrinking — has no precedent inside machine learning or computer science [28][29][31]. ## N1 - Confound Taxonomy: Prior-Art Overlap and the Offline-MBO Residual

### A. What prior work owns: the reality-check shape

Concede the shape openly, because conceding it is what makes the residual credible. The move "name a set of unreported implementation choices, give a protocol that controls them, show that a published ranking reverses" is a recognized and accepted genre, and three papers own it tightly. Henderson et al. own it most closely: they audit continuous-control deep reinforcement learning by isolating one under-reported factor at a time (network architecture, reward scale, random seeds, environment dynamics, codebase) and show each alone flips which algorithm looks best, concluding that "implementation differences which are often not reflected in publications can have dramatic impacts on performance" [4]. Ferrari Dacrema et al. run the same shape in recommender systems, naming "(i) weak baselines; (ii) establishment of weak methods as new baselines; and (iii) difficulties in comparing or reproducing results across papers," then re-running with properly tuned baselines and finding six of seven neural methods lose to simple classical algorithms [5]. Musgrave et al. do it in deep metric learning, naming unfair comparisons, misleading metrics, and test-set feedback, proposing a corrected protocol, and finding that "state of the art loss functions perform marginally better than, and sometimes on par with, classic methods" [6]. A reviewer who has read any of these will not accept "we ran a reality check" as a contribution in itself.

### B. What none of them owns: the offline-MBO residual

But the shape is a template, not a possession of the specific instantiation. Two things in your N1 are absent from every one of these papers. First, the machinery: not one of Henderson, Ferrari Dacrema, or Musgrave computes a variance decomposition. There is no η² anywhere in that literature — no analog of "fixing target scaling alone moves η²_surr from 0.367 to 0.283" or "fixing target scaling and candidate protocol together nets UP to 0.405." Their outputs are qualitative re-rankings and single post-hoc "marginal" verdicts; yours is a two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA — a partition of total outcome variance into main effects and interaction) that attributes a specific fraction of the headline to each factor and to their interaction. Second, the confound *vocabulary* is domain-locked to each source. Henderson's confounds are reward scaling and MuJoCo stochasticity; Musgrave's are embedding dimensionality and augmentation. None of them has an analog of target scaling of a regression surrogate (your ensemble regressed on raw targets spanning −2613 to +36 while both GPs z-scored), a candidate/oracle-selection asymmetry between optimizers (two propose 256 designs and report the oracle-selected best 128 while the third proposes 128), an ensemble-size K ablation on a surrogate's own uncertainty quality, or a shared-β acquisition term whose effective conservatism differs by surrogate σ-scale.

The interpretive beat the sources do not state: a reality-check genre matures by migrating from field to field, and each migration is publishable precisely when the confounds are *field-specific* rather than generic. Henderson was not rejected for re-using the shape of earlier reproducibility work; he was accepted for naming the RL-specific confounds. Your paper occupies exactly that slot for offline MBO — the first field-specific confound set with a variance-attribution method the genre has never used. **N1 verdict: the shape is PRIOR WORK FOUND (Henderson, Ferrari Dacrema, Musgrave); the five-confound offline-MBO set plus the η² decomposition is the residual, and it is NONE FOUND.** That residual is the spine of candidate D.

## N2 - K-Contingency of the Surrogate-Class Ranking

The claim under test is sharper than "ensemble accuracy depends on how many members you average" — that is textbook. Your claim is that the *cross-class ranking* (deep ensemble versus exact GP) flips as a function of ensemble size K, and that the field-standard K=5, cited from Lakshminarayanan et al. [23], is the setting where the ensemble is weakest, with the task-normalized ensemble scoring 0.95 / 0.52 / 0.32 / 0.18 at K = 2 / 3 / 5 / 10, so that at K=2 the ensemble marginal (0.95) exceeds the exact GP's (0.846).

No located paper reports K-dependence of a surrogate-*class* ranking. The exact queries run were: "ensemble size dependence surrogate model comparison Bayesian optimization ranking"; "number of ensemble members Bayesian optimization ranking changes"; "ensemble vs Gaussian process surrogate size Bayesian optimization comparison"; "deep ensemble ablation number of networks Bayesian optimization surrogate outperforms Gaussian process"; and "offline model-based optimization surrogate ensemble size ablation Design-Bench." Abe et al. — the query's own "nearest known" — compare an M-network ensemble against a single larger network and find a capacity story (a single big model reproduces the ensemble's gains), but never against a GP, never in an acquisition context, and never as a ranking (0 hits for "Bayesian optimization," "acquisition function," "rank") [24]. Ghasemipour et al. sweep ensemble size within one Q-ensemble class in offline RL, never against a competing class [27].

The honesty this section demands, and the reason it strengthens rather than weakens the paper: the single closest analog found the *opposite*. Li/Rudner/Wilson ran their own ensemble-size ablation inside a controlled cross-surrogate Bayesian-optimization comparison and reported "the different ensembles perform similarly across many experiments, showing the robustness of our results to this hyperparameter" [3] — but their sweep spans only K ∈ {5, 10}, never reaching your K=2 flip. So the observation is genuinely unreported (NONE FOUND) and, crucially, is *not* a contradiction of L/R/W: the two probe disjoint K ranges. The load-bearing caveat is that your K-decline runs monotonically *backward* from Lakshminarayanan's monotonic improvement-with-M [23] and from L/R/W's flatness [3], which makes the most parsimonious reading of the K=2 spike a small-sample σ-estimation artifact — the standard deviation across two members is an extremely noisy estimator, and a lucky K=2 draw feeding a lower-confidence-bound acquisition (LCB, μ − βσ) is a mundane confound. **N2 verdict: NONE FOUND, but claim the *observation*, not a *law*.** Present K as one of the five confounds that must be controlled — its natural home is candidate D — and explicitly decline to sell it as a discovered mechanism, or L/R/W's robustness finding will be turned against you.

## N3 - Unmatched Effective Pessimism

The claim: because a deep ensemble's σ and a GP's σ live on structurally different scales, a single nominal β applied to both delivers different *effective* conservatism, so the LCB acquisition was never actually matched across surrogate classes.

The general principle that nominal pessimism can diverge from effective pessimism is owned. Ghasemipour et al. state it cleanly — "shared pessimistic targets can paradoxically lead to value estimates that are effectively optimistic" — and it is the nearest existing articulation of the shape [27]. But the mechanism is different in kind: their divergence comes from target sharing across Bellman backups *within one Q-ensemble class*, not from σ-scale mismatch *across* surrogate classes, and their paper never varies surrogate class at all (0 hits for "Gaussian process," "surrogate," "Bayesian optimization") [27]. The premise that different surrogate classes genuinely produce differently behaved σ is itself established — SNGP proves a GP-RBF posterior variance is distance-aware while deep ensembles are not [16] — but that is background support for N3's premise, not a statement of N3's claim.

The place a reviewer would look first is the foundational Bayesian-optimization calibration literature, and it does not own this. GP-UCB's β_t schedule (Srinivas et al.) is a confidence-interval-width schedule indexed by round t, confidence δ, and kernel/decision-space quantities, derived by a union bound over rounds to bound cumulative regret for *one* evolving GP posterior [26]. Every argument to β_t is intrinsic to a single surrogate; a grep returns 0 hits for "surrogate," "ensemble," "different models," or "calibrat" [26]. The classic literature treats "the surrogate" as a fixed input to the calibration problem, never as a free variable compared against a structurally different surrogate under one shared β.

The committed reading: the honest verdict here is **PARTIAL**, and saying so is a strength, not a retreat. Concede that the abstract statistical fact — two differently scaled uncertainty estimators cannot share one confidence multiplier without recalibration — is owned in spirit by conformal/calibration work and by Ghasemipour's within-class analog. Claim only the offline-MBO acquisition-comparison *application*: that in a surrogate-class benchmark using LCB, a fixed β silently confounds the comparison, and that naming and correcting this is unreported. That application is NONE FOUND, and it is a legitimate member of the five-confound set even though the underlying principle is not itself new.

## N4 - Distance-Aware Uncertainty and the Implicit Trust Region

This finding splits into two claims that the evidence separates cleanly, and the split is what protects you.

**Claim (a): a properly specified GP's posterior variance grows away from the training data, while standard deep ensembles do not.** This is owned, and there is no point contesting it. SNGP formalizes "input distance awareness" and proves a GP-RBF posterior variance "increases monotonically toward 1 as x* moves further away from X_IND," while showing ensembles and MC-Dropout are governed by distance-to-decision-boundary instead [16]. DUQ confirms the ensemble failure mode empirically on the genuinely spatial two-moons dataset: "Deep Ensembles is uncertain only along the decision boundary, and certain elsewhere" [17]. Cite these as motivation, not as your own mechanism.

**Claim (b): that variance growth, funneled through LCB, is what wins the offline-MBO comparison by acting as an implicit trust region.** This narrower synthesis is NONE FOUND. Queries run in the source set: "trust region" ∧ "LCB"; "implicit trust region"; "posterior variance" ∧ "acquisition function"; "distance aware" ∧ "Bayesian optimization" — all return nothing beyond the distance-aware background. The nearest paper is the one that argues *against* the mechanism being automatic: TuRBO had to build an *explicit* hyperrectangular trust region with a hand-tuned success/failure counter precisely because unchecked global GP-variance growth under a myopic acquisition "results in an overemphasized exploration and a failure to exploit promising areas" [18]. TuRBO uses Thompson sampling, not LCB, and never claims the acquisition alone produces a trust region [18].

Two scope points decide how to use this. First, a caveat a reviewer might raise — that deep-kernel GPs can fail to get variance growth (feature collapse) — does not apply to your surrogates: your exact GP and SVGP (sparse variational GP — a scalable GP approximation using inducing points) use vanilla Matérn kernels directly on raw inputs, with no learned feature extractor, so the fragile case never arises and the SNGP property holds. Second, and more consequential, your own β=0 control points against a σ-mediated story: with the pessimism term removed the GP-ensemble gap does not collapse. The committed reading: **PARTIAL — the distance-aware mechanism is owned (SNGP); the LCB-implicit-trust-region synthesis and its offline-MBO instantiation are unclaimed.** The strategic move is to relocate the mechanism from σ to the posterior *mean* smoothness, which dovetails with N5 and N7 and is where candidate C's real (if unrun) claim lives. Do not assert the implicit-trust-region mechanism as your explanation; your own control undercuts it, and a reviewer who knows TuRBO will say so.

## N5 - NTK / Spectral Bias and the Finite-Width Objection

This is the strongest theoretical objection to a mechanism paper, and the honest handling of it is what separates a defensible submission from a rejected one. The adversarial reading: infinite-width networks *are* Gaussian processes (Jacot's Proposition 1; Lee's infinite-width-to-GP result), and finite networks are spectrally biased toward smooth, low-frequency functions (Rahaman); therefore a "jagged ensemble posterior mean" might be a finite-width or under-training artifact rather than a surrogate-class property [20][21][22]. Jacot even shows empirically that "for the wider network, the NTK shows less variance and is smoother" [20], which is direct width-produces-smoothness evidence.

Two facts blunt the objection without dismissing it. First, your K-sweep is not valid evidence for the finite-width reading, and presenting it as such is a category error a Bayesian-deep-learning reviewer will catch in one line: ensemble cardinality K (how many members you average) is not per-member width n (how many hidden units each has). None of Jacot, Lee, or Rahaman mentions ensembles at all (0 hits for "ensemble" in each) [20][21][22]; increasing K does not make any member wider. Worse for the finite-width story, your K-sweep runs monotonically *backward* — more members give a *worse* ensemble — the opposite sign from a "more capacity smooths it out" prediction. Second, the objection is already substantially answered by the nearest analog. L/R/W ran a K-fixed architecture-*size* test (their smaller architecture) and reported that "even with the smaller architecture, we find that the relative performance of the surrogate models mostly remains consistent" [3] — shrinking the net did not rescue the ensemble ranking. And L/R/W's own diagnosis of ensemble weakness is not width but data scarcity: "in the low data regime there are fewer settings of parameters that give rise to easily discoverable basins of attraction, making it harder to find diverse solutions" [3] — a domain-matched mechanism, since offline-MBO datasets sit in exactly the small-data regime.

The committed reading: **PARTIAL — the finite-width objection is theoretically real but is (i) not evidenced by your K-sweep and (ii) already answered by an existing architecture-size ablation.** The consequence for candidate choice is decisive: the objection is answerable only with an experiment your paper does not run — a clean width ablation holding K fixed and sweeping hidden units — so the finite-width thread favors the taxonomy paper (name width/data-scarcity as a confound worth controlling) over the mechanism paper (assert surrogate smoothness as a demonstrated causal class property). If you want candidate C, this section names the missing experiment you must run first.

## N6 - The Crossed Surrogate x Optimizer Factorial

This is the load-bearing novelty claim, and it is the cleanest NONE FOUND in the audit. The requirement is specific: a controlled grid crossing a shared set of surrogate *classes* against a shared set of *optimizers*, both manipulated, under one evaluation protocol, with a two-way variance decomposition, in offline MBO. One counterexample would be fatal; none exists.

### A. The field names the gap for you

The most valuable single sentence in the corpus is from Kim et al.'s TMLR 2026 survey — the field's most recent, peer-reviewed, Bengio-co-authored review — which states that "existing benchmarks often emphasize overall optimization performance without clarifying whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance" [1]. This is not the absence of a counterexample; it is the field certifying, one AAAI cycle before your submission, that the exact attribution question a crossed factorial answers is *open*. The same survey independently corroborates your Design-Bench null, noting that benchmarks like TF-Bind-8 "offer overly constrained search spaces where even simple gradient ascent methods can achieve impressive results, making it difficult to distinguish the performance of more sophisticated algorithms" [1]. Note the citation trap: this is Kim et al., TMLR 2026 (camera-ready venue), not "2025" — the arXiv v1 date is 2025 but the certified publication is January 2026.

### B. Every candidate paper fixes one axis or bundles both

The five named candidates were fetched and full-text grepped for "factorial," "ANOVA," "crossed," "two-way," and "variance decomposition"; all returned zero. L/R/W crosses surrogate classes with the acquisition held fixed to Monte-Carlo Expected Improvement for every problem, and it is online BO, not offline MBO (0 hits for "offline") — the *transpose* of your design, not your design [3]. Chemingui et al.'s PGS varies only the optimizer axis (CQL versus IQL versus standard RL as the search policy) while holding the surrogate fixed at a single vanilla MLP, and even imports its baseline numbers wholesale from other papers rather than running a controlled protocol [2]. Tan et al.'s RaM varies five surrogates with gradient ascent *fixed* and computes a Spearman rank-correlation, not a variance decomposition [11]. Design-Bench compares eight end-to-end method bundles, each welding one surrogate to one optimizer (BO-qEI is the only GP baseline and is never re-paired with CMA-ES or REINFORCE); the word "surrogate" never even appears (0 hits), and there is no ANOVA [8].

### C. Name the nearest misses before a reviewer does

Two adjacent papers come closer than the offline-MBO candidates and must be pre-empted. Hutter et al.'s functional ANOVA (fANOVA — a variance decomposition of algorithm performance across hyperparameters) finds that the model-class choice explains 31–58% of Auto-WEKA's performance variance — "the most important hyperparameter was the model class used" — which is the closest "model class as a variance-decomposed factor" result in existence [9]. But it is one-way: all fANOVA runs use data "based on data from one SMAC run," so the search method is fixed and never crossed as a second axis [9]. Liang et al. is structurally even closer — a genuinely crossed grid of surrogate models (RF, five GP kernels, GP-ARD) against acquisition functions (EI, PI, LCB_λ) across five materials domains, mirroring the shape of {ensemble, GP, SVGP} × {gradient ascent, perturbation, CMA-ES} — with the LCB form written out as "LCB_λ(x) = μ̂(x) + λσ̂(x)" [10]. It fails N6 on exactly one axis: no variance-decomposition statistic of any kind (0 hits for ANOVA/η²/factorial), only descriptive "acceleration/enhancement" metrics and ranking, and it is online closed-loop BO, not offline MBO [10].

The interpretive beat: that both nearest misses exist and both fall short in the *same* way — everyone varies the axes, nobody runs the two-way variance decomposition, and nobody does it in offline MBO — is itself the evidence that the gap is structural rather than an artifact of incomplete search. **N6 verdict: NONE FOUND.** State the residual precisely as "a crossed surrogate-class × optimizer factorial with a two-way ANOVA/η² decomposition, in offline MBO," and cite Hutter and Liang defensively as the one-way and the descriptive-online precedents you improve on.

## N7 - Bidirectional Smoothness Manipulation

The N7 claim is the highest-ceiling novelty in the paper: manipulate surrogate smoothness in *both* directions — smooth the network and roughen the GP — to identify smoothness itself, rather than surrogate-class identity, as the causal axis of the performance gap. The verdict is NONE FOUND at the broadest scope searched, and this is not a within-offline-MBO verdict but a within-the-entire-BO-and-kernel-methods-literature verdict.

Three half-literatures each do part of what N7 requires, and none combines them. Within-GP smoothness manipulation is common: ROOT varies GP length-scale and kernel parameters, but only as a data-augmentation device to generate synthetic training functions, never as a roughen-the-GP causal arm [15]; MS-DDEO selects among a pool of fixed-smoothness RBFN tiers for model selection, never touching a GP or making a causal claim (and it is closed-access, so this is a paraphrase-level rule-out) [14]. Cross-surrogate-class comparison is common: Lim et al. directly compare GP against neural-network-ensemble surrogates for BO on materials data — the exact class pairing N7 cares about — and is the single closest genuine hit anywhere in the sweep [12]. And smoothness-as-a-regularized-mechanism is a live research line: IGNITE penalizes neural-network surrogate sharpness with a generalization bound ("reducing surrogate sharpness on the offline dataset provably reduces its generalized sharpness on unseen data"), and its ICML 2024 sibling and NeurIPS 2025 descendant continue it [13]. But IGNITE smooths a network only, always downward, and never touches a GP [13].

The nearest miss is worth quoting because it shows exactly how much room remains. Lim et al. offer a smoothness explanation for the GP's eventual win — the advantage "could be due to the ability of GP to smoothly map out the uncertainty manifolds" — but this is a hedged, post-hoc, one-sentence hypothesis in a conclusion; the only deliberate manipulation in the paper is *widening the GP's own length-scale bounds* to avoid overfitting (a single-direction hyperparameter-fit sweep), the neural ensemble receives no smoothness manipulation at all, and no experiment is designed to isolate smoothness as the causal variable [12]. In roughly fifteen years of BO and GP-regression literature, the two half-literatures that would combine to produce N7 — within-GP smoothness sweeps and cross-class BO comparisons — both exist in volume, yet no paper stitches them into one controlled, bidirectional, causally interpreted experiment. **N7 verdict: NONE FOUND, "first at all," not merely "first in offline MBO."** The load-bearing qualifier, developed in the candidate section: this novelty is real but *conditional on running the manipulation the paper has not yet run*.

## N8 - Platform and Library-Version Dependence of Benchmark Results

The claim: an ensemble cell moves roughly 0.5 normalized units between macOS and Linux on TF-Bind-8 while the exact-GP cell matches to 1.000 and every synthetic cell reproduces to twelve decimal places cross-platform — so platform variance is of the same order as the method differences under study, on a Design-Bench grid whose whole span is roughly 1.0–2.5 and which the paper argues is null.

The general phenomenon — that execution-environment nondeterminism can produce variance comparable to the signals of interest — is owned in deep RL. Nagarajan et al. is the tightest analog: a controlled single-factor ablation holding seed, initialization, exploration, and codebase identical, in which GPU-operation nondeterminism alone produces a learning-curve variance that "looks similar to the curves for exploration and weight initialization," and they single it out because "the GPU is... the only source of nondeterminism that exists outside of the algorithm itself" [33]. Henderson's codebase experiments make the parallel point that reimplementation differences can dominate a nominal inter-algorithm effect [4]. Gundersen & Kjensmo, the AAAI-2018 reproducibility survey the query names, is weaker on this specific point: it only *cites* a caveat — "differences in software and hardware could have significant impact on results because of rounding errors in floating point arithmetic (Hong et al. 2013)" — drawn from a climate-model paper, and never measures a magnitude or compares it to method differences [34].

The committed reading: **PARTIAL — the phenomenon (environment variance of the same order as method signal) is PRIOR WORK FOUND in deep RL; the offline-MBO instantiation, the specific macOS-versus-Linux axis, and a stated same-order ratio are the residual, NONE FOUND.** The strategic value of this finding is that it converts your Design-Bench null from a weakness into a mechanism: if platform variance is of the same order as the cell differences, then a Friedman omnibus (a non-parametric test for differences across the grid) landing at p=0.69 with the whole grid inside one critical difference is *expected*, not anomalous. This is also the finding most likely to be undervalued by a Bayesian-optimization reviewer and most likely to be rewarded by a benchmarking/reproducibility reviewer — a fact that matters directly for venue targeting.

## N9 - The De-Confounding Direction: an Audit That Strengthens

This is the rhetorical asset, and it survives — but only in a narrow, precisely scoped form. Every reality-check paper the genre is built on runs the other way: the audit shrinks or vanishes the headline. Melis states the norm in his own conclusion, that once methodological care increases "effect sizes tend to be much smaller" [29]; his tuned LSTM overturns the claimed superiority of newer architectures, a shrink-to-reversal. Robinson, Glen & Lee reverse Mayr et al.'s "deep learning significantly outperforms all competing methods" to "SVM competitive with deep learning" — a clean shrink, with zero confound-removal framing (0 hits for "confound") [31]. Musgrave and Ferrari Dacrema shrink likewise [6][5]. Your result inverts this: five confounds controlled, and η²_surr *grew* from the published 0.37 to 0.405.

Sixteen adversarial queries across two independent sweeps — including "reanalysis larger effect size machine learning," "controlling confounder increased effect machine learning benchmark," "correction strengthened result benchmark evaluation," "Simpson's paradox confound control effect amplify machine learning," and "regression attenuation measurement error correction larger coefficient machine learning" — returned no ML/CS reality-check or reproducibility audit reporting a corrected scalar effect size exceeding its own published value. So the narrow claim is genuine: **NONE FOUND within ML/CS for the scalar-effect-size-grows-after-de-confounding shape.**

The discipline this section requires is to *not* overclaim. Two ML/CS partials must be pre-empted or a well-read reviewer discredits the whole point. Recht et al. is the first: absolute accuracy drops 3–15% (the shrink), but the model-to-model relative-gain slope exceeds 1 — "every percentage point of accuracy improvement on the original test set translates to a larger improvement on our new test sets... an increased advantage over earlier models" — which is the opposite of diminishing returns and rhetorically close to "the effect grew" [28]. It differs from your claim in the underlying statistic (a relative slope, not a variance-explained scalar audited against its own prior value), but a reviewer who knows Recht will raise it. The second is Agarwal et al., whose "the lack of statistically significant results does not demonstrate the absence of effect" means power analysis can reveal a hidden real effect [32]. Bressan supplies the closest full-shape precedent, but it is psychology and uses p-values not an effect-size statistic: controlling a stimulus-allocation confound restored a "failed" replication to significance and, under a stricter specification, "the results came out stronger" [30]. The committed framing: claim N9 in the exact scalar form — "no ML/CS de-confounding audit has been shown to produce a variance-explained effect-size statistic that exceeds its own originally published value" — cite Bressan as a one-line cross-disciplinary analog, and pre-empt Recht and Agarwal explicitly. The narrow claim is worth naming in the paper because it is the single differentiator that no other reality-check owns; the broad claim ("audits always shrink, we are the exception") is false and must not be made.

## Candidate Contributions: The One Sentence Each Owns (A / C / D)

The test: for each candidate paper, the one sentence a reviewer could not obtain from any prior paper — or a statement that no such sentence exists.

### A — Repaired Measurement

**The one sentence A owns:** "In offline model-based optimization, under a single shared evaluation protocol, a crossed surrogate-class × optimizer factorial attributes the performance variance overwhelmingly to the surrogate (η²_surr ≈ 0.37–0.405) and negligibly to the optimizer (η²_opt ≈ 0.01), the first ANOVA-quantified surrogate-versus-optimizer attribution in this setting." This sentence is genuinely unobtainable elsewhere: PGS fixes the surrogate and cannot produce a number for its effect [2]; L/R/W fixes the optimizer as a nuisance variable, and a fixed-nuisance design is logically incapable of estimating the fixed factor's effect size (holding the optimizer constant is silence, not evidence of η²_opt ≈ 0) [3]; Design-Bench and Tan bundle both axes [8][11]. A is therefore *under*-credited by any "L/R/W already did the surrogate comparison" objection. **The caveat that constrains A:** the *directional* gloss "the field innovates on the axis that doesn't matter" overclaims, because surrogate-over-acquisition primacy is decade-old doctrine — Shahriari et al. state "the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic" [35]. A can honestly say it is the first offline-MBO-specific, ANOVA-quantified *falsification of PGS's own motivating premise* (that offline BBO had neglected the search-strategy lever), not a refutation of field belief. And A must keep this literature claim separate from the still-unresolved internal-validity status of the η²_opt = 0.01 number itself, which is confounded by the unequal oracle budgets.

### C — Mechanism

**The one sentence C would own — conditional on an experiment not yet run:** "Surrogate smoothness, not surrogate-class identity, is the causal axis of the GP-versus-ensemble performance gap, demonstrated by manipulating it in both directions — regularizing the network toward smoothness and roughening the GP's kernel — and observing the gap track the manipulation." Per N7, this is NONE FOUND across the entire BO/kernel-methods literature; Lim only hypothesizes and never manipulates [12], and IGNITE manipulates one direction on one class [13]. So the ceiling is the highest of the three candidates — "first at all." But two constraints make C a bet, not a deliverable. First, the bidirectional manipulation that earns the sentence is, per the paper's own materials, planned but unrun — so the sentence is owned only *contingently*. Second, C's easiest evidence channel is dead: your β=0 control shows the gap survives removal of the σ term, so the mechanism cannot be σ-mediated (N4), and the finite-width version of the story is a category error your K-sweep cannot support (N5). C's viable mechanism is posterior-*mean* smoothness, for which σ-based controls are irrelevant and only the unrun manipulation can adjudicate. **Verdict: C's owned sentence exists and is the strongest in principle, but is unavailable until the manipulation is run.**

### D — Confound Taxonomy

**The one sentence D owns:** "We identify five previously unreported, individually decisive confounds specific to offline-MBO surrogate comparison (target scaling, candidate/oracle-selection asymmetry, optimizer trust-region tuning, ensemble size K, and β-σ pessimism mismatch), give the protocol that removes them, and show that removing them does not shrink the rewarded surrogate advantage but *strengthens* it (η²_surr 0.37 → 0.405)." No prior paper owns this composition. The genre-shape is conceded to Henderson, Ferrari Dacrema, and Musgrave [4][5][6]; the individual principles behind N3 and N4 are conceded to Ghasemipour and SNGP [27][16]; but the *offline-MBO-specific five-confound set*, the *η² variance-decomposition machinery* the genre has never used, and the *strengthening direction* that no ML/CS reality-check has ever reported [29][28][31] are new in combination. **Verdict: D's sentence exists, is fully available now (it depends on no unrun experiment), and rests on the largest number of independent NONE-FOUND and residual verdicts (N1-residual, N3-app, N6, N9-narrow).** D is the recommended paper: it has the safest evidence base, concedes what it must to look credible, and its strengthening direction is a genuine, nameable, one-of-a-kind differentiator.

## The Three Strongest Rejecting Citations

### 1. Li, Rudner & Wilson — ICLR 2024, arXiv:2305.20028 [3]

The single most dangerous citation, because it is the closest methodological analog and it pre-empts three of your findings at once. A hostile reviewer would say: "The controlled cross-surrogate comparison for Bayesian optimization already exists, and it is more thorough than this — it compares seven surrogate classes, diagnoses the ensemble's underperformance via a basin-of-attraction/data-scarcity mechanism, finds ensemble performance *robust* to model count (Fig. A.7), and shows the ranking is *invariant* to architecture size (App. D.1.2)." That threatens N2 (they found K-robustness), N5 (they answered the finite-width objection with an architecture-size ablation you did not run), and shaves the perceived size of the N6 gap (a crossed surrogate comparison for BO is not novel; only the offline setting and the two-way ANOVA are). The defense is exact scoping: L/R/W is *online* BO with the acquisition fixed, disjoint K range, no offline setting, and no variance decomposition — but you must make that scoping explicit and up front, because the reviewer who knows this paper will otherwise assume it covers you.

### 2. Shahriari, Swersky, Wang, Adams & de Freitas — Proc. IEEE 2016 (DOI:10.1109/JPROC.2015.2494218) [35]

The reversal-killer. If any part of the paper frames "surrogate matters more than optimizer" as overturning a field belief, this ~5,900-citation survey rejects it in one sentence: "the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic," restated in its conclusion as the surrogate's importance being primary and the acquisition's "secondary" [35]. The reviewer would say: "Your headline directional result is the organizing thesis of the most-cited BO survey ever written; it is a decade old." The defense is to never make the field-reversal claim — narrow it to falsifying PGS's local premise (candidate A) and lead with the confound taxonomy (candidate D) instead.

### 3. Agarwal, Schwarzer, Castro, Courville & Bellemare — NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2108.13264 [32]

The statistical-rigor rejecter, aimed at your Design-Bench null. Agarwal's thesis — "the lack of statistically significant results does not demonstrate the absence of effect," with Demšar's own rule of thumb of more than ten datasets for the very Friedman test you use — means your seven-task, p=0.69 omnibus is under-powered and cannot license a positive equivalence claim [32]. The reviewer would say: "You conclude the Design-Bench grid is indistinguishable, but with N=7 you are below the threshold for the test you ran; you have shown no *detectable* difference at this power, not equivalence." The defense converts the objection into a strength: present the Design-Bench result as "no detectable difference at this power," attach an explicit power/N specification, and carry N8's platform-variance finding as the *reason* the omnibus is null — implementation and platform variance of the same order as the method differences is exactly why nothing is distinguishable.

## AAAI-27 Venue Fit and Reviewer Pool

The AAAI-27 main technical track (abstracts due 21 July 2026, full papers 28 July 2026, Montréal, February 2027) asks for one mandatory primary keyword and up to five optional secondaries, and its own guidance is explicit that the primary topic should "identify the subarea to which the paper makes its main contribution... a reviewer who is an expert in that subarea will be positioned to evaluate the paper most effectively" [36]. The reviewer pool a paper summons is therefore a direct function of which candidate you submit, and the three candidates summon materially different pools.

- **Candidate D (confound taxonomy) — primary: `ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis`; secondaries: `ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification`, `SO: Algorithm Configuration & Sampling-based Search`.** This summons empirical-rigor and reproducibility reviewers — the community that produced and rewards Henderson, Musgrave, and Agarwal. This is the friendliest pool for D, because these reviewers treat a named confound set with a removal protocol and a variance decomposition as a contribution in itself, and the strengthening direction reads to them as a notable result rather than an anomaly. This is the recommended mapping.

- **Candidate A (repaired measurement / ANOVA attribution) — primary: `ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification` or `ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis`.** If A leads with the surrogate-versus-optimizer attribution, `ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification` summons BO/UQ specialists who will know Shahriari and L/R/W cold — a double-edged pool that will credit the ANOVA rigor but will also apply the two rejecting citations above most sharply. Routing A instead through the benchmarking keyword softens the doctrine objection at the cost of drawing reviewers less invested in the surrogate-comparison question.

- **Candidate C (mechanism) — primary: `ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification`, secondary `ML: Deep Learning Theory & Learning Theory`.** This summons exactly the reviewers most equipped to raise the NTK/spectral-bias objection (N5) and to demand the width ablation, and to notice that the bidirectional manipulation is unrun. C into this pool without the manipulation experiment is the highest-variance submission; C into this pool *with* the experiment is the highest-ceiling one.

The interpretive beat the page implies but does not state: the same body of work can be a benchmarking contribution or a Bayesian-learning contribution depending only on the primary keyword, and because your strongest evidence (the confound set, the platform variance, the strengthening direction) is benchmarking evidence while your riskiest claims (mechanism, implicit trust region) are Bayesian-learning claims, the keyword choice is not cosmetic — it selects whether the reviewers' priors align with your strongest material. D under the benchmarking keyword is the alignment that maximizes acceptance probability.

## What I Could Not Verify and Why

- **The exact β=0 control numbers (N4).** The query cites the GP-ensemble gap moving 0.51 → 0.47 with the pessimism term removed; an independent recomputation from the results file via the paper's own normalization helpers gave 0.504 → 0.511 (flat-to-slightly-up), not the cited ~0.04 shrink. Both agree on the decision-relevant direction (the gap does not collapse when σ is removed, which undercuts the σ-mediated mechanism), but the magnitude is unreconciled and the cited pair is **NOT VERIFIABLE** at the number level. This affects only how hard N4's σ-rejection can lean; it does not change the N4 verdict.

- **The corrected η²_opt = 0.01 (candidates A and the "reversal").** The strengthening headline (η²_surr 0.37 → 0.405) is confirmed as landed via an on-machine 30-seed run and can be asserted. The optimizer effect size behind the reversal is **provisional**: it is confounded by the unequal oracle budgets (256 versus 128 proposals) and by a trust-region constraint on gradient ascent that suppresses the one failure mode (gradient collapse) that would make optimizer choice matter, and those confounds are not yet fully removed. Treat "no one has made this attribution before" (verified) as separate from "and the number is 0.01" (not yet trustworthy).

- **MS-DDEO at the sentence level (N7).** The paper is fully closed-access (403 on the publisher page; null abstract on Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex; not open per Unpaywall), so its rule-out rests on secondary paraphrase, not a verbatim grep. The N7 verdict does not depend on it — MS-DDEO is a within-RBFN selection method that never touches a GP even on the paraphrase — but a reviewer who has institutional access could in principle surface a detail the paraphrase misses. Flagged as **NOT VERIFIABLE** at the verbatim level.

- **N9's scope boundary.** The NONE-FOUND verdict holds for ML/CS reality-check/reproducibility papers indexed by the academic APIs and web search as of this sweep. It does *not* extend to adjacent quantitative fields: the noncollapsibility and regression-attenuation literature in biostatistics establishes that covariate adjustment can mathematically increase an effect magnitude, so the phenomenon is not statistically surprising — it simply has not been instantiated on an ML/CS benchmark audit. If the paper generalizes N9 to "any empirical field," the search does not support it; the verified claim is narrowly "within ML/CS."

- **Forward-citation completeness (N7, N3).** Semantic Scholar was rate-limited (HTTP 429) throughout the sweeps, so forward-citation walks relied on OpenAlex and web search. Coverage of arXiv-indexed BO/kernel work is high, but non-arXiv workshop papers, theses, and non-English venues (relevant given MS-DDEO's evolutionary-computation home) were not exhaustively searched. A bidirectional smoothness manipulation buried in a non-English swarm-and-evolutionary-computation venue cannot be ruled out with the same confidence as the arXiv-indexed literature.

- **The internal reproducibility of the K=2 spike (N2).** Whether the K=2 ensemble result is stable across seeds is an internal question about the audited experiment, not a literature question, and could not be adjudicated from the primary sources. If K=2 does not replicate cleanly, the "small-sample σ-noise" reading in N2 is correct and the observation should be reported more cautiously; if it does replicate, it deserves a mechanistic explanation the current evidence cannot supply.
