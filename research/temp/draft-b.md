# The Steelman Rejection: How Much of the Offline-MBO Factorial Is Already Owned

**Executive summary — the harsh-but-fair bottom line, stated first.** Read this paper the way its most dangerous AAAI reviewer will: as a competently executed but incremental ablation study whose findings, examined one at a time, are largely already owned. Under that reading the arithmetic is unforgiving. Of the nine claims, only three survive as genuinely unclaimed in the primary literature — the crossed surrogate x optimizer factorial with a variance decomposition in offline model-based optimization (offline MBO: optimizing a design against a static dataset with no further oracle queries), the bidirectional smoothness manipulation, and the narrow scalar-effect-size form of the "audit that strengthens." Everything else is either owned outright or pre-empted so heavily that presenting it as new invites a rejection. The reality-check *shape* — name the confounds, give the protocol that removes them, show the ranking reverses — is a decade-old genre whose canonical instance, Henderson et al.'s deep-RL audit, was published at this very conference [1]. The paper's own headline that surrogate choice dominates optimizer choice is not a reversal of anyone's belief: it is the organizing thesis of the most-cited Bayesian-optimization survey in existence, which states that "the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic" [2]. The single closest methodological analog, Li, Rudner & Wilson's controlled surrogate-class comparison for Bayesian optimization, not only exists but found the *opposite* ensemble-size result and pre-emptively answered the finite-width objection the paper's mechanism story would need [3]. The general statistical principle behind the "unmatched pessimism" claim is textbook uncertainty-quantification material [4], and the "lower confidence bound acts as a local search region" mechanism was formally derived at NeurIPS the same season [5]. Even the field's own 2026 survey concedes the attribution question is open [6] — which helps the paper, but only for the one contribution (D, the confound taxonomy) it should have led with. **The strongest publishable contribution available is D, narrowly scoped: the named set of five offline-MBO-specific confounds, the protocol that removes them, and the de-confounding *direction* in which the corrected effect grows rather than shrinks. It is emphatically not the mechanism paper (C), which rests on a category error and a dead measurement channel, and it is not the dramatic "reversal," which re-derives folklore.** What follows makes that case claim by claim, then names the three citations that could sink the paper and states exactly what each would say.

## N1 - Confound Taxonomy: Prior-Art Overlap and the Offline-MBO Residual

The confound taxonomy is the paper's most defensible contribution and simultaneously the one most exposed to a genre objection. Five unreported implementation choices are named and measured under control: target scaling (the ensemble regressed on raw targets spanning -2613 to +36 while both Gaussian-process surrogates z-scored their targets), the candidate/oracle protocol (two optimizers proposed 256 designs and reported the oracle-selected best 128 while the third proposed 128), optimizer tuning (a trust-region constraint on gradient ascent), ensemble size K, and effective pessimism (a fixed beta=2 across surrogate classes whose posterior-standard-deviation magnitudes differ). The move — name each confound, give the protocol that neutralizes it, show the ranking changes — is a recognizable and, crucially, *named* genre. A reviewer does not need to reach far: the template was written for deep reinforcement learning at this very conference. Henderson et al. state the thesis almost verbatim: "We find that implementation differences which are often not reflected in publications can have dramatic impacts on performance" [1], and their paper is organized as exactly this paper is — a sequence of controlled single-factor experiments (network architecture, reward scale, random seeds, environment dynamics, codebase reimplementation), each shown to flip which method looks best [1]. The same shape recurs across at least four other well-cited audits: Ferrari Dacrema et al. diagnose recommender-system progress as an artifact of "(i) weak baselines; (ii) establishment of weak methods as new baselines; and (iii) difficulties in comparing or reproducing results across papers" [7]; Musgrave et al. rebuild the deep-metric-learning protocol and find "state of the art loss functions perform marginally better than, and sometimes on par with, classic methods" [8]; Lucic et al. show that for GANs "most models can reach similar scores with enough hyperparameter optimization and random restarts" [9]; and Agarwal et al. supply the statistical-methodology version, a three-part taxonomy of evaluation confounds with a corrected protocol and demonstrated ranking reversals [10].

The honest concession the paper must make up front is that the *shape* of the contribution is owned five times over. Conceding this is not a weakness — it is what makes the residual credible. And the residual is real but narrow. None of these five audits performs a two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA: a statistical decomposition attributing outcome variance to each experimental factor and their interaction) yielding an eta-squared (the fraction of variance explained by a factor); Henderson, Ferrari Dacrema, Musgrave, and Lucic all report qualitative ranking instability, not a quantified per-confound contribution, and Agarwal reports confidence-interval overlaps, not an effect-size attribution [1][7][8][9][10]. And none names anything resembling offline MBO's specific confound vocabulary: target scaling of a regression surrogate, candidate/oracle selection asymmetry between optimizers, an ensemble-size ablation on uncertainty quality, or a beta-pessimism mismatch across surrogate classes. The interpretive point a rejecting reviewer will nonetheless press is that a residual defined as "the same genre, but with an ANOVA and a domain-specific confound list" is a residual of *composition and bookkeeping*, not of kind. The paper is not the first to audit a subfield; it is the first to audit *this* subfield with *this* statistical instrument. Whether that clears the AAAI main-track novelty bar depends entirely on how much weight the specific five-confound set and the strengthening direction (N9) can carry — because the meta-move cannot carry any.

## N2 - K-Contingency of the Surrogate-Class Ranking

Here the steelman verdict is the most damaging kind: NONE FOUND, but pre-empted by the one paper the reviewer is most likely to already know. The claim is that the ensemble-versus-GP ranking is contingent on ensemble size K, and that the field-standard K=5 is precisely where the ensemble is weakest — the paper's own sweep gives task-normalized ensemble scores of 0.95, 0.52, 0.32, 0.18 at K = 2, 3, 5, 10, so that at K=2 the ensemble marginal (0.95) exceeds the exact GP's (0.846). No prior work reports K-dependence of a surrogate-*class* ranking; that specific observation is unclaimed. But the closest methodological analog in the field ran the identical ablation and reached the opposite conclusion. Li, Rudner & Wilson, comparing roughly seven surrogate classes for Bayesian optimization, swept ensemble size and reported that "the different ensembles perform similarly across many experiments, showing the robustness of our results to this hyperparameter" [3]. Their sweep spans K in {5, 10} and so does not directly touch K=2 — which is exactly why the paper's finding is *technically* new — but a reviewer holding [3] will read the paper's headline as sitting inside a region a stronger paper already characterized as flat, with the entire effect driven by a single untested endpoint.

The direction of the sweep makes this worse, not better. Lakshminarayanan et al., the very paper cited for the "standard K=5" convention, report the monotonic opposite: "as M increases, both the accuracy and the quality of predictive uncertainty improve significantly," and they recommend M=5 only as a practical default, never as an accuracy optimum [11]. So the paper's monotonic *decline* with K runs backward from both of the two nearest empirical anchors. The most parsimonious reading of a 0.95 score at K=2 is not a discovered law of surrogate comparison but small-sample noise: the standard deviation across two ensemble members is a wildly high-variance estimator, and when that noisy sigma feeds a lower-confidence-bound acquisition (LCB: select designs maximizing mu minus beta times sigma), a lucky two-member draw on a modest suite of seven synthetic and seven benchmark tasks is an ordinary statistical artifact. Abe et al. — the query's own "nearest known" — do not rescue the claim either; they show ensemble gains are a capacity effect a single larger network reproduces (Pearson r = 0.81 in-distribution, 0.76 out-of-distribution), but they never compare against a GP and never rank surrogate classes [12]. The committed rejection reading: N2 is a real but fragile observation whose novelty is pre-empted in strength by [3] and whose mechanism is most likely noise. The paper should report it as a curiosity requiring a seed-stability check, not lean on it — and it certainly cannot recycle it as finite-width evidence for the mechanism story, as N5 shows.

## N3 - Unmatched Effective Pessimism

The claim is that a shared beta across surrogate classes with different sigma scales delivers different *effective* conservatism, so the LCB acquisition was never matched across the surrogates being compared. The narrow, offline-MBO-specific application is unclaimed. But the general statistical principle — that applying one fixed confidence multiplier to differently-scaled uncertainty estimators yields unequal effective coverage — is owned cleanly, and by exactly the kind of paper a Bayesian-learning reviewer keeps at hand. Dewolf, De Baets & Waegeman benchmark four classes of prediction-interval method (Bayesian, ensemble, direct-interval, conformal) at one shared nominal confidence level and find that "the uncalibrated models do not approximately saturate the validity constraint. They either underestimate the uncertainty or produce overconservative prediction intervals" [4]. That is N3's premise demonstrated empirically across method classes: one nominal setting, structurally different uncertainty estimators, mismatched effective conservatism. They even prescribe the standard fix — "normalize the nonconformity measure by a dispersion function sigma" [4] — which is the repair the paper's protocol implicitly performs.

Being fair to the paper: Dewolf et al. never cross into an acquisition function, a sequential-decision loop, or "effective pessimism" as a search-quality concept; the words "acquisition," "Bayesian optimization," and "pessimism" appear zero times in their text [4]. So the offline-MBO instantiation is genuinely unclaimed, and the classic single-surrogate GP-UCB confidence schedule (Srinivas et al., ICML 2010) is likewise about calibrating beta for one evolving posterior, not comparing across classes. The rejection reading nonetheless holds the line: the *insight* in N3 — differently-scaled uncertainty estimators cannot share a confidence multiplier without recalibration — is a known statistical fact [4], and dressing it in acquisition-function language does not make the underlying claim new. The paper can honestly say "no one has named this specific miscalibration inside an offline-MBO acquisition comparison," but it cannot claim to have discovered the principle. This is the strongest single confound in the taxonomy precisely because its offline-MBO framing is unclaimed; it is also the confound most vulnerable to a reviewer who reads N3 as re-labeling conformal-prediction folklore.

## N4 - Distance-Aware Uncertainty and the Implicit Trust Region

N4 is where the paper's mechanism ambitions collide hardest with prior work, and where the steelman verdict is PARTIAL with the interesting half already owned. The hypothesis at risk is that the GP wins because its posterior variance grows away from the data, so the LCB acquisition behaves as an implicit trust region. Two distinct claims are bundled here and both cut against the paper. First, the raw distance-aware-variance mechanism is textbook: SNGP formally proves a GP-RBF posterior variance "increases monotonically toward 1 as x* moves further away" from the training manifold, and shows deep ensembles and dropout networks lack this property, being governed instead by distance to the decision boundary [13]; DUQ confirms it empirically on the two-moons dataset, where "Deep Ensembles is uncertain only along the decision boundary, and certain elsewhere" [14]. Second — and this is the citation the paper's N4 narrative did not anticipate — the specific optimization mechanism it wants to claim, that a confidence-bound acquisition confines its own search because the bound is narrow near data and grows away from it, was derived formally and published the same season. Fan et al. prove that minimizing a UCB-style bound "can be viewed as local strategy" precisely because the bound "is small only near the sampled point, and increases as it moves further away" [5]. That is N4's causal chain, stated with a supporting theorem, outside the four-paper distance-awareness set the paper likely surveyed.

What is left genuinely unclaimed is only the *label*: no located paper calls a bare LCB acquisition an "implicit trust region" (Fan uses "local strategy," and the phrase "trust region" appears in his paper only as citations to explicit-trust-region baselines) [5]. A naming novelty is thin ground for a mechanism contribution. And the paper's own control demolishes the mechanism it would name: with the sigma term removed entirely (beta=0), the GP-versus-ensemble gap barely moves — which means the advantage is not sigma-mediated at all. If the edge is the surrogate's posterior *mean*, not its uncertainty calibration, then the entire distance-aware / implicit-trust-region framing is a motivating decoration, not an explanation. A reviewer who knows Ovadia et al. will also flag a fairness problem in any "ensembles are confidently wrong far from data" gloss: Ovadia measured shift as corruption severity, not spatial distance, and found that on image OOD tasks "Ensembles consistently have high accuracy but also high entropy on OOD data" [15] — i.e., ensembles are among the *better* shift estimators there, so the toy-geometry failure in [13][14] must be scoped to low-dimensional spatial extrapolation and not overstated. Net rejection reading: N4's mechanism is owned by [5][13][14] at the level that matters, the naming is a decoration, and the paper's own beta=0 result argues the mechanism is false. This is the clearest evidence that candidate C should not be the paper's identity.

## N5 - NTK / Spectral Bias and the Finite-Width Objection

N5 is the objection that should worry the authors most, because it is unanswerable with the experiments currently in the paper, and because the reviewer pool for a Bayesian-learning submission will raise it reflexively. The adversarial reading: infinite-width networks *are* Gaussian processes, finite networks are spectrally biased toward smooth functions, so a "jagged ensemble posterior mean" is plausibly a finite-width or under-training artifact rather than a surrogate-class property. The theory is among the most robustly reproduced in deep learning. Jacot et al. prove infinite-width networks converge to GPs and show empirically that "for the wider network, the NTK shows less variance and is smoother" [16] — the neural tangent kernel (NTK) being the fixed kernel that governs a wide network's training dynamics. Lee et al. show finite-width trained-network accuracy "approaches that of the corresponding GP with increasing layer width" [17]. Rahaman et al. establish that networks "favour low frequency ones — hence they exhibit a bias towards smooth functions," and their own ablation finds width helps fit high frequencies (though "considerably weaker" than depth) [18]. Stacked together, these say a sufficiently wide, well-trained network *should* be smooth; a jagged finite ensemble is then evidence of under-parameterization or under-training — a fixable implementation confound, favoring candidate D — not an intrinsic class property, which candidate C needs.

The paper's instinct is to cite its own K-sweep (K=2 scoring three times K=5) as evidence for the finite-width reading. This is a category error a Bayesian-deep-learning reviewer will catch in one line: K is *ensemble cardinality* — how many independently trained, equally narrow members are averaged — while the NTK and spectral-bias results are statements about *per-member width* n. Increasing K does not widen any member; none of Jacot, Lee, or Rahaman so much as mentions ensembles [16][17][18]. Worse, the mechanism most native to the paper's small-data regime predicts the opposite surface phenomenon. Li, Rudner & Wilson explain ensemble underperformance in Bayesian optimization by basin-of-attraction collapse under data scarcity — "With minimal training data, the loss landscape is relatively smooth, and separately-trained models are less diverse" [3] — a failure of *too little* diversity, not landscape jaggedness. Fort et al. independently locate ensemble diversity in random-initialization mode selection, using the words "width" and "capacity" zero times [24], so the width story and the diversity story are orthogonal axes the paper conflates. And decisively, L/R/W already ran the de-confounded version of the paper's missing experiment: their Appendix D.1.2 reruns every surrogate, including the K=5 ensemble, at a smaller architecture and reports that "even with the smaller architecture... the relative performance of the surrogate models mostly remains consistent" — the ensemble-versus-GP ranking is invariant to architecture size [3]. That is the finite-width objection pre-emptively answered against the paper: shrinking the net does not rescue the ensemble. Dai et al. confirm that width ablations inside a BO surrogate do exist and that wider networks reduce regret — but only for their own single-network surrogate, never applied to the ensemble baseline [25], which is precisely the controlled width sweep (fixed K, swept per-member width) the paper would need and does not run. The committed rejection reading: the finite-width objection is live, the paper's K-sweep is not valid evidence against it, and the one experiment that would settle it has already been run by a competitor and came out the wrong way for a class-property mechanism.

## N6 - The Crossed Surrogate x Optimizer Factorial

This is where the steelman must concede — and conceding cleanly is what makes the concession credible. No prior work runs a crossed surrogate-class x optimizer-class factorial with a variance decomposition in offline MBO. The five candidate owners each fail on a specific, checkable axis. Li, Rudner & Wilson hold the acquisition function fixed ("We use Monte-Carlo based Expected Improvement... as our acquisition function for all problems") and work in online, not offline, BO [3]. Chemingui et al.'s policy-guided gradient search varies only the optimizer, holding the surrogate fixed at a single MLP throughout [26]. Tan et al.'s learning-to-rank study varies five surrogates with gradient ascent held fixed and computes rank correlations, not an ANOVA [27]. And the field's own 2026 survey does not close the gap but *names* it: existing benchmarks, Kim et al. write, do not clarify "whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance" [6]. That the most current peer-reviewed survey certifies the question as open, one cycle before this submission, is the single strongest external support the paper has.

But a fair rejection does not stop at "no exact match." It names the near-neighbors that a reviewer will use to shrink the contribution to its true size. Two are decisive. Hutter, Hoos & Leyton-Brown's functional-ANOVA paper already decomposes performance variance and finds the model-class choice the single most important factor (31-58% of variance across datasets) — but within one fixed search method (SMAC), so it is a one-way decomposition, not a crossed factorial [19]. Liang et al. run the genuinely crossed grid the paper claims as novel — surrogate models (random forest, GPs with five kernels, GP-ARD) fully crossed with acquisition functions (EI, PI, LCB) across five materials domains — and compare them head to head; they simply report descriptive "acceleration/enhancement" metrics and rankings instead of an ANOVA [20]. So the residual, stated precisely and without inflation, is not "the first crossed comparison" (Liang [20]) and not "the first variance decomposition of model class" (Hutter [19]); it is "the first two-way ANOVA / eta-squared decomposition of a crossed surrogate x optimizer grid, situated in offline MBO." That is a real contribution of *composition* — Liang's crossed grid plus Hutter's variance instrument, transplanted into offline MBO — and a reviewer can reasonably ask whether combining two existing moves in a new subfield is an AAAI-main-track contribution or a workshop increment. The paper's best defense is that the offline-MBO scoping is not incidental: the static-dataset, no-further-queries setting is where the surrogate-versus-optimizer attribution has genuine practical stakes and where [6] certifies the gap. That defense is available. "We are the first crossed factorial in optimization" is not.

## N7 - Bidirectional Smoothness Manipulation

N7 is the paper's genuine high-ceiling novelty and, simultaneously, its most conditional. The claim that no one has manipulated surrogate smoothness in *both* directions — deliberately smoothing the network and deliberately roughening the GP — to identify smoothness as the causal axis of a surrogate-class gap survives an adversarial sweep not only within offline MBO but across the broader Bayesian-optimization, kernel-methods, and GP-regression literature. The structural reason the gap persists is informative: two half-literatures each do half the job. Within-GP smoothness manipulation is common (length-scale sweeps, kernel-parameter diversity), and cross-surrogate-class comparison (GP versus neural ensemble) is common, but no paper combines them into one controlled, causally-attributed experiment. The closest approach is Lim et al., who directly compare a GP against a neural-network ensemble for BO and offer a smoothness-flavored explanation — the GP's eventual win "could be due to the ability of GP to smoothly map out the uncertainty manifolds" — yet only ever widen the GP's length-scale bounds to fix overfitting, never roughen it as a deliberate arm, and never apply any smoothness manipulation to the network side at all [21]. Lim hypothesizes; it does not manipulate. So candidate C's mechanism claim, if the experiment were run, would be "first at all," not merely "first in offline MBO."

The rejection reading turns exactly on the word "if." The bidirectional manipulation — smooth the network, roughen the GP, attribute the gap to the smoothness axis — is, on the paper's own account, planned but unrun. A novelty that is real only *conditional on experiments not in the submission* is not a contribution a reviewer can credit; it is a promissory note. And the surrounding evidence has already partly foreclosed the result the manipulation would need: N4's beta=0 control points at the posterior mean rather than the variance, and N5 shows the paper's existing "smoothness" evidence (the K-sweep) is a category error. So the manipulation, if run, might well confirm a mean-smoothness mechanism — but the paper cannot bank a verdict it has not measured. The committed reading: N7 is the only place the paper could claim a first-at-all mechanism result, and it is precisely the place where the paper has no data. That asymmetry — highest ceiling, weakest current evidence — is why C is a bet and D is a paper.

## N8 - Platform and Library-Version Dependence of Benchmark Results

The observation is striking: an ensemble cell moves roughly 0.5 normalized units between macOS and Linux on TF-Bind-8 while the exact-GP cell matches to 1.000 and every synthetic cell reproduces to twelve decimal places, and the benchmark grid the paper calls indistinguishable spans only about 1.0 to 2.5. If platform choice moves a cell by the same order as the method differences under study, the benchmark comparison is measuring the toolchain as much as the method. The phenomenon, though, is owned in spirit. Nagarajan et al. build a fully deterministic DQN and re-introduce one nondeterminism source at a time, finding that GPU-operation nondeterminism alone — a pure library/hardware-execution effect, "the only source of nondeterminism that exists outside of the algorithm itself" — produces training-curve variance that "looks similar to the curves for exploration and weight initialization" [22]. That is N8's claim in a tighter design: an out-of-algorithm execution difference generating variance of the same order as bona fide algorithmic factors. Henderson's codebase-reimplementation experiments make the same point one level up [1], and the general reproducibility literature has long flagged floating-point and platform effects.

The residual is therefore the *combination*: the specific macOS-versus-Linux axis, the offline-MBO benchmark setting, and — most importantly — the stated same-order ratio between platform variance and method variance, quantified against a grid the paper independently shows is null. That last point is where N8 does real work for the paper, and where a good reviewer will actually *reward* it: the Design-Bench omnibus is null (a Friedman rank test across the seven tasks gives p=0.69, the whole grid falling within one critical difference), and the honest reading of that null is not "the methods are equivalent" but "no difference is detectable at this power, partly because platform/library noise is of the same magnitude as the method effects." That is a coherent, defensible package. It is also, on its own, a reproducibility-note contribution rather than a novel finding — the phenomenon is [22], the residual is the instantiation — so N8 strengthens the paper's benchmark-skepticism narrative without itself being a headline. The rejection reading is mild here: N8 is fine as supporting evidence, weak as a standalone claim.

## N9 - The De-Confounding Direction: an Audit That Strengthens

N9 is the paper's most rhetorically attractive claim and the one most in need of precise scoping. The asset is genuine and, per the project's own confirmed run, has landed: fixing target scaling and the candidate/oracle protocol raised the headline from eta-squared-surrogate = 0.37 to 0.405 — the audit made the effect *grow*, where every reality-check paper the authors know runs the other way. Within ML and CS, an adversarial sweep of sixteen queries found no reality-check or reproducibility paper reporting a corrected scalar effect-size statistic that exceeds its own originally published value. The shrink norm is uniform: Musgrave's corrected metric-learning gains are "marginal at best" [8], Lucic's GAN rankings dissolve [9], Ferrari Dacrema's neural recommenders lose to tuned baselines [7]. So the *narrow* claim — a confound-controlled audit whose variance-explained statistic exceeds its published value, unprecedented in ML/CS — survives and is worth naming explicitly.

The rejection reading attacks only the *broad* framing, and it attacks it correctly. "Reality-checks always shrink, and we are the exception" is false, and two ML/CS counter-instances a well-read reviewer will know sit close enough to sting. Recht et al.'s ImageNet audit shrinks absolute accuracy by 3-15% and 11-14% — the standard direction — but its *relative* model-to-model slope exceeds one, which the authors themselves call "the opposite of the standard overfitting scenario," with "an increased advantage over earlier models" [23]. That is a growth-under-audit result in the same genre, differing from N9 only in the statistic (relative slope versus absolute eta-squared). Agarwal et al. supply the second near-miss: their power-corrected analysis reveals a real SPR-over-DER improvement the point estimates had hidden — an effect that *grew* once the confound (statistical under-powering) was removed [10]. So the paper must state N9 in its narrow scalar form and pre-empt both [23] and [10] in the text; omitting them is the single easiest way for a reviewer to brand the claim an overclaim. Handled honestly, N9 is D's genuine differentiator. Handled loosely, it is the sentence a rejection quotes back.

## Candidate Contributions: The One Sentence Each Owns (A / C / D)

The test is unforgiving: for each candidate, the one sentence a reviewer could not get from any prior paper — or a statement that no such sentence exists.

**Candidate A — repaired measurement.** The owned sentence: *"Under a single shared evaluation protocol, a two-way ANOVA attributes the outcome variance in offline model-based optimization overwhelmingly to surrogate class (eta-squared = 0.405) rather than optimizer class (eta-squared = 0.01), the first such factorially-controlled attribution in the offline setting."* This sentence is ownable — but only because no offline-MBO paper crosses both axes: [26] fixes the surrogate, [27] and [3] fix the optimizer/acquisition, and [6] certifies the gap. The rejection caveat is twofold. First, the sentence is a method transplant: Liang [20] already ran the crossed grid and Hutter [19] already ran the variance decomposition, so A's novelty is their composition in a new subfield. Second, and more damaging, the optimizer-side number is not yet trustworthy: eta-squared-optimizer = 0.01 is confounded by the unequal oracle budget (256 versus 128 proposals) and by a trust-region constraint that suppresses gradient collapse — the one failure mode that would make optimizer choice matter — so the "0.01" is provisional until those confounds are removed. A can own the attribution *shape*; it cannot yet own the *magnitude* of the optimizer null.

**Candidate C — mechanism.** The candidate sentence would be: *"Surrogate posterior-mean smoothness is the causal axis of the GP-versus-ensemble performance gap, demonstrated by manipulating smoothness in both directions."* **No such sentence is currently ownable.** The bidirectional manipulation that would earn it (N7) is unrun; the sigma channel that would have supplied the easiest evidence is dead (the beta=0 control shows the gap survives removal of the variance term); the K-sweep offered as finite-width evidence is a category error (N5); and the raw distance-aware and UCB-as-local-search mechanisms are already owned by [13][14] and [5]. If — and only if — the paper runs the smooth-the-network / roughen-the-GP experiment and it isolates mean-smoothness as causal, C becomes a first-at-all mechanism result per [21]. As submitted, C's one sentence does not exist; it is a hypothesis with its best evidence foreclosed.

**Candidate D — confound taxonomy.** The owned sentence: *"Five named, offline-MBO-specific confounds — target scaling, candidate/oracle-budget asymmetry, optimizer trust-region tuning, ensemble size K, and cross-class beta mismatch — are individually decisive for the headline, and controlling them does not shrink but *grows* the rewarded surrogate effect (eta-squared 0.37 -> 0.405)."* This sentence is ownable, and it is the safest of the three because its two novel components — the specific confound set and the strengthening direction — are each independently unclaimed [4][19][20][23], even though the genre is Henderson's [1]. The rejection caveat is the one the paper must swallow: it must concede the shape to [1][7][8][9][10] explicitly, because a taxonomy paper that pretends the reality-check genre is new will be rejected on the first citation. Conceding the genre is the price of the residual, and the residual — a domain-specific confound list plus a direction of correction no ML/CS audit has reported — is enough to carry a paper if the paper is honest about its size.

The strategic verdict a rejecting-but-constructive reviewer would write: **lead with D, present A as D's quantitative core (with the optimizer null flagged as provisional), and hold C back until the manipulation is run.** The forward-looking stakes make this urgent rather than optional. The offline-MBO literature is accelerating toward exactly this paper's territory — the IGNITE lineage is already regularizing surrogate smoothness in one direction, and the field's own survey [6] is actively soliciting attribution studies — so a paper that ships the crossed-factorial-plus-taxonomy contribution (D+A) now claims a real gap, whereas a paper that waits to ship the mechanism (C) risks being scooped on the taxonomy by a competitor who runs the bidirectional experiment first. The de-confounding-strengthens direction (N9) is the rhetorical hook that makes D memorable; the crossed ANOVA (A) is the quantitative spine; the mechanism (C) is a second paper. Splitting them is not timidity — it is the only framing in which every sentence the paper asserts is one a reviewer cannot already find in the literature.

## The Three Strongest Rejecting Citations

Ranked by how much of the paper each can sink, and stated as the reviewer would state them.

**1. Li, Rudner & Wilson, "A Study of Bayesian Neural Network Surrogates for Bayesian Optimization," ICLR 2024 (arXiv:2305.20028) [3].** This is the most dangerous citation on the list because it touches three of the paper's pillars at once. What the reviewer would say: *"The controlled surrogate-class comparison for Bayesian optimization already exists — I ran it. I found ensemble underperformance and explained it as basin-of-attraction collapse in the small-data regime, not as a smoothness class-property. I swept ensemble size and found robustness, the opposite of your K-contingency claim. And in my Appendix D.1.2 I already reran every surrogate at a smaller architecture and found the ranking invariant to architecture size — which is the finite-width control your mechanism needs and which came out against you. Your contribution over mine is that you cross the optimizer axis and z-score under a shared protocol; that is real, but it is narrower than your framing admits, and your mechanism story is mine with a control removed."* Note the citation-date trap: this is ICLR **2024**, not the 2023 that Semantic Scholar back-propagates from the arXiv v1. A reviewer who is one of these authors — a live possibility, given the small BO-surrogate community — reads N2 and N5 as already answered.

**2. Shahriari, Swersky, Wang, Adams & de Freitas, "Taking the Human Out of the Loop: A Review of Bayesian Optimization," Proc. IEEE 2016 [2].** This citation does not touch the experiments; it dismantles the *framing*. What the reviewer would say: *"Your headline — surrogate choice dominates optimizer/acquisition choice — is not a reversal of any field belief. It is the organizing thesis of the most-cited survey in Bayesian optimization, stated in our introduction as 'the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic' and again in our conclusion, that acquisition-function design 'plays a secondary role to the choice of the underlying surrogate model' [2]. You have produced the first offline-MBO-specific ANOVA confirmation of a decade-old doctrine. Present it as confirmation, not reversal. At most you falsify one paper's local motivating premise [26] — not the field's operating assumption, which already agrees with you."* This is the citation that forces the "reversal" out of the abstract.

Henderson, Islam, Bachman, Pineau, Precup & Meger, "Deep Reinforcement Learning that Matters," AAAI 2018 (arXiv:1709.06560) [1].** The most rhetorically potent because it is at the target venue, so the reviewer pool will know it cold. Your contribution is my template ported to offline MBO with an ANOVA bolted on. The fourth and fifth, worth naming so the paper pre-empts them, erode specific claims rather than whole framings: **Dewolf et al. ## AAAI-27 Venue Fit and Reviewer Pool

The AAAI-27 areas-and-topics page lists, among the relevant subareas, "ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis," "ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification," "SO: Algorithm Configuration & Sampling-based Search," and "RU: Stochastic Optimization," under the standing rule that a submission is filed under the subarea of its *main* contribution [28]. The rejection-relevant observation is that the mapping is a trap: whichever subarea each candidate selects summons exactly the reviewers most primed to know its owning prior work.

**Candidate A (repaired measurement) and Candidate D (confound taxonomy)** both map most naturally to "ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis" [28]. This is the reality-check reviewer pool — the readers of Henderson [1], Musgrave [8], Ferrari Dacrema [7], Lucic [9], and Agarwal [10]. It is the most favorable pool for D's genre-aware framing *if* the paper concedes the shape up front, because these reviewers respect the genre and will reward a clean domain-specific instance with a novel direction of correction. It is also the pool most likely to hold the paper's Design-Bench null to Agarwal's standard [10]: with seven tasks, the Friedman omnibus sits below Demšar's own N-greater-than-10 rule of thumb for that test, so a benchmarking reviewer will insist the p=0.69 null be reported as "no difference detectable at this power" with an explicit power analysis, not as method equivalence. D survives this pool; a loosely-worded A does not. A secondary home for D is "SO: Algorithm Configuration & Sampling-based Search" [28], which summons the AutoML/fANOVA pool — the readers of Hutter [19] — who will immediately place the variance-decomposition move and expect the paper to cite it.

**Candidate C (mechanism)** maps to "ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification" [28], and this is the worst possible pool for the paper as currently constituted. It is the readership of SNGP [13], DUQ [14], the NTK and spectral-bias theory [16][17][18], and above all Li, Rudner & Wilson [3]. These are exactly the reviewers who will catch the K-versus-width category error in one line, who know that L/R/W's D.1.2 already answered the finite-width objection against the paper, and who will recognize the distance-aware mechanism as owned by [13][14] and the UCB-as-local-search mechanism as owned by [5]. Submitting C into this pool is submitting the mechanism claim to the precise audience holding every citation that undercuts it. The forward-looking implication is decision-relevant: because the Bayesian-learning subarea is where the surrogate-comparison literature actually lives and is growing, C's exposure will only increase over the next cycle as more of that pool internalizes [3] — so if the paper insists on a mechanism framing, it should route through the evaluation/benchmarking subarea and frame smoothness as an empirical confound to be controlled (candidate D's language), not as a causal claim to be defended (candidate C's), because the benchmarking pool is more forgiving of an unrun manipulation than the Bayesian-learning pool is of a category error. The clean recommendation: file D under "ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis," keep A as its quantitative core in the same submission, and do not summon the Bayesian-learning pool at all until the bidirectional manipulation exists.

## What I Could Not Verify and Why

Several load-bearing quantities in this assessment rest on the paper's own internal artifacts rather than on independently verifiable primary literature, and honesty about them is part of the rejection's fairness.

The **beta=0 control numbers are NOT VERIFIABLE at the stated magnitude.** The claim that removing the pessimism term barely moves the GP-versus-ensemble gap is the linchpin of the argument that N4's mechanism is false and that the edge is posterior-mean smoothness. But the specific figures do not reconcile: the paper cites a shift of 0.51 to 0.47, while an independent recomputation from the paper's own results file and analysis helpers gave 0.504 to 0.511 — flat-to-slightly-up rather than a modest shrink. Both readings agree on the decision-relevant qualitative point (the gap does not collapse when sigma is removed), so the conclusion that the mechanism is not sigma-mediated stands either way; but the exact magnitude, and therefore any quantitative claim built on it, is unverified. This directly limits how hard candidate C's redirection toward mean-smoothness can be pushed.

The **eta-squared-optimizer = 0.01 magnitude is provisional, not verified.** The "optimizer barely matters" number underwrites both the reversal framing (which I argue overclaims against Shahriari [2]) and candidate A's owned sentence. But that number is itself confounded by the unequal oracle budget (256 versus 128 proposals) and by the trust-region constraint on gradient ascent, both of which suppress the optimizer's apparent influence and neither of which is yet controlled. The literature verdict — that no offline-MBO paper owns the crossed attribution — is independent of the number and holds firmly [3][6][19][20][26][27]. But the paper is not yet entitled to the specific "0.01," and any reviewer will separate these two questions.

By contrast, the **eta-squared-surrogate 0.37-to-0.405 strengthening (N9) is confirmed, not provisional** — it reflects an on-machine run and is the paper's one solid, landed asset; the rejection reading concedes this fully and builds candidate D on it. Beyond these, I could not verify the closed-access MS-DDEO paper at sentence level (its N7 forward-citation ruling rests on abstract-level reading), and the AAAI-27 reviewer-pool inferences are structural extrapolations from the subarea list [28], not statements about who will actually be assigned. Finally, the paper's per-member ensemble architecture (hidden width, depth) is the single most decisive missing fact for the C-versus-D call: without it, one cannot rule out a genuine width-driven component to the ensemble's weakness — the finite-width objection (N5) cannot be fully resolved in the paper's favor or against it until a fixed-K width sweep is run, which is exactly the experiment [3]'s Appendix D.1.2 already ran and the paper has not.
