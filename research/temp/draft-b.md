# The Defence Brief: A Severity Audit of the Audit

**Executive summary — the answer first.** N6 is **CONFIRMED NONE-FOUND**. No prior work in offline model-based optimization (offline MBO: choosing a design that maximizes an expensive black-box objective from a fixed dataset, with no further oracle queries) crosses surrogate model class against acquisition optimizer and reports a two-way variance decomposition. That verdict survived a 2026 recency sweep, a re-confirmation of all three prior near-misses, a forward-citation walk of roughly 347 citing papers, and an adversarial sweep of three regions never previously searched — surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation, AutoML/kernel selection, and simulation-optimization — chosen precisely because a crossed factorial with analysis of variance was *more* likely to already exist there [1][2][3][4][5][7][8][9]. One paywalled lead remains unresolved and is named below. The first contribution stands.

Everything else is improvement, and this is where the defence begins in earnest. The audit produced roughly a dozen mandatory fixes. **Not one of them touches a number the paper computed.** Every quantitative claim checkable against a released artifact reproduced — all four corner effect sizes and their intervals, the corner range, the interval widths, the bootstrap-width validation, the seven figures of Elimination 1 including a paired bootstrap delta and an independent z-score cross-check, the inversion counts across three surrogate classes, and both frozen-cell claims to the individual seed. Every falsifiable line-number claim the paper makes about its own scoring code resolves to exactly what it says it does. The engine-stamping protocol the paper prescribes is one it actually followed. The bibliography has zero undefined citation keys across 67 entries and 41 cited keys. Three confirmed citation-year traps are handled correctly. Of the defects the audit did find, eight concern what a *source* says and one concerns an integer recount in a parenthetical.

The thesis of this brief is that **those two categories are not the same kind of error, and a reviewer who grades them at the same severity is making a mistake.** A paper whose numbers are wrong is not repairable before a deadline. A paper whose sentences over-claim what a citation supports is repairable in an afternoon, and in at least four cases here the corrected sentence is *stronger* than the one it replaces. The correct severity grading puts almost the entire finding list in a single bucket: fix the sentence, keep the result. Three items are genuinely serious and are conceded without qualification — the Demšar threshold does not exist in Demšar, the Fan citation inverts its source, and the K-range claim is false as written. Those three are real. They are also, collectively, three sentences.

## N6 Verdict: The Crossed Surrogate x Optimizer Factorial

### A. The verdict, stated plainly

**CONFIRMED NONE-FOUND**, with one paywalled lead unresolved and disclosed here rather than buried. A kill would require a single paper satisfying all four conjuncts simultaneously: (a) surrogate **model class** varied across at least two genuinely distinct classes; (b) optimizer or search routine varied across at least two routines; (c) the two axes genuinely **crossed**, every level of one run at every level of the other; and (d) a **two-way variance decomposition** of the outcome, not a leaderboard. No such paper was found in any of the regions swept.

### B. What was run to reach it

The 2026 frontier was swept first, since a recent appearance is the failure mode a stale audit cannot see. Eight offline-MBO and offline black-box-optimization papers from January through June 2026 were extracted in full and grepped for twelve decomposition terms — `factorial`, `crossed`, `ANOVA`, `analysis of variance`, `eta squared`, `η²`, `main effect`, `variance decomposition`, `two-way`, `interaction effect`, `attribution`. The result was **zero hits, all eight papers, all twelve terms**. The papers were not marginal, and each fails on a different conjunct: SPADE's ablation swaps only the surrogate backbone while holding the optimizer fixed [15]; the diffusion-LLM optimizer's ablation is leave-one-component-out rather than crossed [16]; CliqueFlowmer *does* vary the optimizer axis but holds one surrogate fixed throughout it [17]; the learnability paper varies the training loss with a single fixed gradient-ascent optimizer in every experiment [18]; and OptBias and the conformal certification paper each bundle surrogate and optimizer into one method identity per row [19][20].

That last pattern is the load-bearing one, and it is the status quo the paper says it dissects: **the offline-MBO literature evaluates surrogate and optimizer as one bundled atomic method per baseline.** Design-Bench, RaM, COMs, RoMA and SOO-Bench all do this without exception [2][10][83]. The subfield's own review certifies the resulting gap in its own words — that benchmarks "often emphasize overall optimization performance without clarifying whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance" [1].

### C. The three prior near-misses: re-confirmed, none extended

All three were re-fetched fresh rather than inherited from the prior audit's verdicts, which is the discipline the task demanded and which paid for itself elsewhere.

**Hutter's fANOVA remains one-way.** Its Table 1 caption states the decomposition is computed "based on data from one SMAC run," and its headline finding is that "the most important hyperparameter was the model class used," at 31%–58% across five datasets [3]. It is a one-way importance analysis inside a single optimizer's own hyperparameter space, and its later lineage extends that within one algorithm's space, never across model class crossed with optimizer.

**Liang's grid remains crossed, descriptive, and online.** It is the closest *structural* analog anywhere: random forest, five GP kernels and GP-ARD crossed against expected improvement, probability of improvement and lower-confidence-bound acquisitions, across five materials-science domains, every surrogate paired with every acquisition [4]. It fails on conjunct (d) alone — zero ANOVA, zero variance decomposition, only custom "acceleration" and "enhancement" metrics against a random-search baseline — and on the offline requirement, since it is closed-loop active learning with live oracle queries.

**Moosbauer names the analysis and declines it.** The verbatim sentence is unambiguous: "any interactions between inputs cannot be detected by an OFAT analysis," appearing immediately after they name ANOVA and fANOVA as the standard method and decline them on cost grounds; their actual methodology is one-factor-at-a-time from a single optimized configuration [5]. This is the most useful of the three for the paper, and section (iii) argues it is being under-used.

The extension check — the half a re-audit usually skips — walked roughly 347 citing papers, restricted to 2023–2026 where volume required. EXPObench compares six surrogate algorithms with zero ANOVA hits, and its "offline" refers to offline surrogate *training*, not the offline MBO setting. None is a kill.

### D. Two new near-misses, and the honest cost of the closest one

The most important N6 finding of this pass is that the previous audit missed something, which is exactly why its verdicts were to be re-checked rather than trusted. **RaM's Table 3 is a genuine crossed grid in offline MBO** — nine methods against two surrogate-loss types, verbatim "fixing their open-source codes by replacing MSE with ListNet when training the forward model," reported descriptively as score ± standard deviation and percentage gain, with zero decomposition language across 172,006 characters of full text [2].

The defence of the residual against RaM initially rested on three grounds, and **one of them was wrong.** RaM's own Appendix E.5 describes four of its nine methods — BO-qEI, CMA-ES, REINFORCE and gradient ascent — as "baselines that optimize a trained model": a clean four-level optimizer factor crossed with a two-level loss factor, precisely the sub-grid the ground claimed did not exist [2]. That is conceded. What survives is narrower and better evidenced: the swapped quantity is a **training objective**, not the mechanism that produces the uncertainty estimate. The criterion is statable rather than ad hoc — a change is a model-class change if it cannot be instantiated without changing the family of the estimator (a GP posterior and an ensemble's cross-member disagreement each require their own native fitting procedure), and a training-objective change if it can be applied at fixed architecture. The field's canonical uncertainty survey applies exactly this separation, cataloguing method family as the top-level taxonomy and loss function as a cross-cutting axis in separately captioned tables [12], and Li, Rudner and Wilson treat posterior-computation method, architecture and inference procedure as constitutively bound rather than independently swappable [14].

The distinction is now backed by a number. A per-task two-way ANOVA computed directly from RaM's published Table 3 gives a **loss axis explaining roughly 2.7% of variance against a method axis explaining roughly 57.7%** — the near-mirror image of the audited paper's own pattern [2]. If training loss and model class were the same kind of factor, swapping one would not be negligible while swapping the other dominates in a structurally similar design.

The second new near-miss, DiBO, crosses a diffusion against an autoregressive backbone at three training stages, descriptive only [6]. It fails the optimizer conjunct more basically than RaM: a training-curriculum stage is not a search routine applied to a frozen surrogate, and DiBO has no optimizer axis at all.

### E. The unresolved lead, named here rather than in the footnotes

**Elsayed & Lacor (2014), "Robust parameter design optimization using Kriging, RBF and RBFNN with gradient-based and evolutionary optimization techniques"** [11]. This title names three surrogate classes *and* two optimizer families — the only title surfaced in the entire audit that names both axes explicitly. Primary text could not be obtained: publisher-direct, open-access location services, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, and two repository routes were all exhausted, and the fetcher correctly refused to render a verdict from secondary snippets. The open question is precise and answerable in minutes with institutional access: **is the crossing genuine, each surrogate run with each optimizer, or sequential — surrogates compared first, then one winner optimized two ways?** A sequential design is not a kill. A genuine crossing with a variance decomposition would be. This belongs in the N6 verdict itself.

### F. Why the residual is a scoping and not a gerrymander

The uncomfortable fact, and the one the paper should state itself rather than have a reviewer state for it: **the audit computed a two-way decomposition from a competitor's published table in fifteen lines of code.** That strengthens the claim "no prior work *reports* a crossed surrogate × optimizer decomposition" and weakens the claim "no prior work *could have*." N6 must therefore be stated as a claim about what the literature reports, never about what the design space permits. The contribution is running the decomposition as the primary analysis on a purpose-built grid, not the arithmetic itself.

That is narrower than what the paper currently claims, and it is the version that survives contact with Tan et al. The required fix is two-part and cheap: **relocate the `tan2025ltr` citation** out of the "surrogate-class comparisons hold the optimizer constant" bucket into a training-objective bucket alongside COMs — which is what RaM actually is — and **add a footnote naming RaM Table 3** with the four-versus-five breakdown and the 0.027-versus-0.577 asymmetry. The five remaining methods are genuinely aliased in the textbook sense — their model family and their search paradigm co-occur on every run and cannot be estimated apart [13] — so the ground survives for them and must simply stop being claimed for all nine. The introduction's sentence is not false about RaM's main experiment; it is incomplete in the one way that matters.

## (i) Claims the Literature Contradicts or That Are Miscited

### A. What passed — stated first, because it calibrates everything after it

An audit that returns a dozen mandatory fixes reads by default as an indictment. That reading is wrong here, and the evidence for the correction is not rhetorical.

**Every quantitative claim checkable against a released artifact reproduced.** All four corner effect sizes match to five decimal places — 0.36695, 0.28279, 0.45015, 0.40455 against the paper's 0.367, 0.283, 0.450, 0.405 — with their intervals matching likewise. The corner range computes to 0.1674 against "0.167"; interval widths to 0.258–0.338 against "0.26–0.34"; the uncorrected bootstrap width to 0.30466 against "0.305". Elimination 1's seven figures all verify, including the one a hostile reader would flag first: the reported increment of 0.203 is not 0.525 minus 0.319 but the artifact's own paired bootstrap delta of 0.20321, interval [0.00716, 0.39552], *p* = 0.0204, with the per-task z-score cross-check reproducing at 0.90374 against 1.47272. The inversion counts verify at 7/7, 3/7 and 2/7 across the three surrogate classes.

**Both frozen-cell claims verify to the individual seed.** On Ant, the two GP cells the paper names return the bit-identical constant 1.5287419557571411 at 16 of 16 exact hits; the third returns 1.32417 ± 0.198, matching the paper's own published correction that it is two of three cells and not three; and the sparse-variational-GP cell hits the same constant at **exactly ten of sixteen**, as stated — a figure an author could easily have rounded and did not. On TF-Bind-8 the three exact-GP cells are frozen at exactly 1.0 in all four corners and the ensemble-with-perturbation cell joins them in exactly two of four, giving four frozen cells of nine there and three in the others, exactly as written.

**The code-level traces resolve exactly.** Line 34 is the target-standardization switch; lines 157–162 show the ensemble training on raw targets when it is off; line 293 is a scikit-learn GP and lines 379–381 a separate torch GP, independently corroborating the paper's own disclosure that a premise-coverage figure belongs to a different GP implementation than the one the grid scores. Lines 35–40 are a source comment stating the two-estimands defect in *more* detail than the paper does. The engine-stamping claim likewise holds: released artifacts carry platform, library versions, a git commit hash, the correction flags, the operating-point coordinates, the seed range and an ISO timestamp.

**The bibliography is clean where it usually is not.** Zero undefined keys across 67 entries and 41 cited keys. No dangling citation — the failure mode that most often survives to camera-ready.

**Three confirmed year traps are handled correctly.** Henderson is AAAI 2018 from the PDF copyright line, with 2017 being the preprint date [71]; Benavoli is JMLR volume 17, 2016, preprint 2015 [49]; Li, Rudner and Wilson is ICLR 2024 from the arXiv comment field, with 2023 being the v1 submission [14]. A fourth quoted-figure check also passes: the replication slopes the paper pre-empts as a partial precedent are verbatim, and its characterization of what they mean is accurate [70].

**And several load-bearing citations a reviewer would assume sloppy hold up.** The doctrine the paper scopes against is genuinely owned by the source it cedes it to: the standard Bayesian-optimization review states it twice, as its own organizing thesis — "the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic," and again that acquisition design "plays a secondary role to the choice of the underlying surrogate model" [25]. The paper's paraphrase, "matters more," *understates* the source, and zero hits for `offline` across 137,300 characters confirms the restriction to online BO is accurate rather than strained. The premise the paper claims to falsify is likewise asserted verbatim by its target, not fabricated: PG-GS's own summary says its perspective "complements prior methods that have focused on improving surrogate models while using fixed search strategies" [26]. The interval-validity citation is correctly and narrowly scoped, with zero occurrences of `acquisition`, `Bayesian optimization`, `pessimis` or `lower confidence bound` across 23,000 words, so the paper's claim to the acquisition-comparison application as its own residual is exactly right [27]. And three of the four kernel-limit citations behind Elimination 2 are faithful: the initialization correspondence, the frozen-kernel training-dynamics result, and the wide-nets-evolve-as-linear-models theorem collectively support "wide networks approach GP behaviour" [42][43][44].

**No fix is needed for any of the above.** That matters for grading what follows.

### B. The three genuine defects, conceded without qualification

**M1 — the Demšar threshold does not exist.** The paper writes that its omnibus test "is recommended for more than ten datasets." A full-text grep of the 30-page JMLR paper returns **zero** occurrences of "more than ten," "at least ten" or "ten or more"; the only two occurrences of "ten data sets" describe Demšar's own power-simulation sampling procedure [22]. This is a specific numeric recommendation attributed to a real source that does not make it, and it inverts the source's actual rationale — Demšar recommends the non-parametric route *because* the number of datasets "is usually much less than 30." **Fix: delete the clause and the citation.** And note what the fix costs: nothing. The power limitation is independently established by the rest of the same sentence — the paired-test calculation requiring an effect of at least 1.27 standardized units for 80% power at *n* = 7 — and by the never-claim-equivalence point, which is verified verbatim against its source [48]. Demšar, read correctly, *endorses* using the Friedman test at *n* = 7. **The paper was conceding a methodological weakness it does not have.** The fix removes a false warrant and an unnecessary self-deprecation at once.

**M2 — the Fan citation inverts its source, in three places.** The paper presents "the reading of a UCB-style acquisition as local search" as established prior work it merely applies, and then attributes its LCB-paralysis mechanism to the same source. Full-text grep of Fan et al. returns **zero hits** for `offline`, `LCB`, `lower confidence bound`, `stuck`, `frozen`, `freeze` and `paralysis`. Their own words are "we **propose** our first algorithm... MinUCB, which **replaces** gradient descent step with a step that minimizes the UCB in GIBO," framed explicitly as their own discovery [23]. Decisively, their Theorem 1 proves **convergence** to a genuine local optimum, contingent on an increasing exploration schedule and continual active resampling every iteration — both structurally impossible on a static offline dataset. The paper cites a convergence result as authority for a failure mode. **Fix: drop the citation from all three sites — the mechanism framing, the LCB-paralysis sentence, and the distance-aware co-citation.**

This is the clearest case in the audit where the fix strengthens the paper. The alternatives were checked and none owns the claim: TuRBO diagnoses the **opposite** pathology — "an overemphasized exploration that results from global acquisition" and "a failure to exploit promising areas" — and its locality is imposed rather than observed [69], while GIBO's exploitation step contains no confidence bound at all. The nearest genuine prior art proves that expected-improvement-driven BO started at the true optimum converges back to that point and is "not dense" [67], but that is a different acquisition, an online setting, and an adversarial starting condition rather than a seed-robust empirical observation; a second adjacent result concerns within-round inner-optimization fidelity, not the outer-loop claim [68]. **The freeze is the paper's own finding and is stronger unattributed than mis-attributed:** bit-identical constants across sixteen seeds, in two GP classes, on a continuous task with no argmax decode, is not subtle. The prescription is a scoping sentence, not a borrowed owner.

**M3 — the K-range claim is false as written.** The paper states its sweep "extends below" the range over which ensemble surrogates were found robust, characterizing that prior range as two levels and claiming two more as its own addition. Li, Rudner and Wilson's Figure A.7 tests **three** ensemble sizes including the very endpoint the paper claims to have added, with the verbatim caption "the different ensembles perform similarly across many experiments, showing the robustness of our results to this hyperparameter" [14]. This was verified twice independently — by extracting the figure legend tokens from the PDF and by rendering the page as an image. The prior work already went to the lower endpoint and found robustness there. **Fix: correct the range, and delete the priority claim.**

But the residual is real and the paper does not currently state it. The prior work measures **performance robustness** of ensemble Bayesian optimization to model count; the paper measures **sensitivity of the variance decomposition** to model count. Those are different quantities and both can be true. Stated explicitly — "prior work finds ensemble *performance* robust to model count over an overlapping range; we find the *variance attributed to the surrogate axis* is not robust" — the claim survives in a narrower and more interesting form. That defence appears nowhere in the paper and costs one sentence.

**These three are the whole of the serious category.** Three sentences.

### C. Five attribution defects that are real and low-severity

**M4 — the distance-aware bounding citation runs against its source.** The paper says its measurement is "bounded by prior work on distance-aware uncertainty," citing SNGP. SNGP's Figure 1 discussion argues the opposite about exactly this model class: "deep ensembles... are based on dense output layers that are **not distance aware**... assigning low uncertainty to OOD examples even if they are far from the data" [24]. The independent corroboration is blunter still: "Deep Ensembles is uncertain only along the decision boundary, and certain elsewhere" [34]. Both sources are also **classification-only** with zero regression experiments, while the paper is regression. **Fix: reframe as a contrast rather than a bound** — "unlike the ensembles these works characterize as distance-unaware, ours shows a modest positive distance correlation." A result running *against* a canonical characterization is more publishable than one leaning on it, provided it is labelled as such. This is the second fix that strengthens what it corrects.

**M5 — the ensemble-quality direction citation reports no direction.** The paper says its decline with ensemble size "runs against the direction reported for ensemble quality," citing a paper that runs **zero** ensemble-size ablation: its configuration is fixed at four members on one benchmark and five on another, fixed by seed availability rather than swept, and its thesis is ensembles versus a single larger model [29]. **Fix: replace with the source that does report it** — the originating deep-ensembles paper's Table 4, sweeping size from one to ten with monotonic, diminishing, never-negative improvement, verbatim "as M increases, both the accuracy and the quality of predictive uncertainty improve significantly" [30] — with the caveat that this is classification calibration, not offline-MBO reward.

**M6 — one citation is asked to certify a claim about a literature.** The paper writes, twice, that audits in this genre "usually shrink" or "normally shrink" the effect they audit, citing a single instance. That instance reports a **ranking reversal** — an older baseline beating newer architectures once hyperparameters are controlled — not a shrinking effect size, and explicitly frames itself as one instance joining others [28]. **Fix, and it is nearly free: recast as an observation over named instances.** The paper already cites all four canonical members of the pattern in its own genre paragraph [55][71][72][73], and a fifth directly-on-target reanalysis exists — a bioactivity benchmark whose deep-learning advantage collapses on re-examination [40]. Cite the set for the pattern; never one for a law.

**M7 — an over-extended analogy.** The paper describes a pessimism-calibration source as the "offline-RL instance" of a cross-model-class incomparability principle. That source's entire scope is one surrogate class, and its axis of comparison is an internal training-procedure choice — shared versus independent targets, full versus weight-shared ensembles — so it is not an instance of a *cross-model-class* principle [64]. Its actual finding is stronger and different in kind: shared targets can flip the sign of the lower bound, turning pessimism into optimism. **Fix: restate accurately.** The accurate version is more useful, because the same source supplies the candidate positive mechanism for the paper's inversion result — see section (iii).

**M8 — a citation motivating the wrong ablation.** Elimination 2 invokes spectral bias to motivate a **width** sweep. The source's theorem holds "for arbitrary width and depth," its defining experiment varies training iterations rather than width, and its own controlled ablation finds that increasing depth "significantly improves the network's ability to fit higher frequencies" while "increasing the width... also helps, but the effect is considerably weaker" — despite width scaling exponentially and depth only linearly [41]. The paper's architecture compounds it: the ensemble is fixed at two hidden layers across the entire width sweep. **Fix, and the better of two options: keep the citation and state the limit.** Say that spectral bias is reported as primarily a depth phenomenon, that the ablation sweeps width at fixed depth, and that the depth-driven version of the objection therefore remains open. This converts a miscitation into a correctly-scoped limitation, costs one clause, and names a concrete follow-up. Note the asymmetry: the paper is already scrupulous about the asymptotic limit of its width sweep ("the sweep stops at 1024 while the kernel limits are asymptotic"); the same scrupulousness has simply not been applied to the depth axis.

### D. Three scoping items where the paper is right on facts and loose on implication

**M9 — the survey quote is verbatim and its framing is over-read.** The sentence the paper quotes from the subfield's review is exact, and the review is genuinely TMLR 01/2026, confirmed from every page header of v2 and independently corroborated by another 2026 paper's bibliography [1]. But the sentence sits in a section on uncertainty-quantification evaluation gaps, not as a call for a crossed factorial. **Fix (light): keep the quote, drop the implication that the survey is calling for this specific design.** This resolves an apparent internal inconsistency **in the paper's favour** — the prose calling it a 2026 survey was right and the bibliography entry calling it a 2025 preprint is stale.

**M10 — the σ dichotomy is a false opposition, and the fix is a scope statement.** The paper frames its ensemble's uncertainty as "a distance signal, not an error signal." In peer-reviewed regression, a committee and a bootstrap-aggregation ensemble reach Spearman correlations of 0.90 and 0.91 between uncertainty and error — roughly thirteen times the paper's figure [31] — and an image-domain result reports 0.715 against per-pixel error [33]. But **both are in-distribution**, and the paper measures where an optimizer has pushed designs off-support; the force-field paper's own out-of-distribution test shows the correlation degrading per-atom while staying informative in aggregate [31], and the large-scale shift benchmark finds deep ensembles the *best-performing* uncertainty method under shift [32]. **The literature does not treat distance-awareness and error-tracking as mutually exclusive.** Fix: scope the claim to this grid — "on our tasks, σ tracks distance more than error" — rather than as a correction to a general belief. One clause, and more defensible than what it replaces.

A related caveat is worth a paragraph of scoping rather than a fix: the paper's ensemble is the MSE-trained, cross-member-empirical-variance construction that the originating paper itself flags as the weaker of two options on interval calibration [30], and standard ensembles carry no diversity guarantee [35]. The counterweight must travel with it — the same construction is treated as the *reliable reference* elsewhere [64], and the repulsive-ensembles paper's own table shows a standard ensemble winning out-of-distribution detection in one setup [35]. Note also that the ensemble-diversity literature concerns function-space mode separation among members [36] and that the distance-awareness program is a classification-side line about how output-layer parametrization encodes distance [37] — neither measures aggregate uncertainty against input-space distance in regression, which is the paper's actual quantity. The correct statement is "this specific σ construction has a known calibration caveat," never "MSE ensembles are broken." 

**M11 — a correct proposition that is practically inert, and the paper never says so.** The supplement's covariate-shift clause defines the reweighting factor as the true density ratio, so the statement is exactly the weighted-conformal theorem and is **formally correct as written** [82]. What is true is that it is inert in the paper's own setting: the proposal distribution is what an optimizer emits after ascending a surrogate, which is where the ratio is least knowable. **Fix (one or two sentences): note the clause requires a known or reliably estimable ratio, that none is available for optimizer-generated proposals, and that the clause is stated for completeness rather than applied.** Then take the free win — the erratic out-of-distribution coverage the supplement already reports is *what one should expect* when the repair's precondition is unavailable. Saying so converts an unexplained table into a correctly-scoped negative result. **Severity: moderate, and it is a scoping omission around a correct proposition, not a defect in a proof.** An earlier and more severe reading of this item was itself an overstatement and was withdrawn on reading the supplement directly.

### E. Bibliography hygiene, and an unusual defect worth naming

Three small items. The review's bib entry should become TMLR 2026 rather than a 2025 preprint [1]. The reward-overoptimization entry has a key saying 2022 against its own year field saying 2023, and a page range that does not match the true PMLR pagination [60]. And the "five other cells tie it" parenthetical should be six: applying the paper's own stated criterion to the released artifact returns seven qualifying cells rather than six. **This is the only figure in the entire numeric sweep that does not reproduce, and it is a bare integer in a parenthetical.**

The unusual defect is the opposite of the usual one. **The paper's own bibliography contains at least three papers that would strengthen it, uncited:** the offline-design paper that articulated its core diagnosis in 2020 [59]; the closest sibling-genre revisiting-design-choices study in offline model-based RL, which holds its policy optimizer fixed and is therefore a *supporting* precedent for the no-prior-instance claim rather than a threat [21]; and the canonical local-BO paper, uncited while a 2024 method paper is credited with the local-search reading [69]. Fixing this direction of defect costs three sentences and converts a liability into the paper's best related-work paragraph.

### F. The contradiction hunt: what the literature does *not* contradict

Two hypotheses were tested adversarially and **neither lands as a contradiction.**

Does anyone show the ensemble's uncertainty *is* an error signal, refuting the distance-signal claim? Yes in-distribution, no in the paper's regime — the scoping is the resolution, not a refutation [31][32][33].

Does any prior de-confounding audit report a corrected variance-explained statistic that *grew*, killing the direction claim? **Confirmed none-found, exhaustively**, across fourteen distinct query formulations spanning the reproducibility-track literature. Three independent, directly-on-target ML benchmark reanalyses — bioactivity prediction, metric learning, neural recommenders — all move in the shrink direction, zero in the grow direction [40][72][73]. The narrow scalar claim stands and is now better evidenced than when the paper made it.

Two sources **bracket** it, and both are fixes rather than kills. In the one ML literature where confound removal increasing an effect has been studied, the documented default diagnosis is leakage: "this common deconfounding approach can leak information such that what are null or moderate effects become amplified to near-perfect prediction" [38]. **Fix: affirmatively rule leakage out** — which the paper can do cheaply, because its two corrections are a target rescaling and a candidate-selection rule and neither regresses a confound out of features, the mechanism leakage requires. But that argument must be *made on the page*. And in the one large systematic recomputation of effect sizes in a mature empirical science, corrections go up about as often as down — 19 larger against 14 smaller [39] — which kills only the surrounding implication that an upward correction is intrinsically surprising. **Fix: cite it at that sentence.** It converts a hedge into a supported statement.

## (ii) Kills

### A. Against N6: none

No paper satisfying all four conjuncts was found in the 2026 frontier [15][16][17][18][19][20], in the three re-confirmed near-misses [3][4][5], in the extension walk over roughly 347 citing papers, in the offline-MBO benchmark suites themselves [10][83], or in the three previously-unsearched adversarial regions.

The adversarial sweep deserves emphasis because it is the most informative negative available. Surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation was the best remaining bet — the community with the strongest design-of-experiments culture. Its most directly-titled multi-surrogate multi-algorithm comparison was fetched and grepped first-hand across 47,342 characters: **zero hits on all nine decomposition terms**, with 18 hits for "surrogate model" and 4 for "Kriging" confirming the extraction was of the right paper [7]. The JMLR paper on evolutionary surrogate model-type selection uses a genetic algorithm as the *fitting* mechanism, never crossed with a black-box optimizer over the design space [9]. In AutoML, the closest structural match found anywhere crosses kernel choice against acquisition function with an isolating ablation — but Matérn, RBF and rational-quadratic are three covariance functions inside one GP model class, and its "offline" label denotes a no-extra-query internal step inside an otherwise fully online loop [8]. In simulation-optimization, the candidate compares polynomial regression against Kriging but runs only one optimizer.

Two adjacent regions were checked for the interaction-reporting precedent and both returned clean. Rice's algorithm-selection lineage selects **solvers**, never surrogates — from SATzilla's empirical hardness models [76] through the exploratory-landscape-analysis instantiation for continuous black-box problems [75] to the feature-free successor, in which "surrogate" does not appear at all [77]. And in general ML benchmarking, the three most likely candidates — the crowded-valley optimizer benchmark, the empirical-optimizer-comparison paper [58] and the vision-transformer augmentation study — return zero hits on every formal interaction term; the last runs a genuine crossed grid, calls it "interplay," and never computes a statistic.

### C. Three homonym traps, and why grep-count-only methods would have failed

## (iii) Scope of Novelty, Ranked

Ranked by value per unit of cost. The unifying observation across the top of this list: **the paper's strongest available assets are already computed, already printed, and already in its own artifacts.** Almost nothing here requires a new run.

### 1. The interaction term is significant, stable, and never interpreted — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

Three independent routes reached this — two depth investigations and direct artifact work, none prompted by the others. That convergence on a quantity the paper prints four times and never discusses is itself a finding.

The interaction effect is **0.146–0.165 across the four engine corners, with bootstrap intervals excluding zero in all four** (lower bounds 0.087–0.107). It is the second-largest effect in every corner, ordered surrogate > interaction > optimizer without exception, at 4× to 33× the optimizer main effect. And it is **9.2× more stable across corners than the headline it would sit beside**: range 0.018 against the surrogate effect's 0.167. The paper's central methodological caution is that any headline is a joint artifact of four operating-point coordinates — and it never notices that the interaction is nearly invariant to the one coordinate it demonstrates the headline is most sensitive to. It survives bias correction intact at 0.134–0.156.

The word "interaction" appears **exactly once** in the paper, in a related-work sentence citing Moosbauer's negative reason for declining the analysis [5]. So the paper argues that interaction detection is what justifies its design, runs the design, finds a significant interaction in all four corners, prints it in a table column, and never mentions it again.

Why this is a major result rather than a technicality. It is **the strongest available vindication of N6** — the empirical payoff a fixed-one-axis study could not have produced, which is exactly Moosbauer's stated reason for declining [5]. It **reframes the optimizer null**, currently the paper's weakest passage: the paper correctly warns that a small optimizer effect licenses "explains little variance" and never "is arbitrary," says the second reading is false on its own data, and never cites the evidence. The interaction *is* that evidence. And it is a **positive, quantitative, significant result in a section that is otherwise seven negatives.**

The positive framing authority the paper lacks is now in hand and quote-verified: the public-domain handbook section stating that "OFAT experimentation leaves us in the dark about factor interactions" — the exact positive mirror of the Moosbauer sentence already on the page [50] — and Box himself, arguing interactions are practically decisive via an industrial fivefold bearing-life case found only once someone stopped varying one factor at a time [51]. The paper's bibliography currently contains zero design-of-experiments methodology citations.

Two qualifications make the promotion safe rather than reckless. **It is synthetic-grid-specific:** on Design-Bench the interaction is 0.006–0.041, subordinate to the optimizer effect there — consistent with the rest of the picture, since where surrogate cells are frozen neither main effects nor interactions involving them can appear. **And it is an η², so it inherits the normalizer sensitivity below.** Report it as an existence-and-ordering claim with the point estimate given as measured under the stated normalizer, exactly as the paper already qualifies its headline. The honest methodological counter cuts *for* the paper: reported interactions are chronically underpowered, median power .18 across 159 coded hypotheses, precisely because typical interactions are small relative to their main effects [52]. This one is larger than an entire main effect. **Cost: zero new computation.**

### 2. What the interaction *is*, in raw units and in a formal simple-effects table — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

The interaction has an interpretable form that the normalization challenge cannot reach. Taking the GP-minus-ensemble gap in **raw oracle units** from the supplement's own full table, per optimizer per task: on **7 of 7 tasks the surrogate gap under perturbation is smaller than under both gradient ascent and CMA-ES**, averaging 5.9% of the aggressive-optimizer gap — roughly a 17× attenuation, spanning 2D to 30D. A formal per-optimizer simple-effects decomposition, computed from stored per-seed data with the shared normalizer and the codebase's own resampling machinery, confirms it with intervals: the surrogate effect collapses to roughly 0.01 under perturbation against 0.39–0.81 under gradient and CMA. The methodological literature requires exactly this decomposition once an interaction is detected, and the handbook's worked example demonstrates it on a design of the same shape [50].

A trap worth stating if the table is added: re-normalizing within a single optimizer column inflates the perturbation-level effect to roughly 0.73 and erases the asymmetry the interaction is about, because min–max rescaling to any three points always restores a full unit spread. The correct computation shares the omnibus model's normalizer.

**The causal reading is not established, and the paper must pose this as a question it tests rather than a result it asserts.** A floor-effect alternative — perturbation attenuates the gap merely because it underperforms — survives its test: the single task where perturbation is the grid's best optimizer has the *weakest* attenuation, which is what a floor effect predicts. That one data point is worth more than the seven-task count, because it is the only case that discriminates. The disentangling analysis is nameable and cheap: condition each optimizer's gap on how much it improves over the seed set, using quantities the released inversion artifact already stores. **Re-analysis, not a new run.**

The finding also **unifies six results the paper reports in five different sections** — the interaction effect, the cross-surrogate coverage table, the inversion result, Elimination 4's finding that more budget *widens* the gap, the frozen Design-Bench cells where GP-with-perturbation is the frozen pairing, and the Design-Bench optimizer inversion where perturbation leads. Every one is the same phenomenon, and together they explain the paper's own biggest puzzle: why the optimizer axis is negligible on synthetic tasks and inverts on Design-Bench.

### 3. The paper already has a mechanistic measurement of the interaction, in a different table — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

The supplement's cross-proposal premise-coverage table shows the ensemble at 0.73 in-distribution, **0.41 on its own proposals, and 0.97 on the GP's** — better on the GP's proposals than the GP's own 0.93. The paper's own reading is correct: "the ensemble is not miscalibrated in general — only where its own gradient optimizer drives it." **That sentence is a description of an interaction**, and the paper never connects it to the interaction term. Coverage figures are probabilities, not min–max-normalized scores, so this contrast is untouched by the normalizer critique. One sentence in each direction. Zero computation.

### 4. The budget axis is an instance of a named line, and the paper does not know it — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

This is the clearest under-stated finding. The paper presents its budget result **defensively**, as an objection it has removed. Read against the literature it is a **contribution to an established methodological category carrying the strongest evidence in the paper** — its only disjoint bootstrap intervals — plus a ranking flip.

The COCO framework formally names budget-dependent benchmarking, with its fixed-budget and fixed-target views, and is uncited [57]. A direct precedent sits in the paper's own bibliography, cited for the genre but not for this: "bad models can outperform good models given enough computational budget" [55]. A sibling field states that budget-matched comparison "has become standard practice" and that raising the budget "may significantly affect the final verdict" [56], and the paper's own cited statistical work establishes that aggregate-metric choice alone can reverse rankings [48]. **Fix: reframe from defence to contribution and cite the line.** Two sentences.

### 5. η² is positively biased at *n* = 7, and correcting it makes the headline stronger — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

Two independent lines converge, one from the literature and one from the paper's own artifacts. The bias is established firsthand from open-access primaries: a Monte Carlo table over a million replications per condition showing η² bias always positive and shrinking with sample size, from +0.054 at ten per group to +0.005 at a hundred [53]; and a peer-reviewed statement that η² "is not an unbiased estimate and is known to have positive bias" that "depends on the sample size," attributing ε² and ω² as the corrections [54]. The first source also **overturns the folk belief that ω² is the least-biased correction — ε² is** — so the recommendation should name ε², or report both.

The paper's own bootstrap artifacts already estimate the bias, unreported: the bootstrap mean exceeds the point estimate in **all four corners**, by +0.0099 to +0.0184. Applying the correction, **the headline claim gets stronger**: the two protocol confounds move the surrogate effect 0.351 → 0.395, a rise of +0.0437, against the reported 0.367 → 0.405 rise of +0.0376. Direction survives; the magnitude of the change grows. One sentence plus one supplementary column buys immunity from the single most obvious statistical objection to reporting variance-explained at seven tasks.

### 6. The normalizer is a fifth operating-point coordinate the paper never names — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

Every effect size in the paper is computed on per-task min–max normalized cell means over nine cells, with the range set by two of the nine arms under comparison. This **endogenous** normalizer is a documented deviation from the benchmark's own convention, which draws the range from a larger external dataset rather than from the compared methods [83]. The critique is citable rather than hypothetical: the exact functional form is named as outlier-exploitable — "algorithms could be near 0 or 1 if there is an outlier algorithm that does very well or poorly... We would like the evaluation procedure to be robust to the addition of poor algorithms" [46] — and the paper that *introduced* the technique documents it flipping a ranking [47]. Ranks are not a clean escape, since rank statistics are pool-dependent [49].

**The resolution is empirical and it favours the paper.** Recomputing under five alternative normalizers across four corners, five pessimism levels and both budget levels — 33 combinations plus leave-one-task-out, on the paper's raw result files — finds **every one of the 33 preserves the ordering of the two main effects.** Not most; all. Magnitudes move; direction does not. That lands almost exactly where the paper's own framing sits: "the direction is invariant to all four coordinates... only the size of the gap moves." The finding is that this is true of a fifth coordinate the paper never names. **The fix is not a retraction; it extends an argument the paper already makes to one more axis, and the extension is supportive.** The machinery exists twice over — an abandoned robustness script in the repository, and the z-score cross-check already used for Elimination 1. One self-correction worth recording: the alarm was raised on the task with a 2,780× outlier spread, and the recomputation found that task is the *smallest* lever on the headline, not the largest.

### 7. Reframe the core diagnosis as a refinement of a named prior position — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

The paper's central diagnosis — which off-distribution maximizers a surrogate's mean admits — was substantially articulated in 2020, including the uncertainty-estimates clause, by a paper sitting uncited in its own bibliography [59]. **This kills nothing.** No factorial machinery, no surrogate-class comparison, no numeric claim threatened. But a reviewer from the offline-design community will note it.

And the fix is a gain. That prior work implicitly treats distance-from-data as the driver of oracle unreliability, and the seventh elimination complicates exactly that: distance predicts aggregate loss strongly (ρ = −0.818 across 5,040 instrumented optima) but **does not discriminate between surrogate classes at matched distance** (medians 0.87, 0.86, 0.84, with the ensemble still worse in true oracle value by −1.406). Framed as a refinement of a named position that is an advance; framed as unprecedented it is a liability. The same move applies to the reward-overoptimization scaling laws, which are class-dependent by optimization method but vary only scale within one architecture family [60][61] — making the paper's result a refinement onto a new axis in a new domain, consistent with the law rather than a counterexample.

### 8. The inversion has a name in the sibling field — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

The paper states its inversion result flatly, as a within-grid demonstration. Offline RL formalized this property a decade ago as **safe policy improvement** — the high-probability requirement that a policy learned offline not be worse than the baseline it was given, with a closed-form slack and a theorem proving the guarantee [63] — and gap-expansion supplies a second reusable frame, guaranteeing the learned value function *widens* rather than merely bounds the in- versus out-of-distribution gap [62]. The reframing is sharper and more transferable than a count: **offline MBO optimizers ship with no safe-improvement guarantee, and the absence bites.** Two sentences, two citations.

### 9. A decisive negative on the landscape question — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP (and FOLLOW-UP-PAPER / EXPENSIVE for the real test)

Tested directly against the paper's own full table under all three optimizers: **no standard landscape covariate predicts the surrogate gap.** Dimension correlates indistinguishably from zero; textbook multimodality and separability classifications actively mis-sort the ordering. This is a negative from looking, not an absence of looking — the mature exploratory-landscape-analysis tradition has never been pointed at surrogate-class choice, operating at benchmark-suite scale to select *solvers* [75][76][77]. But the heterogeneity is not noise: **the per-task gap ranking is stable across optimizers at Spearman 0.84–0.96.** Two or three sentences reporting this closes a hostile reviewer's "isn't this just landscape?" objection with a falsifiable, already-computed answer instead of the current one-line hedge. The genuine test needs suite-scale corpora with standard feature packages and is not foldable before a deadline.

Note what combining this with item 2 yields, which no single investigation produced: the task ranking is preserved under every optimizer while the overall scale of the gap collapses under the weak one. **That rules out both a purely optimizer-side account and a purely task-side account. Any surviving mechanism must be multiplicative — a task factor scaled by an optimizer-aggressiveness factor.** That is the one positive structural statement available about a section that is otherwise seven negatives.

### 10. The paper computed an equivalence bound and does not report it — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

The released statistics code already computed a paired equivalence test: gap ≈ 0.376, 90% interval [−0.108, 0.860], effect bound ≈ 0.484, **not equivalent at either a 0.5 or a 0.3 margin.** The supplement says only that such tests "are computed," without the bound or the verdict. The paper is scrupulous about never claiming equivalence and correctly says a failure to reject is not a demonstration of absence [48] — and it has the actual test in hand, and it is informative. Report it, add the standard reference for paired-means equivalence, which is not in the bibliography [74], and carry its caution: rejecting small effects requires large samples. Zero new computation.

### 11. The under-executed experiments, ranked by cost

The task asked for a specific runnable experiment the literature motivates that converts elimination into positive mechanism.

**11a. Far-field extrapolation geometry — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP, and the highest-value item here.** The theorem that ReLU networks provably converge to linear functions along rays outside the training support yields a falsifiable geometric prediction that is the paper's diagnosis made mechanical: **an ensemble mean growing linearly without bound far from support admits unbounded maximizers at the box boundary; a GP posterior mean, which reverts toward its prior, does not** [45]. Runnable now by fitting lines to existing model outputs along already-stored trajectories, with no new training. It is compatible with Elimination 2 because the two are asymptotic in **different variables** — far-field distance versus width — and Elimination 2 explicitly disclaims any asymptotic-width claim; but one clarifying sentence is needed, because nothing currently distinguishes the two regimes for a reader who meets both.

**11b. Weight-space sensitivity — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.** Perturbing a trained surrogate's own parameters and measuring how far the prediction at the found optimum moves is a genuinely new axis — **none of the seven eliminations touches weight-space fragility** — and it is computable on existing checkpoints [78]. The venue for this source could not be verified; cite it as a preprint.

**11c. Fitting the degradation curve — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP.** The closed-form relation between proxy-optimization distance and true-value degradation is directly fittable to the paper's existing distance-versus-oracle-value data across 5,040 instrumented optima [60]. This is the paper's own uncited bibliography entry.

**11d. Ensemble member independence — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP if per-member predictions were stored, FOLLOW-UP otherwise.** Shared pessimistic targets across members can render an ensemble paradoxically **optimistic**, with member independence the load-bearing condition [64]. That is on point for a paper whose puzzle is a pessimistic ensemble ranking its own hallucinations above real data, and it is **the first candidate positive mechanism the seven eliminations do not already rule out.** Testable by correlating pairwise member predictions at the returned optima against inversion rate.

**11e–g. The expensive tail — FOLLOW-UP-PAPER / EXPENSIVE.** A depth sweep (named by M8); a double dissociation in which an unconstrained GP-with-neural-features should fail like the ensemble while a distance-constrained variant recovers GP-like behaviour, the cleanest available causal test but affordable only on one 2D benchmark; and a graded battery converting the binary inversion into a dose–response curve. All three require new runs rather than re-analysis.

### 12. A precedent in shape for the pessimism decomposition — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

The β = 0 result — pessimism amplifies a mean-quality base rather than creating it — has an uncited precedent in shape: an ablation showing the base algorithm insufficient without its regularization term, robust across a broad coefficient range and degrading only at the extremes [65]. Note what the theory side does not give: the minimax-optimality result for pessimism is pure theory with **no empirical base-versus-conservatism ablation**, so it supports the framing but is not a precedent for the experiment [66]. Stating that distinction rather than citing loosely is the whole subject of this audit.

### 13. Venue framing — FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP

The target venue folds novelty and significance into **one bundled criterion**, prefers work that "explore[s] new territory" over the incremental, and has no contribution-type rubric for audits [79]. The counterfactual is explicit: a separate track elsewhere enumerates "Reproducibility, Auditing, and Stress-Testing of Evaluations" as a named contribution type and pre-authorizes that "negative results are valuable when rigorously supported" [80]. The paper's genre paragraph is factually accurate — every named instance checks out — but of six canonical instances cited, exactly one is from the target venue and it is from 2018 [71]. Genre framing is necessary and not sufficient. Two free moves follow: **lead with the interaction ahead of the seven eliminations**, and **promote the falsification of a named premise held by a paper at this same venue two years earlier** [26] from a scope-limiting aside into an explicit hook. "Paper X claimed Y, we measured Y directly and it is false" requires no genre familiarity to register as a contribution.

### 14. NOT-WORTH-IT

Three items. **Do not add meta-commentary citing the venue's own review criteria into the paper** — it reads as reviewer-gaming and the argument is stronger on the merits. **Do not keep hunting for an owner of the LCB-paralysis claim** — three candidates were excluded and the vocabulary sweep found only a partial, differently-scoped ancestor [67]; the frozen cells are the paper's own strongest evidence and need no borrowed authority. **Do not default to ω² as the bias correction** on folk grounds; the simulation evidence names ε² [53].

### Forward look: what this paper's instrument becomes

The consequential thing here is not any single fix but what the instrument implies for the next few years of this subfield. Every benchmark in offline MBO currently reports one number per bundled method [10][15][16][17][19][83], and the field's own review certifies that this leaves attribution unresolved [1]. The moment a crossed decomposition is published as a primary analysis rather than derived post-hoc from someone else's table, the cost of *not* running one rises for everyone: a leaderboard entry that cannot say whether its gain came from the model or the search becomes a weaker claim than it was the week before. The interaction result sharpens that, because it means the question is not separable in general — which surrogate wins depends on which optimizer it is paired with, and the paper's own cross-proposal coverage table says why. The successors are visible from here: a decomposition at benchmark-suite scale with landscape features would convert the stable per-task ranking into a selection rule [75][77]; a safe-improvement guarantee ported from offline RL would convert the inversion count into a design constraint [62][63]; and a far-field geometry test would convert seven eliminations into one mechanism [45]. Each is a paper. The submission under audit is the instrument that makes them askable, and the strongest argument for accepting it is not that its numbers are large but that they are **reproducible to the seed** — exactly the property the confounded status quo lacks.

## What I could not verify and why

**Access failures.** The primary text of the 2014 robust-parameter-design paper could not be obtained through any free route, leaving the N6 verdict with one named unresolved lead [11]. Two foundational effect-size papers remain paywalled; the claim is now carried firsthand by two open-access primaries [53][54] plus direct computation from the paper's own artifacts. The design-of-experiments textbook's prose on interaction estimation was not reached; the load-bearing citations are the handbook section and Box's technical report, both quote-verified [50][51]. A 2016 landscape-driven surrogate-selection paper was blocked by a bot check and is carried secondhand through a survey [81]. The canonical elitism-guarantee citation returned 403 everywhere; the safe-policy-improvement framing carries the point instead and is fully verified [63].

**Venue attributions I could not confirm — and two errors that were mine, not the paper's.** An investigator reported the surrogate-sensitivity paper as a 2024 conference publication; its preprint v1 is dated 2025, which is chronologically impossible, and no title match resolves, so the venue is withdrawn [78]. Two investigators gave two different venues for the ensemble-pessimism paper, which resolves to a preprint with no recorded venue and is cited as such [64] — load-bearing twice, for M7 and for the candidate inversion mechanism. A further optimizer-comparison paper carries no venue field and is flagged unverified rather than guessed [58]. **The fabrications this pass caught were not in the paper under audit. They were in this audit's own relays.**

**Tooling limits.** The fetch tool cannot ingest PDFs in this environment; every PDF URL form returns a junk-content rejection, traced to a post-fetch filter rather than to extraction. Most stored notes for PDF sources therefore hold only an abstract page, and all full-text verification ran through HTML mirrors or external extraction. **Verdicts do rest on primary full text**, but a later reader cannot re-grep that text from the note. Semantic Scholar was rate-limited for the entire session, so arXiv and OpenAlex carried all citation chaining including the forward-citation walks; no verdict rests on a Semantic-Scholar-only result. OpenAlex full-text search is very noisy here — it returned climate models and echocardiography for optimization queries — so its nulls are weak evidence of absence and the N6 verdict does not rest on them.

**Analyses recommended but not run.** The mixed-effects model with task as a random effect, which the repository's own flaw ledger calls for and which **two independent investigators named as their falsifier**; no normalizer robustness check substitutes for it. The floor-effect disentangling analysis for item 2, whose inputs the released artifact already stores. Bias correction on the interaction beyond the four corners. And the effect-size recomputation from RaM's published table has not been independently reimplemented — **the N6 defence leans on a number this audit computed once.**

**Questions raised without resolution.** Two figures in Elimination 3 sit within rounding of two equivalence-test outputs from a different suite — 0.375 against 0.3762, and "roughly 0.48" against 0.4840. Elimination 3 is described as a synthetic-grid result while the equivalence test is a Design-Bench quantity. Either a three-significant-figure coincidence or a transplanted number in a load-bearing paragraph. **Resolvable by the author in one lookup; neither reading is asserted here.** Whether a depth sweep exists unreported was not confirmed — item M8's force rests on the architecture description in the main text, not on confirmed absence. The supplement was not checked for duplicate or contradictory kernel-limit framing. And the AutoML literature's treatment of model class versus training objective as one factor or two was only partially swept; if a counter-source exists it would attack the first ground of the N6 residual, which currently rests on the uncertainty-quantification survey's taxonomy alone [12].

**Scope boundaries not crossed.** Design-Bench's corrected corner was not re-run through the alternative-normalizer sweep. The paper's code was verified at the line level for two confounds only; the bootstrap implementation, the ANOVA implementation and the statistics module were **not** audited, and every numeric verdict here assumes they are correct — an assumption, not a finding. **No experiment was reproduced.** All numeric verification compares the paper's text against its own stored artifacts; if an artifact is itself wrong, this audit would not detect it. And each of the corner values, inversion counts, frozen-cell counts and raw-units gaps was computed once, by one implementation.
