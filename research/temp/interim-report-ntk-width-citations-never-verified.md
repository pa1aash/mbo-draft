**Locus question:** Do the four NTK/GP/spectral-bias citations backing Elimination 2 actually say what the paper cites them for, and is the recommended Xu et al. 2021 mechanism-citation compatible with Elimination 2 as written?

## What the new sources say

I fetched all four primary sources fresh via arXiv/ar5iv HTML mirrors (PDF fetch is broken per the known systemic bug) through the `hyperresearch-fetcher` subagent, which built claim-rich notes with direct quotes and grep evidence on raw extracted text (16,123 / 6,467 / 18,086 / 12,434 lines respectively).

**1. Jacot, Gabriel, Hongler 2018** (`[[180607572v4-neural-tangent-kernel-convergence-and-generalization-ntk-adversarial]]`). The paper makes two logically distinct claims: Proposition 1 is the classical GP-at-*initialization* result, explicitly attributed to prior work ("As observed in (16;4;7;13;6)..."), not novel here. Theorem 1 — the paper's own new result — is that the NTK (a *different* object, the training-dynamics kernel) "converges in probability to an explicit deterministic limit" and "stays constant during training," which for least-squares loss makes the trained function follow a linear ODE and remain Gaussian for all training time t, not just t=0. Section 6.1's own finite-width ablation (n=500 vs n=10000) is directly on-point: "For the wider network, the NTK shows less variance and is smoother... this effect is much less apparent for the wider network... than for the smaller network." Zero mentions of "ensemble," "acquisition," "surrogate," or "Thompson" anywhere in the paper.

**2. Lee et al. 2018 (NNGP)** (`[[171100165-deep-neural-networks-as-gaussian-processes]]`). Strictly an initialization/prior claim: "an i.i.d. prior over its parameters is equivalent to a Gaussian process (GP), in the limit of infinite network width... This correspondence enables exact Bayesian inference for infinite width neural networks... **without stochastic gradient-based training**." Crucially, the paper explicitly declines to claim SGD-trained finite networks converge to GP behavior: "Whether gradient-based stochastic optimization implements an approximate Bayesian computation is an interesting question... **Further investigation is needed** to determine if SGD does approximately implement Bayesian inference." It also flags an order-of-limits caveat: its primary derivation takes layer widths to infinity *sequentially*, crediting the *simultaneous*-all-layers case to a concurrent paper (Matthews et al. 2018).

**3. Lee et al. 2019** (`[[190206720-wide-neural-networks-of-any-depth-evolve-as-linear-models-under-gradie]]`). This is the paper that actually closes the gap Lee 2018 leaves open. Theorem 2.2: "If η < η_critical, then... as n→∞, f_t(x) converges in distribution to the Gaussian..." — i.e., the network's output *during* gradient descent, not just at t=0, is Gaussian in the width limit, "extending results from Jacot et al." But the paper itself flags: "the NNGP predictive distribution which corresponds to exact Bayesian inference, while similar, is **noticeably different** from the predictive distribution at the end of gradient descent training" — GD-GP and NNGP-Bayesian-GP are both Gaussian but not the same object.

**4. Rahaman et al. 2019** (`[[180608734-on-the-spectral-bias-of-neural-networks]]`). Confirms the direction — "a learning bias of deep networks towards **low frequency** functions... which manifests itself as a frequency-dependent learning speed" — defined and demonstrated as a function of **training iteration** (Experiment 1/Figure 2 varies iterations 100→80,000; width is not varied there at all). The one formal theorem is explicitly *not* a width-limit statement: "Eqn 6 applies to general ReLU networks with **arbitrary width and depth**." Most importantly, the paper's own controlled width-vs-depth ablation (Figs. 15–16) finds: "increasing the depth (for fixed width) significantly improves the network's ability to fit higher frequencies... increasing the width (for fixed depth) also helps, but the effect is **considerably weaker**" — despite width increasing *exponentially* (16→32→64→128) against depth's *linear* increase (3→4→5→6). Structured-claims extraction (`research/temp/claims-180608734-*.json`) independently tags three of five claims with `stance: refutes` against "treating spectral bias as vanishing/inverting in a width limit" and "the claim that width is the dominant/controlling variable for spectral bias."

## Evidence synthesis

The three GP/NTK citations (Jacot, Lee 2018, Lee 2019), read together, do legitimately cover the general claim "wide networks approach GP behaviour": Lee 2018 nails the at-initialization correspondence, Jacot supplies the frozen-NTK mechanism plus a squared-loss training-time argument, and Lee 2019 is the actual load-bearing proof that the network's output *during* gradient descent (Theorem 2.2, conditional on η < η_critical) converges to a Gaussian in the width limit. None of the three is being used for a claim it explicitly contradicts — this is a legitimate three-paper cluster citation for an assembled, well-established fact, standard practice in this literature. The only precision gap is that neither Jacot nor Lee 2019's training-time GP is literally the same object as Lee 2018's Bayesian-posterior NNGP ("noticeably different" predictive distributions, per Lee 2019's own text) — a nuance the blanket phrase "approach GP behaviour" elides but does not falsify.

Rahaman et al. is a different story. The MBO paper's sentence structure sets up an explicit **width-based contrast** — "wide networks approach GP behaviour... **and** finite networks show spectral bias" — using "wide" versus "finite" as opposite poles of the same axis, exactly parallel to the NTK papers' width-limit framing. But Rahaman's own paper actively argues against reading spectral bias as width-controlled: its formal theorem holds "for arbitrary width and depth" (not a width-limit statement at all), its defining experiment varies training iterations, not width, and its own width-vs-depth ablation finds width is the weak lever — "considerably weaker" than depth even though width was scaled up exponentially and depth only linearly. This is not a subtle nuance; it is the paper's own explicit, quantified finding, running in the opposite direction from the reading the MBO paper's phrasing invites.

This compounds with a concrete architectural fact I verified directly in `main.tex` (line 88): the ensemble members are "two-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs (width 96,...)" and the width sweep (line 175) varies *only* width (96→1024) at that fixed two-hidden-layer depth. If Rahaman et al.'s own evidence is right that depth, not width, is the dominant driver of a network's capacity to escape low-frequency bias, then the width-only ablation the paper ran cannot fully discharge the spectral-bias half of the joint hypothesis it was set up to test — a depth sweep would be the ablation that "does" test that branch, and the paper does not report one. The paper's claim "Our K-sweep does not test that... so we ran the ablation that does" is accurate for the *capacity-as-GP-approach* branch (width sweep correctly targets that) but overstated for the *spectral-bias* branch specifically, per the very source cited to motivate it.

On the Xu et al. 2021 tension: Xu's Theorem 1 is proved "in the NTK regime" (i.e., assumes the over-parameterized/wide-network approximation from Jacot et al. 2018) and its asymptotic parameter is **spatial distance along a ray** (x0 = tv, t→∞), not width or training time. Its own robustness check (per the extracted claims file) confirms the linear far-field extrapolation behavior holds "approximately across networks with different depths, **widths**, learning rates, and batch sizes" — i.e., empirically the mechanism does not require literally infinite width to manifest, it degrades gracefully at practical widths. This is exactly compatible with Elimination 2's finding that the GP-ensemble gap is flat across 96–1024: if the qualitative bounded-GP-mean-vs-unbounded-linear-ensemble-mean difference is already present (even approximately) at w=96, the width-invariance of the *gap* is what Xu's mechanism would predict, not what it contradicts. Elimination 2 itself pre-empts the apparent tension by explicitly scoping its own negative claim ("the sweep stops at 1024 while the kernel limits are asymptotic... makes no asymptotic claim") — it never claims the gap would persist as width→∞, only that it survives at practical widths. Xu's theorem is silent on whether the ensemble's off-support behavior would eventually track the GP as width→∞; it only says that far from support, in the idealized limit, extrapolation becomes linear rather than reverting — which is a different (and, per Elimination 2's own text, un-adjudicated) asymptotic axis. No contradiction, but the paper does not currently draw this distinction anywhere in the text, so a reviewer who reads "NTK regime" in Xu and "kernel limits are asymptotic" in Elimination 2 side by side could reasonably suspect the paper is invoking the same asymptotic machinery to prove two opposite things.

## Committed position

Three of the four citations backing Elimination 2 are faithful, if under-specified about which sub-claim (at-init vs. during-training) belongs to which paper; the fourth — Rahaman et al. for "finite networks show spectral bias" — is used in a width-monotonic framing ("wide... **and** finite...") that the source's own controlled ablation contradicts, and this interacts with a verifiable architectural fact (the width sweep holds depth fixed at two hidden layers) to leave the spectral-bias branch of Elimination 2's motivating hypothesis untested by the ablation the paper claims discharges it. The Xu et al. 2021 recommendation is compatible with Elimination 2 as written because the two papers are asymptotic in different variables (far-field distance vs. width) and Elimination 2 explicitly disclaims any asymptotic-width claim — but the paper should add one clarifying sentence if it adopts Xu, because as written a reviewer has no signal that "NTK regime" (Xu) and "kernel limits are asymptotic" (Elimination 2) are not the same axis being played both ways.

- **Position:** Jacot 2018, Lee 2018, and Lee 2019 are FAITHFUL citations for "wide networks approach GP behaviour" (collectively, not individually complete); Rahaman 2019 is MISCITED in effect — not wrong on direction (low-frequency bias exists) but wrong on the width-monotonicity the sentence structure implies, which is the opposite of what Rahaman's own depth-vs-width ablation shows; and the Xu et al. 2021 recommendation is COMPATIBLE with Elimination 2 but requires one clarifying sentence distinguishing the two asymptotic axes to avoid an easy misreading.
- **Confidence:** high for the Rahaman miscitation and the Jacot/Lee2018/Lee2019 faithfulness reads (direct quotes + structured-claims extraction all converge); medium-high for the Xu-compatibility verdict (the logical argument is sound and supported by Xu's own robustness-check language, but no experiment in the paper or corpus directly measures whether the width-96 ensemble is already "NTK-regime-like" — that is an assumption, not a demonstrated fact).
- **Boundary conditions:** the Rahaman miscitation finding holds specifically because the paper's ensemble architecture is fixed-depth (two hidden layers) across the entire width sweep (main.tex line 88, 175) — if the paper had also run a depth sweep, or if Rahaman's ablation had shown width and depth comparably potent, this critique would not apply. The Xu-compatibility verdict holds only as long as Elimination 2 keeps its explicit "makes no asymptotic claim" scoping; if a later section of the paper were to claim the gap would persist as width→∞, that claim would need direct engagement with Xu's (and Lee 2019's) actual asymptotic regime, which the paper does not currently test.
- **What would change this position:** for Rahaman, a demonstration that the paper's specific two-hidden-layer, width-96-to-1024 architecture range shows a comparably strong width effect on spectral bias as Rahaman's Fig. 15 depth effect would undercut the miscitation finding — no such demonstration exists in the corpus. For Xu, a formal or empirical result showing that finite-width (w=96–1024), two-hidden-layer ReLU ensembles do NOT exhibit even approximately-linear far-field extrapolation (e.g., a low R² on the ray test the Xu note itself proposes) would break the compatibility argument and mean Xu's mechanism cannot explain the paper's finding at the widths actually tested.
- **Evidence weight:** 4 primary sources fetched and grepped fresh (Jacot, Lee 2018, Lee 2019, Rahaman) plus 1 already-fetched primary (Xu, from a sibling investigator this session) plus direct verification against `main.tex` lines 88 and 175 for the ensemble's fixed-depth architecture. Structured claims files (`research/temp/claims-180608734-*.json`, `research/temp/claims-200911848-*.json`) independently corroborate the Rahaman depth-dominance finding (3 of 5 claims tagged `stance: refutes` against width-as-controlling-variable) and the Xu robustness-across-widths finding. No source in this batch contradicts the Jacot/Lee2018/Lee2019 faithfulness reading.

## Open questions

- Does any part of the paper (main.tex or supplement) explicitly attribute the at-init vs. during-training halves of "approach GP behaviour" to specific papers, or is it always the blanket three-citation cluster? (I found only the one instance, line 175 — the claim appears exactly once in main.tex.)
- Was a depth sweep ever run and simply not reported, or genuinely never attempted? This bears on whether the Rahaman-miscitation finding is a reporting gap or a real evidentiary hole in the paper's argument.
- Has anyone tested the Xu note's proposed R² ray-extrapolation diagnostic (ensemble-mean vs. GP-mean linear-fit along rays from the data centroid) on the paper's actual trained checkpoints? This is the single experiment that would upgrade the Xu-compatibility verdict from "logically sound" to "empirically confirmed."
