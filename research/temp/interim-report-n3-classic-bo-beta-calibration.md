# Interim report: n3-classic-bo-beta-calibration

**Locus question:** Does the classic BO acquisition-calibration literature (GP-UCB beta_t schedules, Srinivas et al. 2010; kappa tuning) already own N3's "a shared beta delivers different effective conservatism across surrogates with different sigma scales"?
**Flavor:** convergent

## What the corpus already said

Before this pass, the corpus's N3 coverage rested on two notes, both flagged by the loci-analyst as thin (2-3 sources total):

- [[why-so-pessimistic-estimating-uncertainties-for-offline-rl-through-ensembles-and]] (Ghasemipour, Gu, Nachum, NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2205.13703) — the miss-catcher's own "closest analog," verdicted NONE FOUND for N3. It shows that a *shared* pessimistic Bellman target within one Q-ensemble can make a nominally-pessimistic LCB become effectively optimistic — nominal-vs-effective mismatch, but the mechanism is target-independence/correlation-structure across Bellman backups within ONE model class, not sigma-scale mismatch ACROSS model classes. 0 hits in that paper's full text for "Gaussian process," "surrogate," or "Bayesian optimization."
- [[simple-and-principled-uncertainty-estimation-with-deterministic-deep-learning-vi]] (SNGP, Liu et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2006.10108) — establishes, formally, that different surrogate/uncertainty-estimator classes genuinely differ in how their sigma behaves: a GP-RBF posterior variance is provably "distance aware" (monotonically increases away from training data), while deep ensembles and MC-Dropout are NOT. This is important as background for N3 — it is direct evidence that the PREMISE of N3 (different surrogate classes produce structurally different sigma) is true and already established in the UQ literature — but the paper is pure classification/calibration/OOD-detection; 0 hits for "Bayesian optimization," "LCB," "acquisition," or "beta" anywhere in the body. It never poses a shared-beta-across-classes acquisition-comparison question.
- [[miss-catcher-search-log-n2-n3-n6-n9-n1-residual]] — the prior sweep's own verdict: **NONE FOUND** for N3, explicitly noting that "the classic BO beta literature was never checked" as an open gap — precisely the gap this locus was assigned to close.

So going in: the corpus had strong evidence that (a) the general SHAPE of "nominal ≠ effective pessimism" has been said before (Ghasemipour), and (b) the premise that different surrogate classes have structurally different sigma behavior is well-established (SNGP), but no source had actually opened the foundational GP-UCB beta_t paper to check whether the classic single-surrogate calibration literature already subsumes N3's cross-class framing.

## What the new sources say

**1. Srinivas, Krause, Kakade, Seeger, "Gaussian Process Optimization in the Bandit Setting: No Regret and Experimental Design," ICML 2010, arXiv:0912.3995** — [[gaussian-process-optimization-in-the-bandit-setting-no-regret-and-experimental-d]]. Full text fetched and grepped (13,433 words, 6 pages). This is the canonical origin of the beta_t schedule for GP-UCB. Its three theorems give explicit closed-form beta_t definitions:

> "Lemma 5.1 Pick δ ∈ (0, 1) and set βt = 2 log(|D|πt/δ), where Σ(t≥1) πt−1 = 1, πt > 0. Then, |f(x) − µt−1(x)| ≤ βt^(1/2) σt−1(x) holds with probability ≥ 1 − δ. ∀x ∈ D ∀t ≥ 1"

Every argument to beta_t — round index t, confidence parameter delta, and kernel/decision-space quantities (|D|, dimension d, smoothness constants a/b/r, information-gain bound gamma_t, RKHS-norm bound B) — is intrinsic to a SINGLE evolving GP posterior. The paper's own comparisons (GP-UCB vs. EI vs. MPI vs. max-mean vs. max-variance) hold one GP posterior fixed and vary only the acquisition RULE on top of it — the transpose of N3's axis of variation. Grep confirms: 0 hits for "surrogate," "ensemble," "different models," "calibrat" anywhere in the paper; the only 3 hits for "scale" refer to kernel lengthscale. This paper never discusses two structurally different surrogate/uncertainty-estimator classes sharing one beta.

**2. Lu, Polyzos, Li, Giannakis, "Surrogate modeling for Bayesian optimization beyond a single Gaussian process," IEEE TPAMI 2023, arXiv:2205.14090.** Fetched full text and grepped as an adversarial candidate (does the "beyond a single GP" framing in the title mean it addresses cross-class beta mismatch?). It does not: the paper's entire design point is to sidestep the beta-tuning problem altogether by using Thompson sampling over an ensemble of GPs — "requires no additional design parameters" — rather than compare a fixed beta across surrogate classes. 0 hits for "beta," "same coefficient," "different surrogate," "fair comparison," "miscalib," "uncertainty scale," "sigma" in a targeted grep. Ruled out as orthogonal to N3: it combines multiple GPs into one acquisition decision, it never contrasts one nominal beta applied to structurally different model classes.

**3. Benechehab, Thomas, Kégl, "Deep autoregressive density nets vs neural ensembles for model-based offline reinforcement learning," arXiv:2402.02858.** Fetched full text and grepped. This is the closest of the two new adversarial candidates: it directly compares two structurally different uncertainty-estimator classes (bootstrap ensembles vs. autoregressive density nets) inside an offline-MBRL conservatism/penalty framework, and explicitly discusses calibratedness (via a KS-statistic) as the reason the two classes differ in downstream usefulness — 24 hits for "calibrat," 9 for "conservatism," 1 for "penalty coefficient." But its argument is "autoregressive density nets are better-calibrated than ensembles, so replace ensembles with them" — it explicitly aims to REDUCE the hyperparameter-tuning burden by picking the better-calibrated model class, not to diagnose that a SHARED penalty coefficient lambda produces mismatched effective conservatism BECAUSE the two classes have different sigma magnitudes. It never states or measures that framing. Ruled out.

## Evidence synthesis

The three new sources triangulate cleanly around one conclusion: the classic BO acquisition-calibration literature is organized entirely around the axis of **time / decision-space complexity for a single surrogate** (Srinivas), or around the axis of **which surrogate class is better-calibrated in isolation, so as to choose one and discard the beta-tuning problem** (Lu et al.; Benechehab et al.), and never around the axis N3 actually claims: **holding beta fixed while comparing across surrogate classes that have different sigma magnitudes, and measuring the resulting mismatch in effective conservatism**.

This is a load-bearing distinction, not a rhetorical one. GP-UCB's beta_t is a probabilistic-coverage guarantee — it is derived from a union bound over an infinite/growing sequence of rounds t, calibrated against ONE posterior's own confidence-interval width, to bound cumulative regret for a run of the algorithm. It answers "how wide must my confidence band be at round t to keep missing the optimum with probability < delta," which is a question that presupposes a fixed sigma-generating process. It has no mechanism for, and no theorem addressing, what happens when you swap in a second surrogate whose sigma lives on a structurally different scale (e.g., a poorly-calibrated deep ensemble whose sigma might be systematically 3x smaller or larger than a GP's) while holding the SAME nominal beta — which is exactly N3's claim. The SNGP paper (pre-existing corpus evidence) independently corroborates why this mismatch is real and not merely hypothetical: GP-RBF sigma is provably distance-aware (monotone in distance from training data) while ensemble/MC-Dropout sigma is not, meaning the two classes' sigma outputs are not just differently-SCALED but differently-SHAPED functions of the input space — a stronger form of incomparability than a simple constant-scale mismatch, and one that would make a shared beta even more clearly miscalibrated across classes than a naive reading suggests.

The two newly-checked adversarial candidates (Lu et al., Benechehab et al.) show that when the BO/offline-RL literature DOES bring two uncertainty-estimator classes into contact, its instinct is either to fuse them (Thompson sampling over an ensemble of GPs, sidestepping beta) or to pick a winner by calibration quality and discard the loser (autoregressive nets over ensembles) — never to hold one shared beta fixed across the two and diagnose the resulting conservatism mismatch as N3 does. Combined with the pre-existing Ghasemipour finding (mismatch mechanism is target-independence WITHIN one Q-ensemble class, across Bellman-backup iterations — a training-time artifact, not a cross-class-comparison artifact), the corpus now has three independent "adjacent but not identical" mechanisms, and zero direct hits, after five searches spanning the two most likely places a reviewer would look: (1) the foundational GP-UCB single-surrogate schedule, and (2) papers that explicitly compare surrogate/uncertainty-estimator classes.

## Committed position

The evidence now supports a clean, well-stress-tested NONE FOUND for N3's specific cross-surrogate-class sigma-scale-mismatch framing. GP-UCB's beta_t (Srinivas et al. 2010) is the field's canonical answer to "how should beta be calibrated," and it answers a genuinely different question — a time-indexed confidence-width schedule for one fixed surrogate, derived to bound regret via a union bound over rounds — that has no mechanism for, and never gestures at, a cross-model-class comparison. Ghasemipour et al. (2022) remains the closest SHAPE-level analog (nominal ≠ effective pessimism) but its mechanism (target-independence within one Q-ensemble) is orthogonal to N3's mechanism (sigma-scale mismatch across surrogate classes). The two newly-checked candidates that most plausibly could have bridged the gap — Lu et al.'s "beyond a single GP" ensemble-of-GPs paper and Benechehab et al.'s ensembles-vs-autoregressive-nets comparison — both bring multiple uncertainty estimators into contact with each other but resolve the tension by fusing or replacing, never by diagnosing a shared-beta miscalibration. The load-bearing reason for this verdict: every paper in the classic BO calibration literature that touches beta_t treats "the surrogate" as a fixed input to the calibration problem, never as a free variable being compared against a structurally different surrogate under one shared beta — that framing requires N3's specific move of holding beta constant AND varying surrogate class AND measuring the resulting conservatism gap, which no located paper does.

- **Position:** N3's claim — that a shared beta across surrogate classes with different sigma scales produces unmatched effective conservatism in an acquisition-function comparison — is NOT owned by the classic BO beta-calibration literature (GP-UCB / Srinivas 2010) or by any paper that explicitly compares surrogate/uncertainty-estimator classes (Lu et al. 2023, Benechehab et al. 2024); verdict is **NONE FOUND**, with Srinivas 2010 and Ghasemipour 2022 as the two closest, explicitly-distinguishable partials.
- **Confidence:** high (>80%) that the SPECIFIC cross-surrogate-class-beta-mismatch framing is unclaimed in the BO/bandit calibration literature proper (Srinivas-lineage papers), given the definitional/structural mismatch is unambiguous (time-indexed single-posterior schedule vs. cross-class comparison) rather than a matter of interpretation. Medium-high (~70%) confidence that NO paper anywhere in the broader BO/offline-RL/offline-MBO space states this framing, since the adversarial sweep is necessarily incomplete (9 search phrasings + 2 full-text-checked candidates is a real but bounded sample of a large literature, and Semantic Scholar was rate-limited throughout, cutting off one full citation-graph route).
- **Boundary conditions:** This verdict applies specifically to the CLASSIC single-surrogate GP-UCB beta-scheduling literature and to papers that explicitly bring two+ surrogate/uncertainty-estimator classes into contact (the two most likely places a reviewer would point). It does NOT rule out that some corner of the calibrated-uncertainty / conformal-prediction literature (outside this locus's budget) states an abstract version of "two differently-scaled uncertainty estimators cannot share one confidence-multiplier without recalibration" as a general statistical fact divorced from any acquisition-function or BO framing — that adjacent literature was flagged by the adversarial-sweep agent (calibration/conformal-prediction papers surfaced as one of three unrelated-but-adjacent clusters) but not fetched, since it addresses whether one surrogate's uncertainty is well-calibrated in isolation, not whether a SHARED beta across classes yields mismatched effective conservatism in an optimization/acquisition context.
- **What would change this position:** A paper that (a) holds one nominal beta/kappa/lambda fixed, (b) applies it to two or more structurally different surrogate/uncertainty-estimator classes (not just different training runs of the same class), and (c) explicitly measures or argues that the resulting EFFECTIVE conservatism (not just raw calibration quality) differs across classes because of sigma-scale differences, in a BO, bandit, offline-RL, or offline-MBO acquisition/optimization context — would flip this to PRIOR WORK FOUND. A weaker version — any paper naming "beta must be recalibrated when the surrogate class changes" even without the "different sigma scale, same nominal beta" framing — would downgrade confidence from high to medium and warrant explicit engagement in the draft.
- **Evidence weight:** 3 papers fetched/grepped this pass (Srinivas 2010 registered; Lu et al. 2023 and Benechehab et al. 2024 checked full-text, not registered, both ruled out) + 2 pre-existing corpus papers re-read (Ghasemipour 2022, SNGP 2020) + 9 adversarial web-search phrasings run by the sub-agent, all NONE FOUND or off-topic. Zero papers support N3's specific framing; all five checked candidates (across this pass and the prior miss-catcher pass) independently corroborate the same negative result via different, non-overlapping mechanisms (single-surrogate time-schedule; within-class target-independence; ensemble-fusion-to-avoid-beta; calibration-quality-based-model-selection). No contradicting evidence found.
