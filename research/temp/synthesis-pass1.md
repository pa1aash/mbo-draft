## N6 Verdict: The Crossed Surrogate x Optimizer Factorial

### A. The verdict and what a kill would require

**CONFIRMED NONE-FOUND**, with one paywalled lead unresolved and disclosed here rather than in the terminal section, because a reader is entitled to see the residual risk attached to the claim it qualifies.

A kill requires four conjuncts simultaneously, in an offline or fixed-dataset setting: **(a)** surrogate **model class** varied across at least two genuinely distinct classes; **(b)** optimizer or search routine varied across at least two routines; **(c)** the two axes genuinely **crossed**, every surrogate run with every optimizer; **(d)** a **two-way variance decomposition** of the outcome — an attribution of variance to each axis and their interaction — rather than a leaderboard. Conjunct (a) matters because a training-objective swap inside one architecture is a different kind of factor; (b) because a bundled end-to-end method is not a clean optimizer level; (c) because a one-factor-at-a-time (OFAT) design, which holds all inputs fixed but one, cannot estimate interactions; (d) because descriptive per-cell reporting is not an attribution. No paper found satisfies all four.

### B. The queries, run verbatim

Direct-target queries across arXiv and OpenAlex: `crossed factorial surrogate optimizer offline model-based optimization`; `two-way ANOVA surrogate optimizer variance decomposition black-box optimization`; `factorial design surrogate model acquisition optimizer offline`; `eta squared surrogate main effect optimizer main effect benchmark`; `attribution surrogate versus optimizer offline MBO`; `variance decomposition benchmark components machine learning pipeline`; `crossed design machine learning benchmark two-way ANOVA`; `factorial design deep learning benchmark components`.

The 2026 frontier was swept first, since a recent appearance is the failure mode a stale audit cannot see. Eight offline-MBO and offline black-box-optimization papers from January through June 2026 were extracted in full and grepped for twelve decomposition terms — `factorial`, `crossed`, `ANOVA`, `analysis of variance`, `eta squared`, `η²`, `main effect`, `variance decomposition`, `two-way`, `interaction effect`, `attribution`, `OFAT`. **Zero hits, all eight papers, all twelve terms** [6][14][15][16][17][18][19]. The failures are not uniform, which strengthens the negative: SPADE ablates only its surrogate backbone against a fixed evolutionary-plus-LCB optimizer [14]; the diffusion-language-model optimizer's ablation is leave-one-component-out rather than crossed [15]; CliqueFlowmer varies the optimizer but holds one surrogate fixed throughout [16]; the ranking-learnability paper varies training loss across five Design-Bench tasks with a single fixed gradient-ascent optimizer in every experiment [17]; and the meta-learning and conformal-certification papers each bundle surrogate and optimizer into one method identity per row [18][19]. The subfield's own review returns zero hits across 25,965 words — it reviews the literature, it does not run the experiment [1].

That bundling pattern is the load-bearing one, and it is the status quo the paper says it dissects: **the offline-MBO literature evaluates surrogate and optimizer as one atomic method per baseline.** The review certifies the resulting gap in its own words — benchmarks "often emphasize overall optimization performance without clarifying whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance" [1].

The extension check — the half a re-audit usually skips — walked roughly 347 citing papers across Hutter 2014 (62 citers, 2023–2026), Liang 2021 (166), Moosbauer 2022 (4, all years) and van Rijn & Hutter 2018 (115), with six candidates deep-verified and two fetched in full. EXPObench compares six surrogate-assisted algorithms with zero ANOVA hits, and its "offline" means offline surrogate *training*. Bischl et al.'s 2023 hyperparameter-optimization survey — the highest-cited Moosbauer citer at 836 citations — returns zero hits and cites Moosbauer only as related work. PED-ANOVA, the quantum-neural-network fANOVA lineage, CART-ANOVA transfer learning and Instance Space Analysis all extend functional ANOVA (fANOVA — a variance decomposition over a single algorithm's hyperparameter space) *within one algorithm's configuration space*.

Then three regions never previously searched, chosen because a crossed factorial with ANOVA was *more* likely there than in offline MBO: surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation; AutoML, combined algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimization, and kernel selection; and simulation-optimization with design-and-analysis of computer experiments.

### D. Three grounds, one falsified on contact

The paper's residual is defended against RaM on three grounds. One is false as stated, and the paper should concede it before a reviewer does.

| Ground | Status |
|---|---|
| **(1) Loss type ≠ model class** | **SURVIVES**, now with domain support. The canonical uncertainty-quantification survey catalogues method family as the top-level taxonomy and loss function as a separate cross-cutting axis in its own tables [11]. The paper's nearest comparator treats model class as constitutively bound to its fitting procedure [12] — no GP posterior without marginal-likelihood fitting, no ensemble without per-member training — whereas RaM swaps MSE for ListNet *inside one unchanged MLP* [2]. Statable criterion: a change is model-class if it cannot be instantiated without changing the estimator family, and training-objective if it can be applied at fixed family |
| **(2) Bundled methods ≠ a clean optimizer factor** | **FALSIFIED AS STATED.** RaM's own Appendix E.5 describes four of its nine methods — BO-qEI, CMA-ES, REINFORCE and gradient ascent — as "baselines that optimize a trained model." **A clean 4 × 2 crossed optimizer × loss sub-grid exists inside Table 3, in offline MBO, published before this submission** [2]. The ground survives only for the other five: CbAS and MINs are genuinely aliased in the strict design-of-experiments sense [13] — model family and search paradigm co-occur on every run and cannot be estimated apart — while Tri-Mentoring, PGS and Match-OPT layer method-specific machinery on top |
| **(3) Descriptive reporting ≠ a decomposition** | **SURVIVES**, but must be stated as a claim about what RaM *reports*, since this audit itself derived the decomposition from RaM's published table |

**The uncomfortable finding, which the paper should state itself.** Defending N6 against RaM required computing a two-way ANOVA from RaM's own published Table 3, which reports mean and standard deviation per cell over a uniform eight seeds — so both moments and the sample size are available for all ninety cells. That computation takes about fifteen lines of code and yields **a loss axis at η² ≈ 0.027 against a method axis at η² ≈ 0.577** [2]. This *strengthens* "no prior work **reports** a crossed surrogate × optimizer decomposition," which is still true, and *destroys* "no prior work **could have**," which this audit disproved in under a day. **N6 must therefore be stated as a claim about what the literature reports, never about what the design space permits.** The contribution is running the decomposition as the primary analysis on a purpose-built grid, not the arithmetic itself.

It also produces a free result worth keeping: two crossed grids in the same subfield, decomposed the same way, disagree about which axis carries the variance. RaM's loss axis is negligible against its method axis; this paper's surrogate axis dominates its optimizer axis. That is a more interesting sentence than any novelty claim — and it is independent evidence for ground (1), because if loss and model class were the same kind of factor, swapping one would not move 2.7% of variance while swapping the other moves the dominant share.

**The grounds are nowhere on the page.** The introduction files `tan2025ltr` under "surrogate-class comparisons hold the optimizer constant," beside `li2024bnnsurrogates` [12]. That is accurate about RaM's *main* experiment — the five-surrogate Spearman comparison genuinely holds gradient ascent fixed — so the sentence is **incomplete rather than false**, and the distinction matters. But it is incomplete in exactly the direction that makes RaM look safely outside the residual before a reader checks Table 3.

## (i) Claims the Literature Contradicts or That Are Miscited

Twelve mandatory items, ordered by how cleanly a knowledgeable reviewer could use each as a kill. Each carries the claim, the paper's framing, what the source actually says, and the required fix. The section closes with the citations that **pass**, because a list of twelve defects reads as an indictment by default and the passes are what make the grading proportionate.

### A. `demsar2006statistical` — a threshold that does not exist, attached to inverted reasoning

**The claim** (`main.tex:220`, echoed in the contributions list): *"we are below the threshold for the test we ran: this omnibus is recommended for more than ten datasets."*

**The source contains nothing of the kind.** The full thirty-page JMLR paper was extracted (103,393 characters) and grepped directly [41]: `more than ten` = **0**, `at least ten` = **0**, `ten or more` = **0**. The only two occurrences of "ten data sets" describe Demšar's own power-simulation sampling procedure — "samples of ten data sets were randomly selected," "1000 random selections of ten data sets."

This is not an over-read of something stated loosely. **It is a specific numeric recommendation attributed to a real source that does not make it**, in a paper whose entire rhetorical position is that unstated protocol details are the field's central methodological failure. And it inverts his reasoning, which matters more. Demšar recommends "a set of simple, yet safe and robust non-parametric tests... the Friedman test with the corresponding post-hoc tests for comparison of more classifiers over multiple data sets," with no dataset-count floor anywhere, and his stated design context is explicitly the small-*n* regime: "the number of data sets is usually much less than 30." **He recommends the non-parametric route precisely because *n* is small.** His own Figure 5 independently shows Friedman as more powerful and more replicable than ANOVA at small samples.

**Fix — and it is a net gain.** Delete the clause and the citation. The conclusion is unaffected and better supported without them: the power limitation is independently established by the same sentence's paired-test calculation (|d_z| ≥ 1.27 for 80% power at α = 0.05, *n* = 7) and by Agarwal's never-claim-equivalence point, which is verified verbatim and correctly used [42]. **The fix removes a false warrant and an unnecessary self-deprecation at once — the paper was conceding a methodological weakness it does not have.**

### B. `fan2024minucb` — three uses, three failures, one of them inverting a theorem

Fan, Wang, Ng & Hu, NeurIPS 2024; full text extracted and grepped across 1,550 lines [22].

**B1. "UCB-as-local-search" is not established prior work.** The paper writes *"as is the reading of a UCB-style acquisition as local search"* — presenting it as settled. Fan et al.: *"we **propose** our first algorithm... MinUCB, which **replaces** gradient descent step with a step that minimizes the UCB in GIBO,"* and *"This discovery... **opens up possibilities for new designs**."* A 2024 method proposal, not an established reading. The underlying two-stage premise traces to GIBO, which Fan et al. explicitly modify — and **GIBO's exploitation step is plain gradient descent with no confidence bound anywhere** [86].

**B2. The LCB-paralysis citation inverts the source's central result — the most serious item in this deliverable.** The paper: *"The surviving mechanism is LCB paralysis: the GP's lower confidence bound is locally maximal at the data, so the optimizer never leaves... This is the offline instance of a known reading of UCB-style acquisitions as local search, applied rather than discovered."*

Full-text grep of Fan et al. returns **zero hits** for `offline`, `LCB`, `lower confidence bound`, `stuck`, `frozen`, `freeze` and `paralysis` [22]. They define UCB(x) = μ(x) + βσ(x) — the upper confidence bound, an optimism-weighted posterior score — and **minimize** it because their objective is minimization; they never treat a maximization setting and never mirror the transform. Decisively, **their Theorem 1 proves MinUCB converges at a polynomial rate to a genuine local optimum**, contingent on an *increasing* β schedule and *continual active resampling every iteration* — both structurally impossible on a static offline dataset with no further oracle queries. **The paper cites this convergence result as authority for an "optimizer never leaves" failure mode.** That is not a stretched source; it is a source pointing the other way.

**B3. The distance-aware co-citation is unsupported.** Fan has zero hits for `distance-aware` and uses vanilla GP posterior variance throughout.

**Fix (mandatory).** Drop `fan2024minucb` from the LCB-paralysis sentence and the distance-aware co-citation; restate it as a recent method proposal if kept at all. **This costs the paper nothing and removes a reviewer's cleanest kill**, because the paper's own evidence is stronger than the borrowed authority: bit-identical constants across sixteen seeds, in two GP classes, on a continuous task with no argmax decode.

**A correction this audit owes itself.** An earlier pass concluded "nobody owns the trapped-at-the-data failure mode, so claim it outright." That was reached by *excluding* three candidates — TuRBO, whose diagnosis is the opposite pathology of over-exploration and whose locality is deliberately imposed via trust regions [23]; Fan, who proves convergence; GIBO, which uses no confidence bound [86] — without sweeping the vocabulary. **Swept, the "no prior art" phrasing is WITHDRAWN.** Yarotsky (2013), "Examples of inconsistency in optimization by expected improvement," is the closest prior formalization in existence: Theorem 3 proves that expected-improvement-driven Bayesian optimization started at the true optimum has a trajectory converging back to that point and "not dense" [24]. It differs on three real axes — expected improvement rather than a confidence bound, online rather than offline, an adversarial worst-case start rather than a seed-robust empirical observation — so it is **a citation obligation, not a kill**. Two adjacent candidates merit a sentence and are genuinely different: Ament et al. diagnose a *numerical* vanishing-gradient pathology specific to expected improvement [87], and Kim & Choi bound the regret gap between local and global acquisition optimizers, touching GP-UCB but concerning within-round inner-optimization fidelity rather than the outer-loop claim [25].

### C. `liu2020sngp` — cited to bound a claim its own Figure 1 contradicts

**The claim:** *"σ is a distance signal, not an error signal... this is the corrected measurement, **bounded by prior work on distance-aware uncertainty** \citep{liu2020sngp,vanamersfoort2020duq}."*

**SNGP argues the opposite, about the exact model class at issue.** Verbatim: *"deep ensembles... are based on dense output layers that are **not distance aware**. As a result, both methods quantify their predictive uncertainty based on the distance from the decision boundaries, **assigning low uncertainty to OOD examples even if they are far from the data**"* [26]. Its Definition 1 formalizes input-distance-awareness — the property that a model's uncertainty is a monotone function of distance to the training data — as something standard deep models **lack**, and that lack is its entire motivation for a GP output layer with spectral normalization. DUQ corroborates: *"DUQ is certain only on the data distribution, and uncertain away from it: the ideal result. Deep Ensembles is uncertain only along the decision boundary, and certain elsewhere"* [27]. A second, independent scope problem: **all SNGP validation is classification** — a 2D toy, Wide-ResNet on CIFAR, ImageNet, BERT — with zero regression experiments, against a paper that is regression throughout.

**Fix.** The claim cannot be "bounded by" a source that predicts the opposite. Best option: **reframe as a contrast** — "unlike the ensembles SNGP and DUQ characterize as distance-unaware, ours shows a modest positive distance correlation (ρ ≈ 0.26)." That is weaker as an appeal to authority and considerably more interesting as a result. **A finding that runs against SNGP is more publishable than one leaning on it, provided it is labelled as such.** State the classification/regression gap either way.

### D. The σ dichotomy is a false opposition, and the resolution is scoping

**A direct counterexample exists, in regression, peer-reviewed.** Carrete et al., neural-network force fields: *"the Spearman correlation coefficient between uncertainty and error over the validation data set is **0.90 for the committee and 0.91 for the bootstrap-aggregation ensemble**"* [28] — roughly **thirteen times** the paper's ρ ≈ 0.07. A second, independent domain reports deep-ensemble uncertainty correlating with per-pixel error at Pearson 0.715 in seismic-facies segmentation [32].

**But the resolution is scoped, not a rout.** Carrete's 0.90 is measured on validation data from the *same* distribution as training; on their genuinely out-of-distribution test the per-atom correlation degrades while remaining informative in aggregate [28]. The paper measures where an optimizer has pushed designs off-support. SNGP and DUQ measure classification on a 2D toy geometry. **All three can be right — which means the dichotomy itself is the defect.** The literature does not treat distance-awareness and error-tracking as mutually exclusive; SNGP explicitly ties distance-awareness to both calibration and OOD detection [26]. And the large-scale shift benchmark finds deep ensembles the *best-performing* uncertainty method under dataset shift [30].

**A narrower caveat survives, stated precisely because an earlier framing over-reached.** Lakshminarayanan et al. explicitly contrast their NLL-trained heteroscedastic ensemble against *"a commonly used heuristic... to train an ensemble of NNs to minimize MSE and use the empirical variance of the networks' predictions as an approximate measure of uncertainty,"* showing the naive variant *"consistently underestimates the true predictive uncertainty"* — an 80% nominal interval covering roughly 20% of test observations [29]. **The paper's ensemble is exactly that construction.** But the counterweights must travel with it: Ghasemipour et al. treat standard initialization-seed-diversified ensembles — the audited construction — as the *reliable reference*, and what fails in their study is weight-shared "efficient" approximations the paper does not use [49]; D'Angelo & Fortuin note standard ensembles "do not offer any guarantees for diversity," yet their own table shows a standard ensemble winning OOD detection in one setup [31]; and Fort et al. measure function-space diversity rather than input-distance awareness, so they neither support nor undercut the ρ figures [90]. Note also that on Lakshminarayanan's own 1D regression toy, the NLL-trained ensemble's predictive standard deviation *does* grow with distance from data — ensembles are not categorically distance-blind. **Net: "this specific σ construction has a known calibration caveat," never "MSE ensembles are broken."**

**Fix, two parts.** Scope the framing to this grid — "on our tasks, σ tracks distance more than error" — and **drop the dichotomy**, which is a framing fix independent of the numbers. Add one paragraph acknowledging ensemble construction as an unexamined alternative explanation for ρ = 0.07.

### E. `li2024bnnsurrogates` — the "extends below" claim is false as written

**The claim (Confound 3):** *"Our sweep runs over K ∈ {2,3,5,10} and therefore **extends below** the K ∈ {5,10} range over which ensemble surrogates were found robust, **sharing its two upper points and adding K=2,3**."*

**Figure A.7 tests K = {2, 5, 10}.** Verified first-hand rather than by relay: arXiv:2305.20028v2 was fetched directly (39 pages, 111,042 characters) and the Figure A.7 legend tokens on page 28 read `['2 Models', '5 Models', '10 Models']`; a second, independent route reached the same result by rendering that page as an image [12]. Their verbatim finding from the same caption: *"the different ensembles perform similarly across many experiments, showing the robustness of our results to this hyperparameter."* A second K=2 appearance is in Figure A.5, and the main text adds that these hyperparameters "generally have minimal effects on the performance."

**So the range is {2,5,10}, the prior work already went to K=2 and found robustness there, and the claimed residual collapses from "adding K=2,3" to "adding K=3."** This is worse than a wording slip: the K-sensitivity framing exists to establish that the headline sits at the maximum of a sensitive curve, and the closest prior work reports the opposite conclusion over an overlapping range including the very endpoint the paper claims to have contributed. A reviewer from that group — a live possibility in a community this small — reads the sentence as the paper claiming credit for their experiment.

**Fix, and it must be a real fix.** (1) Correct the range wherever `{5,10}` appears. (2) State the residual, which is real and appears nowhere in the paper: **they measure ensemble-BO *performance* robustness; the paper measures whether the *variance attributed to the surrogate axis* is robust.** Those are different quantities and both can be true; made explicit, the claim survives in a narrower and more interesting form. (3) Keep the K-sensitivity result — it is real. Delete only the priority claim. The year trap is clean and the paper has it right: the arXiv comments field reads "ICLR 2024" against a v1 date of 31 May 2023 [12].

### F. `abe2022ensembles` — cited for a direction the source never measures

**The claim (Confound 3):** *"the decline with K runs against **the direction reported for ensemble quality**."*

**Abe et al. run zero ensemble-size ablation.** Full text extracted, 15,095 words; zero hits for `ensemble size`, `number of models` and `size of the ensemble`. Configurations are fixed at M=4 on CIFAR-10 ("combining 4 out of the 5 random seeds") and M=5 on an ImageNet subset, fixed by seed availability rather than swept, and the thesis is ensembles versus a single larger model [44]. **There is no reported direction-with-K in that paper to run against.**

**Fix.** Replace with the source that does report it: Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel & Blundell, Table 4, ImageNet M = 1 to 10, monotonic diminishing-but-never-negative improvement (Top-1 error 22.166% → 18.675%), verbatim *"as M increases, both the accuracy and the quality of predictive uncertainty improve significantly"* [29]. State the caveat: that is classification calibration, not offline-MBO reward, so the cross-domain transfer must be argued rather than assumed. Abe can stay for what it does support.

### G. `rahaman2019spectralbias` — motivates the wrong ablation, and the architecture compounds it

**The claim (Elimination 2):** *"wide networks approach GP behaviour \citep{jacot2018ntk,lee2018dnngp,lee2019widenets} and **finite networks show spectral bias** \citep{rahaman2019spectralbias}... so we ran the ablation that does. At fixed K=5, sweeping per-member **width** over a 10.7× range leaves the gap unchanged."*

**Three of the four citations verify clean, and the audit should say so** — this is the cluster most likely to be assumed sloppy and it holds up. Jacot supplies the neural-tangent-kernel (NTK) mechanism and the training-time Gaussian result [46]; Lee 2018 the at-initialization neural-network-Gaussian-process (NNGP) correspondence [47]; Lee 2019 the proof that wide networks evolve as linear models under gradient descent [48]. Together they cover "wide networks approach GP behaviour," at initialization and during training. The one elided nuance: Lee 2019's own text notes the gradient-descent-limit GP is "noticeably different" from the NNGP Bayesian posterior — a precision gap, not a falsification.

**Rahaman is a different story.** The theorem holds *"for arbitrary width and depth"* — not a width-limit statement at all. The defining experiment varies training *iterations* rather than width. And their own controlled ablation finds: *"increasing the depth (for fixed width) significantly improves the network's ability to fit higher frequencies... increasing the width (for fixed depth) also helps, but the effect is **considerably weaker**"* — despite width scaling exponentially (16→32→64→128) against depth's linear increase (3→4→5→6) [45]. **The paper cites spectral bias to motivate a width ablation when the source says width is the weak lever**, and the sentence structure ("wide... **and** finite...") invites a width-monotonic reading the source argues against.

**The architecture compounds it.** `main.tex:88` fixes the ensemble at a two-hidden-layer MLP across the entire width sweep. **The paper varies the axis Rahaman calls weak and holds fixed the axis Rahaman calls dominant, so the width-only ablation cannot have tested the depth-driven spectral-bias mechanism it invoked Rahaman to motivate.**

**Fix — the second option is better.** (1) Minimal: drop the citation; the width objection stands on the verified NTK/NNGP lineage alone. (2) **Better: keep it and state the limit** — spectral bias is reported as primarily a *depth* phenomenon, the ablation sweeps width at fixed depth 2, and the depth-driven version of the objection **remains open**. One clause converts a miscitation into a correctly-scoped limitation, names a concrete follow-up, and pre-empts a reviewer who knows Rahaman.

**Consequence for the headline framing.** The paper is scrupulous that the sweep "stops at 1024 while the kernel limits are asymptotic, so this answers the objection at practical widths and makes no asymptotic claim." ### H. `melis2018sota` — one instance asked to certify a genre-wide law

**The claim, made twice and load-bearing for Contribution 2:** *"Audits in this genre **usually shrink** the effect they audit"* and *"...where audits normally shrink."*

Full-text search finds no sentence anywhere asserting a genre-wide directional pattern.

**Fix, nearly free.** Recast as an observation over named instances — Melis [33], Lucic [34], Musgrave [35] and Ferrari Dacrema [36] each independently show a *claimed advantage* shrinking or reversing, joined by a fifth directly-on-target reanalysis of a large deep-learning-versus-classical bioactivity comparison [38]. Two brackets belong with it. Recht et al. report slopes above one (1.69 on CIFAR-10, 1.11 on ImageNet), which the paper correctly pre-empts as a *relative* slope; Recht's own headline is an 8–11 point accuracy **drop**, squarely in the shrink family [37]. That does not touch the narrow scalar claim, which is scoped to ML reality-checks; it kills the *implication* that an upward correction is intrinsically surprising. The paper half-anticipates this, so the fix is a citation at that sentence, converting a hedge into a supported statement.

### I. `ghasemipour2022pessimistic` — an over-extended analogy where the accurate version is more useful

**The claim (Confound 4):** the cross-model-class incomparability principle is owned by the interval-validity literature *"and **its offline-RL instance**."*

**Their entire scope is one surrogate class, Q-ensembles**, and their axis of comparison is an *internal training-procedure* choice — shared versus independent Bellman targets, full versus weight-shared ensembles. They never compare across distinct model families, so they are not an instance of a *cross-model-class* principle [49]. Their actual finding is stronger and different in kind: shared pessimistic targets can flip the lower confidence bound's **sign**, rendering an ensemble paradoxically optimistic, rather than merely rescaling an interval.

**Fix.** Restate accurately: *a training-procedure choice within one surrogate class can invalidate, not merely miscalibrate, nominal pessimism.* **The accurate version is more useful**, because the same source supplies the only candidate positive mechanism this audit found for the inversion result — see deliverable (iii).

### J. `fannjiang2020autofocused` — the core diagnosis, articulated in 2020, uncited

### L. The conformal proposition is formally correct and practically inert — and the paper never says so

**This item was initially recorded as the audit's most serious finding, on a relayed report that the proposition omits a load-bearing hypothesis. Reading the supplement directly showed the report was overstated, and the earlier framing is withdrawn** — propagating an overstatement is precisely the failure this audit exists to catch.

`supplement.tex:48` reads: *"Under a shifted proposal Π ≠ P **with density ratio w = dΠ/dP**, validity is restored by weighting the calibration quantile by w."* It defines w as **the true density ratio**, not an estimate. So the statement is exactly Tibshirani et al.'s weighted-conformal theorem and is **formally correct as written. The proposition is not missing a hypothesis and it is not wrong.**

**What is true, and still worth a fix.** The clause is correct and **inert in the paper's own setting**. Tibshirani et al.'s guarantee holds for *known* w; their abstract separates the estimated case and supports it **empirically only** — roughly 91.0% coverage against 90% nominal on airfoil data — with no theorem bounding the coverage gap under an estimated weight [55]. Angelopoulos & Bates corroborate: weighted procedures are *"exact when the magnitude of the distribution shift is known"* [56]. Here Π is the distribution of designs an optimizer proposes after ascending a surrogate — the setting where w is least knowable and arguably not well-defined, since the proposals are the output of a deterministic optimization concentrated on a low-dimensional set. Nothing in the paper estimates w or applies weighted conformal.

**Fix (one or two sentences), plus a free win.** Note after Proposition 2 that the clause requires a known or reliably estimable w, that none is available for optimizer-generated proposals, and that it is stated for completeness rather than applied. **Then take the win:** the supplement already reports that conformal repair *"restores in-distribution coverage to its 0.90 target on every task but leaves OOD coverage erratic"* — 0.00 on five tasks. That is *exactly what one should expect* when the precondition is unavailable. Saying so converts an unexplained table into a correctly-scoped negative result, and reinforces the paper's separate argument that premise coverage is separable from optimization outcome [57]. **Severity: moderate — a scoping omission around a correct proposition, ranked below the Demšar fabrication and the Fan inversion.**

**η² is positively biased at *n* = 7 and the paper never says so.** Okada's Monte Carlo study, one million replications per condition, tabulates η² bias at **+0.054 at n=10 per group falling to +0.005 at n=100, always positive**, while ε² and ω² are always small and negative [61]; Liu states the bias firsthand and attributes the corrections to Kelley (1935) for ε² and Hays (1963) for ω² [62]. **Okada's simulation also overturns the folk belief that ω² is the least-biased correction — ε² is.** The paper's own bootstrap artifacts already contain the bias estimate, unreported: the bootstrap mean exceeds the point estimate in all four corners, by +0.0099 to +0.0184. Fix and strengthening: report ε² or bootstrap-bias-corrected values alongside η². **Bias-corrected, the headline gets stronger** — the two protocol confounds move the surrogate effect 0.351 → 0.395 (+0.0437) against the reported 0.367 → 0.405 (+0.0376). Direction survives; the magnitude of the change grows.

**The per-task normalizer is a fifth operating-point coordinate the paper never names.** The paper's central methodological claim is that any headline η² is a joint artifact of four coordinates — K, β, budget, engine corner — "so a headline that does not state all four is not reproducible." Every η² in the paper is computed on per-task min–max normalized cell means over nine cells, where the min and max are set by two of the nine arms *under comparison*. That endogenous form is a documented deviation from Design-Bench's own convention, which draws the range from a larger external dataset [89], and the critique is citable rather than hypothetical: Jordan et al. name this exact functional form as outlier-exploitable — *"algorithms could be near 0 or 1 if there is an outlier algorithm that does very well or poorly... We would like the evaluation procedure to be robust to the addition of poor algorithms"* [63] — and Bellemare et al., the *origin* of the technique, document it flipping a ranking [64]. Ranks are not a clean escape, since rank statistics are pool-dependent, which the paper already cites Benavoli for elsewhere [43].

**The empirical resolution favours the paper, and it corrects one of this audit's own framings.** A recomputation across five alternative normalizers over four corners, five β levels and both budget levels — 33 combinations plus leave-one-task-out, run on the paper's own raw result files — finds **every one of the 33 preserves η²_surr > η²_opt.** Not most; all. Magnitudes move, up to 3.4× on η²_opt and consistently halving the interaction under rank; direction does not. **That lands almost exactly where the paper's own framing sits** — "the direction is invariant... only the size of the gap moves" — so the fix extends an argument the paper already makes to one more axis, supportively. **And the framing that motivated the concern was wrong: Griewank-30D, the catastrophic-outlier task, is empirically the *smallest* lever on the headline, not the largest** — dropping it moves 0.405 to 0.395, while dropping Styblinski-5D moves it to 0.485. One further disclosure the check surfaced: min-max-normalized residuals reject normality and homoscedasticity while rank-transformed residuals satisfy both, so the point estimate sits on an assumption-violating scale; the nonparametric bootstrap is the right hedge, but the scale is not disclosed.

**But the causal reading is not established, and this audit's own test went against it.** The competing explanation is that perturbation attenuates the gap because it *underperforms*, compressing everything, rather than because it neutralizes surrogate differences. **The one task where perturbation is the grid's best optimizer — Styblinski-5D, GP+perturbation at 36.15 — is the task with the weakest attenuation (14.3%).** That is exactly what a floor effect predicts. A single contrary point does not settle it, but it is the only case that *discriminates*, and it points the wrong way for the causal reading. **The descriptive fact is solid; the causal reading is not. Fold it in as a question the paper poses and tests, never as a result it asserts.** The disentangling analysis is cheap and nameable: condition each optimizer's gap on how much that optimizer improves over the seed set, using `mean_x0_best` and `mean_ret_best`, which the inversion artifact already stores. Re-analysis, not a new run.

**If it holds it unifies six results the paper currently reports separately in five sections** — the interaction η², the cross-proposal coverage table, the x0-inversion, Elimination 4's finding that more budget *widens* the gap, the Design-Bench frozen cells where GP-plus-perturbation is the frozen pairing, and the Design-Bench optimizer inversion where perturbation leads. **And it explains the paper's biggest puzzle** — why the optimizer axis is negligible on synthetic tasks but inverts on Design-Bench — as one fact: where perturbation is competitive, the surrogate axis collapses and the optimizer axis surfaces. It is also the only genuinely practitioner-facing statement available: *if you must use a neural ensemble surrogate, do not pair it with gradient ascent.*

**RANK 3 — The budget axis belongs to a named line the paper does not know it is in. `FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP`. The clearest under-stated finding.**

The paper presents its budget result **defensively**, as an objection removed: "the obvious objection is that the optimizer axis is search intensity wearing a costume." Read against the literature it does not cite, it is not a defence — it is a contribution to an established line, and it carries **the strongest evidence in the paper**: the only disjoint bootstrap intervals anywhere in the decomposition ([0.189, 0.355] against [0.421, 0.719]) plus a ranking flip, with gradient leading at Q=4,352 and perturbation at Q=51,456.

COCO/BBOB §3.5 formally names **budget-dependent benchmarking** as a methodological category with a decade-old apparatus: *"The performance of some solvers depends on the total budget of function evaluations given as an additional parameter to the solver... a single performance assessment of such solvers cannot be expected to faithfully predict their performance for budgets that are different"* — with fixed-budget and fixed-target views and anytime assessment prescribed [65]. COCO is uncited. A sibling-field confirmation exists in metaheuristics, where budget-matched comparison is "standard practice" and raising the budget "may significantly affect the final verdict" [66]. And a direct precedent sits **already in the paper's bibliography, cited for the genre but not for this**: Lucic et al., verbatim, *"bad models can outperform good models given enough computational budget"* [34]. **That is the paper's own finding, stated at NeurIPS 2018.** Note also that the Design-Bench arm makes the same shape of finding on an independent axis — budget matching raises η²_opt by 1.46–1.88× in every corner rather than dissolving it, and moves one corner from failing to reject to rejecting.

**RANK 4 — Report the bias correction, and name ε² rather than ω². `FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP`.** Covered in (i.M). One sentence and one supplementary column buys immunity from the single most obvious statistical objection to reporting variance-explained at seven tasks, and the headline gets stronger rather than weaker [61][62].

**RANK 5 — Report the normalizer robustness check and name the fifth coordinate. `FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP`.** Also covered in (i.M). Two things make it more than defensive: the repository already knew — `docs/FLAW_LEDGER.md` P1-2 flags exactly this and `docs/scripts_eta_robustness.py` is a prior abandoned attempt that reproduces the paper's η² to eight decimal places but was only ever run on the uncorrected published grid — and the machinery exists twice over, since the per-task z-score cross-check is already used for Elimination 1. Cite Jordan [63] and Bellemare [64].

**RANK 6 — The landscape negative, and the one positive structural statement available. `FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP`.**

Tested directly against `tab:sfull` under all three optimizers: **no standard landscape covariate predicts the GP-versus-ensemble gap.** Spearman correlation with dimension is 0.107 (*p* = 0.82) under gradient, −0.109 under perturbation, 0.071 under CMA — indistinguishable from zero under every optimizer. Textbook multimodality **actively mis-sorts**: within the four-function "many local minima" bucket the gap spans 2.09 to 2591.06, three orders of magnitude inside one category, while the only strictly unimodal task has the smallest gap of all. Separability fares no better. **This is a decisive negative from looking, not an absence of looking**, and two or three sentences close a hostile reviewer's "isn't this just landscape?" objection with a falsifiable, already-computed answer instead of the current one-line hedge.

The literature gap is real: the mature exploratory landscape analysis (ELA — characterizing a problem's landscape via computable features such as multimodality and separability) tradition has never been pointed at surrogate-class choice, operating at benchmark-suite scale to select *solvers* [67][68][69], and the nearest structural cousin compares true against surrogate landscape features but switches surrogates online, per-iteration, inside a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm [70]. Malan's survey flags surrogate-landscape analysis as unresolved and reports the finding that makes it hard: features computed *through* a surrogate are "more indicative of the surrogate model than the original landscape" [71] — a circularity that bites when the object being selected is itself the instrument of measurement.

**Now the structural statement, which exists only in cross-section and which no single analysis produced.** The per-task gap ranking is **stable across optimizers at Spearman 0.84–0.96** while the per-task gap *magnitude* collapses to ≈0.01 under perturbation against 0.735–0.762 under gradient and CMA. These look contradictory and are not: one measures relative ordering across tasks, the other absolute magnitude within an optimizer. **That combination rules out both a purely optimizer-side account, which would not preserve task ordering, and a purely task-side account, which would not collapse. Any surviving mechanism must be multiplicative — a task factor scaled by an optimizer-aggressiveness factor.** For a paper whose mechanism section is seven negatives and no positive causal test, that is worth more than an eighth negative. Weight it honestly: the 0.84–0.96 stability is descriptive at *n* = 7 while the collapse is bootstrapped, so the collapse is the better-evidenced half. One documented curiosity is worth a clause: Griewank is known to get *simpler* to optimize with increasing dimension despite more local minima, so attributing the ensemble's collapse there to ruggedness is probably backwards.

**RANK 7 — Report the equivalence bound already computed. `FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP`.** The released `stats.py` already computed a paired two-one-sided-tests (TOST) equivalence bound — the standard procedure for testing whether a difference is small enough to be practically negligible: gap **0.3762**, 90% CI [−0.1078, 0.8602], effect bound **0.4840**, **not equivalent at either a 0.5 or a 0.3 margin.** The supplement's entire treatment is one clause saying such tests "are computed." The paper is scrupulous about never claiming equivalence and correctly says a failure to reject is not a demonstration of absence [42] — **and it has the actual test in hand, and it is informative.** Report the bound and verdict, cite Lakens [82], who is absent from the bibliography, and carry his caution: rejecting small effects in an equivalence test requires large samples. A confirmed negative closes the thread: there is no ML-benchmark-specific equivalence-testing literature.

**RANK 8 — Give the inversion result its name, and advertise the replication the paper obscures. `FOLD-INTO-THIS-PAPER / CHEAP`.** The paper states its inversion flatly: "a demonstration within this grid, descriptive at *n* = 7 tasks, not a mechanism." **It is an instance of a named, decade-old failure category.** Offline RL formalizes it as **safe policy improvement** — P(ρ(π,M) ≥ ρ(π_b,M) − ζ) ≥ 1 − δ, the requirement that a policy learned offline not be worse than the baseline it was given, with high probability [75]; SPIBB proves a ζ-approximate guarantee and measures safety via 1%-CVaR across roughly 100,000 randomly generated MDPs, precisely because algorithms lacking such a guarantee return sub-baseline policies in a nontrivial fraction of runs [74]. CQL's gap-expanding theorem supplies a second reusable frame: the learned value function *widens*, never narrows or inverts, the in-distribution-versus-OOD gap relative to the truth [76]. **The reframing: the inversion is not an awkward number, it is the observation that offline MBO optimizers ship with no safe-improvement guarantee, and the absence bites.** Two sentences, two citations.

Separately and freely: 0.406 and 0.405 are not one measurement at two precisions. They are **two independent runs at the same operating point** — the β-sweep endpoint at β=2 (10 seeds) gives 0.40636; the headline corner (30 seeds) gives 0.40455 — landing 0.0018 apart. **A paper whose central scalar is challenged as an operating-point artifact should be advertising an independent reproduction of it**, not letting a reader assume rounding.

### C. Forward implications

The finding with the longest half-life is neither the headline η² nor the seven eliminations. It is the **conditionality** — that the surrogate-class advantage is largely a property of the surrogate-optimizer *pair* rather than of the surrogate. If that holds beyond this grid, it dissolves the framing question the subfield has been arguing about. The dispute between the surrogate-side lineage and the optimizer-side lineage [52] is not a dispute about which factor matters; it is a dispute about which conditional slice each camp is standing in. A surrogate paper evaluated under gradient ascent and an optimizer paper evaluated on one fixed surrogate are estimating different quantities, and neither is estimating the thing the field reports.

That has immediate consequences for how the next generation is evaluated. The 2026 frontier is generative — diffusion language models [6][15], support-proximity diffusion estimation [14], flow-based domain models [16], meta-learned surrogates [18], ranking-theoretic surrogates [17], conformal per-candidate certification [19]. Almost all bundle the generative model and the search procedure into a single reported number, exactly as the lineage this paper dissects does, and several bundle them *inseparably*: a diffusion policy trained end-to-end through data-augmentation, supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement-learning stages has no clean optimizer factor to vary at all. **If a crossed factorial is going to remain runnable in this subfield, it has to be run now, on the last generation of methods where the two axes are still separable.** That is a timeliness argument the paper does not make and could make in one sentence.

The successors are visible from here. A decomposition at benchmark-suite scale with landscape features would convert the stable per-task ranking into a selection rule [67][71]. A safe-improvement guarantee ported from offline RL would convert the inversion count into a design constraint [74][76]. A far-field geometry test would convert seven eliminations into one mechanism [72]. And the natural sibling to the existing stability benchmark for offline optimizers [20] — a benchmark for *attribution* rather than performance, with the surrogate and optimizer axes shipped separable by construction — does not exist, and this paper's grid is the closest thing to a prototype. Each is a paper. The submission under audit is the instrument that makes them askable, and the strongest argument for it is not that its numbers are large but that they are reproducible to the seed — exactly the property the confounded status quo lacks.
