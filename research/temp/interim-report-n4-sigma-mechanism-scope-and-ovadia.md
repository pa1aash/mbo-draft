# Interim report: n4-sigma-mechanism-scope-and-ovadia

**Locus question:** Is "the GP wins because its posterior variance grows away from data, making LCB an implicit trust region" owned by prior work — and does the paper's own beta=0 control (gap barely moves, 0.51→0.47) undercut a sigma-mediated reading?
**Flavor:** dialectical

## What the new sources say

**Ovadia et al., "Can You Trust Your Model's Uncertainty? Evaluating Predictive Uncertainty Under Dataset Shift"** (NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1906.02530) — fetched fresh via the fetcher subagent (curl+pdftotext, title-verified, 10,450-word extraction), registered as [[can-you-trust-your-models-uncertainty-evaluating-predictive-uncertainty-under-da]]. This is a large-scale UQ-benchmarking paper (7 methods × MNIST/CIFAR-10/ImageNet/Criteo/20-Newsgroups/genomics, all under corruption-intensity or categorical-OOD shift). Its single occurrence of "confidently wrong" is on MNIST-OOD and is attributed to "most methods" in aggregate — *"they are confidently wrong about completely OOD data"* — with SVI, not ensembles, explicitly singled out as the exception (*"SVI gives the highest accuracy at high confidence (or conversely is much less frequently confidently wrong)"*). On the CIFAR-10/ImageNet OOD experiment the paper reports the **opposite** ensemble behavior: *"Ensembles consistently have high accuracy but also high entropy on OOD data."* Grep confirms zero hits for "distance," "far from," "far away" anywhere in the body — the paper's entire notion of departure from training data is corruption/covariate-shift intensity (1–5 severity scale) or categorically different datasets, never continuous geometric distance in input space. GPs appear only as a weak, appendix-tier last-layer-VGP comparison, dropped from the main experiments as "challenging to train... on the 37M example Criteo dataset."

**Audited paper's own GP/SVGP implementation** (`code/mbo.py`, read directly from the repo, not fetched) — this is not a "new source" in the fetch sense but was necessary to adjudicate the DUE scope catch. `fit_exact_gp` (lines 273–291) builds `ConstantKernel(1.0) * Matern(length_scale=..., nu=2.5) + WhiteKernel(...)` via `sklearn.gaussian_process.GaussianProcessRegressor`, applied directly to the raw (unit-hypercube-clipped) input `x`. `fit_svgp` (lines 358–390) builds `gpytorch.kernels.ScaleKernel(gpytorch.kernels.MaternKernel(nu=2.5, ard_num_dims=dim))` inside a `VariationalStrategy` with inducing points drawn directly from `xt` — again, no learned neural feature extractor anywhere upstream of the kernel. Both surrogates are the "classic" GP case SNGP's Definition 1 formalizes, not the deep-kernel composition DUE diagnoses.

**Independent quick reproduction of the β=0 control** (`results/results_camera.json['mbo_beta0']` vs `['mbo']`, using the paper's own `code/analysis.py` helpers `task_norm`/`sei_oei`) — the query's claim that the β=0 control "has no generator in the codebase" is now stale: `code/run_beta0.py` exists and the results file has all 7 synthetic tasks populated for the `mbo_beta0` grid. Running the paper's own per-task min-max-normalization and marginalizing the `botorchgp` vs `ens` gap over the 3 optimizers × 7 synthetic tasks gives: **gap(GP−ens) = 0.504 at β=2 vs 0.511 at β=0** — flat to very slightly *up*, not down. This doesn't exactly reproduce the query's cited "0.51→0.47" (a ~0.04 shrink) — I flag that discrepancy explicitly rather than paper over it — but it agrees on the qualitative and decision-relevant point: **the gap does not collapse toward zero when the pessimism/variance term is removed.**

## Evidence synthesis

Two distinct claims are bundled under "N4" and the evidence splits cleanly along that seam once separated.

**Claim (a): "A GP's posterior variance grows away from training data; standard deep nets/ensembles lack this property."** This is real, established, textbook science, and it is squarely owned. SNGP's Definition 1 formalizes "input distance awareness" and Section 2.2 proves *"u(x*) increases monotonically toward 1 as x* moves further away from X_IND"* for a GP-RBF posterior, while showing deep ensembles/MC-Dropout are governed by distance-to-decision-boundary, not distance-to-data. DUQ independently confirms the ensemble failure mode empirically on two-moons — a genuinely spatial, low-dimensional toy dataset, not a corruption benchmark: *"DUQ is certain only on the data distribution, and uncertain away from it: the ideal result. Deep Ensembles is uncertain only along the decision boundary, and certain elsewhere."* Nobody in this four-paper set disputes this raw mechanism claim; DUE and TuRBO don't touch it at all.

**Claim (b): "That variance growth, mediated through an LCB acquisition function, is what explains the GP's advantage over ensembles in offline MBO by creating an implicit trust region."** This narrower, optimization-specific synthesis is NONE FOUND anywhere in the four-paper set — exhaustive grep across all four extracted PDFs (SNGP ~14,500 words, DUQ ~8,700, DUE ~9,575, TuRBO ~9,290) returns zero hits for "trust region" in SNGP/DUQ/DUE and zero hits for "LCB"/"lower confidence bound" in all four including TuRBO, whose entire paper is *about* trust-region BO. Queries run: `"trust region" AND "LCB"`, `"implicit trust region"`, `"posterior variance" AND "acquisition function"`, `"distance aware" AND "Bayesian optimization"` — all return nothing beyond what's already summarized. TuRBO is the closest adjacent paper and it is the strongest single piece of evidence *against* the mechanism being automatic: TuRBO's authors diagnose unchecked global GP-variance growth under a myopic acquisition function as *causing* the problem TuRBO exists to fix (*"this results in an overemphasized exploration and a failure to exploit promising areas"*), and they build an **explicit**, hand-designed hyperrectangle with a success/failure counter — not an implicit, LCB-derived region — precisely because letting variance drive local behavior on its own does not work. TuRBO uses Thompson Sampling, not LCB, and never claims the acquisition function alone produces a trust region.

### Position A: the mechanism is real and directly relevant — the σ-story survives once correctly scoped

SNGP formally proves and DUQ empirically confirms that a properly-specified GP (RBF/Matern kernel, no pathological feature extractor) has monotonic distance-aware variance growth, and that standard ensembles do not — DUQ's own two-moons result is genuinely spatial evidence, independent of Ovadia, that ensembles are confidently wrong in regions far from the data. Crucially, the corpus's use of DUE's counterexample against this paper is a **scope error I can now correct precisely**: DUE's "a GP does not automatically get variance growth" finding is about **Deep Kernel Learning** specifically — a *learned neural feature extractor* feeding a GP, where the failure mode ("feature collapse") is that the extractor maps OOD inputs into the *same region of feature space* as training data, fooling a GP whose distance-awareness only operates in feature space. DUE's own failure case, GPDNN, is explicitly "a simple Feed-Forward Network as feature extractor" upstream of the kernel. The audited paper's exact GP and SVGP, verified directly against `code/mbo.py`, use vanilla `Matern`/`MaternKernel` applied **directly to the raw (unit-hypercube) input** — no feature extractor, no deep-kernel composition, nothing for "feature collapse" to happen to. **DUE's caveat structurally does not apply to this paper's surrogates.** So the corpus's Side B is currently citing DUE as if it undercuts the mechanism for this paper specifically, when in fact DUE's own theory implies the opposite: an unconstrained-feature-extractor GP is the *fragile* case, and this paper's kernel-on-raw-inputs GP is the *safe* case SNGP already proves has the property. On this narrow scoping question, Position A is right and the corpus needs correcting.

### Position B: the causal, optimization-level story is not owned, and the paper's own control contradicts it

Even granting that this paper's specific GP/SVGP genuinely have distance-aware variance growth (Position A, corrected), that says nothing about *why the GP wins the optimization comparison*. Nobody — not SNGP, not DUQ, not DUE — connects variance growth to an acquisition function or an optimization loop at all; the "LCB creates an implicit trust region" synthesis is unowned in all four papers (NONE FOUND, confirmed by exhaustive grep). TuRBO, the one paper in the set that is actually about trust-region BO, is direct evidence that naive variance growth under a myopic acquisition rule is a *problem to counter*, not a mechanism that spontaneously produces useful local behavior — TuRBO needed an explicit geometric fix. And the paper's own β=0 ablation is a direct empirical test of the causal claim on the audited paper's actual surrogates: strip the σ term out of the acquisition entirely (β=0, pure posterior-mean maximization) and the GP-vs-ensemble gap should collapse toward zero if the advantage is sigma-mediated. It does not. The query's cited number (0.51→0.47) and my independent quick reproduction from `results/results_camera.json['mbo_beta0']` via the paper's own `analysis.py::sei_oei`/`task_norm` (0.504→0.511, i.e., flat-to-up) disagree on the exact magnitude — a discrepancy worth reconciling with whichever locus owns the official recomputation — but agree on the decision-relevant fact: **the gap survives complete removal of the variance/pessimism term.** That is the opposite of what a sigma-mediated implicit-trust-region story predicts, and it is a much more direct, on-point piece of evidence than any of the four literature sources, because it is measured on this paper's own surrogates rather than inferred by analogy from classification/OOD papers.

## Committed position

The N4 hypothesis-at-risk should be split, not accepted or rejected whole. The raw statistical mechanism — "a properly-specified GP's posterior variance grows monotonically away from training data while standard deep nets/ensembles lack this property, being governed by decision-boundary distance instead" — is PRIOR WORK FOUND and cleanly owned by SNGP (Def. 1 + Section 2.2, formal proof) with DUQ providing an independent empirical instance (two-moons). The DUE-based scope catch HOLDS in the paper's favor, not against it: DUE's counterexample is specifically about deep-kernel GPs with unconstrained neural feature extractors, and the audited paper's exact GP and SVGP (verified against `code/mbo.py`) use standard Matern kernels directly on raw inputs with no feature extractor at all — so DUE's caveat is currently over-cited in the corpus's contradiction graph as evidence against the mechanism for this specific paper, and that citation should be downgraded or dropped from Side B. But the actual claim the paper needs — "LCB + growing GP variance = implicit trust region, and that is why the GP wins in offline MBO" — is NONE FOUND across all four sources (exhaustive grep, zero hits for "trust region" ∧ LCB co-occurrence anywhere), meaning if the paper asserted it as fact it would be a genuinely novel synthesis claim, not a repackaging of prior work. The problem is that the paper's own β=0 control is direct, on-point evidence that this causal claim is likely false: the GP-ensemble gap persists (barely moves, and if anything grows slightly) when the variance/pessimism term is removed entirely, which is exactly what TuRBO's literature-level diagnosis (unconstrained variance growth under a myopic acquisition rule is a problem, not a free trust region) would predict. The paper's most defensible framing is therefore: cite SNGP/DUQ for real, established distance-awareness as *background/motivation* (not as an explanation of their own results), and report as their own finding — consistent with the β=0 control — that the GP's advantage in offline MBO survives removal of σ entirely, which argues for a posterior-mean-smoothness / inductive-bias explanation (dovetailing with the N5 NTK/spectral-bias thread that a sibling locus is investigating) rather than a sigma-mediated implicit trust region. Asserting the implicit-trust-region-via-LCB mechanism as the paper's explanation would be an overclaim not supported by their own ablation.

- **Position:** N4's raw mechanism claim (GP variance grows away from data; standard ensembles/nets don't have this property) is prior-owned by SNGP/DUQ and correctly scoped to this paper's vanilla-kernel GPs (DUE's caveat doesn't apply here); but the causal/optimization claim (LCB + variance growth = implicit trust region explaining the GP's offline-MBO advantage) is both unowned in the literature AND contradicted by the paper's own β=0 control, so the paper should not lean on a sigma-mediated story as its explanatory mechanism.
- **Confidence:** high on the literature-ownership/scope questions (direct exhaustive grep across all four full-text PDFs, plus a code-level verification of the audited paper's own kernel implementation — nothing here depends on inference); medium on the causal conclusion itself, because it leans on a β=0 control whose exact numbers I could not fully reconcile (query says 0.51→0.47; my independent quick reproduction says 0.504→0.511) — both point the same direction (gap persists) but the magnitude discrepancy is unresolved and I am not the locus that owns that recomputation.
- **Boundary conditions:** the "DUE doesn't apply" scope catch holds specifically because this paper's exact GP and SVGP use standard Matern kernels applied directly to raw/unit-hypercube inputs with no learned feature extractor (verified in `code/mbo.py` lines 273–291 and 358–390); if a future revision of the paper added any learned embedding, PCA, or neural preprocessing before the kernel, DUE's caveat would immediately become applicable again and the scope catch would no longer protect the mechanism claim. The "NONE FOUND" verdict on the LCB+variance=implicit-TR synthesis is bounded to these four papers plus the corpus's existing coverage; it does not rule out an obscure BO-theory paper elsewhere making this connection (see Open Questions).
- **What would change this position:** (1) if the official, audited recomputation of the β=0 control (owned by another locus/the repro-gate work referenced in recent commits) lands closer to the query's original "0.51→0.47" — a real ~8% relative shrink — rather than my flat/slightly-up reproduction, that would soften but not reverse the conclusion, since even a modest shrink leaves most of the gap unexplained by σ; (2) if a targeted search turns up a BO-theory paper (not in this four-paper set) that explicitly proves LCB-driven exploitation-under-growing-variance behaves like an implicit trust region in a non-adversarial acquisition regime, the NONE-FOUND verdict on claim (b) would need to flip to FOUND; (3) if the audited paper's GP were shown to use *any* learned preprocessing I missed in `code/mbo.py`, the DUE scope-catch would need to be walked back.
- **Evidence weight:** 2 primary sources (SNGP formal proof, DUQ empirical two-moons) support claim (a); 1 code-level verification (this paper's own `mbo.py`) supports the DUE scope-catch; 1 primary source (TuRBO) + 1 empirical control (β=0, independently reproduced with caveats) support claim (b)'s rejection; 1 new primary source (Ovadia) is ambiguous/mixed and does not cleanly support either side — it actively complicates the "ensembles confidently wrong far from data" framing rather than confirming it.
