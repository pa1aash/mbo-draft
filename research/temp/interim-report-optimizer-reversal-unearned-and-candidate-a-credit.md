# Interim report: optimizer-reversal-unearned-and-candidate-a-credit

**Locus question:** Is the "field innovates on the axis that doesn't matter" reversal (eta^2_opt=0.01 vs PGS's premise that search strategy is the neglected axis) a defensible literature claim, and does L/R/W's "optimizer as fixed nuisance" mean candidate A is UNDER-credited for the not-an-optimizer-effect half?
**Flavor:** dialectical

## What the new sources say

I re-read the three assigned corpus notes in full ([[offline-model-based-optimization-via-policy-guided-gradient-search-aaai-2024-arx]], [[a-study-of-bayesian-neural-network-surrogates-for-bayesian-optimization-iclr-202]], [[design-bench-benchmarks-for-data-driven-offline-model-based-optimization-icml-20]]) plus two already-fetched but not-yet-locus-read corpus notes ([[offline-model-based-optimization-comprehensive-review-kim-et-al-tmlr-2026-arxiv2]], [[offline-model-based-optimization-by-learning-to-rank-tan-et-al-iclr-2025-arxiv24]]), and fetched one new primary source outside the assigned three because it is the single most load-bearing missing piece for this specific locus: the canonical Bayesian-optimization survey the "reversal" implicitly claims to overturn.

**[[offline-model-based-optimization-via-policy-guided-gradient-search-aaai-2024-arx]] (PGS, Chemingui et al., AAAI 2024).** The exact premise sentence, verbatim, from the paper's "Summary and Future Work" section: *"This perspective is aimed at improving the search strategy in offline BBO, which complements prior methods that have focused on improving surrogate models while using fixed search strategies."* This is a specific, named, falsifiable-in-spirit belief: it asserts that the offline-BBO literature has concentrated effort on the surrogate axis and comparatively neglected the search-strategy axis, and PGS positions its own contribution (a learned, offline-RL-based gradient search policy) as filling that neglected gap. Confirmed by grep: PGS itself holds the surrogate fixed at a single vanilla MLP throughout (0 hits for "Gaussian process," "ensemble," "ANOVA," "factorial") and only varies the RL/search-policy component in its own ablations (CQL vs. IQL vs. standard RL) — so PGS's own paper is a pure single-axis (optimizer-only) demonstration, never crossed against a competing surrogate class.

**[[a-study-of-bayesian-neural-network-surrogates-for-bayesian-optimization-iclr-202]] (Li/Rudner/Wilson, ICLR 2024).** Confirmed verbatim, twice: *"We use Monte-Carlo based Expected Improvement (Balandat et al., 2020) as our acquisition function for all problems."* (Section 4.1) and restated in Appendix C.1. Acquisition function (their optimizer-equivalent) is a fixed nuisance variable across every one of the ~7 surrogate classes they compare — never manipulated, never ablated across families (their only acquisition-adjacent sweep is a batch-size sweep of the same fixed EI, not EI-vs-UCB-vs-Thompson). Zero hits for "factorial," "crossed," "optimizer," and zero hits for "offline" — this is an online-BO surrogate-class comparison, not offline MBO.

**[[design-bench-benchmarks-for-data-driven-offline-model-based-optimization-icml-20]] (Trabucco et al., ICML 2022).** Confirmed: each of the 8 baselines bundles one fixed surrogate with one fixed optimizer as an inseparable package (BO-qEI is the only GP-surrogate baseline and is never re-paired with CMA-ES/REINFORCE; the ensemble-based baselines are never re-paired with a GP). Zero hits for "surrogate" as a term at all — Design-Bench's vocabulary is "learned objective function," reinforcing that surrogate identity was never conceived of as an independently-manipulable factor in this design.

**[[offline-model-based-optimization-comprehensive-review-kim-et-al-tmlr-2026-arxiv2]] (Kim et al., TMLR 2026 — the field's most recent, peer-reviewed survey, Bengio co-author).** This is the single strongest piece of new evidence for this locus. Section 6, "Discussion and Future Direction": *"existing benchmarks often emphasize overall optimization performance without clarifying whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance."* This is not silence (absence of a counterexample) — it is the field's own most current, certified, canonical self-assessment explicitly naming the surrogate-vs-optimizer attribution question as UNRESOLVED, in January 2026, one AAAI cycle before the audited paper. It also independently corroborates the paper's own Design-Bench Friedman-null finding: *"some benchmarks — such as TFB8 and TFB10 — offer overly constrained search spaces where even simple gradient ascent methods can achieve impressive results, making it difficult to distinguish the performance of more sophisticated algorithms."*

**[[offline-model-based-optimization-by-learning-to-rank-tan-et-al-iclr-2025-arxiv24]] (Tan et al., ICLR 2025).** A third data point confirming the corpus-wide pattern: the closest thing to an optimizer sweep in the offline-MBO literature (BO-qEI / CMA-ES / REINFORCE / Gradient Ascent + 2 ensemble variants) never includes a GP surrogate at all, is embedded in an uncontrolled 22-method leaderboard, and computes no ANOVA or variance attribution. Every offline-MBO paper in the corpus that varies one axis leaves the other axis either fixed-singular (PGS: surrogate fixed) or absent-from-the-comparison-set (Tan et al.: no GP).

**New fetch — Shahriari, Swersky, Wang, Adams, de Freitas, "Taking the Human Out of the Loop: A Review of Bayesian Optimization" (Proc. IEEE, 2016, ~5,948 citations; the most-cited BO survey in the field).** Full text obtained via manual PDF extraction after the `hyperresearch fetch` CLI false-flagged all three mirrors as junk content (documented transparently in [[taking-the-human-out-of-the-loop-a-review-of-bayesian-optimization-shahriari-et]]). Two direct, unhedged, thesis-level quotes: *"We will see that the careful choice of statistical model is often far more important than the choice of acquisition function heuristic"* (Section I.A, paper overview) and, as the paper's closing substantive sentence, *"There has been a great deal of work that has focussed heavily on designing acquisition functions, however we have taken the perspective that the importance of this plays a secondary role to the choice of the underlying surrogate model"* (Section IX, Concluding Remarks). No passage anywhere in the 24-page survey asserts the reverse.

## Evidence synthesis

The corpus is unanimous and well-triangulated on the narrow ownership question: no offline-MBO paper — PGS, Design-Bench, Tan et al., or the Kim et al. survey's synthesis of the whole literature — has ever run both surrogate class and optimizer/search-strategy class as simultaneously manipulated, crossed factors with a variance decomposition. Every existing design either fixes one axis (PGS: surrogate fixed; L/R/W: acquisition fixed, but that paper is online BO, not offline MBO) or bundles both axes together as inseparable, pre-existing method packages (Design-Bench, and most of Tan et al.'s comparison table). The Kim et al. TMLR 2026 survey turns this from an absence-of-evidence into a stated, field-acknowledged gap.

But the locus's real tension is not ownership of the *factorial method* — it is whether the *directional finding* ("surrogate matters, optimizer barely does") is itself new. Here the picture is genuinely split, and this is where the dialectic bites.

### Position A: the reversal is a defensible, novel, offline-MBO-specific literature claim

PGS's premise is a specific, offline-BBO-scoped belief, not a vague gesture: it says the offline BBO literature specifically has "focused on improving surrogate models while using fixed search strategies," and frames PGS's own contribution as complementing that gap by improving search strategy. This is falsifiable: if search strategy were in fact a comparably-leveraged axis, a controlled sweep should show non-trivial eta^2_optimizer. The audited paper's design directly tests exactly this belief, in exactly PGS's own setting (offline MBO, not online BO), with a genuine crossed factorial (3 surrogate classes x 3 optimizers, both manipulated) that none of PGS, Design-Bench, or Tan et al. ever attempted. The Kim et al. 2026 survey's explicit "without clarifying whether observed gains stem from superior surrogate modeling, improved optimization strategies, or mere chance" is the field itself certifying, one cycle before this paper, that the question was open. On this reading, eta^2_opt=0.01 is not a rediscovery of old news but the first quantitative, ANOVA-validated answer to a question the offline-MBO sub-literature had left as an implicit, untested assumption embedded in its own motivating rhetoric (PGS's intro sentence being the clearest instance of that rhetoric on record).

### Position B: the "reversal" is overclaimed — it confirms decades-old BO folklore, and PGS's premise may itself be a rhetorical overclaim

Shahriari et al. (2016) states, as the *organizing thesis of the single most-cited Bayesian optimization survey ever written*, exactly the claim the audited paper frames as a reversal: surrogate/model choice matters more than acquisition-function/search-strategy choice. This was canonical BO wisdom eight years before PGS was written and ten years before the audited paper. On this reading, "eta^2_opt=0.01 contradicts PGS" is a rhetorically convenient but slightly misleading framing: it does not contradict the *field's* prior belief (the field's dominant belief, as expressed by its most-cited survey, is that surrogate matters more) — it contradicts *PGS's own* motivating sentence, which itself reads less like a tested empirical premise and more like a standard "prior work has focused on X, we do Y" motivating move common in introductions, a rhetorical setup to justify a new paper rather than a claim PGS staked serious weight on. If PGS's sentence is read charitably as "search strategy is *comparatively under-explored as a research topic*" (a sociological/bibliometric claim about what gets published) rather than "search strategy *carries comparable variance* to surrogate choice" (an empirical claim about where the performance signal lives), then eta^2_opt=0.01 does not really refute PGS at all — PGS never claimed the axis carried large variance, only that it had received less research attention. Refuting an attention-allocation claim with a variance-decomposition result is close to a category mismatch.

## Committed position

The evidence supports a synthesis that credits Candidate A more than a harsh reading would, but requires the paper to sharply narrow its own novelty claim rather than lean on the dramatic "reversal" framing. On ownership: since PGS fixes surrogate, L/R/W fixes acquisition (and is online-BO, not offline-MBO, to begin with), Design-Bench and Tan et al. bundle both axes as inseparable method packages, and the field's own January-2026 TMLR survey names the surrogate-vs-optimizer attribution question as explicitly unresolved — no existing paper can claim the compound sentence "the GP advantage is a surrogate-class effect, not an optimizer effect, measured under a controlled factorial." A fixed-nuisance design is categorically incapable of producing a quantitative claim about the fixed variable's effect size (zero, small, or large) — L/R/W's holding acquisition constant is silence, not evidence of eta^2_opt≈0, so a harsh "L/R/W already implicitly showed this" objection is a logical error and Candidate A is, on this narrow point, under-credited by that reading. But the "reversal" rhetoric overclaims: Shahriari et al. (2016) already holds, as founding BO doctrine, that surrogate choice dominates acquisition/search-strategy choice, so the paper is not overturning a field-wide belief — it is delivering the first offline-MBO-specific, factorially-controlled, eta^2-quantified confirmation of an old online-BO doctrine, applied to falsify one paper's (PGS's) specific, locally-scoped motivating premise. That is still a real, citable, defensible contribution — but "we refute the field's operating assumption" is not available to Candidate A; "we are the first to test, in the specific offline-MBO setting PGS names, whether search-strategy improvement carries the variance PGS's motivating sentence implies it might" is available, is novel, and is not weakened by Shahriari et al. And this entire literature-side verdict is separate from, and must not be conflated with, the internal-validity caveat already on the record in the paper's own flaw ledger: eta^2_opt=0.01 is itself confounded by unequal candidate/oracle budgets (two optimizers propose 256 designs and report the oracle-selected best 128, the third proposes 128 with no such selection headroom) and by a trust-region constraint on gradient ascent that closes the ensemble's collapse on high-dimensional tasks but not low-dimensional ones — meaning part of why the optimizer axis shows near-zero variance may be that the researchers' own tuning choices suppressed the one failure mode (gradient collapse) that would have made optimizer choice matter, which is a confound, not a null result. The literature verdict (NONE FOUND, novel gap, PGS-specific refutation available) and the internal-validity verdict (the 0.01 number is not yet trustworthy as reported) are both true simultaneously and answer different questions — the paper can say "no one has made this attribution before" without yet being entitled to say "and our number for it is 0.01."

- **Position:** Candidate A's compound claim ("surrogate effect, not an optimizer effect") is unownable by any single prior paper in the corpus and is therefore a genuine literature novelty — but the paper must narrow its rhetorical framing from "refutes the field's premise that search strategy matters" (false; Shahriari et al. 2016 already holds the opposite) to "is the first offline-MBO-specific, factorially-controlled falsification of PGS's own motivating premise that offline BBO specifically had neglected a lever with real headroom" (true and defensible), and must keep this literature claim analytically separate from the still-unresolved internal-validity status of the eta^2_opt=0.01 number itself.
- **Confidence:** medium-high on the ownership/under-crediting finding (high: corroborated independently across two width-sweep passes plus my own re-read, for 5 candidate papers, with verbatim zero-hit grep evidence and an explicit field-survey acknowledgment of the gap); medium on the "PGS premise vs. Shahriari doctrine" reframing recommendation (this is an interpretive judgment about how charitably to read one motivating sentence in one paper's introduction, not a fact with a single verifiable answer).
- **Boundary conditions:** This holds for the *offline* MBO setting specifically (static dataset, no further oracle queries) and for the *synthetic-function* half of the audited paper's own experiment (eta^2_opt=0.01 is reported "on synthetic," per the query brief; the Design-Bench omnibus is a separate null result, Friedman p=0.69, that does not itself decompose variance by axis). It does not extend to online/sequential BO, where Shahriari et al.'s doctrine already governs and no reversal claim is available at all.
- **What would change this position:** (1) discovery of an offline-MBO paper (not found in two independent sweeps here) that already runs a genuine crossed surrogate×optimizer factorial with variance attribution — this would flip the ownership verdict from NONE FOUND to PRIOR WORK FOUND and eliminate Candidate A's core claim entirely; (2) resolution of the internal-validity caveat in a direction that meaningfully moves eta^2_opt away from ~0 once oracle budgets are equalized and the trust-region confound is isolated — if the corrected number is not small, the "reversal" and the "confirmation of Shahriari doctrine" framings both collapse simultaneously, since there would be no optimizer-doesn't-matter finding left to attribute either way; (3) evidence that PGS's own community (its 5 forward-citing papers) has already treated the "search strategy is neglected" premise as tested/settled rather than as motivating rhetoric — I checked OpenAlex forward citations of PGS (5 total, all low-citation 2025-2026 works) and found no paper that engages the premise directly, which supports treating it as untested rhetoric, but a closer read of those 5 papers (not done here, out of budget) could change this.
- **Evidence weight:** 5 offline-MBO primary sources (PGS, Design-Bench, Tan et al., L/R/W, Kim et al. survey) unanimously support NONE FOUND for the crossed-factorial ownership question, corroborated by an independent width-sweep pass; 1 canonical online-BO survey (Shahriari et al., 2016, ~5,948 citations) directly complicates the "reversal" framing by showing the directional claim is old doctrine outside the offline setting; 0 sources found that make the specific offline-MBO surrogate-vs-optimizer attribution; the internal-validity caveat (unequal oracle budgets, trust-region confound) is drawn from the audited paper's own flaw ledger (query brief, not an external citation) and is not independently adjudicated by this locus.

## Open questions

- Does the corrected eta^2_opt (after equalizing oracle/candidate budgets and isolating the trust-region effect) move meaningfully away from 0.01? This is decisive for whether the whole reversal/confirmation debate is moot, and is squarely another locus's territory (the N1 confound-taxonomy investigation), not resolved here.
- A closer read of the 5 works citing PGS since 2024-2026 (found via OpenAlex, not fetched here due to budget) could reveal whether any of them already treats "search strategy is under-explored in offline BBO" as tested or contested — none looked, from titles/venues alone, like they would resolve this, but I did not verify by full-text read.
- Is there a *qualitative* (non-ANOVA) statement anywhere in the offline-MBO literature — e.g., in COMs, RoMA, or BDI papers, none deeply checked by this locus — that already asserts "surrogate quality matters more than optimizer choice" as an informal aside, the way Shahriari et al. does for online BO? If such a sentence exists even informally within offline MBO itself (not just online BO), it would further narrow Candidate A's novelty margin.
