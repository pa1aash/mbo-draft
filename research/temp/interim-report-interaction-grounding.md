# Interim report: interaction-term-buried-and-its-methodological-grounding

**Locus question:** Is there authoritative DOE/ANOVA-methodology grounding for (a) treating a crossed factorial's payoff as interaction estimation, (b) requiring a simple-effects decomposition once an interaction is significant, and (c) precedent for reporting/interpreting a component-by-component interaction in ML benchmarking — and does that grounding justify promoting the paper's buried η²_inter finding to a headline result?

**Flavor:** convergent

## What the corpus already said

The orchestrator's own artifact-level work (`findings-so-far.md`, "THE BURIED RESULT" and "WHAT THE INTERACTION ACTUALLY IS" sections) already established the numbers: η²_inter = 0.146–0.165 across all four engine corners, bootstrap CI excluding zero in all four (lower bounds 0.087–0.107), second-largest effect throughout, 4×–33× the optimizer main effect, 9.2× more stable across corners than the headline surrogate effect, and surviving bias correction at 0.134–0.156. The word "interaction" appears exactly once in `main.tex`, citing Moosbauer et al. (2022) for the negative claim that OFAT cannot detect interactions. The corpus's N6 novelty sweep separately established (independently re-confirmed below) that no offline-MBO or BO-benchmarking paper — Hutter 2014, Liang 2021, Moosbauer 2022, Design-Bench, RaM, COMs, RoMA, SOO-Bench, EXPObench, Bischl 2023 — reports and interprets a surrogate-class × optimizer-class interaction.

## (a) The positive DOE authority — found, fetched fresh, and better than expected

Two independent, ground-truth-tier, fully verified sources supply the positive framing the paper is missing, plus one corroborating-but-caveated third:

1. **NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods, §5.2.1.2, "One variable at a time"** ([[5212-one-variable-at-a-time]], `itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section2/pri212.htm`, public domain, fetched fresh this session). Verbatim, using the paper's own notation style (Y = X1 + X2 model vs. Y = X1 + X2 + X1*X2 model): *"An interactions model of this type has a 'twist' to the response surface. This means that the response Y1 to, say, X1 will be different depending on the setting of X2 (and vice-versa)... OFAT experimentation leaves us in the dark about factor interactions."* This is the exact positive mirror of the Moosbauer sentence already in the paper, from the same public-domain, section-numbered, citable source used elsewhere in this audit's own literature (COCO/BBOB precedent for the budget finding).
2. **George E. P. Box, "Do Interactions Matter?" (1989, Center for Quality and Productivity Improvement, Univ. of Wisconsin–Madison)** ([[do-interactions-matter-by-george-box]], `williamghunter.net`, fetched fresh, ground-truth tier). Box — co-author of *Statistics for Experimenters*, the query's own named canonical candidate — wrote this technical report specifically to argue that interactions are not a statistical curiosity but a practically decisive phenomenon, using Christer Hellstrand's SKF 2³ factorial bearing experiment: *"the life of a bearing can be increased fivefold if the two factors, outer ring osculation and inner ring heat treatment, are increased together... A likely explanation [for the delay in discovering this] is that, because most engineers have, until recently, employed only one factor at a time experimentation, interaction effects have been missed."* This is a stronger, more citable anchor than a generic textbook paraphrase: it is Box himself, making exactly the paper's argument, with a real industrial number (5×) that mirrors the shape of this paper's own claim (the GP–ensemble gap is conditional on the optimizer, not a stable property of the surrogate alone).
3. **Montgomery, *Design and Analysis of Experiments*, via the official SAS/JMP companion supplement** ([[montgomery-design-and-analysis-of-experiments-sasjmp-supplement-excerpt-ofat-vs]], Rushing/Karl/Wisnowski 2013, SAS Institute). Corroborating quote: *"A naïve, one-factor-at-a-time analysis would require many more trials than necessary. Additionally, it would not yield information about whether the relationship between a factor and the target depends on the values of other factors (commonly referred to as interaction effects between factors)."* **Caveat, stated honestly**: this is the SAS/JMP companion volume's own prose citing Montgomery's book for the underlying demonstration, not a verbatim sentence extracted from Montgomery's own Ch. 5 text (repeated attempts to reach Montgomery's own prose hit paywalls: O'Reilly 403, dokumen.pub 403, Google Books quota-exhausted). Use (1) and (2) as the load-bearing citations; cite Montgomery as the standard textbook reference for the general result, not for a verbatim quote.

## (b) Simple-effects requirement — is it required, and is it computable? Yes and yes.

**Methodological source, fetched fresh**: [[34-factorial-anova-simple-effects-statistics-libretexts]] (Kansas State Univ. EDCEP 917 course text, Statistics LibreTexts §3.4, "Factorial ANOVA – Simple Effects"). Verbatim: *"When researchers find an interaction it suggests that the main effects may be a bit misleading... Whenever an interaction is detected, researchers need to conduct additional analyses to determine where that interaction is coming from... it is only necessary to conduct these analyses when an interaction is present. When there is no interaction then the main effects will tell the complete and accurate story."* Critically, this source gives the exact cell count for **a 3×3 design — the paper's own grid shape (3 surrogates × 3 optimizers)**: *"a researcher using a 3×3 design with nine conditions would need to look at 2 main effects and 6 simple effects."* A second NIST source, [[5471-full-factorial-example]] (§5.4.7.1, "Full factorial example," a worked ceramics-grinding 2⁵ ANOVA), states the misleading-main-effects point even more bluntly and demonstrates the fix already: *"Table Speed plays a role in almost every significant interaction term, but is the least important main effect on its own. Note that large interactions can obscure main effects."* Its worked follow-up — re-running the reduced model separately at Direction = −1 vs. Direction = +1 and showing the interaction structure appears at one level and vanishes at the other — is a literal, textbook-sanctioned simple-effects analysis of exactly the same shape I ran below.

**I computed the formal simple-effects decomposition myself, from stored data, at effectively zero marginal cost.** Using `results/corners/corner_*.json` (the exact per-seed p100 file `bootstrap_eta.py` already loads), I computed η²_surr **restricted to each optimizer level**, using the paper's own shared 9-cell min–max normalizer (critical: NOT a re-fit 3-cell-only normalizer — see trap below), bootstrapped with the same task+seed resampling procedure already in the codebase:

| Corner | η²_surr \| perturb | η²_surr \| grad | η²_surr \| cma |
|---|---|---|---|
| off/off | 0.004 [0.001, 0.40]* | 0.666 [0.48, 0.94] | 0.814 [0.64, 0.99] |
| on/off | 0.004 [0.001, 0.026] | 0.388 [0.16, 0.90] | 0.763 [0.53, 0.99] |
| off/on | 0.020 [0.004, 0.70]* | 0.790 [0.61, 0.98] | 0.813 [0.64, 0.99] |
| **on/on (primary)** | **0.007 [0.001, 0.048]** | **0.735 [0.50, 0.98]** | **0.762 [0.53, 0.99]** |

*off/off and off/on retain the target-scaling confound (X1 off), which inflates ensemble-cell variance and widens these two CIs; the X1-corrected corners (on/off, on/on) tighten to [0.001,0.026]/[0.001,0.048].

This is a **formal, quantitative, bootstrap-CI'd confirmation of the paper's own informal 7/7 raw-units result, computed in the paper's own normalized units**, and it is sharper than the omnibus interaction η² because it directly answers the textbook simple-effects question — "is the surrogate main effect real at each level of the moderator" — rather than reporting only the aggregate interaction magnitude. Under perturbation the surrogate class explains essentially none of the variance (0.4–2%); under gradient or CMA it explains 39–81%. New analysis code only, zero new experiments, reusing data the paper's own pipeline already produced.

**A trap I found and avoided, worth stating explicitly if this table is added to the paper.** Re-normalizing to only the 3 cells within a single optimizer column (rather than keeping the shared 9-cell normalizer) inflates η²_surr|perturb to ≈0.73 — statistically indistinguishable from the grad/cma levels — because min–max rescaling to any 3 points always restores a full [0,1] spread regardless of the original magnitude of the gap. This is exactly the naive "textbook recipe" a less careful implementation of simple effects would run, and it would erase the very asymmetry the interaction is about. **A correct simple-effects computation must share the omnibus model's normalizer, not refit one per level** — a point the DOE literature above doesn't need to make explicit (because canonical simple-effects examples are usually on raw, unnormalized DVs) but that matters specifically because this paper's whole pipeline runs on a per-task normalized scale.

**Boundary condition, independently discovered**: the interaction is a synthetic-grid-specific result. `results/db_corners/db_analysis.json` (`corners_5task` and `corners_7task` keys — already computed by the paper's own pipeline, never surfaced in main.tex) gives η²_inter = 0.006–0.041 on Design-Bench, across both 5- and 7-task sets and all four corners — an order of magnitude below the synthetic grid's 0.146–0.165, and smaller than Design-Bench's *own* η²_opt (0.05–0.20) there. The paper's Design-Bench section (main.tex ~line 220) reports η²_surr and η²_opt but never η²_inter either, so the interaction is buried on both grids — but it is only large on one of them. Any promotion must be scoped explicitly to the synthetic grid.

## (c) ML-benchmarking precedent — a sharper claim than "none exists"

Refetching was not needed here; the sharper answer was already sitting in an existing vault note from the width sweep. [[an-efficient-approach-for-assessing-hyperparameter-importance-icml-2014-hutterho]] (Hutter et al. 2014, the canonical fANOVA paper) records **20 grep hits for "interaction"** — e.g., *"interaction effect between S and κ"* — because functional ANOVA, by construction, decomposes variance into main effects *and* pairwise/higher-order interaction terms among hyperparameters, and Hutter et al. report and interpret these routinely, within a single algorithm's hyperparameter space.

**So the precise, defensible claim is not "no one in ML has ever reported an interaction term."** It is: **interaction-term reporting is standard, expected practice within the exact fANOVA lineage this paper's own design descends from — just never between "which surrogate model class" and "which search/optimizer strategy" as two independently manipulated pipeline components.** Van Rijn & Hutter (2018, [[hyperparameter-importance-across-datasets-kdd-2018-van-rijn-and-hutter-arxiv1710]]) extends importance analysis across datasets but analyzes each algorithm's space in isolation (SVM, then RF, then Adaboost, never jointly), never crossing model class against optimizer. Moosbauer (2022) names ANOVA/fANOVA as the standard tool, declines it for cost, and reports zero interaction numbers. This is a **strictly stronger framing than a bare novelty claim**: it converts a contestable "we are first to report this interaction" into "interaction-term reporting is normal in this literature's own closest analog; ours is the first application of it to the specific factor-pair every offline-MBO/BO benchmark (Design-Bench, RaM, COMs, RoMA, SOO-Bench, Liang 2021, EXPObench — all independently grep-confirmed zero hits for factorial/ANOVA/interaction language by the N6 audit) has always bundled into one atomic method instead of crossing."

## (d) The honest counter — the ANOVA-methodology side

**Two fresh fetches, one revealing a genuine and under-appreciated subtlety.**

- **McClelland & Judd (1993), "Statistical difficulties of detecting interactions and moderator effects," *Psychological Bulletin* 114(2):376–390** (DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.114.2.376, ~3,190 citations; verified via OpenAlex metadata/abstract, full text paywalled — recorded as a limitation). Their central, highly-cited claim: *"tests of interactions in field studies will often have less than 20% of the efficiency of optimal experimental tests."* **On a careful read this cuts for the paper, not against it.** McClelland & Judd's whole thesis is that interactions are chronically underpowered in *field/observational* studies with restricted-range, uncontrolled moderators — not in *designed experiments* with manipulated, maximally separated factor levels. This paper's grid is exactly the "optimal experimental test" case: three surrogate classes and three optimizers chosen for architectural distinctness (GP posterior vs. ensemble disagreement; local hill-climbing vs. global CMA-ES), not sampled from a narrow observational range. A naive citation of "interactions are hard to detect" would misapply a field-study-specific result to a controlled factorial.
- **Sommet, Weissman, Cheutin & Elliot (2023), "How Many Participants Do I Need to Test an Interaction?", *Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science*** (DOI 10.1177/25152459231178728, open access, fetched fresh with full text — [[how-many-participants-do-i-need-to-test-an-interaction-conducting-an-appropriate]]). Their preregistered metastudy of 159 published psychology studies found: *"The overall median power to detect interactions of a typical size is .18"* and *"only 4% of the studies reported an adequate power analysis."* This is the genuine, current, high-authority counterweight: most reported interactions in the psychology literature are badly underpowered, and interaction effect sizes are structurally smaller than main effects by construction (their own worked example: a d=0.35 main effect implies only a d=0.175 "fully attenuated" interaction — half the size — because an interaction is a *difference of differences*). Sommet et al.'s own reference list independently corroborates the point with Gelman's widely-circulated 2018 blog title, verbatim: "You need 16 times the sample size to estimate an interaction than to estimate a main effect."

**Why this honest counter does not overturn the recommendation, read carefully.** The Sommet et al. finding is about *typical* interactions being small relative to main effects and hence underpowered by default. This paper's interaction is the opposite pattern: η²_inter (0.146–0.165) is **larger than one whole main effect** (η²_opt = 0.005–0.036, 4×–33× smaller) and only modestly below the other (η²_surr = 0.28–0.45) — a much larger interaction, relative to its own main effects, than the "typical" case these methodology papers are warning about. Combined with McClelland & Judd's point that manipulated factorial designs are comparatively well-powered for interactions, and combined with the fact that the interaction's CI excludes zero independently in *four separate engine corners* and survives the same bias-correction procedure the paper already applies to its main effects (Mandatory Fix 9, corpus-established, not re-derived here) — the honest reading is that this is an unusually well-evidenced interaction by the field's own base rates, not a fragile one. The one qualification that *does* survive from this literature, and should be stated in the paper if the interaction is promoted: n=7 tasks is still a small replicate count, and the paper should say the interaction is established *on this grid* rather than claim general portability — precisely the same epistemic caveat the paper already applies to its main-effect corner intervals.

## Committed position

The paper should **promote the interaction from an uninterpreted table entry to a stated, cited, quantitatively decomposed finding in the main text of Section 3 (immediately after or alongside the optimizer-null discussion), scoped explicitly to the synthetic grid, and should NOT promote it to the abstract without adding that scope qualifier.** Concretely: (1) cite NIST/SEMATECH §5.2.1.2 and Box (1989) — both fetched and quote-verified this session — paired with the existing Moosbauer negative citation, converting "OFAT can't do this" into "OFAT can't do this, and here is what a design that can do it found"; (2) add the simple-effects table computed above (η²_surr conditioned on optimizer level, using the shared normalizer, bootstrapped — new analysis code only, no new experiments, ~30 minutes of engineering given `bootstrap_eta.py` already provides the resampling machinery); (3) state the Design-Bench boundary condition (η²_inter = 0.006–0.041 there, an order of magnitude smaller) so the claim is not overgeneralized; (4) carry forward the same n=7-tasks caveat the paper already applies to every other corner-decomposition number. This is the single highest-leverage, lowest-cost fix identified across this audit: every number already exists in stored artifacts, the missing piece was citable authority, and that authority is now in hand with verified quotes and section numbers.

- **Position:** Promote the interaction to an interpreted main-text finding (not the abstract headline) with the NIST §5.2.1.2 / Box (1989) positive citation, the new per-optimizer-level simple-effects table, and an explicit synthetic-grid-only scope qualifier.
- **Confidence:** High (>80%) that the interaction should be interpreted and cited with positive DOE authority rather than left as a bare table number — the citations are now verified, section-numbered, and directly on point, and three independent primary-source checks (bootstrap JSON, supplement table, bias-correction) all confirm the underlying numbers are solid. Medium (~65%) on abstract-vs-main-text placement specifically — that is a space/framing judgment call the orchestrator and other loci (esp. the normalization-fragility locus and the AAAI-venue-fit locus) should weigh in on, since promoting a number to the abstract carries reviewer-visibility costs the main-text placement does not.
- **Boundary conditions:** This holds for the **synthetic 7-task grid** in all four engine corners; on Design-Bench the interaction is small (0.006–0.041) and subordinate to η²_opt there, so any main-text or abstract statement must say "on the synthetic grid" explicitly. It also assumes the simple-effects table is computed with the shared 9-cell normalizer, not a re-fit per-level normalizer — the latter would produce a false near-null result (η²_surr|perturb ≈ 0.73, indistinguishable from grad/cma) as I demonstrated above.
- **What would change this position:** If a sibling investigator's normalization-fragility check (the dialectical locus on min–max normalization) showed the *direction* of the interaction reverses or collapses under a rank-based or per-task z-score normalizer — not just that its *magnitude* is normalizer-sensitive, which is already conceded — that would undercut promoting it as a stable finding. Per the corpus's own cross-check machinery (already used for the β=0 pessimism claim, giving "the same 0.61 ratio"), this is a cheap check the paper should run before finalizing the promotion; I did not have budget in this locus to run it myself and flag it as the one open dependency on a sibling locus. If that check fails, the correct fallback is exactly what the corpus's dialectical finding already recommends: report the interaction as real-in-raw-units (the 7/7 result, immune to normalization) rather than by its η² magnitude.
- **Evidence weight:** For promotion — 2 ground-truth-tier DOE sources fetched and quote-verified this session (NIST, Box), 1 corroborating textbook source with a stated caveat (Montgomery via SAS/JMP), 1 simple-effects methodology source with an exact 3×3-design match (LibreTexts/Kansas State), 1 existing vault note establishing ML precedent for interaction-reporting generally (Hutter fANOVA), 3 independent primary-source re-derivations confirming the paper's own numbers (bootstrap JSON, supplement table, bias correction), and 1 new original computation (the formal simple-effects table) that strengthens rather than merely repeats the existing informal result. Against unscoped promotion — 1 boundary condition independently discovered (Design-Bench's small interaction), 2 methodological-caution sources (McClelland & Judd; Sommet et al.) that on close reading argue for cautious optimism rather than suspicion, given this is a manipulated-factorial design with an unusually large (not typical-sized) interaction.
